### Issue 1: Version-dominance proof under-justifies that the chain's root document has length #A+2
**ASN-0103, Effect One ("Strict advance over every prior address under A"), subsequent case**: "That root is some true document `d_i = [A,0,i]`: the chain's first k=1 fork required its operand ∈ E_doc, and that operand — a length-#A+2 document beneath A — is exactly `[A,0,i]` with `i ≥ 1`."

**Problem**: The cited justification — "operand ∈ E_doc" — does not establish the asserted conclusion "a length-#A+2 document." This ASN's central thesis is precisely that `Document(·)`/`E_doc` membership does **not** pin length: a version `v` satisfies `Document(v) ∧ parent(v) = A` yet has length `≥ #A+3`, which is exactly why the length filter on `D_A` exists. Since `E_doc` contains versions, an operand of a k=1 fork can be a version (`inc(v', 1)` forks a version off a version, with `v' ∈ E_doc`). So "operand ∈ E_doc" alone admits a length-`≥ #A+3` operand. The step that actually forces length `#A+2` — that the *first* k=1 fork in the chain is preceded by no version, so its operand is reached from `A` only by `inc(A, 2)` then `inc(·, 0)` steps and is therefore a document-chain entity of length `#A+2` — is omitted. The proof uses the weak fact (`E_doc` membership) to reach a conclusion that requires the strong fact, on the very distinction the ASN was built to make.

**Required**: Add the explicit inference that, being the *first* k=1 fork in the ancestry chain, the operand has no version among its predecessors and is thus a true document-chain entity `[A, 0, i]` of length `#A+2`. The identical gap appears in the `D_A = ∅` case ("that operand — a length-#A+2 document beneath A — is the root document `d_i = [A,0,i]`").
