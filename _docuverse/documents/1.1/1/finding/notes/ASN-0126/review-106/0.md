### Issue 1: Retraction and sterilization are analyzed only for Binary-registered [R]; the framework leaves [R]'s registration free
**ASN-0126, Retraction as an attributed Binary / Range sterilization**: "To obtain a gated retraction an app registers R as **Binary** and routes through the one-span wrapper" … "fix a retraction tuple `(b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ` with single-span to-endset `G' = {(g, ℓ)}` — when R is registered Binary, the only to-form a gate-cleared R-tuple carries (P6)"
**Problem**: Nothing in the framework pins [R]'s shape. The registry is app-composed, any coverage class may carry any of the three shapes, and ASN-0086's discipline-commitment device — the layer-level convention that did exactly this kind of pinning — has no analog here. Three legal configurations are therefore admitted but never analyzed: (a) *[R] unregistered* — every `Emit_R` fails precondition (i), so `L_R^Σ = ∅` at every reachable state, `nullified ≡ ∅`, `A_K^Σ = L_K^Σ`, and the substrate is permanently retraction-free with C2/C3 vacuous — a significant framework-level consequence for an app-facing vocabulary, stated nowhere; (b) *Unary-R* — the gate forces `G = ∅`, `coverage(G') = ∅`, so retraction is registered yet inert; (c) *Multi-R* — gate-cleared R-tuples carry multi-span to-endsets, P6 no longer yields the single-span hypothesis, B becomes a finite union of consecutive blocks, and Corollary RangeSterilization's exhaustion arithmetic in (ii) does not apply. The corollary's own question — "how much *future* emission a single such tuple can poison" — is posed generally and answered only under Binary-R. Showing the Binary case does not establish the others.
**Required**: Either state a framework commitment mirroring ASN-0086's device — if [R] is registered, its shape is Binary — and note that the sterilization analysis is complete under it; or close the three corners explicitly (one sentence each for (a) and (b); for (c), state the corollary for finite unions of blocks or scope it out with justification).
