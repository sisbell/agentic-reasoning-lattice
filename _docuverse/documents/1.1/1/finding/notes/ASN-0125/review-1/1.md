### Issue 2: Df-SUCC applies `old`/`new` to all of `S^Σ`, but the accessors are total only on schema-conforming claims
**ASN-0125, Df-SUCC (Successor relations)**: "At any state `Σ`: `succ_h(Σ) = {(old(e), new(e)) : e ∈ S^Σ}`"
**Problem**: EL4 introduces `addr`, `new`, `old` as "total accessors on disciplined claims" — well-definedness (singleton `coverage ∩ dom(Σ.L)`) is established only for claims conforming to Df-DISC(ii). `S^Σ` is the bare coverage-class slice: at undisciplined reachable states it can contain arity-3 `[K_sup]`-class tuples whose endsets are multi-span, cover several link addresses, or cover none, leaving `old(e)`/`new(e)` undefined — yet the comprehension is explicitly offered "at any state." The ill-definedness propagates: EL5(a) asserts `succ_h(Σ) ⊆ succ_h(Σ')` for *every* `Σ →* Σ'`; EL11(b) defines `in(y, Σ)`/`out(x, Σ)` via `old`/`new` over `S^Σ` and identifies them with `Observe_{K_sup}` results (an identity valid only when every slice member is schema-conforming); Df-CUR builds `reach_o`/`current` on `succ_o`.
**Required**: Restrict the defining comprehension to schema-conforming members — e.g. `succ_h(Σ) = {(y, x) : e ∈ S^Σ ∧ e conforms to Df-DISC(ii) with witnesses x, y}` — noting that EL4's proof is per-claim and needs no whole-state hypothesis, so the restricted relations are total at every reachable state and coincide with the current definitions at disciplined states. Apply the same restriction (or an explicit standing discipline assumption) to `in`, `out`, `reach_o`, and `current`.
