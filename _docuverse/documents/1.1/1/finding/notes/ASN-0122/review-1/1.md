### Issue 2: X9's justification overstates what the discarded pairs reveal
**ASN-0122, "Which Positions May Participate"**: lead-in "the link subspace, if admitted, would contribute only what is decidable from the operand syntax alone"; X9's conclusion "discards only pairs whose membership was already decidable without consulting the arrangements — no information about the documents."
**Problem**: The forced diagonal is `{((d,v),(d,v)) : (d,v) a link instance in P ∩ Q}`, and whether `(d, v)` is an instance at all is arrangement information — `v ∈ dom(M(d))` is exactly what the clipping consults. A caller supplying link-subspace spans would receive, via the diagonal, precisely which of those positions are currently arranged — document information, not derivable from operand syntax. What the three sub-arguments (CL-OWN, S3★+SD, CL-UNIQ) actually establish is narrower and sufficient: for any pair involving a link instance, the `corr` predicate reduces to instance equality *without consulting the resolution map*, so the link subspace contributes no cross-position or cross-document correspondence. The proof is right; the gloss it is wrapped in is false as stated, and X12 leans on that gloss ("lossless by X9").
**Required**: Restate the lead-in and X9's conclusion precisely: the discarded contribution is the forced self-diagonal, determined by `P ∩ Q` independently of `res`; it carries arrangement-domain information but no correspondence (sharing) information, which is the sense in which the content-subspace restriction is lossless for this operation.
