### Issue 1: "The Image Set" — claim stated, walked back, then caveated in one chained sentence
**ASN-0099, "The Image Set"**: "the runs intersecting R cover R ∩ dom(Σ.M(d)) disjointly (B1+B2, ASN-0058) — a run's full V-extent may reach outside R when R cuts it, so it is the run-intersections V(βⱼ) ∩ R that partition R ∩ dom(Σ.M(d)), not the full V-extents — but their I-extents may coincide when distinct V-positions share content (S5, ASN-0036; M13/M14, ASN-0058) — the set union absorbs any such overlap."

**Problem**: The sentence asserts a partition claim, then immediately self-corrects it in a middle em-dash clause ("a run's full V-extent may reach outside R … not the full V-extents"), then appends a coincidence caveat, then a closing absorber clause — four propositions on three em-dashes, one of which exists only to retract the first. This is the relocated-correction pattern the anti-bloat pass targets: a prior finding's fix was inserted inline rather than rewriting the sentence, so the reader must skip past the walk-back to reconstruct the actual claim. The whole paragraph is also descriptive color — `image` is already fully fixed by its comprehension — so the tangle adds parse cost without advancing any result.

**Required**: Rewrite as a single forward claim, e.g.: "The image of a contiguous s_C V-span is the union of the I-extents of the mapping blocks whose V-extents meet R, each restricted to V(βⱼ) ∩ R (B1+B2, ASN-0058); these I-extents may coincide where content is shared (M13/M14, ASN-0058), and the set union absorbs any overlap." State the restriction once, in the carrier, rather than as a mid-sentence retraction of the unrestricted version.
