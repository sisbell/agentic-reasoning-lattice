### Issue 1: The "precisely two-fold" atomicity scope is asymmetric between R and C
**ASN-0100, §Atomicity and Canonical Order (the K.ρ-ordering paragraph) and §Operation's Formal Contract / INS.pre (Composite atomicity assumption)**:

The ASN justifies *excluding R from the protected atomicity scope* by an interleaving-composite argument: "Were INSERT to fire K.ρ(a_k,d) before K.μ⁺ places a_k ... another composite's elementaries may legally interleave at that point, and that composite's own R'∖R would then pick up the unmatched (a_k,d) by set difference and fail J1'★." It fixes this by ordering every K.ρ after the K.μ⁺ placement, concluding "R needs no atomicity protection" and that the required scope is "precisely two-fold" — (i) A_C(d)'s chain emission state and (ii) M(d)'s text subspace.

**Problem**: The identical reasoning applied to `dom(C)` is left unaddressed, and unlike the R case it *cannot* be repaired by reordering within the canonical decomposition. The canonical decomposition fires "n successive K.α firings" (step 1) and only later "one K.μ⁺" (step 3). Between step 1 and step 3 the freshly allocated `a_k ∈ dom(C)` are **unplaced**. By exactly the set-difference argument the ASN uses for R: an interleaving composite B whose boundary falls in the K.α→K.μ⁺ window has `a_k ∈ dom(Σ_B'.C) ∖ dom(Σ_B.C)` with `a_k` placed in no arrangement, so B's J0 (AllocationRequiresPlacement) fails — and B's J1★/P7a (provenance for the new C entry) likewise, since K.ρ comes still later. The ASN brings interleaving-composite coupling violations into scope to motivate the K.ρ ordering, but then ignores the symmetric C/placement exposure that the "all K.α first" structure creates.

This forces a dilemma the ASN does not resolve:
- If cross-composite coupling violations matter (as the K.ρ argument assumes), then `dom(C)`/placement-consistency must also be a protected resource — contradicting "precisely two-fold" — or the decomposition must interleave each K.α with its K.μ⁺ placement (mirroring the K.ρ fix), which the stated canonical order does not do.
- If cross-composite coupling violations do *not* matter for INSERT (only INSERT's own post-state determinism does), then the elaborate K.ρ-ordering justification and the "R needs no atomicity protection" reasoning are moot and should be removed.

**Required**: Resolve the asymmetry. Either (a) extend the protected scope to cover C/placement consistency (and re-derive the minimal scope honestly — likely three-fold, or framed as "no interleaving on d's allocator chains and arrangement, plus boundary-consistent ordering"), or (b) adopt an allocation-then-placement interleaving in the canonical decomposition so that no intermediate carries an unplaced/unrecorded `a_k`, paralleling the K.ρ fix, or (c) drop the interleaving-composite framing entirely and scope the atomicity argument strictly to INSERT's own post-state determinism, deleting the K.ρ cross-composite rationale. As written, INS.atomicity and INS.pre are mutually inconsistent on whether other composites' couplings are INSERT's concern.
