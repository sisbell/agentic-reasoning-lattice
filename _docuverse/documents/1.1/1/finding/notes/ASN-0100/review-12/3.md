### Issue 4: Worked example's non-tight alternative uses informal terminology
**ASN-0100, §A Worked Example, Non-tight alternative**: "If tight(e_1, Σ_{e_1}) does not hold — for example, e_1 was incorporated with a span whose coverage {a₂, a₃, a₄} is a non-canonical or sparsely populated tumbler interval at Σ_{e_1} — then LP19a does not apply, and a freshly allocated a_{new0} or a_{new1} may land in coverage(e_1)."
**Problem**: "Non-canonical or sparsely populated" conflates two distinct tightness-failure modes from ASN-0098's `tight` definition: (a) a span (s, ℓ) with ℓ not an ordinal displacement of length #s is unconditionally non-tight (per LP-Fin's infinite F-candidate count for non-canonical spans), and (b) a canonical span (s, δ(n, #s)) is non-tight when some F-candidate in [s, s ⊕ δ(n, #s)) is missing from dom(C) ∪ dom(L) at Σ_e. The two failure modes have different operational character — (a) is a span-shape property, (b) is a state-population property — and the worked example should name them with the foundation's terminology rather than the colloquial "sparsely populated."
**Required**: Replace the informal phrasing with explicit reference to the canonical-form clause and the F-candidate-coverage clause of ASN-0098's tight definition, naming one concrete failure mode (e.g., "if a₄ had not yet been allocated at Σ_{e_1}, then the canonical span (a₂, δ(3, #a₂)) carries a₄ as an F-candidate missing from dom(Σ_{e_1}.C), making the span non-tight").
