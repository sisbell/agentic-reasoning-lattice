### Issue 1: W5's "no re-delivery" is scoped wrongly — the boxed claim is false under its own sole hypothesis (clause 1), and contradicts W9b
**ASN-0108, W5**: the box states
> "*No re-delivery* (**unconditional**): no already-seen link that matches in both the cursor-setting and the resume state is delivered again."

and the scoping paragraph fixes the matching-scope as
> "*no re-delivery* ranges over links matching across a single cursor's transition (both its states)"

while the *proof* that follows establishes something strictly narrower:
> "by a cursor-advance induction: applying clause 1 at each visited cursor, with a **continuously-matching** delivered link as the other link, that delivered link stays at or below every later cursor…"

**Problem**: The proof requires the delivered link to match at *every* intervening state (continuously). The claim asserts no-re-delivery for any link matching at *both states of one cursor's transition* — which is satisfied by a link that orphaned and resurrected earlier in the pass. For such a link the cursor-advance induction does not apply, and the broad claim fails. The sole stated hypothesis is clause 1, and clause 1 does **not** preclude the orphan-resurrect-above-cursor manoeuvre (it quantifies only over links matching in both states of a transition, so a link is *out of scope* exactly at the transitions where it orphans and where it resurrects).

Counterexample — content-position key (non-permanent, so a resurrection can land above a cursor), `N = 1`, links `b, x, y, w`, key = current V-position of matched content:

- Σ₁ `b↦1, x↦2, y↦3, w↦4`. **Call 1** (⊥) → `{b}`, cursor `c₁=b`. *Seen: b.*
- **Call 2** (cursor `b`, Σ₁) → `{x}`, cursor `c₂=x`. *Seen: b, x.*
- `b` orphans (it is below cursor `x`, so `κ(x)` stays computable). Σ₃ `x↦2, y↦3, w↦4`.
- **Call 3** (cursor `x`, Σ₃) → `{y}`, cursor `c₃=y`. *Seen: b, x, y.*
- `b` resurrects at V-position 5 (above `w↦4`). Σ₄ `x↦2, y↦3, w↦4, b↦5`.
- **Call 4** (cursor `y`, Σ₄): `After(y)={w(4), b(5)}`, least is `w` → `{w}`, cursor `c₄=w`. `b` sits undelivered. *Seen: b, x, y, w.*
- Σ₅ = Σ₄. **Call 5** (cursor `w`, Σ₅): `After(w)={b(5)}` → `{b}`. **`b` is re-delivered.**

Clause 1 holds at *every* held cursor: at `c₁` and `c₄` nothing moves; at `c₂` `b` matches only at the pre-state (orphan) so it is out of clause-1 scope, and `x,y,w` keep their positions; at `c₃` `b` matches only at the post-state (resurrect) so again out of scope. Yet across `c₄`'s transition (Σ₄→Σ₅) `b` matches in **both** states — it is squarely inside the box's scope — and `b` is already-seen (Call 1) and delivered again. The box's guarantee is violated with clause 1 intact.

This is also internally inconsistent with W9b, which uses the *correct* narrow form ("That a continuously-matching delivered link never re-enters a later After is W5's cursor-advance induction") and explicitly admits the very re-delivery the box denies: "A delivered link's only way back into the tail is therefore to leave Match and resurrect," and "Under *either permanent key* … no previously-delivered link re-enters the tail … so every link is delivered at most once" — i.e. under a *non-permanent* key satisfying clause 1, a previously-delivered link **can** re-enter and be re-delivered.

**Required**: Narrow no-re-delivery's matching-scope to *continuously-matching from delivery through the resume* — identical to no-skip's scope ("matching continuously across the intervening states"). The two guarantees then differ only in the genuine asymmetry the note already identifies (no-skip needs termination, no-re-delivery does not); the spurious matching-scope difference disappears. Propagate the fix to the scoping paragraph and to the Claims-Introduced row for W5 ("no re-delivery of a **both-states** matcher" → "of a continuously-matching matcher"). Either that, or add a permanent-key hypothesis — but since W9b already shows permanence is what actually buys "delivered at most once," the clean fix is the scope narrowing, leaving permanence as the key-property that *additionally* extends the guarantee to non-continuously-matching links.
