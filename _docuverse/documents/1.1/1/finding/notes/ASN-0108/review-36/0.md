### Issue 1: "matched-content key" and "content-position key" conflated in W6
**ASN-0108, "What κ is" intro vs W6 (body and claims table)**: The taxonomy is introduced as three *coordinate, distinctly-named* candidates — "We call this the **matched-content key**" (Gregory's, permanent least I-address) and "The third candidate is **mutable**: the **content-position key**, keyed on the matched content's *current V-position*." But W6 then writes: "The consequence under *either* matched-content key — Gregory's I-address key as much as the V-position foil — is the *blind spot*," and the claims table: "a matched-content key (least covered I-address, or V-position) is not allocation-monotone."
**Problem**: W6 stretches "matched-content key" to subsume the *content-position key*, which the intro defined as a separate, third, mutable candidate. This collides with the note's central three-key distinction exactly where W6 is asserting that "the two *permanent* keys part" — the content-position key is *not* one of the two permanent keys, yet W6 files it under "matched-content key." A reader tracking the flat three-name taxonomy must silently re-interpret it as a family. W6a, by contrast, uses the umbrella "matched-content boundary" while keeping "matched-content I-address key" and "content-position key" distinct — so the note is inconsistent with itself.
**Required**: Pick one scheme. Either establish "matched-content key" up front as a *family* with two variants (permanent I-address; mutable V-position = content-position) and state it once, or keep the three flat names and have W6 say "either content-drawn key (matched-content I-address or content-position)" rather than "either matched-content key."
