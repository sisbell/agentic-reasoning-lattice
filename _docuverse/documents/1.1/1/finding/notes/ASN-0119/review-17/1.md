### Issue 2: The contiguity analysis builds two overlapping taxonomies behind doubled forward-pointing framing
**ASN-0119, "Links"**: After P7c the note announces one taxonomy — "that precondition is genuinely *only sufficient*, and in two distinct senses, each exhibited by an example below: confinement is *not necessary*... and confinement does *not* entail a single-span result" — gives two examples, then announces a *second* taxonomy — "A contiguous footprint survives as contiguous exactly when `π` lays its region-pieces down adjacently. This holds when... it fails when... **We exhibit all three behaviours** on the worked pivot above" — and gives three more.

**Problem**: The two framings ("two senses" / "three behaviours") set up overlapping case analyses of the same phenomenon, and the reader must reconcile them to follow the actual result. The example coverage overlaps as a consequence: the "confinement is not necessary" example (`{B, E}`, discontiguous→contiguous) and the "Straddling, contiguity preserved" example (`{B, C, D, E}`, contiguous→contiguous) demonstrate the same mechanism — a straddling footprint over two relocated blocks lands contiguous because the relocated pieces re-abut. The note's own gloss on `{B, C, D, E}`, "*both* spanned blocks relocate; the exterior is not involved," holds verbatim of `{B, E}` too. The examples are not the problem (concrete examples are welcome); the doubled meta-framing that motivates redundant ones is.

**Required**: Collapse to a single taxonomy of contiguity outcomes — within-region (run structure preserved, P7c), straddle-of-relocated-blocks that re-abut (contiguous), and the two fragmentation routes (fixed-exterior-meets-relocated; partial coverage) — with one example per distinct behaviour. Drop the duplicated framing sentences and the `{B,E}`/`{B,C,D,E}` overlap.
