### Issue 1: A2's zero-step linearization clause is not reconciled with multi-access reads
**ASN-0134, A1 / A2 / §8**: A1 enrolls `stale` as a single zero-step operation — "a *read-only query* — an `Observe_K`, or any of ASN-0128's behavioral *reads* (D1–D4; BH1–BH3; BH4's `age`/`stale` ...), each committing no `→_sh` step". A2 then says "A zero-step operation has `lin(op)` equal to the index of the state it reads." But §8 establishes that `stale` is intrinsically multi-access: "So `stale` is a multi-read whenever its active slice is non-empty: at member-home count `N ≥ 1` it costs `N + 1 ≥ 2` bounded accesses."
**Problem**: A2's "the index of the state it reads" (singular) has no referent for `stale`, which §8 shows reads `N+1` committed states at distinct indices. The three claims are inconsistent unless A2 is silently read as restricted to single-bounded-access reads — but A1 classes `stale` as one zero-step operation, and A2 quantifies over *all* zero-step operations. The entire V0/V2 distinction turns on single-vs-multi access, yet A2 (which precedes it) assigns every zero-step operation one read index.
**Required**: Restrict A2's single-index clause to single-bounded-access reads, and state that multi-access read operations (`stale`, cross-type joins) lack a single `lin` and are governed by V0/V2 — making the A1→A2→§8 division of labor explicit rather than contradictory.
