### Issue 5: §9 and G0 carry essays justifying what the contract omits and why a non-load-bearing clause is kept
**ASN-0134, §9 ("Two clauses are conspicuous by what they omit ...") and G0**: §9 — "There is no global *allocation*-serialization clause ... And there is no registry-locking clause ... There is, equally, no document-registration clause ..."; "Clause 6 ... is *not* load-bearing ... We keep it not for minimality but so the contract names every consistency obligation an implementer might fear they must discharge"; G0 — "The omission is not an oversight but a consequence of the note's central result."
**Problem**: Defensive justification of composition. The drop-one minimality analysis ("drop 1 and reads tear; drop 2 and same-home allocations collide; ...") is genuine and earns its place. But the surrounding essay — what is *not* a clause, and a paragraph defending keeping a clause that is admittedly redundant — is apologetics. "The omission is not an oversight but ..." defends a design choice rather than advancing a claim.
**Required**: Keep the drop-one counterexample list (it is the minimality proof). Cut the "conspicuous by what they omit" inventory to one sentence noting clauses 7 and 8 are the only non-per-home obligations and that registration/registry need none. Either drop clause 6 (the note itself says it is not load-bearing) or keep it with a one-line note, without the paragraph defending the keep. In G0, delete "The omission is not an oversight but a consequence" — the SC-vs-serializability distinction stands on its own derivation.
