### Issue 4: B2's `tip`/`chain` are ill-defined under branching and cycles, which the note's own text shows are possible
**ASN-0128, B2 (transitive-closure)**: "`tip(addr) → addr` (forward-walk to the maximal element of the chain rooted at addr…), `chain(addr) → ordered list of addrs`."
**Problem**: The note itself stresses (B3, D3) that Binary fixes each *tuple's* `|G| = 1`, "not the number of active tuples per source, so functionality in F is an app convention, not a shape guarantee." Then the active K-graph may branch — two active tuples with the same source coverage and different targets — and "the chain rooted at addr" is not a chain; `tip` returning a single address is ill-defined. Worse, nothing prevents cycles: emit `(F ∋ a, G ∋ b)` and `(F ∋ b, G ∋ a)` — both gate-pass, neither is I0-equal to the other, both land active — and the forward walk diverges; no termination argument exists (L-fin gives finiteness of the tuple set, not acyclicity of the walk). B3's `target_of` handles exactly this multiplicity with an explicit `⊥`; B2's predicates handle nothing. Since `supersedes` ships with B2 on every substrate (S2), this is not an exotic corner — it is the shipped configuration.
**Required**: Either restrict B2's predicates to states where the active K-graph is functional and acyclic (with a stated verdict — `⊥`, error — outside that domain), or define them on the general finite digraph (set of reachable sinks; cycle detection) with a termination argument.
