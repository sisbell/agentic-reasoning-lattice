### Issue 3: AD's two-regime classification contradicts the definitions of `is_in_chain` and `target_of`
**ASN-0128, Denotation and views**: "*Membership predicates* (`is_K`, `is_filtered`, `is_in_chain`) take an address argument and test coverage — `addr ∈ coverage(F)` — total over all tuples, address-denoting or not"
**Problem**: B2 defines `is_in_chain(addr, target)` as "`target ∈ chain(addr)`", and `chain()` enumerates *denoted* vertices — exact addresses, not coverages. An extension `y` of a chain element `x` satisfies the coverage reading (`x ≼ y`) but not list membership; the two definitions disagree, and `is_in_chain` is not "total over all tuples" in the coverage sense, since non-denoting edges never enter the walk at all. In the same denotation regime, B3's `target_of` parenthetical "(`addrs(G) = {y}`: one span by Binary, denoting one address by AD)" overclaims: AD guarantees denotation only for *surface-emitted* endsets; a raw-emitted Binary tuple's single G-span may be non-unit-depth, giving `addrs(G) = ∅` — a case the ⊥ clause ("⊥ when none or several", which counts tuples, not spans) does not cover. `targets_keyed` also leaves unstated whether ⊥-valued types are omitted from the returned map.
**Required**: Reclassify `is_in_chain` as enumeration-derived (or redefine it by coverage in B2 — pick one and say so); condition `target_of`'s parenthetical on an address-denoting G and assign the non-denoting case its verdict (presumably ⊥, per AD's omission rule); state `targets_keyed`'s treatment of ⊥ entries.
