### Issue 5: `retract_stale`'s harmlessness argument cites dedup in cases where dedup does not fire
**ASN-0128, Behaviors (B4)**: "a target already retracted — by interleaving, or earlier in the batch — is unproblematic, the repeat wrapper call deduplicating to the existing retraction tuple by S3's idem identity."
**Problem**: I0-identity for R is (retracting-document subtree, target subtree) — S3's own observation ("idem identity degenerates in F: the from-fill is per-document"). A target already retracted by an interleaved writer from a *different* document is not I0-equal to the batch's call, so the call deposits a fresh tuple — no dedup occurs. Likewise, if the prior retraction tuple has itself been nullified (retraction-of-retraction), it is absent from `A_R`, the dedup check misses (I2), and a fresh deposit occurs. Both outcomes are harmless, but for a reason the text does not give: any admitted unit-depth wrapper call on an existing link address is contract-conforming and cannot sterilize (DR). As written, the justification is false in two of the three sub-cases it claims to cover.
**Required**: Replace the blanket dedup justification with the case split: same-document re-retraction of an active retraction dedups (hit); the different-document and nullified-prior cases deposit a fresh, P-tgt-valid, unit-depth retraction — redundant but harmless under DR.
