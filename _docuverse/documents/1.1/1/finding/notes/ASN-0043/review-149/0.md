### Issue 1: Forward-reference meta-prose deferring to L0b/L1c is repeated across L0, L1a, and the Notational convention
**ASN-0043, L0 (paragraph after the invariant)**: "Although L0b and L1c are stated below for expository reasons, they are logically prior to L0: every link address is T4-valid, so `E(a)₁` exists and `subspace_I(a)` is well-defined on all of `dom(Σ.L)`."

**ASN-0043, L1a (paragraph after the home definition)**: "The fact that closes this on `dom(Σ.L)` is **L0b** ... derived from the axiom **L1c** ...; both are logically prior to L1a despite their later presentation. Citing L0b/L1c, `home(a)` is well-defined on every `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` ..."

**Problem**: This is forward-reference accretion of exactly the kind the anti-bloat classifier targets. The same downstream location (L0b/L1c) is deferred to in two separate sections, and both paragraphs justify the document ordering itself ("stated below for expository reasons," "logically prior despite later presentation") rather than advancing the argument. The two paragraphs say the same thing in different words: each establishes that `subspace_I`/`home` are well-defined on `dom(Σ.L)` because every link address is T4-valid, a fact owned by L1c. A precise reader must skip past the ordering apologetics to follow the actual claims. The root cause is structural: L0 and L1a depend on a fact (T4-validity of every link address) that is only stated later, so the prose patches the gap three times instead of fixing the order.

**Required**: Reorder so the licensing fact precedes its uses — present the L1c axiom and its L0b T4-validity postcondition before L0/L1a, then state L0 and L1a using `subspace_I`/`home` directly with a single citation to L0b. Delete the "logically prior despite later presentation" / "stated below for expository reasons" justifications and the duplicated well-definedness paragraph; the well-definedness then holds by ordering, not by repeated meta-prose.
