### Issue 1: L11a shared-home case asserts chain routing through `d.0.s_L` without the forcing argument
**ASN-0043, L11a — LinkUniqueness, *Shared home* sub-case**: "Second, the sibling sweep `inc(·, 0)` from `d.0.1` to the link subspace `d.0.s_L` is deterministic: `s_L` is the fixed link-subspace identifier (L0), so both chains reach the *same* sibling `d.0.s_L` within this one subspace-level allocator's domain."

**Problem**: L1c guarantees only the *existence* of some `k₁ = 2`, length-increasing T10a chain per link — not that the chain passes through `d.0.s_L` as a depth-1 sibling. The proof needs both chains to share the edge into `d.0.s_L`, but the stated reason ("`s_L` is fixed") establishes only that both addresses *carry* subspace identifier `s_L`, not that both *chains route through* `d.0.s_L` before their second spawn. The actual forcing is: the subspace identifier sits at position `#d + 2`, which is the `sig` (terminal) position only while the chain is at element-field depth 1; the moment any child-spawn descends, `sig` moves deeper and position `#d + 2` is frozen at its current value. Hence to reach subspace `s_L ≠ 1` the chain *must* sibling-advance at depth 1 up to `d.0.s_L` before any descent. This is the step that makes "both chains reach the same sibling `d.0.s_L`" true, and it is exactly the kind of "deterministic, by L0" assertion that the standards forbid in place of a derivation.

**Required**: Add the one-to-two-line forcing argument — descent freezes the position-`#d + 2` subspace component, so advancing it to `s_L` is only possible by depth-1 sibling advance, forcing every L1c chain for a link address through `d.0.1 → … → d.0.s_L` before the second spawn.
