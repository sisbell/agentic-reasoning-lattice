### Issue 1: The stated H-SFAIR is too weak to imply H-FAIR at the strength Q6's regime (i) needs
**ASN-0133, H-SFAIR and Q6**:
- H-SFAIR: *"every `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at *infinitely many* indices `Σ_k` along σ is eventually real-fired. This is the standard *infinitely-often-enabled ⟹ eventually-taken* strengthening"*
- The implication: *"`H-SFAIR ⟹ H-FAIR`: an infinitely-recurring argument is real-fired by H-SFAIR (discharging H-FAIR by firing), and an argument trigger-true only *finitely* often is, past its last true index, permanently removed-or-falsified"*
- Q6 regime (i): *"a non-quiescent constant tail would leave a trigger-true argument forever unfired, unremoved, *and unfalsified* … contradicting H-FAIR"*

**Problem**: These three uses cannot all be sound under one reading of the hypotheses, because the H-FAIR discharge obligation is quantified ambiguously and H-SFAIR is stated in the weak (F-taken) form.

*Regime (i) forces a per-occurrence reading of H-FAIR.* Its constant-tail contradiction works only if every trigger-true *index* in the tail must be followed by a discharge. Read once-per-argument ("every `(ρ,x)` that is ever trigger-true is discharged once"), the argument fails: take a rule that is **not** extinction-disciplined, an argument `x` trigger-true at every index, real-fired once at step 5 with an emission that does **not** falsify `T_ρ(x)`, environment never touching `x`. After step 5, `x` is continuously trigger-true through the (eventual) constant tail, yet it was already *discharged once* (the real fire) — so a once-per-argument H-FAIR is satisfied while the tail is non-quiescent. Regime (i) therefore needs per-occurrence H-FAIR.

*But per-occurrence H-FAIR is not implied by the stated H-SFAIR.* The very same σ is a counterexample to "H-SFAIR ⟹ H-FAIR":
- H-SFAIR (as stated): `x` is trigger-true at infinitely many indices and *is* eventually real-fired (at 5). **Holds.**
- H-FAIR (per-occurrence): `x` is trigger-true at index 6, and is never discharged at any `m > 6` — never real-fired again (a single fire), never removed, never falsified. **Fails.**

So H-SFAIR ⇏ H-FAIR. The note's proof of the implication supplies exactly **one** discharge per argument (one real fire for the infinitely-recurring case, one removal/falsification for the finitely-recurring case), which establishes only the once-per-argument obligation — not the per-occurrence obligation regime (i) consumes. The note is caught between the two: the reading that validates its implication breaks regime (i), and the reading regime (i) needs breaks its implication.

The root cause is the wording *"eventually real-fired"* / *"infinitely-often-enabled ⟹ eventually-taken"* — this is the weak (F-taken) form, whereas standard strong fairness is "infinitely-often-enabled ⟹ infinitely-often-taken" (GF-taken).

**Required**: Pin both hypotheses down so the chain is consistent:
- State H-FAIR explicitly per-occurrence: for every `(ρ,x)` and every `k` with `(ρ,x)` trigger-true at `Σ_k`, some `m > k` discharges it.
- State H-SFAIR as the standard strong form — *real-fired infinitely often* (GF-taken), not "eventually real-fired."

Under these, the implication holds (an infinitely-recurring argument now has a real fire after *every* trigger-true index, discharging each occurrence; finitely-recurring arguments are discharged by the eventual removal/falsification past their last true index), and regime (i) is sound. Note that the load-bearing use of H-SFAIR — Q6 regime (ii) for the **all-SF, extinction-disciplined** case — is unaffected: there one real fire already settles the argument permanently (SF + extinction), so even the weak form forces finitely-recurring, and strengthening H-SFAIR only helps. The defect is confined to the general "genuinely stronger" comparison and to regime (i)'s applicability to non-extinction-disciplined registries (which it advertises as "general (any registry)"). The proof prose of the implication also needs updating to reflect that GF-taken supplies a discharge *after each* occurrence, not one per argument.
