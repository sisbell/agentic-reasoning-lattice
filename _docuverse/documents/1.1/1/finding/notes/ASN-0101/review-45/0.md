### Issue 1: Triplicated justification that DEL is primitive, not composite
**ASN-0101, "The operation"**: Paragraph A — "It is *not* a derived composite of `K.μ⁻` ... and `K.μ~` ..., for two reasons. First, ... a strictly longer elementary-transition history ... Second, link-subspace interior deletion has no composite substitute at all ..." Paragraph B — "Nelson treats DELETEVSPAN as a primitive precisely to forbid the distinguishable intermediate and to cover the cases where no composite suffices ... DEL accordingly enters the foundation's transition vocabulary as a new elementary transition kind, closing the gap in a single indivisible step."

**Problem**: Paragraph B's logical content restates Paragraph A in different words: "to forbid the distinguishable intermediate" = A's first reason (history-length/atomicity), "to cover the cases where no composite suffices" = A's second reason (link-subspace has no substitute). The clause "DEL accordingly enters the foundation's transition vocabulary as a new elementary transition kind" also re-states the earlier "We adopt this stance: `DEL[d, σ]` is a *new atomic transition kind* extending the foundation's transition vocabulary." This is the anti-bloat "two paragraphs say the same thing" pattern, and it is prose justifying *why the new transition kind is needed* rather than *what it does* — the reader must skip past it to reach the operation's actual specification (D0). The substantive two-reasons content in Paragraph A suffices; the Nelson/Gregory framing in Paragraph B repeats it.

**Required**: Collapse Paragraph B into Paragraph A. Keep the Nelson/Gregory citation as a one-clause attribution if desired, but remove the restatement of the two reasons and the duplicate "enters the transition vocabulary" sentence.
