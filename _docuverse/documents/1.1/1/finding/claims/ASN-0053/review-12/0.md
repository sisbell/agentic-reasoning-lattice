### "level-uniform span" precondition omits the well-formedness it silently requires
**Class**: REVISE
**Foundation**: S6 (LevelConstraint); T12 (SpanWellDefinedness); TumblerAdd / TA0 (reach defined).
**ASN**: S6 defines the term with length only — "A span σ = (s, ℓ) is *level-uniform* when level_compat(s, ℓ), i.e., #s = #ℓ." But S6's own postcondition chain is explicitly conditioned on well-formedness: "for a *well-formed* level-uniform span, start, width, and reach all share one tumbler length … Drop a precondition — say Pos(ℓ) — and s ⊕ ℓ need not be defined." The downstream callers state their preconditions *without* "well-formed": S1 — "α and β are level-uniform spans"; S4 — "σ = (s, ℓ) is a level-uniform span"; S8 — "component spans are level-uniform and mutually level-compatible." Yet each proof invokes reach(α)/reach(σ) (requires s ⊕ ℓ defined, i.e. Pos(ℓ), actionPoint(ℓ) ≤ #s) and S6's reach-length conclusion (requires well-formedness). By contrast S3 ("well-formed level-uniform spans") and S6 itself ("well-formed level-uniform span") carry the qualifier.
**Issue**: One term, two meanings. If a "span" is well-formed by convention (the intro's "by TA-strict every span is non-empty" and S2's "every *well-formed* span") then "well-formed" in S3/S6 is redundant; if it is not — and S2's contract treats well-formedness as a *precondition* on (s,ℓ), implying non-well-formed pairs exist — then S1/S4/S8 admit a level-uniform-but-not-well-formed input (e.g. ℓ with Zero(ℓ), #ℓ = #s) for which reach is undefined, S6 does not apply, and the proof has no foundation. A downstream consumer reading S1's stated precondition literally can supply such a pair.
**What needs resolving**: Fix the term to one meaning — either state once that every span is well-formed (and drop the redundant "well-formed" qualifier where it appears), or add "well-formed" to the S1, S4, S8 (and any other bare "level-uniform span") preconditions so the chain to S6/T12 is unbroken at the stated boundary.
