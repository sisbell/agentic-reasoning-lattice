### D-SEQ omits NAT-order and NAT-closure despite using both throughout its proof
**Class**: REVISE
**Foundation**: NAT-order (NatStrictTotalOrder, ASN-0034); NAT-closure (NatArithmeticClosureAndIdentity, ASN-0034)
**ASN**: D-SEQ (SequentialPositions). Its Depends list is exactly "S8-depth, S8a, D-CTG-depth, D-MIN, T1, T0, D-CTG, S8-fin, subspace" — no NAT-* entry at all. Yet Step 3 writes "its leading m − 1 components are all **1 ∈ ℕ**" (the component value fed to T0's comprehension), and the proof reasons over ℕ-order throughout: "Let **k₁ < k₂**", "For any k ∈ ℕ with **k₁ < k < k₂**", "the last component k satisfies **k > k₁ ≥ 1**", "every integer with **1 ≤ k ≤ n** is attained", "Let **n = max(k-values)**", "n ≥ 1".
**Issue**: The constant `1` written as a component value is grounded only by NAT-closure (`1 ∈ ℕ`); the ASN states this convention explicitly — D-CTG-depth's own NAT-closure entry says "T0 ... grounds it through T0's own dependence on NAT-closure rather than re-exporting it ... so this consumer — writing 1 as a first-class component value — grounds it here directly," and D-CTG-depth, performing the identical `[1,…,1,k]` construction, does cite NAT-closure. The k-value comparisons and `max(k-values)` are ℕ-order facts, not tumbler-order facts, so the cited T1 does not supply them (T1, like T0 with `1`, consumes NAT-order internally without re-exporting it for arbitrary naturals). D-SEQ writes both families of symbols and cites neither definer, leaving them ungrounded in its contract — a tool resolving symbols from Depends finds `1 ∈ ℕ` and `<`/`≤`/`max` on ℕ unsupported.
**What needs resolving**: Add NAT-closure (for the component constant `1 ∈ ℕ` and `n ≥ 1`) and NAT-order (for the k-value `<`/`≤`/`>` comparisons and the well-definedness of `max(k-values)` over a finite ℕ-set) to D-SEQ's Depends, matching D-CTG-depth's parallel construction.
