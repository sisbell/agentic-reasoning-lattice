  (RETRIEVEV), replication and inter-server protocol (BEBE)
question: What happens when a link is to be edited — its endsets
  modified — given that the substrate makes links permanent and
  immutable once created (L12)? What is allocated and what record
  is made of the relationship between what was and what is now,
  what relationship must the edited link bear to the original — must
  the original remain readable by its address, must readers be able
  to recognize the edit as supersession rather than as an
  independent new link, and by what mechanism does the system express
  this relationship — among alternatives that include a separate
  supersession link, a supersedes field on the link's structure, a
  version-of-link convention analogous to document versioning, or a
  typed relation distinct from any of these, what does committing to
  one such architecture (rather than another) reveal about the
  difference between editing-as-permanence-preserving and
  editing-as-mutation, and what invariants must any choice maintain
  about the original link's continued discoverability and resolution,
  the chain semantics under repeated edits, multi-editor convergence
  and conflict, and the reader's ability to identify the current
  successor without ambiguity?
