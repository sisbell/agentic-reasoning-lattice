### D-CTG-depth's second postcondition advertises a D-SEQ consumption that does not occur
**Class**: REVISE
**Foundation**: D-CTG-depth (SharedPrefixReduction) — second postcondition; D-SEQ (SequentialPositions) — Step 3
**ASN**: D-CTG-depth states, twice, that its betweenness-reduction postcondition is consumed downstream: in the proof, *"This equivalence is the second postcondition and the reduction D-SEQ consumes: D-CTG's multi-component betweenness guard u < v < x collapses, modulo the shared prefix, to the single ordinal betweenness uₘ < vₘ < xₘ,"* and in the Postconditions slot, *"This is the citable form of the reduction D-SEQ consumes."* But D-SEQ Step 3 derives betweenness for its specific witness independently: *"For any k ∈ ℕ with k₁ < k < k₂, form the tuple w = [1, 1, …, 1, k] … This w satisfies … v₁ < w < v₂ (again by T1(i), since w agrees with both on components 1 through m − 1 and k₁ < k < k₂ at component m)."* And D-SEQ's Depends rationale for D-CTG-depth cites only the **first** postcondition: *"supplies the shared-prefix result (components 2 through m − 1 identical) used in Step 1 Case m ≥ 3."*
**Issue**: D-SEQ consumes D-CTG-depth's *first* postcondition (shared prefix, in Step 1) and re-derives the betweenness collapse itself via T1(i) for the concretely-constructed `w = [1,…,1,k]` (which already shares the full prefix by construction, so the general reduction is never needed). No claim in the ASN cites the second postcondition — it is orphaned, and the prose asserting "D-SEQ consumes" it is false. A reader auditing the dependency graph hits a mismatch: D-CTG-depth claims a downstream consumer that, on inspection, takes a different path. This is the cross-claim structural inconsistency a per-claim check cannot see — the math of the second postcondition is correct, but its stated role in the system is not.
**What needs resolving**: Reconcile the two claims. Either rewrite D-SEQ Step 3 to actually invoke D-CTG-depth's betweenness reduction (making the "D-SEQ consumes" claim true), or drop the "the reduction D-SEQ consumes" assertions from D-CTG-depth and justify the second postcondition by an actual consumer — or remove it as unconsumed, leaving D-CTG-depth carrying only the shared-prefix result that D-SEQ does use.
