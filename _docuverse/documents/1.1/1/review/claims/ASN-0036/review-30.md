### S7d's common-allocator derivation establishes address-distinctness, contradicting its own closing disclaimer
**Class**: OBSERVE
**Foundation**: T10a.7 (EnumerationInjectivity), GlobalUniqueness — both ASN-0034
**ASN**: S7d (DocumentAllocationDiscipline). Common-allocator regime: *"by T10a.7 (EnumerationInjectivity) the indexing `n ↦ tₙ` is injective, so `tₘ ≠ tₙ` are distinct outputs of `A`."* Closing disclaimer: *"S7d separates documents at the level of events, not yet of addresses — two distinct events could, for all S7d says, still emit one tumbler value — and lifting event-distinctness to address-distinctness is GlobalUniqueness's step."*
**Issue**: The two passages are inconsistent for the common-allocator regime. The derivation explicitly concludes `tₘ ≠ tₙ` — an address-level distinctness fact about two co-allocator documents — by invoking T10a.7. The disclaimer then asserts, as a blanket statement about everything S7d establishes, that "two distinct events could, for all S7d says, still emit one tumbler value." That blanket statement is false for the co-allocator events at indices `m ≠ n`: T10a.7 forbids them from emitting one value. The disclaimer is accurate only for the *disjoint*-allocator regime, where S7d genuinely stops at event-distinctness and hands off to GlobalUniqueness. The deliverable S7 actually consumes (event-distinctness for all document pairs) is sound, and the over-delivery is harmless to S7's uniqueness conclusion — but the claim's self-description of its own scope is internally contradictory, which a consumer (a formalization tool extracting S7d's contract) would hit. Separately, T10a.7's invocation appears superfluous to the stated conclusion: for co-allocator documents at distinct indices `m ≠ n`, event-distinctness already follows from index-distinctness (the index-`m` event differs from the index-`n` event); routing through `tₘ ≠ tₙ` is what produces the address claim the disclaimer disavows.
**What needs resolving**: Reconcile the derivation and the disclaimer. Either (a) restate the common-allocator step to conclude event-distinctness from index-distinctness (`m ≠ n ⟹` distinct events) without routing through T10a.7's `tₘ ≠ tₙ` address conclusion, so S7d cleanly stops at events as the disclaimer promises; or (b) scope the disclaimer to the disjoint-allocator regime, acknowledging that the common-allocator regime already yields address-distinctness via T10a.7 and that GlobalUniqueness is required only across disjoint allocators.
