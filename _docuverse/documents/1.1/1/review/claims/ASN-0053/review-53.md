### S9 Depends omits T1 and TA-strict, both consumed by the proof
**Class**: REVISE
**Foundation**: T1 (LexicographicOrder, ASN-0034); TA-strict (StrictIncrease, ASN-0034)
**ASN**: S9 (NormalizationUniqueness). Its standalone `Depends` lists exactly one entry: "TA-LC (ASN-0034) — supplies the left-cancellation axiom ... used to rule out the equal-start-equal-reach divergence case." Yet the proof body repeatedly invokes the tumbler order: "N2 on Σ̂₁ gives reach(αⱼ) < start(αⱼ₊₁), and repeated N1 on Σ̂₁ gives start(αⱼ₊₁) ≤ start(αᵢ); chaining, reach(βⱼ) < start(αᵢ)"; the Case 2a chain "start(βᵢ) = start(αᵢ) < reach(αᵢ) = p < reach(βᵢ)"; and the membership tests against ⟦σ⟧ = [start, reach). It also uses "non-emptiness (start(αᵢ) < reach(αᵢ))" in Cases 2a/2b.
**Issue**: The chaining of N1 (`≤` on starts) with N2 (`<` on reaches) to derive fresh strict inequalities like `reach(βⱼ) < start(αᵢ)` is exactly T1's strict transitivity (postcondition (c)) plus the `≤`/`<` composition; the half-open denotation membership is T1's order on T. Neither is derivable from TA-LC (a cancellation axiom that says nothing about order). Likewise the per-span non-emptiness `start(γₖ) < reach(γₖ)` is TA-strict's `a ⊕ w > a` applied to `(start, width)` — not stated by TA-LC either. The sibling order-reasoning claims SC, S0, S1, S5, and S11d all cite T1 for precisely this kind of `≤`/`<` composition (the recent revision explicitly "close[d] T1/Divergence citation gaps in S1, S5, S11d"); S9 does the same reasoning but leaves the symbol ungrounded. A downstream consumer auditing S9's cone would find its order arithmetic unsupported by its declared dependencies.
**What needs resolving**: Add T1 (LexicographicOrder) to S9's `Depends`, identifying the consumption sites (strict-order transitivity for the N2+N1 chains yielding `reach(βⱼ) < start(αᵢ)`/`reach(αⱼ) < start(βᵢ)`, the strict chains in Cases 2a/2b, and the `[start, reach)` membership semantics), and add TA-strict (StrictIncrease) for the non-emptiness `start(σ) < reach(σ)` the case analysis relies on — matching the citation discipline already applied to SC, S0, S1, S5, and S11d.
