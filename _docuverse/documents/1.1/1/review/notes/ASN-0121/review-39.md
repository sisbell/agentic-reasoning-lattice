# Review of ASN-0121

This ASN is in strong shape. I checked every derivation: the forcing argument for FL-DEF (including the R_min/R_max slack analysis), the two monotonicity facts over the full ASN-0047 vocabulary, all three FL-WP cases (the ghost-pre-coverage conjunct in (a), the self-retraction term in (b), and the exact `nullified` membership equation in (c) — all verified correct, including the L_R^{Σ'} bookkeeping), the FL-DIR witness, the wide-element-span home example (`p ⊕ ℓ = [1,0,1,0,2,1,1,1]` computes correctly and the document tumbler `[1,0,1,0,2]` does lie in the span), and all seven traces address-by-address (zeros counts, frontier discipline, prefix-incomparability claims, and each per-link `sat` evaluation check out). The two remaining findings are both instances of one root cause: membership in the answer is *not* per-link through the `nullified` conjunct — retraction tuples couple links — and two prose glosses assert per-link independence more broadly than the formal lemmas (which carry the correct hypotheses) actually deliver.

## REVISE

### Issue 1: FL-JUNK's preamble asserts unconditional insensitivity that the lemma's own hypothesis exists to deny
**ASN-0121, "Non-impedance: junk links do not obstruct"**: "`sat` is decided per link, independently of every other link in the store; a link's match status is a function of its own value, its own address, its own retraction status, and the fixed request. Consequently the result is insensitive to the presence of non-matching links."
**Problem**: The "consequently" overclaims. Per-link independence holds for `sat`, but answer membership is `a ∈ addressable(Σ) ∧ sat(a, q, Σ)`, and the addressability conjunct is decided by *other* links' values: `nullified(Σ)` is computed from retraction tuples held at other addresses. A non-matching link can therefore change the result — an arity-3 link with retraction-class type that fails `sat(·, q, ·)` for the request nonetheless nullifies any existing match its to-coverage names, removing that match from the answer (exactly the exit route FL-WP(c) computes). The formal FL-JUNK statement is correct precisely because its first hypothesis, `nullified(Σ') ∩ dom(Σ.L) = nullified(Σ)`, excludes this interference; the gloss preceding it states an unconditional principle the lemma does not and cannot deliver. The "its own retraction status" clause half-concedes the coupling (retraction status is a function of the rest of the store) and then the next sentence ignores it.
**Required**: Qualify the gloss to match the lemma: per-link independence holds for `sat`; membership is additionally conditioned on `nullified`, which non-matching *retraction* links can grow; insensitivity therefore holds for additions that nullify no existing link — which is what the first hypothesis says. One clause fixes it.

### Issue 2: FL-RET's non-obstruction parenthetical cites FL-JUNK, which cannot be instantiated there — and the claim itself needs care for retracted retractors
**ASN-0121, "Retraction is permanent absence from current inquiry"**: "A retracted link neither lingers as a phantom result nor obstructs retrieval of the links that still satisfy the inquiry (FL-JUNK applies to its absence as to any non-match)."
**Problem**: Two layers. First, the citation does not typecheck: FL-JUNK quantifies over store-growing sequences in which no existing link becomes nullified — retraction events are expressly outside its hypotheses — so it cannot be instantiated to say anything about a state in which `a` is nullified. Second, the sentence itself is false under the ASN's own usage of "satisfy" (= `sat`) in one realizable case: if the retracted `a` is itself a retraction tuple, its tuple remains in the audit slice `L_R^Σ` and continues to nullify its targets even after `a` is nullified (R6b, ASN-0086 — retraction-of-retraction is a non-fixpoint operation), so `a`'s stored value permanently excludes other `sat`-satisfying links from the answer. What is true, and what the sentence should say, is narrower: `a`'s *exclusion* alters nothing else — for `b ≠ a`, neither `b ∉ nullified(Σ)` nor `sat(b, q, Σ)` reads `a`'s membership status — while `a`'s stored value, if retraction-typed, still bears on others' addressability through `L_R`. The same loose attribution appears at the close of FL-WP(c): "its failure is the sole route by which a match leaves the answer, which is FL-RET" — the sole-exit-route fact is the WP analysis's own result; FL-RET is the downstream permanence claim, not the exit-route claim.
**Required**: Replace the parenthetical with the correct per-link basis (others' membership conjuncts never read `a`'s status), scope the non-obstruction claim to exclude the retracted-retractor case (or note R6b explicitly), and reword the FL-WP(c) appositive so the exit-route observation and FL-RET's permanence are not conflated.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: Enumeration order of the returned "list"
Nelson's quote says the operation "returns a list"; the ASN forces the answer as a set and says nothing about ordering. That is the right abstraction here — any ordering, cursoring, or batching semantics belongs with the paginated FINDNEXTNLINKSFROMTOTHREE, which the scope list already excludes.
**Why out of scope**: Ordering is new territory owned by the pagination operation, not an error in the membership specification.

### Topic 2: Coherence with ASN-0086's Observe_K
Two query regimes now exist over the same link store: Observe_K (per-type, pattern-subset matching `F̂ ⊆ coverage(F)`, view-selected) and `findlinks_FTT` (four-set, touch/intersection matching, addressability-filtered). The ASN carefully differentiates itself from F-FIND but is silent on whether Observe_K's oper view is expressible as a specialization of this operation.
**Why out of scope**: A reconciliation claim would be a new cross-regime result; nothing in this ASN depends on it.

VERDICT: REVISE
