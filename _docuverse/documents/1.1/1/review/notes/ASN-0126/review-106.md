# Review of ASN-0126

## REVISE

### Issue 1: Retraction and sterilization are analyzed only for Binary-registered [R]; the framework leaves [R]'s registration free
**ASN-0126, Retraction as an attributed Binary / Range sterilization**: "To obtain a gated retraction an app registers R as **Binary** and routes through the one-span wrapper" … "fix a retraction tuple `(b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ` with single-span to-endset `G' = {(g, ℓ)}` — when R is registered Binary, the only to-form a gate-cleared R-tuple carries (P6)"
**Problem**: Nothing in the framework pins [R]'s shape. The registry is app-composed, any coverage class may carry any of the three shapes, and ASN-0086's discipline-commitment device — the layer-level convention that did exactly this kind of pinning — has no analog here. Three legal configurations are therefore admitted but never analyzed: (a) *[R] unregistered* — every `Emit_R` fails precondition (i), so `L_R^Σ = ∅` at every reachable state, `nullified ≡ ∅`, `A_K^Σ = L_K^Σ`, and the substrate is permanently retraction-free with C2/C3 vacuous — a significant framework-level consequence for an app-facing vocabulary, stated nowhere; (b) *Unary-R* — the gate forces `G = ∅`, `coverage(G') = ∅`, so retraction is registered yet inert; (c) *Multi-R* — gate-cleared R-tuples carry multi-span to-endsets, P6 no longer yields the single-span hypothesis, B becomes a finite union of consecutive blocks, and Corollary RangeSterilization's exhaustion arithmetic in (ii) does not apply. The corollary's own question — "how much *future* emission a single such tuple can poison" — is posed generally and answered only under Binary-R. Showing the Binary case does not establish the others.
**Required**: Either state a framework commitment mirroring ASN-0086's device — if [R] is registered, its shape is Binary — and note that the sterilization analysis is complete under it; or close the three corners explicitly (one sentence each for (a) and (b); for (c), state the corollary for finite unions of blocks or scope it out with justification).

### Issue 2: `Nullify_Binary` has no operation contract
**ASN-0126, Retraction as an attributed Binary**: "The *operation set* an app invokes refines ASN-0086's `{Emit_K, Observe_K, Nullify}`, in step with the transition relation, to `{Emit_K, Observe_K, Nullify_Binary}`."
**Problem**: ASN-0086 gives each of its three operations an explicit precondition/effect/frame block; this note's replacement operation gets only an inline `≡`-definition with its guarantees scattered across two long paragraphs. Missing from any single place: the precondition set (P0 `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`; *R registered Binary* — without which the call has no `→_sh` image, a failure mode ASN-0086's `Nullify` could not have and which is never stated as a precondition); the effect (deposit of `({r}, {(a, δ(1, #a))}, R)` at `a_emit(Σ, d_retr)`); the frame; and the postconditions — in particular that `a ∈ nullified(Σ')` holds iff `a ∈ A_rel^{Σ'}` (a ghost target is *not itself* nullified; only the link addresses under it are, which is exactly the sterilization case), and that single-tuple scope holds iff P-tgt, demoted to "app obligation" but never collected into the operation's spec. An implementer of the app-facing surface currently has to reassemble the contract from prose.
**Required**: A contract block for `Nullify_Binary` with the same discipline as ASN-0086's operations: preconditions, effect, frame, the conditional single-tuple-scope guarantee (iff P-tgt), and the nullified-set effect for non-P-tgt targets.

### Issue 3: The frontier-landing of raw `K.λ_sh` deposits is asserted, not derived
**ASN-0126, The shape-gated emit**: "The refinement is confined to the guard: `K.λ_sh` adds only preconditions to `K.λ`, so its C/M/L effect, its fresh emission address `a_emit(Σ, d)`, and its C/M/L frame are identical to `K.λ`'s" — relied on by Range sterilization (ii): "No emission homed at `d` can route around the block — every one, of every registered type, lands at the frontier (FrontierUnification)."
**Problem**: ASN-0086's StateTransition contract, as cited, guarantees only that a K.λ-step extends `dom(Σ.L)` "at a fresh key" — freshness, not chain position. The pinning of *every* K.λ deposit (not just `Emit_K`-mediated ones) to `a_emit` is what makes RangeSterilization (ii)'s no-routing-around claim true; if a raw `K.λ_sh` step could deposit at any fresh conforming address, an app could skip past the block and the exhaustion count `k` would fail. The pinning is derivable — pre- and post-state both satisfy L-ContiguousPrefix (B2-transferred), the deposit grows exactly one home's homed-set by one element, and a one-element extension of a contiguous initial segment that remains a contiguous initial segment must add the frontier slot — but this derivation is nowhere in the note; effect-identity simply attributes the pinned address to `K.λ`'s contract.
**Required**: One sentence deriving frontier-landing of every `K.λ`/`K.λ_sh` deposit from L-ContiguousPrefix at the pre- and post-state (or an explicit citation of the contract clause that pins it), placed where effect-identity or FrontierUnification can invoke it.

### Issue 4: Up-set clause overclaims when the frontier has not yet entered B
**ASN-0126, Range sterilization, Corollary (ii)**: "And when B is an up-set there is no exit: every future emission homed at `d` is born nullified — `d`'s link chain is sterilized outright."
**Problem**: The sentence sits after the branch "If instead `f_d^{Σ'} < min B`, intervening emissions land below the block, unconstrained by this tuple," which has dropped the hypothesis `f_d^{Σ'} ∈ B`. For an up-set with `min B > f_d^{Σ'}` — realizable, e.g. a to-span whose action point sits at the `s_L` position, covering `[chain_d(j₀), d.0.(s_L+1))` with `j₀ > f` — the next `j₀ − f` emissions are *not* born nullified by this tuple. "Every future emission" is true only from the frontier's entry into B onward. (The note's own realizability witness, the ghost root, has `min B = 0`, masking the gap.)
**Required**: Condition the clause: once the frontier enters B (immediately, if `f_d^{Σ'} ∈ B`), every subsequent emission homed at `d` is born nullified and there is no exit.

### Issue 5: TA5(c) applied to `ℓ_prev` without discharging T4-validity
**ASN-0126, Retraction as an attributed Binary**: "whose `#E(ℓ_prev) ≥ 2` (L1b again) survives the sibling advance — it increments only the terminal component (TA5(c), as in CPP, ASN-0043), leaving every zero in place and `#E` unchanged"
**Problem**: TA5(c) modifies the *sig* position; that the sig position is the terminal position requires the input be T4-valid (TA5-SigValid — CPP's sibling-advance case carries exactly this hypothesis). `ℓ_prev ∈ dom(Σ.L)` is T4-valid by L0b at the `→*`-reachable `π(Σ)`, but this is never cited. The Range sterilization section discharges the identical hypothesis explicitly ("every input here is T4-valid by the discharge above"); this earlier instance was missed by the same revision that added that discharge.
**Required**: Cite L0b (carried by B1) for `ℓ_prev` before invoking TA5(c)/TA5-SigValid.

### Issue 6: Worked illustration never exhibits the registry; gate precondition (i) and C0 are checked only nominally
**ASN-0126, Worked illustration**: "Consider five registered coverage classes." … "so any number of Multi types may coexist, provided their keys differ (`[K_citation] ≠ [K_touched]`)."
**Problem**: The section opens "We fix concrete addresses to check P3 and P4 against a real scenario," yet the five type endsets are never given — every address in the example is content- or link-side; the registry keys remain symbolic. Consequently the gate's precondition (i), asserted at every step ("(i) `retract` is registered"), is never exercised through the CoverageEqualityDecidable comparison that actually decides it, and the C0 key-uniqueness check is hypothetical — "provided their keys differ" assumes exactly what a concrete registry would discharge. P3's registration conjunct is thus verified nowhere in the note against a concrete value.
**Required**: Exhibit five concrete type endsets (unit-depth singletons at distinct ghost addresses under some vocabulary prefix suffice), and walk one (i)-decision — one CoverageEqualityDecidable comparison against a stored representative — so registration and C0 uniqueness are checked, not assumed.

### Issue 7: Duplicated statements accumulated across sections (anti-bloat)
**ASN-0126, The registry / Registry permanence / The shape-gated emit / Retraction as an attributed Binary**: "the construction of `Σ_init` is the only act that writes the registry (invariance across the dynamics is P1, Registry permanence)" … "The registry is fixed when `Σ_init` is defined (The registry); we now show it never drifts." … "**P1 (RegistryInvariance).** … the registry never drifts."
**Problem**: Two facts are each stated three times in different words. (a) Registry fixity: declared in The registry with a forward pointer to P1, restated in Registry permanence's opening sentence, and stated again as P1 itself — the forward pointer plus section-intro recap are accretion around the one claim that carries the content. (b) The empty-from no-`→_sh`-image fact: derived in full in The shape-gated emit, recapped in the Retraction section's opening sentence ("we re-express the un-gateable empty-from Nullify (The shape-gated emit)"), and restated a third time in the attribution paragraph ("so under `|F| = 1` that empty-from form has no `→_sh` image (The shape-gated emit)") whose only new content is the inexpressibility of unattributed retraction.
**Required**: One statement each. Drop the forward-pointer parenthetical and the Registry permanence recap sentence, letting P1 carry fixity; in the Retraction section, keep the cross-reference in one place and let the attribution paragraph state only its new content.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: Registry evolution after `Σ_init`
Runtime registration, deregistration, migration of a type's shape, or versioned registries. **Why out of scope**: this note deliberately commits to an immutable registry (C0, P1) and the commitment is load-bearing for P2/P4/P6; relaxing it is a successor framework's design question (adjacent to, but distinct from, the note's Open Question 4 on pre-registered types).

### Topic 2: Concurrent emission at a shared home
`a_emit` is a function of state and two writers racing on one home chain would contend for the same frontier slot. **Why out of scope**: neither ASN-0086 nor this note models concurrent steps; `→_sh` is a sequential one-step relation, and atomicity/serialization of emission belongs to a future operational-semantics ASN — the same successor the note's open questions already anticipate.

VERDICT: REVISE
