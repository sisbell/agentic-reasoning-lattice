# Review of ASN-0130

The note's architecture is sound — the three foundation facts (immutability, origin identity, total event order) do carry the design, and PR0's wp analysis, PR2's event-wise acyclicity argument, and the lint-exactness derivation in PR5 are genuinely careful work. The off-discipline counterexamples flanking PR0's wp equivalence are exactly the right kind of rigor. The problems are concentrated in the definitional grounding of the encoding/signature layer and in the variable-hygiene steps of PR3/PR3a, where the proof asserts a disjointness that the note's own worked example violates.

## REVISE

### Issue 1: `sig` and "valid encoding" are circularly grounded for reference-bearing runs

**ASN-0130, PR-ENC / PR-SIG**: "An *encoding* is an injective map from *signed* PL terms — a pair of an ordered, sorted parameter context … and a body in ASN-0129's syntax extended with *definitional references* (PR-SIG), well-typed as `Γ_D ⊢ body : C_D` … — to finite content-value sequences" and "`sig` is a function of immutable content (S0), defined wherever a valid run starts."

**Problem**: The encoding's domain is *well-typed* signed terms, and `C_D` is "the result sort WT derives for its body." But for a reference-bearing body, well-typing under WT-ref consults `sig(r)` at each referent — so "this run holds a valid encoding" is not a property of the run's content alone, and `sig` is not a function of immutable content: it is defined in terms of itself through the reference structure. The definition is not well-founded in general. Concretely: allocate two runs, X referencing Y's start and Y referencing X's start, neither registered (K.α validates nothing). Whether X's body well-types needs `sig(Y)`, which needs Y's `C_D`, which needs `sig(X)` — a loop with no grounding; under a least-fixed-point reading neither is "valid," under a greatest-fixed-point reading both could be, and the note picks neither. The circularity also infects PR-ENC-uniq: prefix-freeness is stated as a fixed property ("no valid encoding sequence is a proper prefix of another"), but if validity includes typing, the set of valid sequences is store-relative. The note itself equivocates — PR0 (ii) treats validity as parse success ("the run's values are exactly one valid encoding — the self-delimiting parse from `a` succeeds") with typing as the separate condition (iii), which is the correct operational structure, but PR-ENC's definition contradicts it.

**Required**: Stratify the definition layer. Make the encoding's domain *syntactically well-formed* signed terms (parse structure only), so that parse-validity, the run's extent, `Γ_D`, and prefix-freeness — everything PR-ENC-uniq and resolution consume — are content-intrinsic. Define type-validity and `C_D` separately: checked at registration via WT + WT-ref, well-defined on ever-registered addresses by induction on first-registration order (the induction PR2 already supplies; PR3's "induction down the DAG carries parse- and type-validity to every leaf" is the right argument, currently doing definitional work it was never assigned). Restate PR-SIG's domain accordingly.

### Issue 2: PR3a's variable hygiene is asserted, not arranged — and the note's own worked example violates it

**ASN-0130, PR3a**: "…which after the capture-avoiding renaming of `expand(r)`'s binders away from `Γ` (PR3) are the parameters and the renamed binders, none clashing with `Γ`" and "the arguments' free variables lie in `Γ`, never among the `xⱼ`, so the sequential substitutions neither capture nor interfere."

**Problem**: The renaming discipline (PR3) renames the referent's *internal binders* — PC1/PC2a domains and PC2 binder guards — but the referent's *parameters* `x₁, …, x_k` are free variables of `expand(r)` and are never renamed. Nothing prevents a parameter name from coinciding with a variable in `Γ`. The worked composition exhibits exactly this: `gate` has parameter `t` and references `a₁(t)`, where `quiescent_v1`'s parameter is also `t` — so `expand(a₁)` mentions free `t`, `t ∈ dom(Γ)`, and both quoted assertions are false ("none clashing with `Γ`" — `t` clashes; "never among the `xⱼ`" — the argument's free variable `t` *is* `x₁`). The example survives by accident (same name, same sort, identity substitution); in general the proof breaks twice: (a) the weakened judgment `Γ, x₁ : C₁, …, x_k : C_k ⊢ expand(r) : C_r` is ill-formed when some `xⱼ ∈ dom(Γ)` at a different sort — WT's context "assigns each free variable a sort," and the extension is then not a function; (b) sequential substitution `[x₁ := E₁]…[x_k := E_k]` interferes when a later `xⱼ` occurs free in an earlier-inserted `Eᵢ`, which the disjointness was supposed to exclude. Additionally, the `k` applications of PC2's composition rule need the arguments weakened into the intermediate extended contexts (`Eⱼ` is typed at `Γ` but used at `Γ, x_{j+1} : C_{j+1}, …`), a step the proof doesn't mention — it rests on the same unsecured disjointness, and on a weakening property of WT + WT-ref that gets one inline clause rather than a stated lemma.

**Required**: Extend the capture-avoiding discipline to the referent's parameters: α-convert `expand(r)` so its parameters are fresh with respect to `dom(Γ)`, the other parameters, and the arguments' free variables, with the substitution targeting the renamed parameters — or adopt and state an explicit nameless/shadowing convention. State the weakening lemma for WT + WT-ref once (the syntax-directed argument is fine, but it must be conditioned on the freshness the renaming now guarantees), and re-run the `k` PC2 applications under it, including the weakening of arguments into intermediate contexts.

### Issue 3: `expand` is not a function as defined, but PR3 and PR5 treat it as one

**ASN-0130, PR3 / PR5**: "two evaluators expanding the same address at any two states obtain the same term (S0)" and "the certified object is itself a fixed pure spelling, frozen exactly as the artifact is — the certified object and the classified object coincide, at the expansion."

**Problem**: Capture-avoiding renaming is nondeterministic — the choice of fresh names is not fixed by content. S0 guarantees the *bodies* read at every state are identical; it does not determinize the renaming choices, so `expand(a)` as defined is a term *up to α-equivalence*, not "the same term." Two claims lean on the stronger reading: PR3's determinacy claim quoted above, and PR5's identification of the certificate's object as "a fixed pure spelling." If the certified object is only an α-class, then well-posedness of the certificate additionally requires that PD0-class membership (and the view-independence scan, and denotation) be α-invariant — plausible, since PD0's rules never mention variable names, but nowhere stated or proved, and the note's "the certified object and the classified object coincide" silently assumes the object is unique.

**Required**: Either fix a canonical, content-determined renaming scheme as part of the definition of `expand` (e.g., binders numbered in a fixed traversal order), making `expand` a genuine function and the quoted claims literally true — this composes with the parameter-renaming fix of Issue 2 — or weaken PR3/PR5 to α-classes and prove the three invariance facts the certificate then needs (denotation, PD0 membership, view-independence under α).

### Issue 4: the certification operation has no contract

**ASN-0130, PR5 / PS2**: "The registration surface may, on request, classify a validated definition against ASN-0129's PD criteria and emit a certification classifier" … "Emitted only by the certification path of the registration surface, through the wrapped `Emit_pd_stable(Σ, d, {a}, ∅)`."

**Problem**: PR0 sets the note's own standard for an operation: named surface, validation conditions (i)–(iv), branch semantics under I1, rejection stance, postcondition against the born-nullified boundary, wp. The certification surface — which the registration discipline's very definition references ("every `L_pd_stable`-growing step the certification branch of PR5") and on which the lint's meaning depends — gets none of this. Unpinned: the operation's name and signature (is it a flag on `register_pred` or a separate call? "on request" is ambiguous, and the worked example doesn't disambiguate); the precondition on the target's registration status (must `a` be *actively* registered, or merely ever-registered? may one certify an unregistered but valid run?); the order and rejection semantics of the three checks (view-independence scan, ST classification, target status) — in particular whether a certification request attached to a registration that fails certification rejects the registration too or just the certificate (the example's "certification is correctly *refused*" shows a refusal but not its scope); idem-⊤ hit behavior for re-certification; and the PR1-analogue (every `pd_stable` tuple entered with the checker's verdict ST at its pre-state) is delegated to "PR1's permanence applies to certificates identically" rather than stated for the slice it governs.

**Required**: A PR5-level operation block, even brief: signature, preconditions (target's registration status; view-independence of the expansion; checker verdict), the I1 branch behavior at the `pd_stable` class, rejection semantics for each failing check and their order relative to registration when bundled, and the slice's validation-permanence statement under the discipline.

### Issue 5: the worked composition uses an operation and an atom that don't exist in the system

**ASN-0130, Worked composition, steps 1–2**: "INSERT the encoded term of `quiescent_v1` … into the builder's document `d_b`; the content lands at run `A₁`" and "body `a₁(t) ∧ under_cap(t, 3)`."

**Problem**: The note's transition relation is `→_sh ≡ K.σ ∪ K.α ∪ K.λ_sh`; there is no INSERT in this system — content arrives by a sequence of K.α steps, and the difference matters here, since run contiguity (PR0 condition (i)) is a property of how those K.α steps interleave, which "INSERT" papers over. And `under_cap` is neither a member of ASN-0129's `V_atom` nor a registered definitional reference (the example explicitly says only the *first* conjunct is an applied reference) — so `gate`'s body, as written, does not well-type under WT + WT-ref and step 2's registration would be rejected by the note's own condition (iii). The worked example is the note's verification against a concrete scenario; an unverifiable body defeats its purpose. (Step 2 is also where the Issue-2 parameter collision occurs, unremarked.)

**Required**: Replace "INSERT" with the K.α-step description the parenthetical already gestures at; spell `under_cap` either as genuine PL (e.g., a PC2a count comparison over a QD filter) or as a second registered reference with its registration shown; and note the `t`/`t` parameter coincidence as an instance of the (fixed) renaming discipline.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: authorization and ownership of registration, de-registration, certification, and supersession
**Why out of scope**: Inherited from the foundations, not introduced here — `register_pred(d, A_def)` does not require `d` to be the run's origin document, `Nullify_Binary`'s P-tgt carries no ownership clause (anyone may de-register anyone's definition), and `supersedes` emission is unrestricted (the note correctly assigns adjudication to readers). Who *may* perform these acts is protocol-layer policy, the same fence ASN-0129 draws around activation; a future ASN on authorization, not an error in this one.

### Topic 2: atomic authoring of multi-address runs
**Why out of scope**: A run's chain contiguity can be destroyed by interleaved K.α allocations in the same document between the author's value writes; the note correctly handles this by rejection at PR0 (i), making contiguity the author's risk. A batched content-append operation or an authoring-level contiguity guarantee would be a new substrate operation — future territory, not a gap in this note's guarantees.

VERDICT: REVISE
