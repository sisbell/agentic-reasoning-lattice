## REVISE

### Issue 1: FD-VDYN(d)'s absorption claim carries no in-note support
**ASN-0124, Dynamics / FD-VDYN(d)**: "Image motion is necessary for answer motion but not sufficient: the comprehension can absorb a swing whose swapped-in addresses witness the same documents (the analogue of D-ABSORB, ASN-0127)."
**Problem**: The necessity direction is derived (from the stability condition plus the reorder's fixed-`I` invariance). The non-absorbed case is witnessed by the worked illustration (`d_C` drops from the re-issued query). But the insufficiency direction — "*can* absorb," a possibility claim — gets neither a derivation nor a reachable witness; it leans entirely on "the analogue of D-ABSORB." D-ABSORB is an ASN-0127 result about `findlinks_V`, whose absorbing entity is a link's multi-span endset coverage; the `finddocs_V` absorber is a structurally different object — an *answer-document* whose content range happens to hold both the swapped-out and swapped-in I-address. D-ABSORB therefore cannot establish a `finddocs_V` fact; it is at best a gesture at an analogous phenomenon. This is inconsistent with the note's own standard: its other possibility claims (FD-NEUT(c) "can fail to qualify," FD-LOSSY "there are reachable states") each exhibit a reachable construction. (To be clear, this asks the note to support its *own* document-query layer, not to rebuild ASN-0127's image machinery — that is correctly out of scope.)
**Required**: Either exhibit a reachable state where a K.μ~ reorder of a named document moves `image_C` while leaving `finddocs_V` fixed (e.g. query document `d_q` and a second document `d_x` both arranging two I-addresses `a, b`; the reorder swaps the named region's image `a ↔ b`; `d_x` arranges both, so `finddocs_V` is unchanged), or derive the document-side absorption mechanism inline, rather than offering the link-specific D-ABSORB as the sole justification.

### Issue 2: FD-ASKER's answer-equality half forward-references FD-V for its entire justification
**ASN-0124, Naming the Material / FD-ASKER**: "`resolve(Q₁, Σ) = resolve(Q₂, Σ) ⟹ finddocs_V(Q₁, Σ) = finddocs_V(Q₂, Σ)` (immediate once FD-V below is seen to be a function of the resolved set)."
**Problem**: FD-ASKER sits in the resolution section, but its answer-equality statement mentions `finddocs_V`/FD-V, defined only in the next section; the justification is *only* the forward reference. The genuinely resolution-level content — the transclusion identity (`resolve({(d_t,{v})}) = {a} = resolve({(d_o,{u})})`) and "the asker's starting document is consumed entirely at resolution" — belongs here and stands on its own; the answer-equality lead-in does not. This is precisely the forward-reference residue a recent revision pass targeted (`trim … forward-reference bloat`).
**Required**: Keep the transclusion identity in the resolution section; relocate the answer-equality consequence to FD-V's definition, where it is a one-line corollary of `finddocs_V ≡ finddocs ∘ resolve`, or drop the forward-referencing lead-in.

### Issue 3 (minor): the per-candidate-arrangement-filter requirement is stated in two sections
**ASN-0124, Historical Companion (closing ¶) vs Implementation Evidence (deviation 1)**: "recovering the live operation from it requires filtering each candidate through its current arrangement — the per-candidate test `χ(d, I, Σ)`, a lookup against `M(d)`, not a re-search … has … implemented FD-HIST instead" vs "A conforming implementation of the live contract must filter each candidate through its current arrangement … or else declare itself an implementation of the historical companion."
**Problem**: The conclusion — a live-contract implementation must filter each candidate through its current arrangement, else it is FD-HIST — is delivered in full in both places. The historical-companion paragraph's distinctive content (J1★/J1'★ pinning the index from below and above) is not duplicated; only the filter conclusion is. The two paragraphs serve different roles (abstract index strategy vs green-specific finding), so this is a small overlap, not a structural defect.
**Required**: State the filter requirement once — the abstract historical-companion paragraph is its natural home — and have deviation #1 cite it rather than re-state it.
