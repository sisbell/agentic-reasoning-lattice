# Review of ASN-0100

## REVISE

### Issue 1: K.μ⁻ case (i.b) "Forced" framing understates vocabulary scope
**ASN-0100, Substrate Decomposition, case (i.b)**: "(i.b) — Forced under INS.frame.subspace. ... Within the K.μ⁻ + K.μ⁺ vocabulary (K.μ⁺ adds only s_C positions per its content-subspace restriction in ASN-0047), the shrunken s_L positions cannot be restored; every admissible K.μ⁻ firing leaves the post-state with strictly fewer s_L positions and violates the boundary frame."
**Problem**: The header label "Forced" implies an intrinsic structural impossibility, but the qualification "Within the K.μ⁻ + K.μ⁺ vocabulary" reveals the restriction is scoped to the canonical decomposition's chosen sub-vocabulary. Under ValidComposite★'s full vocabulary, an alternative decomposition could fire K.μ⁻ admissibly: shrink V_{s_L}(d) via K.μ⁻, add Insertion via K.μ⁺, then restore the shrunken s_L positions via successive K.μ⁺_L firings (each placing one link at shift(max(V_{s_L}(d)), 1) per D-CTG★ in ASN-0047 — the V-position selection rule supports sequential re-construction of the original arrangement). Case (i.b) is therefore symmetric in character to case (ii) — both are canonical-vocabulary choices, not intrinsic-structural forcings. A reader matching the "Forced" / "Canonical choice" headers to the cases will conclude the wrong taxonomy.
**Required**: Rename case (i.b) to align with case (ii)'s "Canonical-decomposition choice" framing, or add an explicit clause noting that case (i.b) is forced under the canonical K.μ⁻ + K.μ⁺ sub-vocabulary but not under ValidComposite★'s full K.μ⁻ + K.μ⁺ + K.μ⁺_L vocabulary. The distinction matters when readers compare with case (i.a), which IS intrinsically forced (K.μ⁻'s `dom(M(d)) ≠ ∅` precondition fails outright).

### Issue 2: INS.frame.subspace formal claim is one-directional
**ASN-0100, Claims Introduced table, INS.frame.subspace**: "Non-content subspaces of d are unchanged: M'(d) agrees with M(d) on positions with subspace ≠ s_C"
**Problem**: The formal contract's quantifier `(A v : v ∈ dom(M(d)) ∧ subspace(v) ≠ s_C : v ∈ dom(M'(d)) ∧ M'(d)(v) = M(d)(v))` says pre-state non-s_C positions persist with same images, but says nothing about whether new non-s_C positions appear in dom(M'(d)). The verbose justification argues both directions ("step 3's K.μ⁺ adds only content-subspace V-positions"), and INS.inv.cross-subspace separately states `V_{s_L}(d') = V_{s_L}(d)` for the s_L specialization. The two claims together give bidirectionality (combined with S3★-aux's enumeration of subspaces), but INS.frame.subspace alone permits a reading in which new non-s_C positions appear in M'(d). The reading is excluded only by combining claims — the formal frame should stand on its own.
**Required**: Strengthen INS.frame.subspace's formal statement to `{v ∈ dom(M'(d)) : subspace(v) ≠ s_C} = {v ∈ dom(M(d)) : subspace(v) ≠ s_C} ∧ (A v in that set :: M'(d)(v) = M(d)(v))`, or fold it into INS.inv.cross-subspace as a single bidirectional claim. The current redundancy between INS.frame.subspace and INS.inv.cross-subspace also suggests consolidation.

### Issue 3: Projection derivation's Step 2 formula tacitly assumes K.μ⁻ fires
**ASN-0100, §Coverage and link discoverability, Step 2**: "Step 2 — after K.μ⁻ (when fired). K.μ⁻ retains R_kept := L ∪ V_{s_L}(d) where L := {v ∈ V_{s_C}(d) : v < p}; the Right region is removed from dom(M(d)). By LP10 (ContractionMonotonicity; ASN-0098), the projection contracts to its intersection with the kept domain: `project(ℓ, i, d, Σ_μ⁻) = P_0 ∩ R_kept = P_0^L ∪ P_0^{s_L}`"
**Problem**: When K.μ⁻ does not fire (cases i.a, i.b, ii), the intermediate Σ_μ⁻ does not exist as a separate state. The formula given is only meaningful when K.μ⁻ fires. The final formula `project(ℓ, i, d, Σ') = P_0^L ∪ {shift(v, n) : v ∈ P_0^R} ∪ P_0^{s_L} ∪ N_I` happens to reduce correctly in K.μ⁻-omitted cases because P_0^R = ∅ in those cases (empty arrangement or append leaves R empty), but the coincidence is not explicit. A skeptical reader has to verify independently that the derivation chain composes correctly when Step 2 is absent.
**Required**: Either insert a clause after Step 2 — "When K.μ⁻ does not fire (cases i.a, i.b, ii), Σ_μ⁻ coincides with the post-Step-1 state, P_0^R = ∅, and the formula reduces to project(ℓ, i, d, post-Step-1) = P_0^L ∪ P_0^{s_L}" — or restructure Step 2 to handle the conditional explicitly with a case split.

### Issue 4: Worked example's non-tight alternative uses informal terminology
**ASN-0100, §A Worked Example, Non-tight alternative**: "If tight(e_1, Σ_{e_1}) does not hold — for example, e_1 was incorporated with a span whose coverage {a₂, a₃, a₄} is a non-canonical or sparsely populated tumbler interval at Σ_{e_1} — then LP19a does not apply, and a freshly allocated a_{new0} or a_{new1} may land in coverage(e_1)."
**Problem**: "Non-canonical or sparsely populated" conflates two distinct tightness-failure modes from ASN-0098's `tight` definition: (a) a span (s, ℓ) with ℓ not an ordinal displacement of length #s is unconditionally non-tight (per LP-Fin's infinite F-candidate count for non-canonical spans), and (b) a canonical span (s, δ(n, #s)) is non-tight when some F-candidate in [s, s ⊕ δ(n, #s)) is missing from dom(C) ∪ dom(L) at Σ_e. The two failure modes have different operational character — (a) is a span-shape property, (b) is a state-population property — and the worked example should name them with the foundation's terminology rather than the colloquial "sparsely populated."
**Required**: Replace the informal phrasing with explicit reference to the canonical-form clause and the F-candidate-coverage clause of ASN-0098's tight definition, naming one concrete failure mode (e.g., "if a₄ had not yet been allocated at Σ_{e_1}, then the canonical span (a₂, δ(3, #a₂)) carries a₄ as an F-candidate missing from dom(Σ_{e_1}.C), making the span non-tight").
