# Review of ASN-0128

## REVISE

### Issue 1: DR's unconditional-postcondition claim contradicts the wrapper's own idem hit branch
**ASN-0128, Standard registrations (DR)**: "For `Nullify_Binary` itself, once `P0 ∧ P-reg ∧ P-tgt` admits the call, every postcondition of ASN-0126's contract holds unconditionally — in particular single-tuple scope, which yields the displayed wp"
**Problem**: R ships `idem = ⊤` (S3) and `Nullify_Binary ≡ Emit_R`, so the wrapper inherits I1's de-duplication — S3 says so itself ("re-retracting the same target from the same document dedups to the existing tuple"). On a hit, I1 takes no step: "Σ' = Σ, nothing is deposited, and ASN-0086's emit postconditions are forfeited wholesale." ASN-0126's Nullify_Binary contract includes Effect clauses — fresh emitter `b ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, `b ∈ dom(Σ'.L)`, `home(b) = d_retr`, `A_rel^{Σ'} = A_rel^Σ ∪ {b}` — and every one of them fails on an admitted hit call. Even restricted to the Postconditions block (coverage nullification, target-nullification-iff-residence, single-tuple scope, persistence), those clauses are postconditions of *the step*; on a hit there is no step, and re-establishing them at Σ' = Σ requires a different argument: hit ⇒ an I0-equal active retraction tuple exists with to-coverage `subtree(a)` ⇒ `a ∈ nullified(Σ)`; surface-discipline ⇒ `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` (the existing tuple's target was P-tgt-valid, and subtree coverage determines its root uniquely); single-tuple scope then needs R0a's antichain. None of this appears in DR, and the displayed wp inherits the gap because it is derived from the unconditional-contract sentence.
**Required**: Split DR's conclusion by branch. Miss: ASN-0126's contract verbatim. Hit: no step, Effect clauses forfeited, plus an explicit proof that nullification, single-tuple scope, and persistence already hold at Σ via the pre-existing tuple (surface-discipline for `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)`, R0a for scope). Since this note *is* the operational layer, state the wrapper's full surface contract — preconditions, branch condition, per-branch postconditions — in one place.

### Issue 2: RP's transfer principle is invoked for path-quantified claims it does not cover
**ASN-0128, The registration record (RP)**: "Hence every ASN-0126 claim whose conclusion reads only C, M, L, registration status (key-side, ρ-preserved), and shapes — the gate, P3–P6, the projection bridge onto ASN-0086, FrontierUnification, RangeSterilization — holds at extended-record states by evaluation at ρ(Σ)"
**Problem**: "Evaluation at ρ(Σ)" is the single-state move (ASN-0126's B1/B2 analog). RangeSterilization is successor-quantified — its clauses constrain post-states Θ and every `→_sh*`-successor Θ' — so it is not a predicate of one state and cannot be obtained by evaluating anything at ρ(Σ) alone. The same shape is load-bearing elsewhere: S3 imports ASN-0126's Nullify_Binary postconditions including persistence ("`nullified(Σ') ⊆ nullified(Θ)` at every `→_sh*`-successor"), and I2 applies RangeSterilization at extended-record states. ASN-0126 itself models the correct practice by separating B2 (single-state) from B3 (path) with an explicit scope restriction; RP compresses both into one phrase. Separately, RP states the step-*lift* direction (ASN-0126 step ⇒ extended-record step) only for P5's instance, but exposing `Nullify_Binary` as a surface operation at extended states needs the general lift for the wrapper's miss-branch step.
**Required**: State RP's transfer clauses explicitly, mirroring ASN-0126's apparatus: (a) single-state conclusions by evaluation at ρ(Σ); (b) path-quantified conclusions via derivation projection — extended `→_sh*` derivations project under ρ by (ii)/(iii), and per-successor conclusions pull back through shared components; (c) the general step lift (preconditions read only ρ-preserved data, effects determined on C/M/L, registry framed). Cite the appropriate clause at each use site (I2, S3, DR).

### Issue 3: AD's two-regime classification contradicts the definitions of `is_in_chain` and `target_of`
**ASN-0128, Denotation and views**: "*Membership predicates* (`is_K`, `is_filtered`, `is_in_chain`) take an address argument and test coverage — `addr ∈ coverage(F)` — total over all tuples, address-denoting or not"
**Problem**: B2 defines `is_in_chain(addr, target)` as "`target ∈ chain(addr)`", and `chain()` enumerates *denoted* vertices — exact addresses, not coverages. An extension `y` of a chain element `x` satisfies the coverage reading (`x ≼ y`) but not list membership; the two definitions disagree, and `is_in_chain` is not "total over all tuples" in the coverage sense, since non-denoting edges never enter the walk at all. In the same denotation regime, B3's `target_of` parenthetical "(`addrs(G) = {y}`: one span by Binary, denoting one address by AD)" overclaims: AD guarantees denotation only for *surface-emitted* endsets; a raw-emitted Binary tuple's single G-span may be non-unit-depth, giving `addrs(G) = ∅` — a case the ⊥ clause ("⊥ when none or several", which counts tuples, not spans) does not cover. `targets_keyed` also leaves unstated whether ⊥-valued types are omitted from the returned map.
**Required**: Reclassify `is_in_chain` as enumeration-derived (or redefine it by coverage in B2 — pick one and say so); condition `target_of`'s parenthetical on an address-denoting G and assign the non-denoting case its verdict (presumably ⊥, per AD's omission rule); state `targets_keyed`'s treatment of ⊥ entries.

### Issue 4: the surface `Emit_K`'s precondition on `d` is branch-dependent and never stated
**ASN-0128, Idem operational semantics (I1, hit branch)**: "The `d` argument is consulted only on a miss."
**Problem**: ASN-0086's `Emit_K` carries precondition `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, and `a_emit` is total only on `(Σ, d ∈ dom(Σ.M))` — so the miss branch silently requires it, while the quoted sentence says the hit branch ignores `d` entirely. Admission of a call with `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)` then depends on whether a hit exists — a state-dependent precondition the caller cannot evaluate — and the note never says whether such a call that *misses* is rejected (like a gate failure) or simply undefined. The note pins the gate-versus-dedup evaluation order with an explicit observability argument; the `d`-validation order is equally observable and left open.
**Required**: Add `d`'s clause to I1's contract: either `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` is checked alongside the gate (uniform precondition, hits included), or the hit branch admits arbitrary `d` and a miss with `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)` is rejected with no step and no address — state which.

### Issue 5: `retract_stale`'s harmlessness argument cites dedup in cases where dedup does not fire
**ASN-0128, Behaviors (B4)**: "a target already retracted — by interleaving, or earlier in the batch — is unproblematic, the repeat wrapper call deduplicating to the existing retraction tuple by S3's idem identity."
**Problem**: I0-identity for R is (retracting-document subtree, target subtree) — S3's own observation ("idem identity degenerates in F: the from-fill is per-document"). A target already retracted by an interleaved writer from a *different* document is not I0-equal to the batch's call, so the call deposits a fresh tuple — no dedup occurs. Likewise, if the prior retraction tuple has itself been nullified (retraction-of-retraction), it is absent from `A_R`, the dedup check misses (I2), and a fresh deposit occurs. Both outcomes are harmless, but for a reason the text does not give: any admitted unit-depth wrapper call on an existing link address is contract-conforming and cannot sterilize (DR). As written, the justification is false in two of the three sub-cases it claims to cover.
**Required**: Replace the blanket dedup justification with the case split: same-document re-retraction of an active retraction dedups (hit); the different-document and nullified-prior cases deposit a fresh, P-tgt-valid, unit-depth retraction — redundant but harmless under DR.

### Issue 6: B2's Effect sentence claims a transitivity that no provided predicate realizes
**ASN-0128, Behaviors (B2)**: "Substrate treats the relation as transitive and exposes walk predicates over the active view's denoted graph."
**Problem**: Nothing cashes out "treats the relation as transitive." `succs` is one-step; `chain`/`tip`/`is_in_chain` are determinate-walk only and stop at the first branch — on a branched graph, a genuinely reachable target is invisible to all four predicates. The behavior is *named* transitive-closure, yet the catalog ships no closure or reachability predicate, and no shipped semantics depends on transitivity. The note's own canonical case — `supersedes` with competing claims — is precisely where the walk stops and reachability questions become unanswerable.
**Required**: Either add the closure predicate the name promises (e.g., `reach(x, y)` over the denoted digraph — finite and decidable by the same L-fin argument the note already makes), or delete the "treats as transitive" sentence and rename the behavior for what it provides (determinate-walk), recording reachability as an explicit deferral.

### Issue 7: `chain()`'s termination bound quantifies over an infinite vertex set
**ASN-0128, Behaviors (B2)**: "vertices are addresses" … "Termination: the sequence is injective into the denoted graph's finite vertex set, so `t = |vertices| − (n + 1)` is a decreasing bound."
**Problem**: With "vertices are addresses," the vertex set is all of `T` — infinite — and `|vertices|` is meaningless. The finiteness claim in the text ("the graph is finite — L-fin bounds the edges") is about edges; the termination argument needs the *vertex* set pinned, and the set actually in play is the denoted endpoints of active edges plus the start address (which may carry no edge yet still heads a chain of length one).
**Required**: Define the denoted graph's vertex set as the finite set of endpoints of its edges, note that `chain(addr)` prepends `addr`, and restate the decreasing bound over that set.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: Multi-writer serialization machinery
I4 fixes interleaving as the concurrency model and posits a "serializing authority" that orders calls before they become steps. Specifying that authority — ordering protocol, fairness, what concurrent callers observe mid-race — is genuine new machinery.
**Why out of scope**: the note explicitly inherits ASN-0086's sequential relation and draws the boundary correctly; a concurrency model is a successor's territory, not an error here.

### Topic 2: Caller-facing result algebra
Hit, miss, and rejection are three observably distinct outcomes (step + fresh address; no step + existing address; no step + no address), but whether a caller can distinguish them, and what a rejection reports, is unspecified.
**Why out of scope**: the state semantics and returned addresses are fully pinned; typing the observable result channel is API-surface work for a successor note.

### Topic 3: Per-type counting and cardinality predicates
No shipped predicate reports `|A_K^Σ|` or `|members(K)|`, and per-type bag-versus-set reporting under `idem = ⊥` is unaddressed even before the cross-type composites of the note's Open question 2.
**Why out of scope**: this belongs with the predicate-composition successor the note already designates; the atomic predicate set here is coherent without it.

VERDICT: REVISE
