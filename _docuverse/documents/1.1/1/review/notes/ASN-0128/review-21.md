# Review of ASN-0128

This note is technically tight. The load-bearing machinery checks out under scrutiny: RP's projection argument correctly mirrors ASN-0126's π/B1–B3 pattern and is careful about which transfer clause carries each result (RangeSterilization via RP-b rather than RP-a, FrontierUnification via RP-a, transition invariants per-step); I0's case closure over the slots a separating pair could inhabit is complete (F by single-span identity under P6's `|F| = 1`, Unary/Binary G vacuously or by the same identity, Multi G read only in result position); I0a's two inclusions are both proved; I1a's induction covers the K ~ R wrapper instantiation, born-nullified deposits, and shrink-only effects on other classes; I6's wp is argued for necessity as well as sufficiency, with the attainability convention made explicit and the idem-⊥ corollary derived rather than asserted; DR's derivation (target enters the link domain at deposit, persists by L12a, distinctness at the later emit's pre-state, antichain R0a at its post-state) is sound, and its hit branch correctly rules out the self-emit disjunct on-discipline while exhibiting the off-discipline counterexamples that show the SD hypothesis is real. The example section exercises hit suppression, born-nullified landing, branch/cycle verdicts, and batch retraction against the contracts. The remaining findings are anti-bloat items — the note carries the classifier, and the accretion is real.

## REVISE

### Issue 1: ASN-0126 open-question bookkeeping is stated at three layers
**ASN-0128, intro / What this note commits / Idem operational semantics ¶1 / Standard registrations ¶1**: intro: "What ASN-0126 deliberately defers to a successor (its Open Questions 1–4) — what idempotence *means* at emit, which behaviors the substrate provides, what predicates every registered type receives, and whether any types ship pre-registered — is the subject of this note." Then each commit bullet: "Answers ASN-0126 Open Question 1/2/3… settling Open Question 4… settling Open Question 7." Then the section bodies again: "ASN-0126 leaves what idem(K) does — and even what 'the same tuple' means — to this note (its Open Question 1)" and "ASN-0126 leaves open whether each substrate's `Σ_init.registry` is composed entirely of app-declared entries (its Open Question 4). This note answers:…"
**Problem**: The same provenance mapping is carried three times — the intro's parenthetical enumerates the four questions the commit bullets then restate one-by-one, and two section openings restate their question's provenance a third time before doing any work. This is the "two paragraphs say the same thing in different words" pattern, compounded across layers; a reader following the idem contract must step over the same bookkeeping at every level of descent.
**Required**: Carry the open-question mapping in exactly one place — the commits list is the natural home. Trim the intro's enumeration to the bare "its Open Questions 1–4," and open the Idem and Standard-registrations sections with the technical question itself, not its provenance.

### Issue 2: View selection paragraph defers to the same location three times
**ASN-0128, Denotation and views, View selection**: "The two readings are genuinely two: where they coincide, and why no constructible substrate makes them coincide everywhere, is BH1's Rewrite scope's analysis (Φ-nonemptiness and the coincidence cases), which owns it." and, two sentences later, "…the two rewritten surfaces are fixed in BH1's Rewrite scope."
**Problem**: One paragraph points at BH1's Rewrite scope three times (the parenthetical citation in sentence 1, the quoted "which owns it" sentence, and the closing clause). The quoted sentence advances no semantics — the paragraph's first two sentences already define the selector and bind its two readings to D1/D3 and the BH1 rewrites; the rest is ownership bookkeeping of exactly the "multiple deferrals to the same downstream location" form.
**Required**: One citation to BH1's Rewrite scope suffices. Delete the "The two readings are genuinely two… which owns it" sentence and the trailing "are fixed in BH1's Rewrite scope" clause.

### Issue 3: S3 restates the P-tgt promotion's effect inside its own paragraph
**ASN-0128, S3 (Retraction)**: "…*promotes P-tgt from postcondition hypothesis to enforced precondition*: `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` or `a = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)`." followed, after the ghost-target scenario, by "The wrapper's unit-depth to-span closes ASN-0126's first gap by construction; the P-tgt rejection closes the second; together they convert the contract's iff-P-tgt clauses into unconditional guarantees of the operation surface and make sterilization (Corollary RangeSterilization) unreachable through substrate-mediated retraction."
**Problem**: The policy's effect is stated, then re-summarized in different words in the same paragraph — and the re-summary also repeats the commits bullet ("turning the P-tgt-*conditional* postconditions … into guarantees"). The ghost-target sterilization scenario between them is load-bearing motivation and should stay; the recap sentence is the relocated shadow of the promotion sentence, and the actual conversion of iff-clauses into guarantees is proved where it belongs, in DR.
**Required**: State the policy and its consequence once. Keep the promotion sentence and the ghost-target scenario; cut the "closes the first gap… closes the second… together they convert" recap (or reduce it to the single clause naming sterilization-unreachability, if that claim is to be made before DR proves it).

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: The serialization model behind I4
I4 correctly scopes `→_sh` as a sequential, interleaved relation and posits "a serializing authority orders the two calls before either becomes a step." What that authority is, what atomicity granularity it guarantees (the dedup-consultation-plus-deposit of a single surface call must be one unit, or I1's hit/miss branch condition is evaluated against a stale state), and how it composes across homes are unspecified.
**Why out of scope**: Concurrency semantics are deliberately outside the step relation, inherited from ASN-0086's model; specifying the authority is a topology/runtime concern for a successor, not an error in this note's per-call contracts.

### Topic 2: Caller-visible rejection discrimination
Rejection is uniformly "no step, no address" across gate failure, the `K ≁ R` exclusion, invalid home, and P-tgt failure. Whether a caller can distinguish these outcomes — an error model for the operation surface — is unaddressed.
**Why out of scope**: The note fixes the state-level semantics of rejection completely (and S3 explicitly fixes the error semantics it needs); discriminated error reporting is surface-API territory for a future note, alongside the predicate-composition successor.

VERDICT: REVISE
