# Review of ASN-0128

## REVISE

### Issue 1: `retract_stale`'s "voiding the batch rather than truncating it" is contradicted by its own parenthetical
**ASN-0128, Behaviors, BH4 (age-staleness), `retract_stale`**: "conversely, a `d_retr ∉ dom(Σ.M)` fails P0 at every pre-state the batch's own steps produce (`K.λ_sh` frames `Σ.M`, so only an interleaved allocation of `d_retr` could change the verdict mid-batch), and each such call is rejected — no step, no address — the constant argument voiding the batch rather than truncating it."
**Problem**: The same paragraph permits interleaving ("The batch is a *sequence* of `→_sh` steps … interleaving with other operations is permitted"), and the parenthetical concedes that an interleaved K.σ step can allocate `d_retr` mid-batch. Under exactly that scenario, constituents before the allocation are rejected and constituents after it are admitted: the batch is *front-truncated* — neither voided nor complete. The universal "each such call is rejected" quantifies only over "pre-states the batch's own steps produce," but the relevant pre-states include those produced by interleaved steps, which the void claim must survive and does not. The main clause asserts a dichotomy its own parenthetical refutes.
**Required**: Either condition the void claim explicitly — absent interleaved allocation of `d_retr`, an invalid `d_retr` voids the batch; with such an allocation the batch resumes from that point, and say whether that partial execution is acceptable — or specify that `retract_stale` evaluates P0 once at the batch's initial state and refuses to issue any constituent on failure, making the void semantics true by construction.

### Issue 2: The wp analysis stops at Case 1 — no consolidated contract or weakest precondition for idem=⊤ `Emit_K`
**ASN-0128, Standard registrations, DR**: "Both branches thus deliver single-tuple scope at the call's post-state — by the step's contract on a miss, by the pre-existing tuple on a hit — which yields the displayed wp, derivation now per branch, and completes the wp Case-1 parallelism ASN-0126's review trail left open."
**Problem**: Case 1 (the wrapped retraction) gets the full treatment: uniform preconditions, branch condition, per-branch postconditions, and a derived per-branch wp. Case 2 (an emit landing active) gets nothing analogous at this note's surface, even though this note is precisely what invalidates the inherited analysis: ASN-0086's wp Case 2 and ASN-0126's WP are computed for the raw, always-stepping gated emit, and under `idem = ⊤` the surface operation may take no step. On a hit, the returned address `a'` bears the incumbent's stored `(F', G')`, not the presented `(F, G)`, so the inherited postcondition `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}` does not even type-check against what the surface returns. The pieces of the surface contract exist but are scattered — hit-returns-active-incumbent (I1), miss inherits the gated contract (I1), the born-nullified caveat (I3), and the load-bearing "for non-R types every deposit lands active" fact buried as the last sentence of I4, a concurrency item. No single statement gives the caller-facing postcondition — e.g., "the returned address bears an active K-tuple whose coverage pair equals `(coverage(F), coverage(G))`" — or its weakest precondition.
**Required**: A DR-style consolidated contract for `Emit_K` under `idem = ⊤`: per-branch postconditions in one place, and the derived surface wp for the returned-address-active postcondition — on a surface-disciplined substrate, for `K ≁ R`, this should reduce to gate-conformance ∧ (hit ∨ `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`), with the C2/C3 vacuity argued from DR rather than left for the reader to assemble. State it and derive it per branch, as DR does for Nullify.

### Issue 3: R-C0's BH4 compatibility row is stipulated, never argued — and the Multi requirement is unused by BH4's own machinery
**ASN-0128, The registration record, R-C0**: "age-staleness (BH4) requires Multi with `idem = ⊥`."
**Problem**: The other three rows are load-bearing in the predicate definitions they govern: `is_filtered` quantifies over `(a, F, ∅)` tuples (Unary's form), BH2's one-edge-per-tuple reading and BH3's `target_of` both use Binary's `|G| = 1`. BH4's three predicates — `age`, `stale`, `retract_stale` — read only the tuple's address, its home-chain index, and the active subset; none reads G, the arity, or the shape. The Multi restriction is therefore exercised by nothing in the note. The `idem = ⊥` restriction has an inferable rationale — under `idem = ⊤` a re-emission of an identical event hits and returns the incumbent's address, so age cannot be refreshed by re-emitting, defeating lease renewal — but the note never states it. This is not a cosmetic omission: R-C0 is a well-formedness condition that R-VAL enforces by *failing construction* — a Binary `idem = ⊥` event type attaching age-staleness yields no `Σ_init` at all, on a constraint with no derivation, in a note that argues every comparable choice (I0 at length, rejection semantics, the `d_retr` parameter).
**Required**: Derive each clause of the BH4 row or relax it to what the machinery requires: state the dedup/age-refresh argument if it is the reason for `idem = ⊥`; give the reason Multi is required or drop the shape restriction.

### Issue 4: Enumeration predicates match their arguments inconsistently — denotation-keyed forward, coverage-keyed reverse — and AD's regime taxonomy is silent on it
**ASN-0128, Default predicates D3**: "`targets_of(x) = ⋃ { addrs(G) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ ∧ x ∈ addrs(F) }`" — versus **Behaviors, BH3**: "`sources_to(target)` … the argument is tested by coverage (a membership test, decidable), the result is denoted addresses (finite, AD)."
**Problem**: AD's two-regime taxonomy fixes *result* typing — membership predicates test coverage, enumeration predicates return denoted sets — but says nothing about how enumeration predicates match their *arguments*. The instances then fork: `targets_of`, `succs`, `target_of`, and `targets_keyed` key the argument on exact denotation (`x ∈ addrs(F)`), while `sources_to` — equally an enumeration predicate — keys its argument on coverage (`target ∈ coverage(G)`). The forward choice sits in unacknowledged tension with I0's own doctrine, argued at length there: "a denoted address asserts its entire subtree." Under that reading, a tuple whose F denotes `t` asserts for `t.x` — and indeed `is_K(t.x) = ⊤` by D2 — yet `targets_of(t.x) = ∅`. Each choice may be defensible (denotation-keying keeps BH2's walk on exact vertices; coverage-keying on the G-side accommodates range-valued to-coverages such as retraction subtrees), but the note pins both silently and argues neither. D2 reconciles the regimes for `is_K` against `members`; the argument-side fork is the case left hanging.
**Required**: State the argument-matching rule for the enumeration family explicitly in AD (or per predicate), and argue the forward/reverse asymmetry or unify it — in particular, reconcile `targets_of`'s denotation-keyed argument with I0's assertion-as-subtree reading, in the style of D2's bridge.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: Default-view dominance where BH1 meets BH2/BH3 (filtered mid-chain elements, `sources_to` of a filtered address)
**Why out of scope**: The note commits the active-view semantics fully and flags the default-view dominance choice as Open question 1 with both options on the table; either choice is a fresh design commitment over machinery this note already pins, properly a successor's decision — though since `retired` and `supersedes` both ship, that successor should come soon.

### Topic 2: Predicate composition algebra over the default and behavior-unlocked predicates
**Why out of scope**: Connectives, nesting, and cross-type composite queries (Open question 2, and ASN-0126's deferred composition question) are a new layer over the atomic predicates this note defines; nothing in this note's contracts depends on the composition rules.

### Topic 3: Multi-app registry composition and collision resolution
**Why out of scope**: C0/R-C1 state the constraint (key uniqueness, designation non-collision) and R-VAL enforces it at construction; the *protocol* by which several apps' declarations merge into one `Σ_init.registry` is genuinely new machinery — a construction-time coordination story, not a gap in the operational semantics specified here.

VERDICT: REVISE
