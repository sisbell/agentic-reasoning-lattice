# Review of ASN-0128

## REVISE

### Issue 1: Where the idem check lives — transition relation or operation surface — is unresolved, and the blanket transfer claim depends on the answer
**ASN-0128, What this note commits / Idem operational semantics / The registration record**: "Specifies what `K.λ_sh` does when the emitted tuple is 'the same' as an existing active tuple…" versus "every ASN-0126 claim — the gate, P3–P6, the projection bridge, gate realizability — holds over extended-record states without modification."
**Problem**: These two claims cannot both stand. If de-duplication is part of `K.λ_sh` itself (as the bullet and section framing say), then the duplicate-deposit step is removed from the relation, and ASN-0126's P5 (GateRealizability) — which asserts that for *any* reachable Σ, registered K, and Sh-conf-passing `(F, G)` there *exists* a `→_sh` step depositing `(F, G, K)` at the fresh address — becomes false at any state holding a coverage-equal active tuple of an idem=⊤ type. If instead dedup lives in the `Emit_K` operation (as "read by the *operations and predicates of this note*, never by the gate" suggests), the relation is untouched and P5 survives, but the framing "what `K.λ_sh` does" is wrong and must be restated. Separately, the transfer of "every ASN-0126 claim" to the extended-record state space is asserted in one sentence. The state space changed (registry values are now triples, not shapes); ASN-0126's own precedent for exactly this move is a full apparatus — π, ProjectionBridge, B1–B3 — not a remark. A record-forgetting projection (each entry `(shape, idem, behaviors) ↦ shape`) with a step-preservation argument is required: gate verdicts agree because Sh-conf reads only the shape component, frames agree componentwise, `Σ_init` projects to ASN-0126's `Σ_init`.
**Required**: Pin the locus — state explicitly that `→_sh` is unchanged and `Emit_K` is a surface operation that consults `A_K^Σ` and either invokes `K.λ_sh` or returns without taking a step (or, if the relation is being refined, re-prove P5 and everything downstream of it). State the extended-record transfer as a lemma with the projection and the (short) induction, per ASN-0126's own precedent.

### Issue 2: I1's dedup-hit branch is underdetermined and silently violates the inherited Emit_K contract
**ASN-0128, I1 (IdemDedupSemantics)**: "if some `(a, F', G') ∈ A_K^Σ` is the same as `(F, G)` in the sense of I0, the emit returns the existing address `a` and produces no new tuple. The check consults only the `(F, G, K)` values…"
**Problem**: Three gaps. (i) *Choice*: "the existing address `a`" presupposes a unique match. Nothing proves at most one coverage-equal active tuple per idem=⊤ type exists — on a non-surface-disciplined substrate (which the note explicitly admits: I3's born-nullified case, DR's quantifier discussion), raw `K.λ_sh` steps can deposit coverage-equal duplicates, and then `Emit_K` is not a well-defined function. Even on the disciplined surface, the uniqueness invariant ("at most one active K-tuple per `(coverage(F), coverage(G))` pair, for idem=⊤ K") is the obvious guarantee idempotence buys, is needed by I1, is provable by an easy induction (coverage is a pure function of the endset, so equality never changes post hoc; each surface emit dedups or deposits into a match-free active subset; nullification only removes) — and is stated nowhere. (ii) *Home*: `Emit_K(Σ, d, F, G)` takes a caller-supplied home, and ASN-0086's contract promises `home(a) = d`, `a ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, `a ∈ dom(Σ'.L)`. On a dedup hit across homes — caller `d₂`, existing tuple homed at `d₁` — every one of those postconditions fails, and the `d` argument is silently ignored. I1's exclusion list ("proposer, emission time, source app are not part of the identity") never mentions the home, so cross-home dedup is the implied semantics, but the divergence from the foundation's Emit_K contract is never acknowledged or respecified. (iii) *Ordering*: for an emit that fails the gate but matches an active tuple under I0 (sameness is coverage-based, so a two-span F can match a one-span F'), is the call rejected or does it return the existing address? The order of gate versus dedup check is unspecified and observably different.
**Required**: A full hit/miss contract for `Emit_K` under idem=⊤: gate-first ordering (or the alternative, stated); the uniqueness invariant proved for disciplined surfaces plus a choice rule (e.g., T1-least match) off-discipline; an explicit statement that on a hit the returned address's home may differ from `d` and which ASN-0086 postconditions are forfeited.

### Issue 3: "Enforced precondition" P-tgt contradicts Open Question 3's fallback-admit option, and the fallback re-enables total sterilization through the wrapper
**ASN-0128, S3 / DR / Open question 3**: "*promotes P-tgt from postcondition hypothesis to enforced precondition*… make sterilization (Corollary RangeSterilization) unreachable through substrate-mediated retraction… containment is complete" versus OQ3: "what are the error semantics when this note's P-tgt precondition fails — rejected call, or fall back to ASN-0126's contract behavior (the call is admitted…)?"
**Problem**: If fallback-admit is open, "enforced precondition" means nothing, and DR's definition of surface-disciplined — "every retraction is wrapper-routed with a P-tgt-valid target," where the "i.e." glosses wrapper-routed *as* P-tgt-valid — breaks: a legal-surface history could contain a P-tgt-failing wrapper call. The consequence is not cosmetic. An admitted call with ghost target `a = d.0.s_L` (any tumbler is T12-admissible as a unit-depth to-span target, as the note itself recalls) deposits to-coverage `subtree(d.0.s_L)`, which contains every chain slot `d.0.s_L.m` — every future `a_emit(·, d)` — so every subsequent emission homed at `d` is born nullified: total sterilization of `d`, achieved *through the wrapper*. The headline claims (OQ7 settled, "containment is complete") are true only under rejection semantics, so rejection is not open — it is load-bearing.
**Required**: Fix rejection (no step) as the semantics of a P-tgt-failing `Nullify_Binary` call, in the S3/DR body; restrict OQ3 to the gate-refinement question. Alternatively, keep fallback open and weaken every containment claim accordingly — but the note cannot claim both.

### Issue 4: B2's `tip`/`chain` are ill-defined under branching and cycles, which the note's own text shows are possible
**ASN-0128, B2 (transitive-closure)**: "`tip(addr) → addr` (forward-walk to the maximal element of the chain rooted at addr…), `chain(addr) → ordered list of addrs`."
**Problem**: The note itself stresses (B3, D3) that Binary fixes each *tuple's* `|G| = 1`, "not the number of active tuples per source, so functionality in F is an app convention, not a shape guarantee." Then the active K-graph may branch — two active tuples with the same source coverage and different targets — and "the chain rooted at addr" is not a chain; `tip` returning a single address is ill-defined. Worse, nothing prevents cycles: emit `(F ∋ a, G ∋ b)` and `(F ∋ b, G ∋ a)` — both gate-pass, neither is I0-equal to the other, both land active — and the forward walk diverges; no termination argument exists (L-fin gives finiteness of the tuple set, not acyclicity of the walk). B3's `target_of` handles exactly this multiplicity with an explicit `⊥`; B2's predicates handle nothing. Since `supersedes` ships with B2 on every substrate (S2), this is not an exotic corner — it is the shipped configuration.
**Required**: Either restrict B2's predicates to states where the active K-graph is functional and acyclic (with a stated verdict — `⊥`, error — outside that domain), or define them on the general finite digraph (set of reachable sinks; cycle detection) with a termination argument.

### Issue 5: The predicate layer is typed at addresses while the data is spans — coverages are infinite address sets, and "the G-address" is ill-typed
**ASN-0128, D1/D3, B1–B4**: "`members(K) → set of addrs` … `members(K) = ⋃ { coverage(F) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ }`"; B3: "returns the G-address when exactly one active K-tuple carries that F"; B2: walk predicates returning addresses.
**Problem**: Shapes count *spans*, not addresses. A single unit-depth span `(x, δ(1, #x))` covers `subtree(x) = {t : x ≼ t}` (PrefixSpanCoverage, ASN-0043) — an infinite set. So with even one active tuple, `members(K)` is an infinite set of tumblers and cannot be "returned"; the same applies to `targets_of` and `sources_to` (the latter also says "all F such that…" while typed "set of addrs" — endsets are not addresses). "The G-address" (singular) for a Binary tuple presupposes the one span denotes one address, which no shape constraint guarantees — `Nullify_Binary`'s own to-span covers an entire subtree. B2's walk steps from an address into `coverage(G)`, a *set* — which element does the walk continue from? The worked example papers over all of this by writing `F = a_src`, `G = [a_t1, a_t2, a_t3]`, silently treating spans as single addresses without ever committing to that convention.
**Required**: Either commit the predicate layer to a canonical span-to-address convention (e.g., unit-depth spans with the span start as the denoted address — stated as a discipline, with the verdict for nonconforming tuples), or retype results as span sets/endsets with membership predicates (which are decidable by T2), stating finiteness and computability per predicate. The example must then conform to whichever choice is made.

### Issue 6: D1's formula and B1's implicit exclusion define `members(K)` twice, and "audit view" conflates two different escape hatches
**ASN-0128, B1 / D1 / example**: D1: "`members(K) = ⋃ { coverage(F) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ }`"; B1: "Implicit exclusion of `addr` from `members(K')`… unless the caller requests the audit view explicitly"; example: "The original `marker` tuple is still in `L_K^Σ`; only its visibility in default queries is filtered."
**Problem**: When a B1 type is registered, D1's equation and B1's exclusion assign two different values to the same name `members(K')` — the note never says which is the definition and which the derived view. And the named escape hatch is wrong: the filtered `marker` tuple is still *active* (nothing nullified it), so the view that recovers it is the unfiltered-active view, not the "audit view" — in this corpus's vocabulary the audit slice is `L_K`, which additionally includes *nullified* tuples, a semantically different set. There are three views in play (audit = `L_K`; active = `A_K`; default = active minus B1-filtered) and the note names only two. Finally, B1's rewrite scope is unpinned: it explicitly covers `members` and `targets_of`, OQ1 leaves `sources_to` (B3) open — but B1×B2 is not even raised as open, although `retired` and `supersedes` both ship: `tip()` over a chain whose mid-element is retired is undefined, and that is the canonical lifecycle scenario.
**Required**: A view taxonomy (audit/active/default) with predicates parameterized by view; D1–D3 stated as the active-view definitions, B1 as the default-view rewrite; an explicit list of surfaces B1 rewrites (and a statement that raw `Observe_K` is untouched); the B1×B2 interaction either specified or added to the open questions alongside B1×B3.

### Issue 7: B4 promises wall-clock semantics over a state that has no clock
**ASN-0128, B4 (age-staleness)**: "using the emission ordering of `Emit_K` as the time reference… `stale(threshold_seconds)`… `age(event_addr) → seconds`"; example: "The age-staleness behavior records the emission timestamp."
**Problem**: Σ has exactly four components — C, M, L, registry — none of which holds a timestamp, and R1 forbids the registry from acquiring one. "Records the emission timestamp" has nowhere to record; "emission ordering" yields ordinal information (per-home chain index), not seconds. As specified, B4's interface is unimplementable on the specified state — `age` is a function of nothing in Σ. Additionally, `retract_stale` is a batch of `→_sh` steps; whether it is an atomic surface operation or an interleavable sequence is unstated.
**Required**: Either introduce a specified time source (a new state component with frame conditions across all step kinds — a real extension that must be treated with the same care as the registry extension), or restate B4 ordinally in terms the state supports; and one sentence on `retract_stale`'s composition (sequence of steps, interleaving permitted or not).

### Issue 8: The three shipped registrations have no designated coverage classes
**ASN-0128, Standard registrations**: "three coverage classes ship in `Σ_init.registry` for every substrate."
**Problem**: A registry entry is keyed by a coverage class of a representative endset; the note names `retired`, `supersedes`, `R` but never says which tumbler addresses their classes cover, what representatives sit in `Σ_init.registry`, or — critically — that the three designated classes must be pairwise non-`~`-equal. C0's key uniqueness makes a colliding designation fail construction, which for *mandatory* shipped entries means no substrate exists; yet no condition on the designation prevents this, and R-VAL's checks don't mention it. Without designated keys, "ships in `Σ_init.registry`" is not a constructible commitment, and the example's emits ("emit `retired` on `a_x`") cannot be formalized — there is no K to emit with. ASN-0086's precedent (a "designated coverage class `[R]`" as a substrate parameter) shows the fix.
**Required**: State the three classes as substrate parameters (in the manner of `s_L`, `s_C`), add pairwise non-`~`-equivalence of the shipped designations to R-C0/R-VAL's construction checks, and say how the operation surface exposes the representatives. (The multi-app merge protocol stays in OQ8.)

### Issue 9: I4's unconditional "the winner produces the active tuple" is false in the born-nullified case that I5 is careful to exclude
**ASN-0128, I4 (ConcurrentEmitFirstCommit)**: "For `idem = ⊤`, the winner produces the active tuple at the fresh address; the loser's emit… finds the now-active tuple by I1 and returns its address. For `idem = ⊥`, both emissions produce distinct addresses; both appear in `A_K^Σ`."
**Problem**: If the winner's deposit is born nullified (I3's C3 failure, reachable off-discipline by the note's own admission), the winner's tuple is not active, the loser's dedup check misses, and the loser deposits a second tuple — so both halves of the idem=⊤ claim fail, and the idem=⊥ claim's "both appear in `A_K^Σ`" fails too. I5 carries exactly the needed qualifier ("modulo I3's born-nullified cases"); I4 omits it. Also, "both appear in `A_K^Σ`" names the pre-state; it should be the second post-state.
**Required**: Add the I3 caveat to both branches of I4 (or scope I4 to surface-disciplined emits with a DR citation), and fix the state superscript.

### Issue 10: DR's antichain step is applied to an address that is not yet a link address, and the wp claim names no postcondition
**ASN-0128, DR (DisciplineRestoration)**: "link addresses form a prefix-antichain at every reachable state (R0a…), so no later link address lies strictly under `a`; nor can it equal `a` (key freshness)… and the weakest precondition of `Nullify_Binary` at this surface is `P0 ∧ P-reg ∧ P-tgt`."
**Problem**: At the state Σ where C3 is evaluated, the fresh address `f = a_emit(Σ, d)` is *not* in `dom(Σ.L)`, so R0a at Σ says nothing about `f`. The argument goes through only by instantiating R0a at the post-state of the prospective emit — the step fires regardless of C3 (C3 governs active-vs-born-nullified, not whether the step occurs), so the post-state is reachable, both `a` and `f` lie in its link domain, and the antichain there refutes `a ≺ f`. That instantiation is the load-bearing move and is nowhere stated; "no later link address lies strictly under `a`" hides it. (The equality case is fine: `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` in both P-tgt branches — pre-existing, or the deposit's own emitter — versus `f ∉ dom(Σ.L)`.) Separately, "the weakest precondition of `Nullify_Binary`" is asserted without naming the postcondition; wp is a function of the postcondition, and the reader must infer single-tuple scope from "wp Case-1 parallelism."
**Required**: Spell out the post-state instantiation of R0a (or replace it with the state-local argument: every wrapper to-target is in `dom(Σ.L)`, every fresh address is a chain slot `d.0.s_L.m` with `#E = 2`, and a strict prefix of such a slot that satisfies L1/L1b would need `#E ≥ 2`, forcing full length — contradiction). Name the wp's postcondition explicitly.

### Issue 11: "B2/B3" names a lemma citation and a behavior pair simultaneously
**ASN-0128, S3**: "persistence (via B2/B3 transfer of R6a/R6c)."
**Problem**: Inside this note, B2 and B3 are the transitive-closure and typed-reverse-lookup behaviors; the citation means ASN-0126's bridge lemmas B2 (LemmaTransfer) and B3 (PathTransfer). A reader of this note resolves "B2/B3" to behaviors and the sentence becomes nonsense.
**Required**: Qualify the citation ("ASN-0126's B2/B3") or rename the behavior labels.

### Issue 12: Reference to a non-foundation ASN
**ASN-0128, What this note doesn't cover**: "the territory ASN-0095 occupied before retirement."
**Problem**: ASN-0095 is not a foundation ASN; numbered cross-references outside the foundation set are barred — each ASN must stand without them, and a pointer to a retired note is the least verifiable kind.
**Required**: Drop the number and describe the deferred territory in its own words.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: The serializing authority behind I4
**Why out of scope**: I4 correctly reduces concurrency to serialization ("a serializing authority orders the two calls before either becomes a step"), inheriting ASN-0086's sequential model. The authority itself — its ordering guarantees, fairness, failure behavior — is new machinery for a future note, not a defect here.

### Topic 2: Lifecycle taxonomy for retired-versus-nullified on tuple addresses
**Why out of scope**: L4 generality lets a `retired` from-span cover a *link* address, producing a tuple that is hidden (B1) but active — a state distinct from nullified. The interplay of the two lifecycle mechanisms on relation tuples is coherent future territory once Issue 6's view taxonomy exists; it is not an error in this note.

### Topic 3: Discovery of shipped types by applications
**Why out of scope**: Once Issue 8 fixes the designation of the shipped coverage classes, how an application *learns* the representatives (a query surface, a well-known convention) is operational API territory for a successor.

VERDICT: REVISE
