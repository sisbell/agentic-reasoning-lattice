# Review of ASN-0134

This is a careful note. The core arguments — H0/H1/H2's conflict structure, G1's per-home confluence, the V0/V2/V1 verdict triad, and the §4 identification that idempotent de-duplication resists per-home serialization — are sound, and the degenerate cases that matter for *correctness* (φ=0 first-emission, the both-miss interleaving same-home, P-tgt rejection on the losing order) are handled honestly. I found no correctness bug in the load-bearing machinery.

What I did find, and what this note carries the `anti-bloat` classifier for, is a substantial layer of defensive justification, repeated forward-reference scaffolding, and thrice-stated content that a precise reader must skip past. Those are surfaced below at source, plus one precision gap where the note's own degenerate-case standard was not applied.

## REVISE

### Issue 1: The m=0 / m=1 degenerate-batch atomicity is stated three times
**ASN-0134, A1, A5, and Claims table**: A1's parenthetical — "an `m = 1` fire ... is realized as *exactly one* step, atomic and indistinguishable from a single state-changing operation ... and an `m = 0` fire ... is realized as *zero* steps, vacuously atomic"; A5 — "an `m = 1` fire is a single step, indivisible by A0 exactly as a lone operation is, and an `m = 0` fire ... commits no step, so there is no interior index to land on"; table row A5 — "(an `m = 1` fire is one step and atomic ...; an `m = 0` fire is zero steps and vacuously atomic)".
**Problem**: The identical fact (degenerate batches are atomic; the `m ≥ 2` bound is essential) is asserted in full in A1, re-asserted in full in A5, and re-asserted a third time in the table. Two of the three are pure restatement.
**Required**: State the degenerate-batch atomicity once — it belongs in A5 (NoBatchIsolation), whose whole content is the `m ≥ 2` boundary. Strip A1's long parenthetical to a one-clause pointer ("degenerate batch sizes are A5's"), and let the table say "not atomic for `m ≥ 2`" without re-enumerating m=0/m=1.

### Issue 2: A6 is a single monster paragraph carrying defensive justification of its own package composition
**ASN-0134, A6**: "The two registry *stability* invariants ... are deliberately *not* conjuncts of this package"; "The stack's append-only monotonicity ... are, all three of ASN-0093, deliberately *not* in this package"; and "Nor is ASN-0093's `M1` recoverable from the per-state package: that package's `C2`/`L1a` forbid removing a document that still *hosts* content, but say nothing against removing a *childless* one, so document permanence is genuinely independent."
**Problem**: Two distinct problems. (a) Most of A6's bulk explains *why certain invariants are excluded* from the package rather than stating what canonicity *is* — the "deliberately not" / "must be carried in its own right" passages are package-composition apologetics, the flagged "prose around an axiom explaining why it is needed rather than what it says" pattern. (b) The childless-document justification imagines a case the invariant being defended (M1) already excludes: *no `→_sh` step removes any document*, childless or not, so "removing a childless one" is unreachable. The argument is a metalogical independence claim wearing the costume of a reachable scenario.
**Required**: Reduce A6 to its definitional content — name the per-state package conjuncts and the companion transition clause — and move the logical-independence remarks (P2/R2 as corollaries, M1 not derivable) to at most one sentence, without the imagined childless-document removal. Break the paragraph; a definition this central should not be one unscannable block.

### Issue 3: Repeated cross-section deferrals and an intro that previews §8/§9 as an abstract
**ASN-0134, intro / §1 / §4 / §5 / §6**: the intro flags "two disciplines necessarily resist it ... the reader-side verdict pinning of §8, and ... the *global* per-coverage-class serialization that idempotent (`idem=⊤`) de-duplication demands (§9 clause 8)"; Open Question 5 is deferred to from §1 ("we defer it to Open Question 5") *and* §6 ("the strictly stronger, separately-deferred property of Open Question 5"); Open Question 9 is referenced from §4 ("the race W5 and Open Question 9 already isolate") *and* §5 ("removed only by ... emit-before-retract discipline (Open Question 9)").
**Problem**: This is the flagged "multiple paragraphs in different sections defer to the same downstream location" pattern, plus a preview-as-abstract in the intro that duplicates conclusions §8 and §9 will state in full. The deferrals add no reasoning; they are scaffolding.
**Required**: Defer to each Open Question once, at the single site where the gap is most naturally exposed (OQ5 at A5/§6, OQ9 at W5). Cut the intro's enumeration of §8/§9's resisting disciplines down to the single sentence "per-home order is a writer-side result; §8 and §9 isolate where it does not suffice," and let those sections make the case.

### Issue 4: The "cross-doc cross-subspace pair CrossDocumentDisjointness leaves unnamed" observation is repeated across H0, H1, W1 — and inside H1 itself
**ASN-0134, H0 proof / H1 proof / W1**: H0 — "the cross-home, cross-subspace pair ... which neither `CrossDocumentDisjointness` nor `DisjointSubAllocatorChains` names on its own — is absorbed by the first"; H1 — "including the cross-document, cross-subspace `S ≠ S'` pair that `CrossDocumentDisjointness`'s single-`·` statement leaves unnamed" then again "covering uniformly both the same-subspace instance ... and the cross-document, cross-subspace `S ≠ S'` case it leaves unnamed"; W1 — "uniformly across subspaces, the cross-subspace `S ≠ S'` pair included."
**Problem**: The same meta-observation (the foundation lemmas don't name the d≠d′ ∧ S≠S′ case, but origin/framing absorbs it) appears in three proofs, and twice within H1's single proof. W1 explicitly says it contributes "only the *model-intrinsic* label" — so it is restating H1, not adding to it.
**Required**: Make the "origin settles d≠d′ uniformly across subspaces, foundation single-subspace statements notwithstanding" point once, in H1's proof, and have H0 and W1 cite H1 rather than re-narrate the unnamed case. Delete H1's internal second statement of it.

### Issue 5: §9 and G0 carry essays justifying what the contract omits and why a non-load-bearing clause is kept
**ASN-0134, §9 ("Two clauses are conspicuous by what they omit ...") and G0**: §9 — "There is no global *allocation*-serialization clause ... And there is no registry-locking clause ... There is, equally, no document-registration clause ..."; "Clause 6 ... is *not* load-bearing ... We keep it not for minimality but so the contract names every consistency obligation an implementer might fear they must discharge"; G0 — "The omission is not an oversight but a consequence of the note's central result."
**Problem**: Defensive justification of composition. The drop-one minimality analysis ("drop 1 and reads tear; drop 2 and same-home allocations collide; ...") is genuine and earns its place. But the surrounding essay — what is *not* a clause, and a paragraph defending keeping a clause that is admittedly redundant — is apologetics. "The omission is not an oversight but ..." defends a design choice rather than advancing a claim.
**Required**: Keep the drop-one counterexample list (it is the minimality proof). Cut the "conspicuous by what they omit" inventory to one sentence noting clauses 7 and 8 are the only non-per-home obligations and that registration/registry need none. Either drop clause 6 (the note itself says it is not load-bearing) or keep it with a one-line note, without the paragraph defending the keep. In G0, delete "The omission is not an oversight but a consequence" — the SC-vs-serializability distinction stands on its own derivation.

### Issue 6: §4 instance (i) buries a load-bearing lemma (I1a literal-vs-operative) under editorial framing and restates its conclusion
**ASN-0134, §4 instance (i)**: "Followed through, 'the wrong axis' is sharper than 'no remedy'"; "the point the cross-home setup `d ≠ d'` obscures"; "This argument names only `Σ ∪ {T_X}` and never the two emits' homes, so it carries the same-home pair (`d = d'`) verbatim."
**Problem**: The actual content here — that I1a's *literal* "K-surface-emitted" clause and the *operative* requirement its induction uses (each deposit a miss against *its own* pre-state) diverge under concurrent realization, so per-home MIC permits an idempotent duplicate — is the most important result feeding clause 8, and it is correct. But it is delivered as one enormous "instance" paragraph laced with editorial connectives ("sharper than," "obscures") and with its conclusion ("per-home order does not even prevent the duplicate") stated twice. A reader must work to extract the lemma from the rhetoric.
**Required**: Promote the I1a literal/operative divergence to a named result (it earns a label — it is what clause 8 restores). State it once for the general case, note the same-home and cross-home instantiations as two lines, and delete the "wrong axis / obscures / verbatim" connective prose. This is a trim of presentation, not of substance — the argument should survive intact and become *more* visible.

### Issue 7: "stale is ... never single-index" is false at the empty-active-slice boundary
**ASN-0134, §8** (and SAFE(d)): "So `stale` is a multi-read at *every* member-home count `N ≥ 1` — `N + 1 ≥ 2` bounded accesses, the single-home case `1 + 1 = 2` included — never single-index, hence clause 7's province, not clause 4's."
**Problem**: When `A_K^Σ = ∅` there are zero member homes (`N = 0`), so `stale(h)` is computed from a *single* `Observe_K(oper)` read returning `∅` — one bounded access, hence single-index. The categorical "never single-index" is therefore false at `N = 0`, and the same `N = 0` case is silently excluded again in SAFE(d) ("a `stale` enumeration (single- or multi-home)"). The consequence is benign (an empty verdict straddles nothing), but the note holds itself to a strict degenerate-case standard everywhere else (m=0/m=1 batches, φ=0, first-emission), and this universal claim violates it. The cross-type-join analog has the same boundary: a `targets_keyed` join over a *single* registered BH3 type is one access.
**Required**: Either restate as "stale is multi-read whenever its active slice is non-empty (`N ≥ 1`); for `N = 0` it is a single `Observe_K` and clause 4 discharges it trivially," or argue why the empty case is still routed through clause 7. Fix the parallel omission in SAFE(d). Note the single-type-join boundary similarly.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: Joint satisfiability of clauses 2 and 8 for one idem=⊤ emit
An `idem(K)=⊤` emission must, per clause 8, hold a global per-coverage-class critical section over its dedup-read-and-deposit, while per clause 2 its deposit must hold the per-home allocation section — two simultaneous exclusions per operation. Whether these compose without deadlock, and in what acquisition order, is a real question a reader will ask.
**Why out of scope**: This is "the concrete concurrency-control mechanism," which the note's Scope explicitly defers. MIC is a contract; clause composability is the implementer's discharge of it. Correctly deferred — no change needed.
