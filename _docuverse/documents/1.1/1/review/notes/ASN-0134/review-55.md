# Review of ASN-0134

This is a rigor-careful note — H0–H3, G0, G1, and V2 are genuinely well-proven (I checked the SC cycle in G0, both converse witnesses in V2, the exhaustiveness of H0's case split, and the validity-before-canonicity ordering in G1(i); all hold). I found no proof *error*. The findings below are one precision gap and, as the `review-mode.anti-bloat` classifier directs, several instances of prose that restates rather than advances.

## REVISE

### Issue 1: A2's zero-step linearization clause is not reconciled with multi-access reads
**ASN-0134, A1 / A2 / §8**: A1 enrolls `stale` as a single zero-step operation — "a *read-only query* — an `Observe_K`, or any of ASN-0128's behavioral *reads* (D1–D4; BH1–BH3; BH4's `age`/`stale` ...), each committing no `→_sh` step". A2 then says "A zero-step operation has `lin(op)` equal to the index of the state it reads." But §8 establishes that `stale` is intrinsically multi-access: "So `stale` is a multi-read whenever its active slice is non-empty: at member-home count `N ≥ 1` it costs `N + 1 ≥ 2` bounded accesses."
**Problem**: A2's "the index of the state it reads" (singular) has no referent for `stale`, which §8 shows reads `N+1` committed states at distinct indices. The three claims are inconsistent unless A2 is silently read as restricted to single-bounded-access reads — but A1 classes `stale` as one zero-step operation, and A2 quantifies over *all* zero-step operations. The entire V0/V2 distinction turns on single-vs-multi access, yet A2 (which precedes it) assigns every zero-step operation one read index.
**Required**: Restrict A2's single-index clause to single-bounded-access reads, and state that multi-access read operations (`stale`, cross-type joins) lack a single `lin` and are governed by V0/V2 — making the A1→A2→§8 division of labor explicit rather than contradictory.

### Issue 2: The §3 lead-in re-derives all of G0 before G0 states it
**ASN-0134, §3 (paragraph beginning "First the client model")**: "The model is *serializable* by construction... On its own the cross-home reordering is observationally benign — distinct-home operations commute (H1)... and is *not yet* a failure of *sequential consistency*; what witnesses non-SC is a *third party* reading the program-order violation, which G0 exhibits. And — this is the subtle and characteristically Xanadu part — the order is *logical, not temporal*."
**Problem**: Every claim here — serializable-by-construction, cross-home reordering benign via H1, not-yet-SC-failure, third-party witnesses non-SC, logical-not-temporal, sequential-client-recovers-by-acknowledgment — is restated in full in G0's statement immediately below (G0 even formalizes them as steps (1) and (2)). Only the *definition* of the pipelining client (first sentence) is needed by G0 ("the pipelining client above"); the rest is a complete preview of the claim. This is pattern-7 duplication (two paragraphs say the same thing).
**Required**: Trim the lead-in to the pipelining-client definition plus a pointer to G0; let G0 carry the serializability/SC argument once.

### Issue 3: "A critical section buys only contiguity, not atomicity" is argued in both §1 and §6
**ASN-0134, §1 (post-A5) and §6 (post-W2)**: §1 — "Contiguity does **not** construct atomicity... What a critical section buys, and all it buys, is contiguity." §6 — "Contiguity is all W2 buys: it keeps the run an unbroken interval of `A_{s_C}(d)` — the writer-side half of read-atomicity, and no more (A5)."
**Problem**: The same conclusion — a (d,s_C) critical section secures contiguity but not reader-atomicity — is established at length in §1 (around A5) and re-established at §6 (around W2). The §1 passage already walks through "even a perfectly contiguous content run *passes through* every interior state... a partial batch *structurally*"; §6 repeats the point. One site should carry the argument and the other cite it.
**Required**: Make §6's W2 the carrier of the contiguity-vs-atomicity scoping and reduce §1's post-A5 elaboration to introducing the two modes of non-atomicity, citing §6/W2 for the "contiguity ≠ atomicity" conclusion (or vice versa).

### Issue 4: MIC clauses re-argue their source claims' justifications rather than invoking them
**ASN-0134, MIC clause 4 ↔ V0, clause 6 ↔ V2**: Clause 6 — "It is strictly stronger than soundness alone needs but is adopted because pinning to one index discharges soundness a fortiori, sparing the recognizer from classifying which steps affect `Q` (V2)" — reproduces V2 almost verbatim: "MIC clause 6 *adopts* it for safety — pinning all `p` reads to one index discharges the soundness requirement a fortiori, sparing the recognizer from having to classify which steps are `Q`-affecting." Clause 4 — "the discriminator is *access count*, not `Observe_K`-grade-ness, so `age` qualifies despite reading a cross-type frontier (V0)" — reproduces V0's "The discriminator is *access count*, not type-confinement."
**Problem**: A contract section may state the obligation and cite the claim, but these clauses re-argue the *justification* already given at the source claim (the "strictly stronger but adopted because..." rationale, the "discriminator is access count" reasoning). The justification belongs at the claim; the clause should state the obligation and reference it. As written, V2's and V0's reasoning is duplicated into MIC.
**Required**: Reduce clauses 4 and 6 to the obligation plus a citation (e.g., "single-bounded-access reads evaluate against one committed state (V0); multi-read verdicts pin all reads to one index (V2)"), dropping the re-argued rationale.

### Issue 5: The model-intrinsic-contiguity derivation appears in both A6's final paragraph and W1
**ASN-0134, A6 (final paragraph) and W1**: A6 — "the allocator deposits only at the next sibling of a home's current chain-maximum (`inc(max,·)`...), no step ever removes a slot (`C0`/`L12`), and a step into any other home or subspace leaves this home's population untouched — so a gapless initial segment stays a gapless initial segment step by step, under *any* interleaving." W1 — "(its preservation under every valid step carried by A6). The failure mode without per-home serialization is therefore a *collision*, never a hole: a *hole*... would require depositing past the true frontier, which `inc(max,·)` never does."
**Problem**: A6 already proves contiguity preservation via `inc(max,·)` + `C0`/`L12` + cross-home framing. W1 cites "carried by A6" — and then re-derives the same hole-impossibility ("would require depositing past the true frontier, which `inc(max,·)` never does"). The re-derivation is redundant with A6; W1's genuinely new content is the collision-not-hole reading and the contiguity-vs-uniqueness split.
**Required**: Have W1 invoke A6 for hole-impossibility and confine itself to its new content (the collision failure mode and "serialization buys uniqueness, not contiguity"), dropping the re-derivation.

### Issue 6: §4 instance (i)'s `age` paragraph routes through an excluded case to reach a consequence no clause consumes
**ASN-0134, §4, first family, instance (i)**: "The dependence reaches even a home-relative behavior, though only *indirectly*. BH4 `age` cannot be read on `K` itself — BH4 requires `idem = ⊥` (R-C0), and the scenario fixes `idem(K) = ⊤`, so `age(·)` is simply not a defined query on a `K`-tuple. What does shift is the age of *other* tuples..."
**Problem**: Two anti-bloat patterns at once. (a) The paragraph raises `age` on the scenario's `K`-tuple only to note the `idem = ⊤` precondition excludes it ("imagines a case the precondition already excludes"), then pivots. (b) The consequence it lands on — order-dependence reaching the `age` of co-homed `idem = ⊥` tuples — feeds no MIC clause and no SAFE sub-claim; instance (i)'s load-bearing conclusion is the surviving `idem = ⊤` duplicate that motivates clause 7, which this digression does not strengthen. It is a dead-end consequence reached via an excluded case.
**Required**: Cut the `age`-indirection paragraph, or compress to a single clause if the breadth-of-consequences point is wanted; the duplicate-survival thread reaches clause 7 without it.
