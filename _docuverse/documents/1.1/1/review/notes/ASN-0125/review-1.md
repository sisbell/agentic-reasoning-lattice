# Review of ASN-0125

## REVISE

### Issue 1: EL6(iv)/EL7(iv) — the `nullified` equation is stated without the discipline hypothesis it needs
**ASN-0125, EL6 (AssertionContract), clause (iv)**: "Moreover `nullified(Σ') = nullified(Σ)`: the new tuple's slot-3 coverage is `coverage(K_sup) ≠ coverage(R)`, so the `[R]`-slice did not grow."
**Problem**: The justification proves only the restricted statement `nullified(Σ') ∩ A_rel^Σ = nullified(Σ)` — no *pre-existing* address changes status. The full equation additionally requires the fresh address `b ∈ A_rel^{Σ'}` to escape every pre-existing `[R]`-tuple's to-coverage, and EL6's preamble assumes only "a reachable `Σ` satisfying its precondition," not edit-discipline (clause (iii) carries that hypothesis; clause (iv) does not). At a merely reachable state the counterexample is concrete: a raw arity-3 `K.λ` of class `[R]` with a wide to-span `(b₀, δ(5, #b₀))` over upcoming positions of `A_L(d_a)`'s chain is L3-conforming and blocked by nothing in ASN-0047's composite clauses; the next emission `b` then lands in `coverage(G')`, giving `nullified(Σ') = nullified(Σ) ∪ {b}`. ASN-0086 itself anticipates exactly this: wp Case 2's third conjunct `¬(E (b,F',G') ∈ L_R^Σ :: a_emit(Σ,d) ∈ coverage(G'))` is non-vacuous precisely at undisciplined states, and is discharged only by the disciplined-domain simplification. EL7(iv) inherits the same overclaim, doubled — both `a'` and `b` can be born nullified.
**Required**: Either condition the equation in EL6(iv) and EL7(iv) on edit-disciplined `Σ` (mirroring clause (iii)), or split the clause: unconditionally `nullified(Σ') ∩ A_rel^Σ = nullified(Σ)` — which is the design-bearing half, "the original is exactly as active as before" — plus the full equation under the discipline hypothesis, citing the unit-depth-discipline + R0a argument that discharges wp Case 2's third conjunct.

### Issue 2: Df-SUCC applies `old`/`new` to all of `S^Σ`, but the accessors are total only on schema-conforming claims
**ASN-0125, Df-SUCC (Successor relations)**: "At any state `Σ`: `succ_h(Σ) = {(old(e), new(e)) : e ∈ S^Σ}`"
**Problem**: EL4 introduces `addr`, `new`, `old` as "total accessors on disciplined claims" — well-definedness (singleton `coverage ∩ dom(Σ.L)`) is established only for claims conforming to Df-DISC(ii). `S^Σ` is the bare coverage-class slice: at undisciplined reachable states it can contain arity-3 `[K_sup]`-class tuples whose endsets are multi-span, cover several link addresses, or cover none, leaving `old(e)`/`new(e)` undefined — yet the comprehension is explicitly offered "at any state." The ill-definedness propagates: EL5(a) asserts `succ_h(Σ) ⊆ succ_h(Σ')` for *every* `Σ →* Σ'`; EL11(b) defines `in(y, Σ)`/`out(x, Σ)` via `old`/`new` over `S^Σ` and identifies them with `Observe_{K_sup}` results (an identity valid only when every slice member is schema-conforming); Df-CUR builds `reach_o`/`current` on `succ_o`.
**Required**: Restrict the defining comprehension to schema-conforming members — e.g. `succ_h(Σ) = {(y, x) : e ∈ S^Σ ∧ e conforms to Df-DISC(ii) with witnesses x, y}` — noting that EL4's proof is per-claim and needs no whole-state hypothesis, so the restricted relations are total at every reachable state and coincide with the current definitions at disciplined states. Apply the same restriction (or an explicit standing discipline assumption) to `in`, `out`, `reach_o`, and `current`.

### Issue 3: EL7 omits the discipline-preservation clause that DC(ℓ') exists to secure, and EL12 silently relies on it
**ASN-0125, EDITop / EL7 (EditContract)**: "with the *discipline-conformance precondition* `DC(ℓ')`: if `coverage(ℓ'.e₃) = coverage(K_sup)` then `ℓ'` itself satisfies the claim schema of Df-DISC(ii)…" — followed by contract clauses (i)–(v), none of which states that `Σ₂` is edit-disciplined.
**Problem**: DC(ℓ')'s entire purpose is that an edit cannot mint a malformed inhabitant of `[K_sup]` or `[R]`, i.e. that `editlink` preserves edit-discipline — but the contract never cashes this out. EL6(v) covers only `assert_sup`, not step 1's bare `K.λ`. The omission is load-bearing: EL12 runs `editlink` twice "from any disciplined reachable state" and claims both pairs enter `succ_o` at birth via EL6(iii); the second invocation's active-at-birth conclusion requires the *intermediate* state to be edit-disciplined, which only the missing clause supplies.
**Required**: Add to EL7 a clause "(vi) `Σ₂` is edit-disciplined when `Σ` is," with the two-step proof: step 1 preserves Df-DISC by DC(ℓ') (schema witnesses persist under domain monotonicity; a value in neither class is unconstrained), step 2 by EL6(v). Then EL12 can cite EL7(vi) for its second invocation.

### Issue 4: EL13 conflates "per-asserter" with "per-home"
**ASN-0125, EL13 (TemporalErasure)**: "Within one home the opposite holds: the chain enumeration is strictly increasing (T9; ChainEnumerationInjectivity, ASN-0093), so one asserter's claims are totally ordered by their addresses — *per-asserter* 'latest' is well-defined and state-recoverable."
**Problem**: What the chain enumeration orders is the claims homed at one *document* — one `A_L(d)` chain. Asserters are principals, and a principal's ownership domain spans many documents (account-tier prefix and `odom`, ASN-0042 — the very framework EL8(b) invokes to resolve home → owner). Two claims by the same principal homed at two of their documents fall squarely under EL13's own commutation argument: their order is not a function of the state. So per-asserter "latest" is not state-recoverable in general; per-home "latest" is. The conflation recurs in the claims table ("'latest' is per-asserter only") and in EL14(d)'s example reader policy ("follow per-asserter latest").
**Required**: Replace "per-asserter" with "per-home" (per-document-chain) in EL13, the claims-table row, and EL14(d). If a per-asserter notion is wanted, state explicitly that it is well-defined only under the added assumption that the asserter homes all their claims at a single document — or present it as the approximation a reader policy may choose, not as a state-recoverable fact.

## OUT_OF_SCOPE

### Topic 1: Formal bridge from this ASN's state to ASN-0042's principal/registry model
**Why out of scope**: EL8(b) gestures from `home(addr(e))` to an effective owner "by prefix (ASN-0042)," but ASN-0042's `ω_Σ` is defined over a state with a baptismal registry and principal set that `(C, L, E, M, R)` does not carry. The attribution this ASN actually needs — home computable and decidable from the address alone — is fully discharged by T4b/T6 within its own state; constructing the cross-model identification (link addresses as registry members with effective owners) is integration work for a future ASN, not an error here.

### Topic 2: Authority invariants for `Nullify` over claims
**Why out of scope**: The worked example exhibits H's owner nullifying a claim homed at P — legal under ASN-0086's preconditions, which impose no ownership relation between `d_retr` and the target. Whether retraction of another's supersession claim should require authority is a genuine open design question, and the ASN correctly lists it as its first Open Question rather than legislating it; the same applies to temporal witnesses beyond per-home order and to stratification of claim-targeting claims.

VERDICT: REVISE
