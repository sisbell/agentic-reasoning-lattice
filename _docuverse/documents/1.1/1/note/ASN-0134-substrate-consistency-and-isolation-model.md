# ASN-0134: Substrate Consistency and Isolation Model

*2026-06-13*

*What the sequential substrate promises a concurrent client: the step is the atom, the home is the unit of contention, and the snapshot is the only honest referent for a verdict.*

**Depends:** ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0093 (Allocation Substrate), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework), ASN-0128 (Substrate Type Operational Semantics).

We are handed a substrate whose entire dynamics is a *sequence*. The governing axiom — `SequentialTransitionAxiom`, asserted in ASN-0093 and inherited verbatim by ASN-0128's gated relation `→_sh` — says that transitions `Σ → Σ'` are atomic, uninterruptible, and totally ordered, and that `Σ →* Σ'` is the reflexive-transitive closure of single steps. The operation surface of ASN-0128 is defined the same way: `Emit_K`, `Nullify_Binary`, `Observe_K` are specified by what one step does to one state. And yet every layer raised on this substrate presumes a crowd. A predicate may be evaluated by an *arbitrary* observer. Quiescence — "is everyone done?" — must be recognizable *while writers may still be active*. A definition's content must be allocated as one contiguous run *even as other agents allocate*. The very corpus this note belongs to was produced by a runner driving many workers against one store. So the question is forced upon us: a definition written in the singular is being *used* in the plural, and we must say exactly what survives the translation.

This note does not add a concurrency-control mechanism — no lock, no transaction manager, no scheduler. Nelson never specified one (his own consultations are candid that "no locking protocol... no concurrent-editing semantics" appears in *Literary Machines*, and that the back end was left to supply it), and Gregory's `udanax-green` supplies exactly one realization (a single-threaded event loop) rather than a contract. What we owe is the *contract* that sits between them: the weakest discipline an implementation must honour so that the sequential semantics is faithfully presented to concurrent clients. We will find that the contract is much weaker than the global serialization Gregory's loop happens to provide — it is *per-home*, not global — and that this is not an accident of engineering but precisely Nelson's design intent, where "order is always local, fixed at one home, by order of arrival," never "a synchronous agreement reached among all observers." This localization is a *writer-side allocation* result; two disciplines necessarily resist it, and we flag them where they arise — the reader-side verdict pinning of §8, and the subtler one this revision makes explicit: the *global* per-coverage-class serialization that idempotent (`idem=⊤`) de-duplication demands (§9 clause 8) — a discipline no per-home order supplies, and one the duplicate needs *regardless of whether the two racing emits share a home*. Per-home order suffices for what the substrate *allocates*; it does not, on its own, make idempotent emission de-duplicate — not across homes, and not even within one.

## What this note commits

- **The atomic unit is the step.** A single `→_sh` step is the grain of all-or-nothing. A typed emission (`Emit_K` miss, `Nullify_Binary` miss) is *exactly one* step; an idempotent hit, a read-only query (an `Observe_K` or any behavioral read), and a precondition-rejected call are *zero* steps. A *multi-step* (`m ≥ 2`) fire/batch (a `retract_stale` over two or more stale events, a multi-atom definition run) is *many* steps and is **not** atomic — but the degenerate sizes go the other way: an empty (`m = 0`) fire is *zero* steps and vacuously atomic, and a singleton (`m = 1`) fire is *one* step, atomic and behaving as a single operation. (A0–A5.)
- **Observations read committed snapshots.** An `Observe` is a total function of one state `Σ_k`. It never witnesses a partial step. It *can* witness the partial effect of any multi-step batch — contiguous or split. Every state it can land on is structurally canonical. (A3, A4, A5.)
- **Linearization is by construction, and it is local.** Each state-changing operation appears to take effect at one index — its linearization point. Any one execution is globally serial, but the order an implementation must *force* is necessary only within each home; cross-home steps commute, so every linearization of a per-home-serial schedule of raw steps is valid and reaches the same committed state (the operation level adds two *families* of order-dependence caveat — a lone idem=⊤ `Emit` whose hit/miss verdict, reading the *global* `A_K`, is flipped by any concurrent toggle of a coverage-equal *active* tuple, whether a competing emit (which, without §9 clause 8, duplicates rather than merely reorders — and duplicates *regardless of home*, §4 instance (i)) or a nullify of the incumbent; and a `Nullify` racing the emission of its own cross-home target — both in §4). (A1, A2, H0–H2, G0, G-PO, G1.)
- **An invariant partition.** Monotonicity, immutability, cross-home uniqueness, the active/audit-slice relationship, and registry stability are preserved by the model itself (per-step atomicity is enough). Same-home uniqueness, dense chain contiguity, and run contiguity are preserved *only* by serialization — and only per-home serialization is required. (W0–W6.)
- **A minimal isolation contract** with eight clauses, and a theorem that under it no reader sees a torn effect, no single operation is double-applied, idempotent emission de-duplicates (by the one clause — 8 — that is per-coverage-class, not per-home; per-home clauses alone permit an `idem=⊤` duplicate *regardless of home*), no writer collides with a concurrent writer's allocation, and no multi-read verdict straddles a write. (MIC, M1.)
- **Quiescence as a snapshot predicate.** A single-bounded-access verdict — a single `Observe_K`, or an `age` read of one home's frontier — is sound *about its read index* and only about it. A multi-read verdict — the realistic case, joining across types, or any `stale` enumeration (whose global active-membership filter makes it several reads even when its members share one home) — costs several reads and is sound about one state when no `Q`-affecting step falls between them; a reader-side critical section secures that by pinning the reads to one index, which the substrate must have constructed, not provided. This reader-side isolation is *not* per-home: the "per-home, not global" liberation above is a *writer-side* result and does not carry over to the reader — clause 7's one-index pin is a *global* reader-side exclusion (any writer at any home moves the index), and even V2's weaker sufficient condition is type-scoped, not home-scoped. Durability past the read index is a further hypothesis about writers' linearization points, which the substrate does not supply. (V0, V2, V1.)

## 1. The grain: a step, not a batch

We begin by fixing what "an execution" is, because every later claim is a statement about it. By `SequentialTransitionAxiom` the substrate's behaviour under any number of agents is a single sequence of states

`𝔼 :  Σ₀ → Σ₁ → Σ₂ → ⋯`

in which each `Σ_i → Σ_{i+1}` is one atomic step `σ_i` drawn from the step vocabulary of ASN-0128's gated relation, `K = {K.σ, K.α, K.λ_sh}` — that is `→_sh ≡ K.σ ∪ K.α ∪ K.λ_sh`: document registration, content allocation, and gated link/tuple emission. We anchor the sequence at its initial state, `Σ₀ = Σ_init` — ASN-0126/0128's registry-adjoined initial state, `→_sh*`-reachable from itself — so that, every `σ_i` being a `→_sh` step, each `Σ_k` on `𝔼` is `→_sh*`-reachable from `Σ_init` via the prefix `Σ₀ → ⋯ → Σ_k`; A6 will lean on this as its base case. (We commit `𝔼` to this one substrate stack — ASN-0093's allocation model carried up through ASN-0086/0126/0128 — rather than mixing in a second foundation's arrangement-and-entity steps; the operation surface every later claim invokes, `Emit_K`/`Nullify_Binary`/`Observe_K`, is defined over exactly this stack. One consequence of that choice surfaces in §4: in this stack document registration `K.σ` takes a *caller-supplied* `d` — there is no document sub-allocator, that apparatus being precisely the excluded entity layer's — so while `K.σ` is a state-changing step that the step-level claims A0–A7 govern like any other (canonicity, A6, included), it is not an *allocation* into a sub-allocator, and §4's conflict analysis and the contract built on it scope to the allocation steps `K.α`, `K.λ_sh`, treating document-address freshness as an assumed precondition.) Write `idx(σ) = i` for a step's position. We index states by their position in this one order; `Σ_i →* Σ_j` holds exactly when `i ≤ j`. This sequence is not a description of how an implementation is built — Gregory's loop builds it one way, a future per-home scheduler might build it another — it is the *meaning* the implementation must present. The agents, the scheduler that places their proposals, the fairness with which it does so: all of that is upstream of `𝔼` and out of scope here (it is the coordination layer's named hypothesis, the implementer's territory). What we reason about is `𝔼` and the states on it.

The first thing to extract is the observational reading of atomicity. The axiom says steps are *uninterruptible*; observationally this means:

> **A0 (StepAtomicity).** There is no state strictly between `Σ_i` and `Σ_{i+1}`. The states an observer can ever name are exactly `{Σ_k : k ∈ 𝔼}`.

A0 is not a new assumption; it is `SequentialTransitionAxiom` read as a constraint on what can be seen. Its force is entirely negative and entirely decisive: a half-applied step is not a state of `𝔼`, so it is not nameable, so it cannot be read. Every consequence below is a cash-out of A0.

Now we connect operations to steps. An agent *proposes* an operation; the substrate *realizes* it as activity on `𝔼`. We must be precise about the realization count, because it is where "the unit of atomicity" is decided.

> **A1 (Realization).** A *single* state-changing operation — one not issued as a batch — is realized as *exactly one* atomic step, at a unique index; distinct such operations occupy distinct indices. (A multi-step *batch* (`m ≥ 2`) — a `retract_stale` issuing one `Nullify_Binary` per stale event, a definition's content run issuing one `K.α` per atom — is *not* a single operation in this sense: it is realized as *many* steps and is **not** atomic. Its decomposition and divisibility are A5's subject, and the one-step claim of this paragraph is not asserted of it. The *degenerate* batch sizes — `m` counting realized steps, as in A5 — collapse back onto the single-operation cases and must be named so the taxonomy stays exhaustive: an `m = 1` fire — a one-atom content run, or a `retract_stale` over a single stale event whose constituent commits — is realized as *exactly one* step, atomic and indistinguishable from a single state-changing operation, so this paragraph's one-step claim *does* hold of it despite its being issued as a batch; and an `m = 0` fire — a `retract_stale` over an empty stale set — is realized as *zero* steps, vacuously atomic, the degenerate-batch member of the zero-step cases enumerated next.) An operation that leaves the state unchanged is realized as *zero* steps — it reads a state and returns — and this happens in four ways: a *read-only query* — an `Observe_K`, or any of ASN-0128's behavioral reads `members`, `is_K`, `targets_of`, `succs`, `chain`, `tip`, `is_in_chain`, `sources_to`, `target_of`, `targets_keyed`, `age`, `stale`, `is_filtered` (D1–D4, BH1–BH4), each committing no `→_sh` step — the zero-step property A1 asserts of them, which holds uniformly; an idempotent `Emit_K` hit; the hit branch of a `Nullify_Binary`; and a *rejected* call, one whose precondition fails at the state it is evaluated against — a gate failure, a `P0` failure, or a `P-tgt` failure (ASN-0128 S3, I6). The first three return a value read off the state; the fourth returns failure and adjoins nothing. These four are the *single-operation* zero-step cases; the empty `m = 0` fire noted in the batch parenthetical above is the fifth, degenerate-batch zero-step case, and the five together exhaust the zero-step realizations. One refinement, since §8 turns on it: the load-bearing property here is the *zero-step* count, and it holds uniformly for every read just listed — but *single-index read-atomicity* does not, and the two must not be conflated. Each behavioral read is *definitionally* a function of one mathematical state `Σ`; their *realizations* over the per-type surface the substrate actually exposes differ. A read confined to one type's active view — `Observe_K` itself, and `is_K`, `members(K, active)`, `targets_of(x, active)`, `succs`, `chain`, `tip`, `is_in_chain`, `sources_to`, `target_of`, the per-type `is_filtered` — is realized as one `Observe_K`-grade read touching one index, so its definitional single-state form and its realized single-index form coincide. A read that *joins across types* does not: `targets_keyed` joins `target_of` across every Binary BH3 type, and the default-view forms `members(K', default)` / `targets_of(x, default)` join `is_filtered` across every BH1 type (ASN-0128 BH1, BH3) — each is precisely §8's *multi-read*, realized as several `Observe_K` calls whose read indices may drift, and it acquires single-index atomicity only by construction (V2, MIC clause 7), never free. Two of ASN-0128's reads resist the first camp, and an earlier draft mislabelled them by forcing them into it: BH4's `age` and `stale`. `age(a) = f_d^Σ − 1 − j` reads the *home* `d = home(a)`'s link-subspace *frontier* `f_d^Σ`, and that frontier interleaves *every* link type homed at `d` — BH4 says so outright, "the chain interleaves every type homed at `d`, so age counts the home's subsequent link traffic, *not K-events alone*." So `age` touches one home's *cross-type* link chain, not any one type's active view, and `Observe_K` — per-type by construction (ASN-0086) — does not expose it; it is *not* an `Observe_K`-grade read. Yet `age(a)` for a single address is *not* a §8 multi-read either, and the reason is the crux: what secures its single-*index* status is not type-confinement but that `f_d^Σ` is recovered in a *single bounded access*. By `L-ContiguousPrefix` (ASN-0086) the home's link population `{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d}` is a contiguous initial segment of the *one* chain `chain_d`, so `f_d^Σ` is simply its length — recoverable in one bounded access over that home's link subspace, *not* by unioning several per-type views at drifting indices (the link subspace carries no per-type sub-chains to union; every link type homed at `d` shares `chain_d`). Gregory's implementation confirms the single access rather than a cross-chain scan: the home link frontier is recovered by one bounded granfilade descent under the home's link-subspace upper bound (`findpreviousisagr`), never by maxing across per-type chains — there are none to max over. `age(a)` is therefore a *home-relative single-snapshot* read — one index, hence free under clause 4 (A3), exactly like the first camp but reading a single-state cross-type frontier rather than one type's active view. `stale`, though, does not inherit that status at all, and the discriminator is *how many bounded accesses the realization needs*, **not** that the read is a "function of one state `Σ`" — a description every read here satisfies, the cross-type joins included, so one that separates nothing. `stale(h) = {a ∈ A_K^Σ : age(a) > h}` enumerates type-`K`'s *active* subset and computes `age` per member, and the *active* qualifier is what an earlier draft underweighted. A type-`K` tuple `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ` is *active* — a member of `A_K^Σ` — iff `a ∉ nullified(Σ)`, and `nullified(Σ) = {a ∈ A_rel^Σ : (E (b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ :: a ∈ coverage(G'))}` (ASN-0086) reads the *global* retraction slice `L_R^Σ`. A retraction nullifying a tuple homed at `d` may itself be homed at *any* `d_retr ≠ d` — `Nullify_Binary`'s `P0` constrains only `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)` (ASN-0128), and §4's *target-residence race* turns on exactly this cross-home freedom — so the nullifying emitter lives in `A_L(d_retr)`, *not* in `d`'s link subspace. Hence a descent of `d`'s link subspace yields `d`'s type-`K` tuples and the frontier `f_d^Σ`, but *not which of them are active*: enumerating `d`'s *active* type-`K` members demands a *second*, global access — the `Observe_K(oper)`-grade consultation that filters `L_K` against `nullified` — over and above the frontier descent. So `stale` is a §8 multi-read at *every* member-home count `N ≥ 1`: its realization is one type-`K` active-view read (global, consulting `L_R`) *plus one granfilade frontier descent per distinct member home* — `N + 1 ≥ 2` bounded accesses whose read indices may drift exactly as a §8 multi-read's several `Observe_K` calls do, so it is sound about a single state only when its accesses are pinned to one committed index by a reader-side critical section (V2, MIC clause 7), never free under clause 4. The earlier draft's *single-home* exemption was the error: it fused the active-view read into the link-subspace frontier descent, as though one descent of `d`'s subspace could both enumerate `d`'s *active* type-`K` members and carry `f_d^Σ` — but active-ness is precisely what that descent cannot see, the nullifying retractions being cross-home. Even at `N = 1` the two accesses stay distinct, so single-home `stale` is `1 + 1 = 2` accesses, a multi-read no less than the multi-home case (a staleness-keyed quiescence recognizer therefore falls under clause 7, not clause 4 — its drifting active-view and per-home frontier reads can manufacture the very "states that never coexisted" error of V0/V2). What stays single-index is `age` *alone* among the BH4 reads: `age(a)` is handed its address and computes `f_d^Σ − 1 − j` by one frontier descent, with no active-membership filter to globalize it. The same access-count discriminator settles the asymmetry an earlier draft left dangling: `targets_keyed` and the default-view joins `members(K', default)` / `targets_of(x, default)` are multi-reads *because* each is realized as several `Observe`-grade accesses at possibly drifting indices — identical in kind to `stale`'s active-view-plus-frontier realization, differing only in ranging over many *types* rather than (also) many *homes*. The classification is then uniform on access count — *one* bounded access ⟹ single-index (single-type `Observe_K`-grade reads, `age`); *several* ⟹ §8 multi-read, single-index only by clause 7's construction (cross-type joins, `stale` at every member-home count). A1 grants the cross-type joins — and `stale` with them — only their *zero-step* count, not single-index atomicity, the latter being §8's to build; `age`, by contrast, secures single-index status outright, as just shown. Whether a *would-be* state-changing operation realizes as one step or is *rejected to zero* can itself depend on the state it meets, hence on the linearization: a `Nullify_Binary` whose `P-tgt` holds at one index and fails at another fires in the first case and is rejected in the second. §4 leans on this — an operation's very presence in the realized step set can be order-dependent.

A1's "exactly one / exactly zero" is read straight off ASN-0128. An `Emit_K` *miss* invokes `K.λ_sh`: one step. An `Emit_K` *hit* under `idem = ⊤` "takes no step: `Σ' = Σ`" (ASN-0128 I1): zero steps. `Nullify_Binary` is `Emit_R(…)`, one `K.λ_sh` on a miss and "no step" on a hit (ASN-0128 S3). `Observe_K` "leaves Σ unchanged" (ASN-0086): zero steps, and ASN-0128's behavioral reads (`members`, `is_K`, `targets_of`, `succs`, `chain`, `tip`, `is_in_chain`, `sources_to`, `target_of`, `targets_keyed`, `age`, `stale`, `is_filtered`; D1–D4, BH1–BH4) are defined identically — each a pure function of the read state `Σ` (its per-type active slices `A_K^Σ`, its link store for the home-relative `age`/`stale`, or the registry), committing no `→_sh` step: zero steps. A *rejected* call — its gate, `P0`, or `P-tgt` precondition failing at the state it is checked against — likewise "takes no →_sh step" (ASN-0128 S3, I6): zero steps, returning failure rather than a value. And distinctness of indices is just the totality of the order: two steps cannot share a position. The one surface this enumeration deliberately routes elsewhere is the multi-step *batch* (`retract_stale`, a content run): its `m ≥ 2` constituents are themselves single operations — each one step at its own index — but the batch *as a whole* is the non-atomic composite A5 governs, "a sequence of wrapper steps, not an atomic operation" (ASN-0128 BH4), never a "single state-changing operation" the one-step clause speaks of. We have therefore answered the first half of the headline question already, but it is worth saying it as a claim rather than letting it hide inside A1:

> **A2 (LinearizationPoint).** A state-changing operation `op` realized as step `σ` has linearization point `lin(op) = idx(σ)`; its *entire* effect is the single transition `Σ_{lin(op)} → Σ_{lin(op)+1}`. A zero-step operation has `lin(op)` equal to the index of the state it reads, and its effect on state is the identity.

The phrase "its entire effect is the single transition" is the operative content. A typed emission does not dribble its effect across several states; it is in `Σ_{lin}` not at all and in `Σ_{lin+1}` in full. This is what makes the *typed emission* coincide with the *step* as the atomic unit. The third candidate the question offers — "a whole fire/batch" — is a different animal, and we now show it is *not* atomic once it spans two or more steps.

A *batch* (we use the words *fire* and *batch* interchangeably) is a finite collection of operations issued as one logical act by one agent or one rule firing — a `retract_stale` issuing one `Nullify_Binary` per stale event (ASN-0128 BH4, which states outright it is "a sequence of wrapper steps, not an atomic operation"), or a definition's content run, one `K.α` per atom of the definition. Its constituent steps land at some indices `i₁ < i₂ < ⋯ < i_m` in `𝔼`. Call the batch **contiguous** when `{i₁,…,i_m} = {i₁, i₁+1, …, i₁+m−1}`, i.e. no foreign step interleaves; otherwise **split**.

> **A5 (NoBatchIsolation).** A multi-step batch (`m ≥ 2`) is not atomic, in two independent senses. *(Partial visibility — always.)* For *any* batch, contiguous or split, an `Observe` at an interior index `k` with `i₁ < k ≤ i_m` (e.g. `k = i₁+1`) witnesses a strict, non-empty *prefix* of the batch's own effects: a reader may land at any index (A0, A3), and the batch's first atom is committed at `Σ_{i₁+1}` while its last is not committed until `Σ_{i_m+1}`. This needs no foreign step — it follows from `m ≥ 2` alone. *(Foreign interleaving — when split.)* If the batch is *additionally* split, some foreign step at index `j` with `i₁ ≤ j < i_m` interleaves, so an interior `Σ_{j+1}` also carries effects *not* belonging to the batch. Either way the batch's partial effects are observable; the substrate's all-or-nothing guarantee stops at the single step (A0). The bound `m ≥ 2` is essential, not cosmetic: the *degenerate* batch sizes are atomic after all — an `m = 1` fire is a single step, indivisible by A0 exactly as a lone operation is, and an `m = 0` fire (an empty `retract_stale`) commits no step, so there is no interior index to land on and the partial-visibility argument has no foothold. Non-atomicity is a strictly `m ≥ 2` phenomenon.

The contrast with A2 is the whole point. A single step is indivisible by A0; a batch is divisible, and we must keep its two modes of divisibility apart, because a layer that wants a batch to *appear atomic* must defeat both and they are not the same thing. *Contiguity* — no foreign step wedged between the batch's first and last step — is a *writer-side* critical section (W4, §6); it defeats the second mode (foreign interleaving) and nothing else. *Atomicity* — no reader ever observes a partial batch — is strictly stronger: it must additionally keep a reader out of the batch's *interior states*, and a `(d, s_C)` critical section, which excludes only writers, does nothing toward that. So contiguity does **not** construct atomicity: even a perfectly contiguous content run leaves every interior index `Σ_{i₁+1}, …, Σ_{i₁+m−1}` readable as a strict prefix (A5). The minimal *additional* contract that closes this reader gap — making a multi-step batch appear all-or-nothing to an observer — is genuinely harder, and we defer it to Open Question 5. What a critical section buys, and all it buys, is contiguity; the one batch the corpus demands be (locally) contiguous — the definition's content run — we return to in §6.

## 2. What an observation witnesses

We can now say exactly what `Observe_K`, evaluated by any agent at any time, sees relative to an `Emit_K` or `Nullify_Binary` in flight. The answer is fixed by A0 and A1, and it is clean.

> **A3 (SnapshotRead).** An `Observe` realized at index `k` is a total function of the single state `Σ_k`. Its result depends on no other state.

This is just the zero-step half of A1 together with the definition of `Observe_K` in ASN-0086 as a function of `Σ`. From A3 and A0 we get the read-isolation theorem:

> **A4 (NoTornStep).** No `Observe` witnesses a partial step. For an `Observe` at index `k` and a state-changing `op` with `lin(op) = i`: if `k ≤ i` the `Observe` sees none of `op`'s effect; if `k ≥ i+1` it sees all of it. There is no third case.
>
> *Proof.* `op`'s effect is precisely the transition `Σ_i → Σ_{i+1}` (A2). The reader's state `Σ_k` is either at-or-before `Σ_i` (`k ≤ i`, effect absent) or at-or-after `Σ_{i+1}` (`k ≥ i+1`, effect present). The excluded middle — a state strictly between `Σ_i` and `Σ_{i+1}` — does not exist (A0). ∎

So the disjunction in the headline question — "only committed state, or can it witness a partial effect?" — resolves to the first horn for any single emission or nullification: **only committed state**. An `Emit_K` "in flight" is, from the snapshot's vantage, either not-yet-linearized (its step has not happened; the reader sees the pre-state) or linearized (its step has happened; the reader sees the full post-effect). "In flight" never means "half-applied," because there is no half-state to be in. This is the formal core of Nelson's intent that "a torn read is impossible": content lives at permanent addresses and is never rewritten in place, so the only thing a step changes is which addresses are *present* in a store (and, for a link tuple, which it relates) — and that change is the step itself, indivisible.

The one honest qualification is A5's: while no *step* is ever torn, a *multi-step batch* is not protected. A reader can land between the constituent nullifications of a `retract_stale` and see some events retracted and others not; it can land partway through a definition's content run and see only a prefix of its atoms. We must therefore characterize *what kind of state* a mid-batch snapshot is, so that a reader who lands there is not misled about its validity.

The `→_sh` stack gives a sharp answer — sharper, in fact, than the read-isolation story usually told of a transactional store. Every invariant the stack carries is *step-local* — either a per-state predicate that every reachable state satisfies on its own, or a transition invariant that every single `→_sh` step preserves — with no class of properties reserved for the boundaries of multi-step composites: the per-state store partition, level, scoping, and chain conformances of ASN-0093 (`SD` store disjointness, `C1c`/`L1c`, and their level/scoping/finiteness companions `C1`/`C2`/`L0`/`L1a`/`C-fin`/`L-fin`, among others), the append-only immutability and document-set monotonicity of ASN-0093 *quantified over steps* (`C0` content immutability, `L12` link immutability, and ASN-0093's `M1` document-set monotonicity `dom(M) ⊆ dom(M')` — transition invariants, not snapshot shapes), the shape and registry stability of ASN-0126 (`P6` ReachableConformance, `P1`, `P2`), and their ASN-0128 liftings (`R1`, `R2`). There is, in this stack, no provenance relation to leave dangling and no arrangement to leave half-coupled — the substrate carries no coupling obligation that a *later* step must discharge.

> **A6 (CanonicalState).** Call a state *structurally canonical* iff it satisfies the *per-state canonicity package* of the `→_sh` stack — *every* invariant of the stack that is a predicate of a single state, each evaluated *at* that state. This package is the conjunction of ASN-0093's per-state store invariants (inherited by ASN-0086) — store disjointness `SD`, the element-level and field-depth invariants `C1`/`C1b`/`L1`/`L1b`, the allocator conformances `C1c`/`L1c`, the *chain-contiguity* invariants `ChainMembershipForOrigin` (ASN-0093 — each home's content and link populations form a gapless initial segment of their sub-allocator chains) and its link-store form `L-ContiguousPrefix` (ASN-0086), *properly stronger* than the conformances `C1c`/`L1c` (which assert only that *a* chain reaches each address, not that the homed set fills it without gaps), the home-scoping invariants `C2`/`L1a` (each store address homed at an allocated document), the subspace partition `L0`, the link-structure invariant `L3`, document well-formedness `M0` and empty-arrangement `M2`, and the store-finiteness invariants `C-fin`/`L-fin` — together with ASN-0126's `P6` (ReachableConformance) and the *registry-fixity predicate* `Σ_k.registry = R₀`, where `R₀ := Σ_init.registry` is the canonical initial record — a fixed substrate constant pinned once at construction (ASN-0128 `R-VAL`) and named here as an external referent, not a second state. This last conjunct is the genuine per-state instance of ASN-0126's `P1` / ASN-0128's `R1`, whose statements ("`Σ.registry = Σ_init.registry` at every reachable state") are exactly this predicate asserted at each state. The two registry *stability* invariants — ASN-0126's `P2` (ShapeStability) and ASN-0128's `R2` (IdemStability), "`shape(K)` (resp. `idem(K)`) takes the same value at every reachable state" — are deliberately *not* conjuncts of this package: each quantifies over the *set* of reachable states and asserts a value constant *across* them, which a reader handed `Σ_k` alone provably cannot check. They enter instead as *cross-state corollaries* of registry-fixity — since every state's record equals the one fixed `R₀`, any quantity read off the record (`shape(K)`, `idem(K)`) necessarily reads identically at every state, which is precisely `P2`/`R2`; stability is a consequence of `R₀`-fixity holding at all states, not a property a lone snapshot exhibits. This package is genuinely single-state — a reader handed `Σ_k` alone, together with the fixed constant `R₀`, can in principle check every conjunct, each being a predicate of `Σ_k`'s own components (the registry-fixity conjunct comparing `Σ_k`'s record against the external constant `R₀`, never against another state). *Every* state on `𝔼` is structurally canonical. One scope question the *chain-contiguity* members force into the open: A6's subject is canonicity *on `𝔼`* — what holds at every state of the one totally-ordered execution — not §5's strictly *model-intrinsic* notion (robustness to *arbitrary* non-serialized interleaving), and the two part company exactly at contiguity. On `𝔼` the contiguity invariants hold *because* `𝔼` is totally ordered: the total order serializes every same-`(d, S)` allocation pair, so each home's population is a gapless prefix — this is the very reachability the transfer below rides. §5's `W3` ("contiguity is serialization-borne") is the *separate*, dual claim that an *implementation* must impose per-home serialization to *produce* such an execution at all; an implementation that interleaves same-home allocations without it does not realize a state of `𝔼` but a collided-or-gapped configuration outside it. So contiguity belongs in A6 (every state of `𝔼` exhibits it) *and* is serialization-borne in `W3`'s sense (per-home order is what keeps a realized execution on `𝔼`) — no contradiction, only one invariant read two ways: at a state of `𝔼`, versus under arbitrary interleaving. The stack's append-only monotonicity and value-immutability — `C0` (content) and `L12` (link) — together with *document-set monotonicity*: ASN-0093's `M1` (ArrangementMonotonicity), `(A Σ → Σ' :: dom(M) ⊆ dom(M'))`, that no step removes a registered document — the invariant that keeps every home `d` in `dom(M)` once registered, on which `H0`'s frontier discipline and every confluence argument of §4 silently rest (distinct from *this* note's `M1`, SafetyUnderMIC of §9; the label collision is unfortunate, so the foundation reference is hereafter qualified as ASN-0093's `M1`) — are, all three of ASN-0093, deliberately *not* in this package: they are *transition* invariants of the form `(A Σ → Σ' :: …)` with no single-state form (their content is inherently relational — no-overwrite, and no-document-removal, are properties of a *step*, and the only single-state shadow is the trivial "the store is a function"). They are carried instead by a companion *transition clause*, quantified over the incoming step: for every `k ≥ 1`, the step `Σ_{k-1} → Σ_k` on `𝔼` preserves `C0`, `L12`, and ASN-0093's `M1`. (Nor is ASN-0093's `M1` recoverable from the per-state package: that package's `C2`/`L1a` forbid removing a document that still *hosts* content, but say nothing against removing a *childless* one, so document permanence is genuinely independent and must be carried in its own right.) W0 is exactly this clause read as a concurrency guarantee. We keep both — the per-state package for what a single snapshot exhibits, the transition clause for what every step maintains — but do not conflate them, because A6's reader-facing point is precisely what holds at one index `Σ_k`, and no-overwrite is not something a lone snapshot can exhibit.
>
> Both rest on one reachability base. Every `Σ_k` on `𝔼` is `→_sh*`-reachable: the base `Σ₀ = Σ_init` is `→_sh*`-reachable from itself (§1), and since each `Σ_i → Σ_{i+1}` is a `→_sh` step, the prefix `Σ₀ → ⋯ → Σ_k` witnesses `Σ_init →_sh* Σ_k`. Reachability is necessary but not by itself the whole argument, because the cited invariants are *not* uniformly quantified over `→_sh*`-reachable *extended-record* states — only ASN-0128's `R1`/`R2` are native theorems about those. The rest land on `𝔼`'s states through the foundations' own transfer machinery, which we invoke rather than assert a quantification they do not carry. *Per-state package.* ASN-0126's `P6` and `P1` — each a genuine claim about a single reachable state, reading only the C/M/L components and the registration data the record-projection preserves (`P1` read as the registry-fixity predicate `Σ.registry = R₀`) — transfer to the extended-record states by ASN-0128's `RP-a`, where registry-fixity also holds natively as `R1`; the stability *corollaries* `P2`/`R2`, being cross-state, are not transferred as single-state conjuncts but follow from registry-fixity holding at every reachable state. ASN-0093's per-state store invariants — `SD`, `C1`/`C1b`/`C1c`, `C2`, `L0`, `L1`/`L1a`/`L1b`/`L1c`, `L3`, `M0`, `M2`, `C-fin`/`L-fin`, and the chain-contiguity invariant `ChainMembershipForOrigin`, inherited by ASN-0086 — together with ASN-0086's own link-store form `L-ContiguousPrefix` — are *each* a single-state C/M/L predicate, so one and the same `B2` transfer carries *all* of them to ASN-0126's states, and the same record-projection (`RP-a`) lifts every one to the extended-record states; no member of the set needs an argument the three originally named (`SD`/`C1c`/`L1c`) did not already exemplify. *Transition clause.* The permanence members `C0`/`L12` and document-set monotonicity (ASN-0093's `M1`), being quantified over steps, ride `B2`'s transition clause across each genuine `→_sh` step to ASN-0126's states and `RP-b` to the extended-record states — the step-quantified path, evaluated across the incoming transition, never at a lone state. The inductive step — preservation across one step — is the foundations'; what §1 supplies is the base, without which the induction has nothing to stand on. Reachability, discharged once above, thereby yields *every* per-state invariant of the stack through this one transfer — so "*fully* per-state-canonical" names the entire package, not a privileged subset of it. No weaker "boundary-only" class of property exists: a `→_sh` state is either fully per-state-canonical or unreachable, and every step maintains the three transition invariants — `C0`, `L12`, and ASN-0093's `M1` — there is no fourth class of obligation reserved for composite boundaries.

A6 is the formal reading of Nelson's "canonical order is an internal mandate of the system" — the property that "all changes, once made, left the file remaining in canonical order," with *nothing* uncanonical in between. There is nothing uncanonical in between because there is no state in between (A0), and every state there *is* satisfies the full per-state package (A6), while every step between two such states maintains the transition invariants (A6's transition clause, W0).

But A6 is a statement about *structure*, and it is silent about *settledness* — and the two must not be run together. A reader who lands mid-batch sees a state that is *completely* canonical: addresses partitioned correctly, every stored tuple shape-conformant and registered, every chain gapless and conformant. Nothing in it is marked "mid-batch." A `retract_stale` that has nullified three of its five targets leaves a state indistinguishable, *by every invariant the stack carries*, from a settled state in which only those three were ever to be retracted; a content run halted after two of its atoms is indistinguishable from a settled two-atom definition. Canonicity does not encode "more is coming." So there is no "precise and only sense of incomplete" that a snapshot exposes — incompleteness is not a property of the state at all, but a *relation* between the state and a batch that happens to span it, and that relation is invisible from inside the snapshot. An observation is therefore never *corrupt*, yet its canonicity does not certify that it is *final*. This gap — canonical but possibly mid-batch — is the substrate's actual read-isolation guarantee, the one Nelson left "correctly open" at the multi-step level while the single-step level was closed by construction. It is also the seed of §8's V1: a verdict's *durability* needs a hypothesis about the future of the order that the present snapshot, however canonical, cannot supply.

## 3. Linearization: how independent proposals become one order

The question asks whether there is a linearization point and how independently-proposed operations become positions in the one order. A2 already named the point. What remains is to say what an implementation must do to *honour* it, since the abstract model simply postulates the order while an implementation must produce it from genuinely concurrent proposals.

The model is *serializable* by construction: there exists a single total order of atomic steps, each operation appearing atomic at its `lin`. What it is *not* is *sequentially consistent* — that would require the order to additionally preserve each agent's program order, which §4's cross-home liberation deliberately abandons (G0). And — this is the subtle and characteristically Xanadu part — the order is *logical, not temporal*. Nelson is emphatic that "time is not included in the tumbler; time is kept track of separately." The order on `𝔼` carries no wall-clock; it is a sequencing of *arrivals at a home*, not a stamping onto a global timeline. So the abstract guarantee is serializability, and the address space deliberately refuses to encode the global temporal coordinate a stronger, linearizable guarantee would demand.

> **G0 (Serializability).** The substrate realizes a single total order of atomic steps (`SequentialTransitionAxiom`), each operation appearing atomic at its `lin`; so every execution is *serializable* — equivalent to the serial order `𝔼`. The order is logical (a per-home arrival sequencing), carrying no global temporal coordinate that observers must agree on. We name this *serializability*, and pointedly *not* *sequential consistency*. SC is strictly stronger: it requires the serial order to preserve each agent's *program order* — the order in which one agent issued its own operations — and this note neither models per-agent program order nor discharges any obligation to preserve one. The omission is not an oversight but a consequence of the note's central result: G1 below frees every pair of `≺`-incomparable cross-home steps to be reordered, and two operations a single agent issues into distinct homes are exactly such a pair, so a valid linearization may serialize them in either order — reversing that agent's program order whenever the reordering bites. Cross-home operations of one agent therefore carry *no* program-order obligation; an agent that needs its own cross-home operations ordered must serialize them itself (issuing each only after the prior's acknowledgment, A7), for the substrate will not. With program order unconstrained across homes, SC degenerates to bare serializability here — and that degeneration *is* the per-home liberation of §4–§6 read as a consistency level, not a guarantee mislaid.

An implementation strengthens G0 to *linearizability* — the order respecting real-time precedence between a response and a subsequent invocation — exactly when it commits the effect before it acknowledges the operation. Gregory's evidence pins the realized linearization point precisely: the in-memory enfilade *is* the shared canonical state, and a write becomes visible to every other session at the completion of the `xanadu(&task)` dispatch — "the mutation is applied at mutation-function-return time, but it is not reachable by any other session until the cooperative event loop returns." That is a clean linearization point at commit. But Gregory also surfaces a place where the realization *under*-honours the contract: for `INSERT`, `DELETEVSPAN`, and `REARRANGE`, the response is emitted *before* the mutation executes ("response-before-check"), so a client can receive "success" for an effect not yet committed (and which may silently fail). An implementation honouring the contract must not do this. We therefore record acknowledgment ordering as a contract clause, not an optional nicety:

> **A7 (CommitBeforeAcknowledge).** For the linearization point of A2 to be presentable to a concurrent client, an operation's response/return value must be produced only at or after `lin(op)`. An acknowledgment emitted before the committing step is unsound: it lets a caller act on an effect that is not yet (and may never be) in the order.

With A7, a reader or caller never observes — never *acts upon* — an uncommitted effect, which is the linearizability strengthening of A4 from "reads" to "the responses agents build their next move on."

How, then, do two independently-proposed operations *become* positions? They are *placed* — by whatever scheduler the implementation runs — into distinct indices (A1). The placement mechanism is out of scope; its *existence and injectivity* is `SequentialTransitionAxiom`. The interesting question is not *that* a placement exists but *how much* the placement is allowed to vary without disturbing the semantics — because that is what tells an implementer how much concurrency they may safely permit. That is the subject of the next two sections, and it is where the global order turns out to be far more freedom than the invariants actually need.

## 4. Where the order must live: per-home, not global

Before the conflict analysis, one scoping commitment forced by §1's stack choice. Throughout this section and the contract of §9, an *allocation step* is a `K.α` or a `K.λ_sh` — an emission into one of a home's sub-allocators. Document registration `K.σ` is deliberately *not* part of the conflict analysis. It remains a step of `𝔼` — the step-level claims A0–A7 apply to it unchanged, canonicity (A6) included — but it is not a sub-allocator emission, and the theory below is a theory of sub-allocator frontiers. Its own conflict structure is real, and we state it rather than hide it: in the ASN-0093 stack `d` is caller-supplied with precondition `d ∉ dom(M)`, so two agents proposing the *same* fresh `d` against a common pre-state both pass the precondition, and the first to commit forces the second's to fail — a same-address collision, structurally like H2 below, but one the substrate resolves by *rejecting the loser* rather than by advancing a frontier, because there is no document frontier to advance (that apparatus is the excluded entity layer's `A_doc`/`A_v`). Note the difference from allocation: serializing two same-`d` registrations yields one success and one clean rejection, not two distinct fresh addresses — there is nothing for a frontier-style clause to space apart. There is also a *register-before-allocate* dependency: an allocation into `d` presupposes `d ∈ dom(M)`. We discharge both by hypothesis, not by clause — document-address freshness (that distinct agents do not propose the same `d`) and prior registration are *assumed preconditions*, supplied by the entity-allocation layer this note excludes, exactly as G-PO below starts from a state whose homes are already registered. The *conflict* analysis that follows quantifies over allocation steps; `H3` (stated after G1) then re-admits registration to the *confluence* result, lifting `G1` to full executions that contain `K.σ` — freshness and register-before-allocate doing for registration what the frontier discipline does for allocation — while the *contract* of §9 rests on per-step atomicity and the per-home facts directly: its safety theorem `M1` cites `H1`/`H2` and the clauses, never full-execution confluence, so it needs no registration clause regardless.

Consider what an allocating step actually reads and writes. By ASN-0093 and the `FrontierUnification` lemma of ASN-0126, an emission into a home `d`'s sub-allocator for subspace `S ∈ {s_C, s_L}` reads the home's current *frontier* and deposits at it. Let us make the frontier uniform across both subspaces. For `S ∈ {s_C, s_L}` write `dom_S(Σ)` for `dom(Σ.C)` when `S = s_C` and `dom(Σ.L)` when `S = s_L`, and define the home's `S`-population and `S`-frontier index

`P_S(d, Σ) = {a ∈ dom_S(Σ) : origin(a) = d}`,  `φ_S(d, Σ) = |P_S(d, Σ)|`.

By `ChainMembershipForOrigin` (ASN-0093), `P_S(d, Σ)` is a *contiguous initial segment* of the chain `A_S(d)`, so `φ_S(d, Σ)` is the count of already-emitted slots and the next emission lands at chain slot `φ_S(d, Σ)`, advancing the count by one. (For `S = s_L` this is exactly ASN-0126's `a_emit(Σ,d) = chain_d(f_d^Σ)`; for `S = s_C` it is `SubsequentEmissionFreshness`'s `inc(a_prev, 0)` with `a_prev` the home's content maximum. The two are one mechanism.)

> **H0 (FrontierDiscipline).** Along `𝔼`, for fixed `(d, S)`, `φ_S(d, ·)` is non-decreasing, increases by exactly one at each `S`-allocation to `d`, and is left unchanged by every other step.
>
> *Proof.* An `S`-allocation to `d` adjoins one fresh address with `origin = d` and subspace `S` (ASN-0093 K.α / ASN-0128 K.λ_sh effect), so `|P_S(d, ·)|` rises by one. Any other step is either a `K.σ`, which registers a document and frames both stores (`C' = C ∧ L' = L`, ASN-0093 K.σ), leaving every `dom_S` fixed; or an allocation into a different `(d', S') ≠ (d, S)`, which adjoins one fresh address `a'` with `origin(a') = d'` to store `dom_{S'}` and frames everything else. That `a'` falls outside `P_S(d, ·) = {a ∈ dom_S(·) : origin(a) = d}` needs no disjointness lemma — only the set's own two filters, store-membership in `dom_S` and `origin = d`, against the fact that `(d', S') ≠ (d, S)` diverges along at least one axis. *If `S' ≠ S`* (whether or not `d' = d`): the step writes only `dom_{S'}` and leaves `dom_S` framed, so `P_S(d, ·) ⊆ dom_S` is untouched outright. *If `S' = S`* (which forces `d' ≠ d`): the step does add `a'` to `dom_S`, but `origin(a') = d' ≠ d` fails the `origin = d` filter, and — `origin` being a structural projection fixed per address and `dom_S` append-only — no prior member departs either, so `P_S(d, ·)` is again unchanged. These two cases exhaust `(d', S') ≠ (d, S)`; in particular the cross-home, cross-subspace pair (`d' ≠ d ∧ S' ≠ S`) — which neither `CrossDocumentDisjointness` nor `DisjointSubAllocatorChains` names on its own — is absorbed by the first. In every case `P_S(d, ·)` is unchanged. ∎

H0 is the lever. It shows that the *only* steps that touch `(d, S)`'s frontier are the `S`-allocations to `d` themselves. From this the conflict structure of allocation falls out immediately.

> **H1 (CrossHomeIndependence).** Two allocation steps into distinct `(d, S) ≠ (d', S')` *commute*: neither reads or writes state the other touches, and applying them in either order yields the same state.
>
> *Proof.* The first reads `φ_S(d,·)` and writes one address on chain `A_S(d)`; the second reads `φ_{S'}(d',·)` and writes on `A_{S'}(d')`. We show the two chains are disjoint as address sets, across the two axes along which `(d, S) ≠ (d', S')` can differ. *If `d ≠ d'`:* we argue by *origin*, not by anchor position, because the two documents need not be prefix-incomparable — the ASN-0093 stack admits *nesting* homes (`d ≼ d'` with `d ≠ d'`, e.g. `d = [1.0.1.0.1]` and `d' = [1.0.1.0.1.1]`, both `T4`-valid with `zeros = 2` and both `K.σ`-registrable), and for such a pair the document components do *not* diverge, so a "document-level divergence" argument would be false. We do not need it. The address the first step deposits joins `P_S(d,·)`, defined by the `origin = d` filter, so it carries `origin = d`; the second's deposit carries `origin = d'`. Since `origin` is a structural projection fixed per address (H0) — a function of the address alone — `a = a'` would force `d = origin(a) = origin(a') = d'`, contradicting `d ≠ d'`. Hence the two deposits are distinct and `A_S(d) ∩ A_{S'}(d') = ∅`, *regardless of subspace and regardless of nesting*, with no appeal to anchor incomparability — covering uniformly both the same-subspace instance `CrossDocumentDisjointness` names and the cross-document, cross-subspace `S ≠ S'` case its single-`·` statement leaves unnamed. (The separator-vs-nonzero-continuation argument `CrossDocumentDisjointness` itself uses would also serve: `b_S(d)` carries the field-separator `0` at index `#d + 1` — the position immediately after `d`'s last component, with the subspace digit following at `#d + 2`, per ASN-0093's `DisjointSubAllocatorChains` — whereas `d'`'s continuation there is necessarily non-zero — a zero would force `zeros(d') > 2` — so the anchors diverge at index `#d + 1`; origin is simply the cleaner route.) *If `d = d'`* (which forces `S ≠ S'`): the two chains share an origin, so origin cannot separate them; instead the sibling-subspace anchors `[d.0.s_C]` and `[d.0.s_L]` diverge at the subspace digit, so by T10 (PartitionIndependence) the chains are disjoint — `DisjointSubAllocatorChains`. In every case the deposits are distinct and the chains disjoint. By H0 neither step changes the other's frontier population. So each step's precondition (its frontier value) and effect (its deposited key) are on state disjoint from the other's; they commute. ∎

> **H2 (SameHomeConflict).** Two allocation steps into the *same* `(d, S)` conflict: each reads `φ_S(d,·)`, and the first to commit increments it (H0), so the two *cannot* read the same frontier value without colliding. If both read frontier `φ`, both deposit at chain slot `φ` — the *same address* — violating freshness.
>
> *Proof.* Two emissions into `(d, S)` against a common pre-state `Σ_pre` read the same population `P_S(d, Σ_pre)`, and the emission rule is a function of that population, so they compute the same deposit address — in *either* of its two branches.
>
> *Interior case* (`P_S(d, Σ_pre) ≠ ∅`): `SubsequentEmissionFreshness` gives `a = inc(a_prev, 0)` with `a_prev = max P_S(d, Σ_pre)`; both read the same `a_prev`, hence compute the same `a`.
>
> *First-emission boundary* (`P_S(d, Σ_pre) = ∅`): here `max P_S(d, Σ_pre) = max ∅` is undefined and the subsequent rule does not apply. Instead both emissions satisfy ASN-0093's *first-emission* predicate `{a' : origin(a') = d} = ∅` and both compute the determinate first slot `a = [d.0.S.1]` (`FirstEmission`). This is the race for the *first* allocation into a fresh home, and it collides just as squarely.
>
> In either case the two compute one address; whichever commits first deposits it, and the second's freshness precondition — `a ∉ dom_S`, discharged in the interior case by `SubsequentEmissionFreshness` and at the boundary by `FirstEmissionFreshness` — then fails. The conflict is identical at the boundary and in the interior. ∎

H1 and H2 together are the whole answer to "is ordering per-home-independent or globally serialized." The *conflicts* — the places where order matters — are confined to a single `(home, subspace)`. Everything across homes (and across the two subspaces of one home) commutes. This is not our invention; it is Nelson's "owned numbers" made operational. His account of two simultaneous creators is exactly H1+H2: they are "either in different regions (no shared allocator) — and there is no race to lose — or under the same single allocator, which serializes them." The address space is a tree of owned sub-allocators; two writers contend only when they reach into the *same* sub-allocator, and then a single authority sequences them. Collision is "impossible by construction," with "zero coordination" required across regions.

We can now state the equivalence that liberates an implementation from global serialization. To state it *non-vacuously* we must first name an execution model weaker than the total order `𝔼` — for a total order *already* serializes every pair of steps, cross-home included, so against `𝔼` alone "per-home serialization suffices" would collapse into the empty tautology "the total order is total." The content we are after lives one level below `𝔼`, in the *schedule* an implementation commits to before any particular interleaving is realized.

> **G-PO (PartialScheduleModel).** A *schedule* over a reachable start state `Σ` is a finite set `O` of allocation steps (`K.α`, `K.λ_sh`) into homes already registered at `Σ` — no `K.σ` is scheduled *in this base model*, the register-before-allocate dependency discharged in advance per this section's scoping; `H3` below lifts the result to schedules that do register documents — equipped with a partial order `≺` — the order the implementation *forces* — such that for every `(d, S)` the `S`-allocations to `d` in `O` are pairwise `≺`-comparable (*per-home serialization*), while steps into distinct `(d, S) ≠ (d', S')` need not be. A *linearization* of `(O, ≺)` is any total order extending `≺`; run from `Σ`, each linearization is one execution `𝔼`. The total order of §1 is the special case in which `≺` is itself total.

G-PO admits exactly the schedules an implementer cares about: those that force an order *only within each home* and leave cross-home steps `≺`-incomparable. Against this model the liberation is a genuine claim, not a tautology.

> **G1 (PerHomeSerializabilitySuffices).** Let `(O, ≺)` be any per-home-serialized schedule over a reachable `Σ`. Then (i) *every* linearization of `(O, ≺)` is a valid execution preserving every per-state invariant at every state and every allocation invariant in its final state; and (ii) all linearizations are *confluent* — they reach one and the same final state, with the same address at every chain slot of every home. The cross-home order is free to vary, and no invariant and no committed address depends on how a linearization resolves it.
>
> *Proof.* (i) Per-state invariants are preserved by every single step (A6), hence hold at every state of every linearization with no appeal to a global order. The allocation invariants (same-home uniqueness, dense chain contiguity) depend only on each `S`-allocation to `d` reading the current frontier: per-home comparability totally orders `(d, S)`'s allocations, so by H0 each reads the frontier its predecessor left and lands at the next slot — gapless, collision-free — and by H1 no cross-`(d, S)` step disturbs that frontier wherever the linearization places it. The *other* preconditions a step carries are reordering-*invariant*, so frontier-freshness is the only one a linearization can disturb: a `K.λ_sh` gate reads only data no transposition moves — `Sh-conf(K, F, G)` the presented value `(F, G)` and the registry-fixed `shape(K)` (ASN-0126 `P4`, ShConfStateIndependence), arity-3 the value alone, and K's registration the registry keys — all immutable across runtime steps (W6 / ASN-0128 `R1`); and every allocation's home precondition `d ∈ dom(M)` survives once met, since `G-PO` schedules only into homes already registered at `Σ` and ASN-0093's `M1` (document-set monotonicity, named in A6's transition clause) admits no document removal, so `d` stays in `dom(M)` at every position. Each step's precondition — frontier-freshness secured per home above, the rest reordering-invariant — is thus met at its position, so the linearization is a valid execution. (ii) Any two linearizations of a finite partial order differ by a finite sequence of transpositions of adjacent `≺`-incomparable steps; incomparable steps here are into distinct `(d, S)` (per-home comparability makes same-`(d, S)` steps comparable), so each transposed pair commutes (H1), leaving the surrounding states and every committed address unchanged. Confluence to a single final state follows, and ASN-0093's sequential results, holding along any one linearization, thereby describe them all. ∎

G1 as proved governs an allocation-only schedule, but a real execution also *registers* documents — `𝔼` carries `K.σ` steps. Lifting confluence to cover them needs one further commutation, the registration analog of H1.

> **H3 (RegistrationIndependence).** Under this section's document-address freshness hypothesis (distinct agents propose distinct fresh `d`), a `K.σ` registering `d_new` *commutes* with every `→_sh` step other than an allocation into `d_new` itself. *(a)* Against an allocation `K.α`/`K.λ_sh` into a home `d ≠ d_new`: `K.σ` writes only `dom(M)` (adjoining `d_new`) and frames the stores (`C' = C ∧ L' = L`), while the allocation writes only `dom(C)`/`dom(L)` and frames `M` — disjoint components — and each precondition survives the other (the allocation's `d ∈ dom(M)` under `dom(M)`'s growth, `K.σ`'s `d_new ∉ dom(M)` under the allocation's frame on `M`). *(b)* Against a `K.σ` registering `d'_new ≠ d_new`: each adjoins a distinct fresh document and frames the stores, and `d'_new ≠ d_new` preserves both freshness preconditions across the swap. The lone *non*-commuting pair is `K.σ(d_new)` against an allocation into `d_new`, whose `d_new ∈ dom(M)` holds only *after* the registration — the register-before-allocate dependency, hence `≺`-comparable (registration first) and never transposed. (Two registrations of the *same* `d_new` collide as §4's scoping notes, resolved by rejecting the loser; freshness excludes them from one schedule.)
>
> *Proof.* The two cases commute for *different* structural reasons, and folding them together would misdescribe (b). *(a)* is genuinely a disjoint-write commutation — the registration analog of H1's disjoint-state argument: `K.σ` writes only `dom(M)` while the allocation writes only a store and frames `M`, so the two touch disjoint components, each precondition surviving the other (as the statement details). Here `dom(M)` does play the role a sub-allocator chain plays in H1 — a component one step writes and the other does not. *(b)* is *not* a disjoint-write: both `K.σ` steps *read* `dom(M)` (the precondition `d_new ∉ dom(M)`) *and write* `dom(M)` (inserting their target), so they share read-and-write access to the *one* component `dom(M)` — there is no second chain to separate them the way H1 has two. They commute instead by *distinct-element non-interference*: a membership-test-and-insert of `d_new` and one of `d'_new` do not interfere precisely because `d_new ≠ d'_new` (the freshness hypothesis), so each step's `dom(M)`-read verdict and inserted element are unchanged by the other's insertion, and both freshness preconditions survive the swap. The lone *non*-commuting pair is exactly the register-before-allocate dependency §4 discharges by hypothesis. ∎

With H3 the schedule model and its confluence lift to full executions. Augment `G-PO`'s `O` to admit `K.σ` steps, under two `≺`-constraints already forced by §4's hypotheses — *register-before-allocate* (`K.σ(d_new) ≺` every allocation into `d_new`) and pairwise comparability of any same-target registrations (vacuous under freshness among distinct agents) — leaving registration/allocation pairs into distinct homes, and registration/registration pairs of distinct targets, `≺`-incomparable. Then G1 extends verbatim. *(i) Validity:* a `K.σ` step's sole precondition `d_new ∉ dom(M)` is met at its position — `dom(M)` only grows, and with `d_new`'s registrant unique (freshness) no preceding step adds `d_new`, since every other registration targets a distinct document and every allocation frames `dom(M)`; the allocation-step argument is unchanged, register-before-allocate placing each allocation after its home's registration so the home is in `dom(M)` by the allocation's position. *(ii) Confluence:* two linearizations still differ by transpositions of adjacent `≺`-incomparable steps, now of three kinds — allocation/allocation into distinct `(d, S)` (commute by H1), registration/allocation into distinct homes, and registration/registration of distinct targets (the latter two by H3) — so each transposition leaves the surrounding states and every committed address unchanged, while the `≺`-comparable register-before-allocate and same-target pairs are never transposed. Confluence to one committed final state therefore holds for the *full* execution, not merely its allocation fragment: per-home serialization suffices even when documents are registered mid-execution.

G1 is the practical payload, and it is a claim about a *fixed* schedule `O` of raw steps. An implementer is *not* obliged to commit a total schedule — Gregory's single global loop is one such, and a maximally serial one. They may instead commit a schedule that forces an order *only within each home*, leaving agents in different homes `≺`-incomparable and so free to proceed with no coordination whatsoever; by G1(i) every interleaving of `O` the runtime then produces is a valid execution, and by G1(ii) they all reach the same committed state — for that fixed set of raw steps, observationally indistinguishable, for all of the substrate's invariants, from the global order the axiom postulates. Gregory's loop over-satisfies the requirement; per-home serialization is the least that meets it. This is exactly Nelson's distributed intent (each server "at all times unified and operational," progress never gated on a global agreement) rescued from the implementation's incidental global lock.

One seam between *steps* and *operations* must be marked before the contract leans on G1. G1(ii)'s confluence is proven for a fixed `(O, ≺)` of raw `→_sh` steps, and at that level it is exact: `K.λ_sh` does no de-duplication, so two same-value deposits into distinct homes are distinct steps that commute (H1). But an `Emit_K` *operation* under `idem(K) = ⊤` is realized either as a step or as a zero-step hit, and which one depends on the *global* active subset `A_K` at the moment it runs (ASN-0128 I1, whose dedup ranges over all of `A_K`, not per-home). So the operation→schedule realization is itself order-dependent — in two distinct *families*, which we take in turn. *The first family — the active-membership toggle.* A lone `idem = ⊤` `Emit_K`'s hit/miss verdict reads the *global* active subset `A_K`, never a per-home slice (ASN-0128 I1), so *any* concurrent operation that flips whether a coverage-equal tuple is *active* flips that verdict with it — cross-home *or* same-home, since the read it races is global either way — and the survivor, the realized step count, and the committed state turn on the race. Two instances populate this family, one toggling membership *on* and one *off*. *Instance (i) — a competing coverage-equal emit.* Two concurrent operations that are `idem = ⊤` with coverage-equal `(F, G)`, the hit/miss branch reading the *global* `A_K`. Let two agents in homes `d ≠ d'` both call `Emit_K(F, G, K)` with `idem(K) = ⊤` and coverage-equal `(F, G)`. In the order `X;Y`, X deposits at `a_emit(Σ, d)` and Y is a hit returning X's address; in `Y;X`, Y deposits at `a_emit(Σ, d')` and X is a hit. Both orders presuppose that the second emit's dedup-read sees the first's deposit — i.e. that the dedup-read-and-deposit is serialized across the coverage class, the very "serializing authority [that] orders the two calls before either becomes a step" on which ASN-0128 I4's single-survivor explicitly rests. *That authority is global, not per-home* — and per-home MIC (clauses 1–7) does not supply it. Drop it and a third interleaving opens, and — the point the cross-home setup `d ≠ d'` obscures — it opens *regardless of home*. X reads the global `A_K` against `Σ` (no match → miss), Y reads it against the *same stale* `Σ` (no match → miss), then X and Y each deposit at their respective frontiers, and the post-state carries *two* coverage-equal active `K`-tuples. What the both-miss interleaving actually requires is only that the two deposits land at *distinct* addresses; the two stale dedup-reads supply the double miss with no reference to home. *Cross-home* (`d ≠ d'`) the distinct addresses come from H1 — distinct origins, `a_emit(Σ, d) ≠ a_emit(Σ, d')`, no allocation collision. *Same-home* (`d = d'`, two coverage-equal `idem = ⊤` emits into one `(d, s_L)` — and this is the *primary* idempotent case, a retry or two workers asserting the same edge in one document) the distinct addresses come instead from *clause 2's own spacing*: the dedup-read precedes the deposit and is not itself an allocation, so clause 2 — the per-home serializer — does not order it; it orders only the two *deposits*, landing them at consecutive frontier slots `φ, φ+1` (H0), distinct and fresh and *both born active*. Either way two coverage-equal active tuples stand. Nothing in clauses 1–7 forbids it: clause 4 pins each dedup-read to *some* committed state (here the stale `Σ`), never to its own deposit's pre-state (`Σ ∪ {T_X}` for Y) where it would be a hit; and clause 2 spaces the same-home deposits apart rather than suppressing them, never reaching the global dedup-read that precedes them. The resulting execution is a valid `→_sh` execution, and whether ASN-0128 I1a's single-survivor reaches it turns on what "`K`-surface-emitted" is taken to mean — for I1a's *literal* definition and its *operative* one part company exactly here, and the gap must be drawn out rather than stepped over. Read literally, I1a calls a derivation `K`-surface-emitted iff "every `L_K`-growing step … is the deposit branch of an `Emit_K` invocation"; by that clause the both-miss derivation *qualifies* — X's deposit is the deposit branch of X's `Emit_K`, Y's of Y's, each a bona fide `Emit_K` — so a naive reading would (wrongly) conclude I1a applies and no duplicate forms. But I1a's *proof* asks more than the literal clause grants: its induction step reads the deposit as "the deposit branch of an `Emit_K` — which fires only on a miss: at the pre-state its I0-class had no active member," and "the pre-state" there is *the deposit step's own pre-state*. The operative notion is therefore the stronger one — a derivation is `K`-surface-emitted in I1a's working sense only when each `L_K`-growing deposit is a *miss evaluated at that step's own pre-state*. Under ASN-0128's sequential operation→step identification the two readings coincide: an operation's dedup-read input state *is* its step's pre-state, so "fired on a miss" and "a miss at the step's own pre-state" are one fact, and the literal clause suffices. A concurrent realization, whose dedup-read and deposit pre-state diverge, breaks the coincidence: Y's `Emit_K` dedup-reads the *stale* `Σ` and so fires on a miss, but Y's *deposit step's* pre-state is `Σ ∪ {T_X}`, at which the I0-class already holds the active `T_X` — Y "would be a hit" there. Y's deposit is thus *not* a miss-against-its-own-pre-state; I1a's induction fails precisely at it; and the derivation, `K`-surface-emitted under the literal clause, is *not* `K`-surface-emitted in the operative sense — so I1a's single-survivor does not reach it, and the duplicate stands. This argument names only `Σ ∪ {T_X}` and never the two emits' homes, so it carries the same-home pair (`d = d'`) verbatim: there too Y's deposit-step pre-state already holds the active `T_X`, the induction breaks identically, and clause 2's slot-spacing — not H1 — is what keeps the two deposits at distinct addresses. Clause 8 (§9) is exactly what restores the coincidence: by fusing each `idem = ⊤` dedup-read to its own deposit's pre-state, it makes every deposit a genuine miss-against-its-own-pre-state, so the realized derivation regains operative `K`-surface-emittedness, I1a applies, and the duplicate is suppressed. So the order-dependence here is not merely in *which* tuple survives — under the two serialized orders the single survivor sits at a *different* chain slot of a *different* home, observably (`Observe_K` returns `a` vs `a'`) — but, absent a global per-coverage-class dedup serialization, in *how many* survive: one under serialization, two under the both-miss interleaving. The dependence reaches even a home-relative behavior, though only *indirectly*. BH4 `age` cannot be read on `K` itself — BH4 requires `idem = ⊥` (R-C0), and the scenario fixes `idem(K) = ⊤`, so `age(·)` is simply not a defined query on a `K`-tuple. What does shift is the age of *other* tuples: the survivor's deposit advances *its own* home's chain frontier (`f_d` vs `f_{d'}`, H0), and since `age` is denominated in a home's link traffic (ASN-0128 BH4), the age of any co-homed `idem = ⊥` tuple — of *some other* type homed at `d` versus `d'` — reads differently across the two outcomes. The age that moves is read on those tuples' type, never on `K`. These two operations are cross-home, hence `≺`-incomparable, hence free-running — so per-home serialization offers no remedy: it is the wrong axis entirely. Followed through, "the wrong axis" is sharper than "no remedy": per-home order does not even *prevent the duplicate*, only a *global* per-coverage-class serialization of the dedup-read-and-deposit does (§9 clause 8). This is the one operation-level guarantee that demands a non-per-home discipline — exactly the carve-out the §9 contract makes explicit.

*Instance (ii) — a nullify of the active incumbent.* The same verdict flips with no second emit in sight, when a concurrent retraction *removes* the very incumbent a lone emit would deduplicate against. Fix a state `Σ` carrying an active incumbent `T = (a_T, F_T, G_T) ∈ A_K^Σ`, `idem(K) = ⊤`; let agent A call `Emit_K(F, G)` at home `d_A` with `(F, G)` coverage-equal to `T`, and agent B call `Nullify_Binary(a_T)` at home `d_B`, targeting the *incumbent* — resident at `Σ`, so `P-tgt` holds by residence in *either* order, and B fires in both. Choose `d_A`, `d_B`, `home(T)` pairwise distinct, so A and B are cross-home and `≺`-incomparable. In the order `A;B`: A reads `A_K^Σ` with `T` active → *hit*, zero steps, returns `a_T` (ASN-0128 I1); then B nullifies `a_T`. Final: class `K` has *no* active tuple, and `dom(L)` gained only B's emitter. In the order `B;A`: B nullifies `a_T` first, so at A's state `T` is gone from `A_K` (ASN-0128 I2) → A *misses* and deposits a fresh active `A' = (a_A, F, G)` at `a_emit(·, d_A)` — `a_A` lying on `d_A`'s chain, outside the unit-depth scope `{a_T}` of B's retraction, so it is born active, not nullified. Final: class `K` has active `A'`, and `dom(L)` gained B's emitter *and* `a_A`. The committed states differ — `Observe_K(F-pattern, oper)` returns `∅` versus `{A'}`, the realized step count 1 versus 2 — yet *neither* instance (i)'s hypothesis nor the target-residence race's holds: there is only one emit (so not instance (i), which needs two coverage-equal emits), and B targets the pre-existing incumbent rather than A's emission output (so not the race below). Decisively, *this instance survives the disciplines that tame the others*: B's retraction of `T` obeys emit-before-retract (W5) — `T` was emitted long before B, so emit-before-retract, the discipline that excludes the target-residence race, is vacuously satisfied in *both* orders and constrains nothing here — while clause 8 (§9), the discipline that suppresses instance (i)'s duplicate, serializes only racing coverage-equal *emits* and so does not reach this lone-emit instance; the order-instability persists under both. (The derivation is incidentally surface-disciplined as well, but surface-discipline tames none of the three order-dependences — see the summary below — so it is no part of what (ii) survives.) So the toggle is the general fact: instance (i) toggles a coverage-equal tuple's membership *on*, instance (ii) toggles it *off*, and either flips a lone `idem = ⊤` emit's verdict precisely because that verdict reads the global `A_K`.

*The second family — the target-residence race.* This source turns not on de-duplication but on a retraction's target precondition `P-tgt`, which reads `A_rel = dom(L)` rather than the active subset `A_K` — so `idem` governs it not at all. Let agent A call `Emit_K` at home `d`, landing — deterministically, hence predictably — at `a = a_emit(Σ, d)` (`FrontierUnification`), and let agent B call `Nullify_Binary(a)` at a *different* home `d_retr = d' ≠ d`; both are proposed against the common `Σ`, cross-home and `≺`-incomparable. In the order `A;B`, A deposits its tuple at `a`, so at B's state `a ∈ A_rel`, `P-tgt` holds, B fires its `Emit_R`, and `a` is *nullified*. In the order `B;A`, at B's state `a ∉ dom(L)` and `a ≠ a_emit(Σ, d')` (B's home is `d'`, not `d`), so `P-tgt` *fails* and B is *rejected* — no step (ASN-0128 S3; A1's rejection case) — after which A deposits at `a`, leaving `a` *active*. The committed final state differs, nullified versus active, by cross-home order alone, with no `idem = ⊤` coverage-equal pair anywhere in it: A is type `K`, B is type `R`, and their `(F, G)` are not coverage-equal. The seam is the same operation→schedule realization one (A1), now seen from the rejection side rather than the hit side: fix the raw set `O = {A's step, B's step}` with *both* firing and it is confluent — B's retraction tuple covers `a`, `a ∈ dom(L)`, so `a` is born nullified whichever step commits first (H1), exactly as G1(ii) promises — but whether B *is in `O`* at all, i.e. whether B realizes as a step or is rejected to zero (A1), is itself the order-dependent fact. This is the race W5 and Open Question 9 already isolate: under the coordination layer's *emit-before-retract* discipline (W5) — order every retraction after the emission of its target — order `B;A` is forbidden and the outcome is determinate; absent that hypothesis the race is real, and the substrate's response to the losing order is a clean rejection (W5), never a dangling retraction.

The honest statement is therefore two-level: G1's confluence is a *step*-level guarantee — fix `O`, and every linearization reaches one committed state — while at the *operation* level we have isolated two **families** of order-dependence, both untouched by per-home serialization — not because they are confined to distinct homes (the toggle's duplicate, below, is not) but because their governing reads are *global*: `A_K` for the toggle, `A_rel` for the target-residence race, neither a per-home quantity. *The active-membership toggle:* a lone `idem = ⊤` `Emit_K`'s hit/miss verdict reads the *global* `A_K`, so any concurrent step that adds or removes a coverage-equal *active* tuple flips it — instance (i), a competing coverage-equal `idem = ⊤` emit (whose both-miss interleaving, absent §9 clause 8's per-coverage-class dedup serialization, leaves not one survivor but *two* — a duplicate per-home MIC permits *regardless of the two emits' homes*, cross-home the deposits separating by H1 and same-home by clause 2's frontier spacing, not merely a reordering), and instance (ii), a `Nullify` of a coverage-equal active incumbent. *The target-residence race:* a `Nullify` racing the emission of its own cross-home target, its `P-tgt` firing or rejecting by order. The two families part company under discipline, and this is the load-bearing correction. The target-residence race is excluded a priori by W5's emit-before-retract *alone* — order every retraction after its target's emission, forbidding the losing order `B;A`. *Surface-discipline does not exclude it*, and the reason is decisive: in the losing order `B;A`, B's target `a` is not yet in `dom(L)` and is not B's own frontier slot, so `P-tgt` fails and B is *rejected* — zero steps, hence *no* `L_R`-growing step — and "every `L_R`-growing step is a `Nullify_Binary`" (ASN-0128 SD) then holds *vacuously*, making `B;A` itself surface-disciplined; yet `B;A` leaves `a` active where the equally surface-disciplined `A;B` leaves it nullified. Two surface-disciplined orders, divergent outcomes: against this race surface-discipline supplies not determinacy but only the substrate's clean `P-tgt` rejection of the losing order. Surface-discipline in fact excludes *none* of the three order-dependences — it is orthogonal to the order each turns on. Clause 8 (per-coverage-class dedup serialization, §9) is what suppresses instance (i)'s *duplicate*, restoring idempotent uniqueness (a benign which-survivor non-confluence remains, M1(b)); the toggle's incumbent-nullify instance (ii) is reduced by *neither* discipline — its target `T` is an already-emitted incumbent, so emit-before-retract is vacuously satisfied in both orders and constrains nothing, while clause 8 governs only racing coverage-equal *emits*, of which (ii) has but one — so an `idem = ⊤` emit's order-instability persists even on the fully disciplined domain. The sharp contrast the families draw is exactly this: instance (ii)'s target is *already* emitted, so emit-before-retract is vacuous in both orders, whereas the target-residence race's target is *not yet* emitted, so emit-before-retract bites and forbids `B;A`. We name these the families *we have found* and do not claim them closed: because the toggle is driven by the global reach of the dedup read, any further cross-home operation that toggles a coverage-equal tuple's active-membership would extend the first family rather than open a third. §9's M1(b) notes the toggle family from the de-duplication side, and W5 and Open Question 9 the target-residence race.

## 5. The invariant partition: what the model keeps, what serialization keeps

We can now answer, invariant by invariant, the question of which guarantees are intrinsic to the model and which are bought only by serialization. The discriminator is the conflict analysis of §4: an invariant is *model-intrinsic* if per-step atomicity (A0) alone preserves it under arbitrary interleaving; it is *serialization-borne* if it additionally requires the per-home ordering of H0/H2.

> **W0 (MonotonicityStepLocal).** Append-only monotonicity and value-immutability — `C0` (ContentImmutability) and `L12` (LinkImmutability), together with document-set monotonicity (ASN-0093's `M1`, ArrangementMonotonicity, `dom(M) ⊆ dom(M')` — distinct from this note's `M1` of §9), all of ASN-0093 — are model-intrinsic. Each step's effect either adjoins a fresh key — a fresh document under `K.σ`, a fresh content or link address under `K.α`/`K.λ_sh` — or frames the store; no step removes a key (`dom(M)` included) or rewrites a value. So under any interleaving of atomic steps, `dom(C)`, `dom(L)`, and `dom(M)` only grow and existing values never change. Needs A0, nothing more.

W0 is Nelson's deepest point about permanence, and it is worth quoting his reasoning because it *is* the proof: the permanence guarantees "had to be operation-intrinsic, because a guarantee asserted as absolute cannot depend on a reconciliation mechanism the design leaves open." There is no overwrite primitive; therefore "no scheduling of writers can produce an overwrite." Monotonicity is safe from concurrency *because of the shape of the operations*, not because of any ordering discipline.

> **W1 (CrossHomeUniquenessStructural).** Uniqueness of addresses allocated in *distinct* homes (or distinct subspaces of one home) is model-intrinsic. For `d ≠ d'` the two deposits carry distinct origins (`origin = d` vs `origin = d'`) and so are unequal *regardless of subspace and regardless of whether the homes nest* — the origin argument of H1, which needs no anchor incomparability and is therefore immune to the nesting homes (`d ≼ d'`) the ASN-0093 stack admits; this settles the cross-document, cross-subspace pair as well, not only the same-subspace instance `CrossDocumentDisjointness` names. For `d = d'` (which forces distinct subspaces) the chains diverge at the subspace digit — `DisjointSubAllocatorChains`. Either way `a ≠ a'` holds *independent of order* (H1). Needs no serialization at all.

> **W2 (SameHomeUniquenessSerial).** Uniqueness of two allocations into the *same* `(d, S)` is serialization-borne. By H2 it holds iff the two emissions read distinct frontier values, i.e. iff they are serialized within `(d, S)`. Without per-home ordering, two emissions collide on one address.

> **W3 (ChainContiguitySerial).** Dense chain contiguity — `ChainMembershipForOrigin`'s property that `P_S(d, ·)` is a gapless initial segment of `A_S(d)` — is serialization-borne in the same per-home sense as W2. Given per-home ordering, every emission lands at the unique current frontier slot (H0), so the segment stays gapless; without it, two emissions either collide (a repeated slot) or, if an implementation "fixed" collisions by skipping, leave a hole. Gregory's evidence is decisive here and we adopt his framing as confirmation: the dense chains are "a property of serialization, not the data model — a truly concurrent allocator would preserve uniqueness and global monotonicity but lose contiguity whenever allocations interleave."

> **W5 (ActiveSliceStepLocal).** The relationship between the audit slice `L_K` (every type-`K` tuple ever emitted) and the active subset `A_K` (`L_K` minus `nullified`) is model-intrinsic. Both are *pure functions* of the state read; an `Observe` at index `k` computes both from `Σ_k` consistently (A3). Nullification only grows `nullified` (R6a, RetractionStability), and each `Nullify` evaluates its target precondition `P-tgt` — `a ∈ A_rel^Σ ∨ a = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)` — at *its own* linearization state, so neither branch of that disjunction ever leaves a dangling retraction, though only one is a rejection. A nullify ordered before its target exists, where the target is *another* home's not-yet-emitted address, is *rejected*: at that state `a ∉ A_rel` and `a ≠ a_emit(Σ, d_retr)` (the slot lies on the other home's chain, not the retractor's), so both disjuncts fail and the call takes no step. A *self-emit* nullify of the retractor's *own* frontier slot — `a = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)` — is *not* rejected: the second disjunct holds, the nullify fires, and it deposits its R-tuple at exactly `b = a_emit(Σ, d_retr) = a`, so the tuple lands on `a` and nullifies itself (a self-nullified retraction, not a dangling one). Either way no retraction is left targeting an absent address — the cross-home pre-target case by declining, the self-emit case by pre-nullifying its own slot. No interleaving can yield an incoherent slice: at every state, `A_K = L_K ∖ nullified` exactly. Needs only A0.

W5 deserves a word, because "audit/active-slice coincidence" is where one might fear a read could see a tuple that is both present and absent. It cannot. At any single `Σ_k` the two sets are derived together, so they are mutually consistent by construction. The only cross-agent subtlety is *which* order a nullify and its target's emission take — and that is resolved soundly by `P-tgt` being checked at the nullify's linearization state (ASN-0086, ASN-0128): if a writer means to retract another's tuple, the coordination layer must order the retraction *after* the emission, or the substrate will (correctly) reject it as targeting a non-existent address. The substrate never loses or duplicates; at worst it declines an out-of-order retraction. That declination is information, not corruption. This order-sensitivity is precisely §4's *target-residence race* — the second family of operation-level non-confluence: whether the nullify fires or is declined — hence whether its target is left nullified or active — turns on the cross-home order of the retraction against its target's emission, and only the coordination layer's emit-before-retract discipline removes the dependence (Open Question 9).

> **W6 (RegistryRaceFree).** The registry is immutable across all runtime steps (`RegistryInvariance` / P1 of ASN-0126, R1 of ASN-0128: `Σ.registry = Σ_init.registry` at every reachable state). No step in `K` writes it. Hence the question "may two agents writing the same registry interleave?" has *no realization*: registry writes are confined to the single, pre-execution construction of `Σ_init` (`R-VAL`), which is not a step of `𝔼`. Runtime registry access is pure read of an immutable structure — always consistent, never torn, by definition. The write-write race is *vacuously absent*.

W6 closes the "writing the same registry" horn of the question outright: there is no concurrent registry writing because there is no runtime registry writing. Registration is a single-authority construction act, and its immutability is load-bearing for the entire shape/type framework above it.

Collecting the partition: monotonicity (W0), cross-home uniqueness (W1), the active/audit relationship (W5), registry stability (W6), and the structural canonicity of every state (A6) are *kept by the model itself* — with the one boundary A6 already drew. A6's package is canonicity *on `𝔼`*, so it *contains* the chain-contiguity members `ChainMembershipForOrigin`/`L-ContiguousPrefix`; those members hold at every state of `𝔼` but are *not* model-intrinsic in this section's sense — they are exactly W3's serialization-borne contiguity. Every *other* conjunct of A6 is kept by A0 alone. Same-home uniqueness (W2) and dense chain contiguity (W3) are *kept only by per-home serialization*. Nothing on the list requires *global* serialization — which is precisely G1's conclusion, now seen invariant by invariant.

## 6. The contiguous run

A higher layer may ask for something the bare frontier discipline does not give: that a definition's content occupy *one contiguous run* of addresses, even as other agents allocate. We must locate this requirement exactly, because it is stronger than W3 and weaker than a global lock.

Be careful to separate two contiguities. *Chain* contiguity (W3) says `P_S(d, ·)` is a gapless prefix of `A_S(d)` — every frontier slot, whoever filled it, is occupied. It holds under per-home serialization regardless of *which agent owns which slot*. *Run* contiguity is the stronger property a definition needs: that *this definition's* atoms occupy a *consecutive* block of slots, with no foreign atom wedged among them. Chain contiguity is about the chain having no holes; run contiguity is about *one author's* atoms forming an unbroken interval *within* the chain.

> **W4 (RunContiguityCritical).** A multi-atom content run for a definition in home `d` (a batch of `K.α` steps into `(d, s_C)`) occupies a contiguous block of `A_{s_C}(d)` iff no foreign allocation into `(d, s_C)` is interleaved between the run's first and last step. This is a *per-`(home, subspace)` critical section spanning the run* — strictly stronger than the per-step per-home serialization of W2/W3, strictly weaker than any cross-home exclusion.
>
> *Proof.* By H0 each `s_C`-allocation to `d` lands at the then-current frontier and advances it by one. If the run's `m` steps are the only `s_C`-allocations to `d` in the index interval they span, they land at consecutive slots `φ, φ+1, …, φ+m−1`: a contiguous block. If a foreign `s_C`-allocation to `d` lands at some interior index, it consumes a frontier slot between two of the run's atoms, and the run's remaining atoms land *past* it — the run's slots are `{φ, …}` with a foreign slot embedded, no longer an interval. By H1, a foreign allocation into any *other* `(d', S')` leaves `φ_{s_C}(d, ·)` fixed and cannot break the run. ∎

So the run's *contiguity* requirement is sharply scoped: hold a critical section on `(d, s_C)` for the run's duration; let every other home and the link subspace proceed freely. This is the one place the corpus genuinely needs *batch* contiguity (A5's general denial notwithstanding), and W4 says exactly how much exclusion buys it — no more than a single sub-allocator, for the length of one definition. Contiguity is all W4 buys: it keeps the run an unbroken interval of `A_{s_C}(d)`, but it does *not* make the run appear atomic *to a reader*, who may still land at an interior index and read a strict prefix (A5). Read-atomicity for the run is the strictly stronger, separately-deferred property of Open Question 5; W4 is its writer-side half, not the whole.

One implementation caveat earns a mention because it is a real divergence between the abstract model and Gregory's code, and an implementer must know which they are honouring. In the *abstract* model, content and links inhabit disjoint subspaces `s_C` and `s_L` with separate frontiers (`DisjointSubAllocatorChains`), so a link allocation to `d` *cannot* break a content run's contiguity — it advances `φ_{s_L}(d)`, not `φ_{s_C}(d)`. In Gregory's *implementation*, link orgls and text atoms share one granfilade allocation, so a `CREATELINK` interleaved into a text run *does* break text contiguity (his "insert-link-insert I-address gap"). The abstract claim W4 is the cleaner one and is the contract we specify: content run contiguity is threatened *only* by a concurrent content allocation to the same home. An implementation that, like Gregory's, fuses the subspaces inherits a coarser exclusion obligation; an implementation faithful to the subspace partition does not.

## 7. A worked allocation scenario

The analysis of §4 and §6 runs entirely on a generic `(d, S)`. It is worth grounding it once in explicit addresses — both to make the conflict structure concrete and to exhibit the exact collision a missing serialization produces.

Fix a node `[1]`, an account `[1.0.1]` under it, and two documents under that account:

`d = [1.0.1.0.1]`,  `d' = [1.0.1.0.2]`  — each with `zeros(·) = 2`, hence valid document homes.

By `FirstEmission` (ASN-0093) the content sub-allocator `A_{s_C}(d)` has anchor `b_C(d) = [d.0.s_C] = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1]` (with `s_C = 1`) and enumerates the content slots

`a_k := [d.0.s_C.k] = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.k]`,  `k = 1, 2, 3, …`,

each successor `a_{k+1} = inc(a_k, 0)` advancing the final digit. Write `a'_k := [d'.0.s_C.k] = [1.0.1.0.2.0.1.k]` for `d'`'s content chain. Every `a_k` has `zeros(a_k) = 3` and `origin(a_k) = d`, as a content address must.

*Same-home collision (H2, interior).* Suppose `φ_{s_C}(d, Σ) = 3` — slots `a_1, a_2, a_3` are filled — and two writers `w₁, w₂` both propose a content allocation into `d` against this *same* `Σ`. By `SubsequentEmissionFreshness` each reads `a_prev = max P_{s_C}(d, Σ) = a_3` and computes its deposit as `inc(a_3, 0) = a_4 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.4]`. *Both compute the identical address `a_4`.* Whichever commits first deposits `a_4`; the second now finds `a_4 ∈ dom(C)`, so its freshness precondition `a_4 ∉ dom_{s_C}` fails (H2). Absent a discipline forcing the two to read distinct frontiers, they collide on one address.

*Same-home collision (H2, first-emission boundary).* The same collision occurs at `φ_{s_C}(d, Σ) = 0`. With `P_{s_C}(d, Σ) = ∅`, `max ∅` is undefined and the subsequent rule does not apply; instead both writers fire the first-emission predicate `{a' : origin(a') = d} = ∅` and both compute the determinate first slot `a_1 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.1]` (`FirstEmission`). They collide on `a_1` exactly as the interior case collides on `a_4`, now against `FirstEmissionFreshness`.

*Cross-home commutation (H1).* Now let `w₁` allocate into `d` and `w₂` into `d'`, against the same `Σ` with `φ_{s_C}(d, Σ) = 3` and `φ_{s_C}(d', Σ) = 0`. Then `w₁` lands `a_4 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.4]` (with `origin(a_4) = d`) and `w₂` lands `a'_1 = [1.0.1.0.2.0.1.1]` (with `origin(a'_1) = d'`). Because `d ≠ d'`, the two deposits carry distinct origins and so are distinct — the general argument of H1, which holds whether or not the homes are prefix-comparable. Here the homes happen to be *sibling*, so the anchors `b_C(d) = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1]` and `b_C(d') = [1.0.1.0.2.0.1]` also visibly differ at the document digit; but had we chosen the *nesting* pair `d' = [1.0.1.0.1.1]` the document digits would not diverge (`d ≼ d'`), so only the *document-digit-divergence* reading of the anchor argument would fail. Two routes still settle distinctness for that pair. The anchor-*separator* argument survives: `b_C(d) = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1]` carries the field-separator `0` at index `#d + 1 = 6` — the position immediately after `d`'s last component, the subspace digit `s_C` following at `#d + 2 = 7` per ASN-0093's `DisjointSubAllocatorChains` — whereas `b_C(d') = [1.0.1.0.1.1.0.1]` continues at that position (index 6) with the nonzero digit `1` (a zero would force `zeros(d') > 2`); the two anchors *agree* through index `#d = 5` and first diverge at index `#d + 1`, so they stay prefix-incomparable — `CrossDocumentDisjointness` applies to the nesting pair exactly as to the sibling one. And the origin argument settles it too. H1 is nonetheless *stated* by origin not because nesting demands it — both routes cover nesting — but because origin is the uniformly general route: it alone covers the cross-document, cross-subspace pair (`d ≠ d' ∧ S ≠ S'`, e.g. `A_C(d)` vs `A_L(d')`) that `CrossDocumentDisjointness`'s single-`·` statement (`p_i := b_·(d_i)`) leaves unnamed. By H0 neither writer's frontier read is disturbed by the other. Applying the two in either order — `w₁` then `w₂`, or `w₂` then `w₁` — reaches the identical final state: the steps commute (H1). No coordination between the two writers is needed or useful.

*Run fragmentation (W4).* Finally let one author issue a 3-atom content run into `(d, s_C)` from frontier `φ_{s_C}(d) = 3`, intending the contiguous block `a_4, a_5, a_6`. If the run's three `K.α` steps are the only `s_C`-allocations to `d` in the index interval they span, they land exactly at `a_4, a_5, a_6` — one interval. But suppose a foreign `K.α` into `(d, s_C)` interleaves after the run's first atom: the run deposits `a_4`, the foreign step takes the next frontier slot `a_5 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.5]`, and the run's remaining two atoms land *past* it at `a_6, a_7`. The author's run now occupies `{a_4, a_6, a_7}` — fragmented, the foreign `a_5` wedged inside (W4). A foreign allocation into the *link* subspace `(d, s_L)`, or into any *other* home, advances a different frontier (H1) and cannot fragment the run — which is exactly the scope W4 claims for the critical section: `(d, s_C)`, and nothing wider.

These four vignettes are the whole conflict theory in miniature: collisions live at a single `(home, subspace)` (the `a_4`/`a_1` clashes), everything cross-home commutes (the `a_4` vs `a'_1` independence), and a multi-atom run needs exclusion on its own `(home, subspace)` for its duration and on nothing else (the `a_5` intrusion). Under MIC clause 2 the collisions vanish: serializing the two `d`-writers makes `w₁` read frontier 3 and deposit `a_4`, then `w₂` read frontier 4 and deposit `a_5` — distinct, fresh, and gapless (M1(c)).

## 8. Quiescence as a snapshot predicate

We come to the sharpest of the questions, and the one Nelson answered most directly: what does a quiescence verdict mean if it is read while a write is in flight? A higher layer wants quiescence — "is everyone done editing?" — recognizable from inside the predicate language even as writers remain active. We must say when such a verdict is *sound* and when it is a *race*.

A quiescence predicate `Q` is a predicate over states; a verdict is its truth value at the index its `Observe` reads. Two things must be settled. The *referent*: against what state is the verdict taken? And — forced on us by the operation surface — the *read count*: how many `Observe` calls a verdict costs, because the substrate exposes no whole-state read, only the per-type `Observe_K` (ASN-0086; ASN-0128 D1–D4 / BH1–BH4). A predicate confined to one type is one `Observe_K`; a predicate over several types or homes — the realistic quiescence condition, and ASN-0128's `targets_keyed` already *joins across every Binary type* — is several. We must therefore be explicit about how a multi-read `Q` is *computed* from the surface the substrate actually exposes, because the soundness argument turns on it. With no whole-state read available, a `p`-read verdict is realized as

`Q = g(c₁(Σ_{r₁}), …, c_p(Σ_{r_p}))`

for a combining function `g` over the `p` *bounded-access constituents* `c₁, …, c_p`, each `c_i` a single-state read taken at its own read index `r_i`, the indices non-decreasing (`r₁ ≤ ⋯ ≤ r_p`, since zero-step reads do not advance `𝔼`). The constituents are deliberately *not* uniformly `Observe_K`-grade, and that generality is forced by `stale`: a cross-type join realizes each `c_i` as one type's active-view read `Observe_{K_i}(oper)`, but a `stale` enumeration realizes its constituents as a *single* active-view read `Observe_K(oper)` together with *one per-home frontier descent* `f_{d_i}` per distinct member home — and a frontier descent is exactly the bounded access A1 places *outside* the `Observe_K`-grade camp, reading a home's cross-type link frontier rather than any one type's active view. So `c_i` ranges over both kinds — active-view reads and frontier descents alike — and the soundness argument below must be stated over the `c_i`, not over `Observe_{K_i}`, on pain of leaving the frontier constituents ungoverned. `g` is left *arbitrary*: a conjunction `⋀_i Q_{K_i}` is one special case, but a verdict that joins across types (a `targets_keyed`-style cross-type condition) or recovers a per-home quantity from a frontier constituent (`stale`'s per-member `age(a) = f_d^Σ − 1 − j` at `d = home(a)`) is an equally admissible `g`, and we do *not* assume `Q` decomposes as a conjunction. Define a writer step to be **`Q`-affecting**, relative to a read window, when it changes the value of some constituent `c_i` whose read has not yet been taken — i.e. `c_i` evaluated just before the step differs from `c_i` just after, for some such `i`. The quantifier ranges over *every* constituent, frontier descents included: a step that advances a not-yet-read frontier `f_{d_i}` *is* `Q`-affecting even when it disturbs no active view — exactly what a `K' ≠ K` link emit into member home `d_i` does, advancing `d_i`'s cross-type link frontier (BH4) while leaving every `A_K` fixed. Were `Q`-affecting scoped to the `Observe_{K_i}` constituents alone, that frontier-advancing step would be wrongly certified harmless, and a `stale` verdict that banked its active-view read against it would then compute each member's `age` against a *moved* frontier — unsound about the first read's state; scoping the definition to all of `c₁, …, c_p` is what forecloses that. A step that alters no not-yet-read constituent `c_i` in the window leaves every still-pending argument of `g` untouched, hence leaves the verdict `g(…)` untouched — whatever `g` is. The single-read and multi-read cases diverge on exactly the soundness premise, so we take them in turn.

> **V0 (VerdictSnapshotLocal).** A verdict obtained by a *single bounded access* — a single `Observe_K` (a predicate on one type's active view), or equally an `age` read of one home's single-state frontier `f_d` (A1) — is `Q(Σ_r)` for the one index `r` that access reads (A3): it is a sound statement *about the one state* `Σ_r`, and about no `Σ_{r'}` with `r' ≠ r`. The discriminator is *access count*, not type-confinement: what makes the verdict snapshot-local is that its lone access lands on one index, whether that access reads a type's active view or a home's cross-type frontier (A1). Because every state on `𝔼` is structurally canonical (A6), `Σ_r` is always a coherent referent — there is always a well-defined "single frozen view" to evaluate `Q` against.

V0 is Nelson's answer made precise for the *single-read* verdict, and his framing is the right intuition: the verdict must be "taken against one version of the store at one instant — one cross-section of the space-time vortex." The reason a one-index verdict is *trustworthy* and a many-index verdict is *accidental* is exactly his: if you evaluate different constituent reads of `Q` across an intervening edit — one `Observe_{K_i}` against `Σ_{r₁}` *before* writer A's edit, another `Observe_{K_j}` against a later `Σ_{r₂}` *after* writer B's edit — you have "quantified over a collection of states that never coexisted," feeding `g` arguments that held at no common index, and may report "everyone done" for a configuration that held at *no single instant*. A condition that *secures* soundness is therefore stark and simple: **let all of the verdict's reads occur at one index** — the verdict is then literally `Q(Σ_r)`. This is not the *weakest* such condition (V2 sharpens it: soundness in fact needs only that no *`Q`-affecting* step fall between the reads), but one index is the stronger, conveniently constructible condition that MIC clause 7 adopts. For a single `Observe_K` this is free — one read, hence one index — and A6 guarantees that snapshot is always a real, canonical state, A3 that it is read whole, A0 that a concurrent write cannot smear it. So a single-read verdict taken "while a write is in flight" is *never corrupted by the write*: the in-flight write linearizes at some index `j`, and relative to `Σ_r` it is either already counted (`j < r`, its effect is in the snapshot — it is *committed*, not "in flight," from the snapshot's vantage) or not yet counted (`j ≥ r`, its effect is absent). The atomicity of the step (A0) forbids any "half-counted" writer. This is what it means for the verdict to be a snapshot rather than a race: it is the truth about the prefix of the order up to `r`.

The multi-read verdict keeps that *same* premise — all reads at one index — but no longer gets it for free, and here the per-type surface forces a seam into the open, mirroring §4's step/operation seam. A multi-read `Q` is realized as `p` bounded-access constituent reads `c₁, …, c_p` combined by `g` (above) — for a cross-type join, `p` active-view reads `Observe_{K₁}, …, Observe_{K_p}`; for a `stale` enumeration, one active-view read `Observe_K(oper)` plus one frontier descent `f_{d_i}` per member home — each read zero-step (A1). Zero-step reads do not advance `𝔼` — the index moves only when a *writer* step linearizes — so the `p` reads observe one state iff no foreign step at all interleaves among them, and possibly several — between 2 and `p` — distinct states otherwise (one interleaved foreign step splits the reads across exactly two states, not `p`; the count of distinct observed states climbs toward `p` only as further foreign steps fall between consecutive reads). But "all reads at one index" and "the verdict is sound" are not the same condition, and we must not run them together. Soundness asks less — a weaker condition suffices: that no *`Q`-affecting* step — one that changes the value of some not-yet-read constituent `c_i` (above) — linearizes between the first and last read. A foreign writer step that is *not* `Q`-affecting advances the index, scattering the reads across distinct `Σ_{r₁}, …, Σ_{r_p}`, yet cannot corrupt the verdict — and the reason is the one the definition makes exact, *not* that the step "leaves every constituent reading identically." It need not: a non-`Q`-affecting step is free to change an *already-read* constituent (the trace below exhibits exactly this — a `K₁`-emit that flips `A_{K₁}` *after* `K₁` has been read), merely *banking* the value already captured. What it cannot touch is a *not-yet-read* constituent, by the very definition of `Q`-affecting. Run that out per constituent against the first read's index `r₁`: for each `i`, `c_i` is not-yet-read throughout `[r₁, r_i)`, so any step in that interval that changed `c_i` would *be* `Q`-affecting; since none is, `c_i` is constant on `[r₁, r_i]` and the captured value is `v_i = c_i(Σ_{r_i}) = c_i(Σ_{r₁})` (changes to `c_i` *after* `r_i` are immaterial — `v_i` is banked). This holds for every `i` — for a `stale` enumeration's frontier constituents `c_i = f_{d_i}` no less than for active-view constituents — so `g(v₁, …, v_p) = g(c₁(Σ_{r₁}), …, c_p(Σ_{r₁})) = Q(Σ_{r₁})`: the verdict is sound *about the first read's state* `Σ_{r₁}`, the one index every banked value traces back to. Only when a `Q`-affecting step strides between the reads does the verdict commit the "states that never coexisted" error, now inside the recognizer's *own* evaluation: some `c_i` taken against `Σ_{r_i}` *before* the affecting step and some `c_j` against a later `Σ_{r_j}` *after* it, so `g` is applied to a tuple of constituent views that held at no single index. So a sound multi-read verdict is, for `p > 1`, not a primitive the surface supplies but a *construction obligation* with a determinate shape:

> **V2 (VerdictReaderSnapshot).** For a multi-read verdict — `p ≥ 2` constituent bounded-access reads composing one `Q`, whether `Observe_K` calls across types or the active-view read and per-home frontier descents of a `stale` enumeration (A1) — three conditions stand in a chain of *strict* implications, not in equivalence:
>
> `[all p reads at one committed index Σ_r] ⟹ [no Q-affecting step linearizes between the first and last read] ⟹ [the verdict is sound about a single state].`
>
> The middle condition — *no `Q`-affecting step between the reads* — is the weakest *sufficient* condition this note establishes, **not** a necessary one: the implication to soundness is genuinely *strict*, since a verdict may come out sound by other routes — `g` insensitive to the disturbed constituent, or `g`'s value coinciding with `Q` of some single state — even when a `Q`-affecting step does fall between the reads (the banking argument above proves only the sufficient direction, no `Q`-affecting step ⟹ verdict `= Q(Σ_{r₁})`, and offers nothing converse). A short witness exhibits that converse-failure as concretely as §8's trace exhibits the *first* implication's, lest the strictness rest on a bare reason: take the *short-circuit* combiner `g(v₁, v₂) = (v₁ ≠ ∅) ∨ (v₂ = ∅)` over two active-view reads, against a start state with `A_{K₁} = {T₁} ≠ ∅`. Read `v₁ = Observe_{K₁}(Σ_{r₁}) = {T₁}` at `r₁`: the first disjunct fires, so `g` short-circuits to `⊤` whatever `v₂` holds, and `Q(Σ_{r₁}) = g({T₁}, ∅) = ⊤`. Now let a writer emit a `K₂`-tuple between the reads, flipping the *not-yet-read* `K₂` constituent `A_{K₂}` from `∅` to `{T₂}` — by definition a *`Q`-affecting* step (it moves the not-yet-read constituent `c₂ = Observe_{K₂}`), so the middle condition **fails**. Yet read 2 returns `v₂ = {T₂}` and the verdict is `g({T₁}, {T₂}) = ⊤ ∨ ⊥ = ⊤ = Q(Σ_{r₁})`: *sound about `Σ_{r₁}`* despite the intervening `Q`-affecting step, because `g`'s short-circuit discards the very constituent the step disturbed. Soundness holds where the middle condition does not — the second implication's converse fails exactly as the first's does. As a sufficient condition it tolerates any number of non-`Q`-affecting writer steps interleaving (each moves the index and may even change an *already-read* constituent, but disturbs no *not-yet-read* one — so every banked `v_i` still equals `c_i(Σ_{r₁})` and `g`'s value is unchanged). The first — *all reads at one index* — is *strictly* stronger: a non-`Q`-affecting step falsifies it while preserving soundness. We single it out not because soundness needs it but because a reader-side critical section conveniently *supplies* it, and MIC clause 7 *adopts* it for safety — pinning all `p` reads to one index discharges the soundness requirement a fortiori, sparing the recognizer from having to classify which steps are `Q`-affecting. This is a *reader-side* obligation, dual to W4's writer-side run contiguity *in role but not in scope*. W4 holds a *single* `(d, s_C)` against foreign *writer* steps for a run's duration so the run stays one interval — a *local* exclusion, strictly weaker than any cross-home one (§6); V2's one-index construction holds the read sequence against *every* interleaving writer step for the verdict's duration — and that is *global*, because any writer step at any home advances the index off which the `p` reads must all agree. The roles are dual (writer-side vs reader-side); the scopes are not (local vs global). Even V2's *weaker* sufficient condition — no `Q`-affecting step between the reads — is not per-home: a `Q`-affecting step is any that moves a *not-yet-read* constituent `c_i`, and which homes it must exclude depends on the constituent's kind. For a cross-type join the constituents are active-view reads `Observe_{K_i}`, and a type-`K_i` tuple may be homed anywhere, so the excluded steps are *type-scoped* — ranging across all homes carrying `Q`'s types. For a `stale` enumeration the constituents additionally include the frontier descents `f_{d_i}`, and a frontier `f_{d_i}` is moved by *any* link emit into member home `d_i` whatever its type, so the excluded steps are there *home-scoped* — every emit into a member home, not just the type-`K` ones. Either way the exclusion ranges wider than a single `(d, S)`, so neither the minimal condition nor the one-index construction clause 7 adopts is per-home; the per-home liberation of §4–§6 is a *writer-side* result that does not extend to reader-side multi-read isolation. The one-index construction is *constructible* (hold the exclusion, take the `p` reads, release: they then all read one `Σ_r`) but *not automatic* from the per-type `Observe_K`, which pins each call to its own index and no further. The substrate neither provides nor forbids it.

V2 is the reader-side counterpart of §4–§6 — a counterpart in *role*, not in *scope*. Per-home serialization (MIC clause 2) and the run critical section (clause 5) discipline *writers*, and do so *locally*, per `(d, S)`, leaving every other home free — the whole content of §4–§6's "per-home, not global"; V2 disciplines a *reader*, and clause 7's one-index construction does so *globally*, excluding every interleaving writer for the verdict's duration. The two are different in kind — one governs who may deposit and where, the other what a single logical read may span and against whom — which is why the contract must name both, why the per-*call* snapshot guarantee (MIC clause 4, A3, free) cannot stand in for the per-*verdict* one (clause 7, V2, constructed), and why the "per-home" promise of this note is a *writer-side* promise that does not carry over to the reader's multi-read verdict. A single-bounded-access verdict — a single `Observe_K`, or an `age` read (A1) — needs only clause 4; the multi-read verdict a real recognizer needs — a cross-type join, or any `stale` enumeration — is sound only under clause 7.

It is worth grounding V2 once in an explicit trace, in §7's style, because the error it warns of is the note's subtlest postcondition and "states that never coexisted" is precisely the claim a concrete trace makes checkable. Take the two-type quiescence predicate `Q ≡ (A_{K₁} = ∅) ∧ (A_{K₂} = ∅)` — "no active tuple of either type" — realized as `g(v₁, v₂) = [v₁ = ∅ ∧ v₂ = ∅]` over the two reads `v_i = Observe_{K_i}(oper)`. Fix a start state `Σ_n` carrying exactly one active tuple: a `K₂`-tuple `T₂` at link address `a_{T₂} = [1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1]` — the first link slot (`s_L = 2`) of home `d = [1.0.1.0.1]` — with `A_{K₁}^{Σ_n} = ∅`. A recognizer that lets its two reads *drift* across indices realizes the verdict so:

- *Read 1, at `r₁ = n`.* `v₁ = Observe_{K₁}(Σ_{r₁}) = ∅` — no active `K₁`-tuple. The `K₁` constituent is now banked.
- *Foreign step at `r₁`* (`Σ_{r₁} → Σ_{r₁+1}`). A writer emits a `K₁`-tuple `T₁` (`Emit_{K₁}` miss, one `K.λ_sh`), landing at the next link frontier slot `a_{T₁} = inc(a_{T₂}, 0) = [1.0.1.0.1.0.2.2]`. This flips `A_{K₁}` from `∅` to `{T₁}` — but `K₁` was *already read* at `r₁`, so the step is **not** `Q`-affecting: `g`'s first argument stays pinned to `∅`.
- *Foreign step at `r₁+1`* (`Σ_{r₁+1} → Σ_{r₁+2}`). A writer nullifies `T₂` (`Nullify_Binary(a_{T₂})` miss, one `K.λ_sh`), depositing a retraction emitter `b = [1.0.1.0.1.0.2.3]` whose unit-depth to-span covers `a_{T₂}`, so `a_{T₂} ∈ nullified` and `T₂` leaves `A_{K₂}`. `K₂` has *not yet been read*, so this is the lone **`Q`-affecting** step.
- *Read 2, at `r₂ = r₁+2`.* `v₂ = Observe_{K₂}(Σ_{r₂}) = ∅` — `T₂` is now nullified.

The verdict is `g(∅, ∅) = ⊤`: "quiescent." Yet `Q` holds at *no* state the trace touches. At `Σ_{r₁}`: `A_{K₂} = {T₂} ≠ ∅`. At `Σ_{r₁+1}`: `A_{K₁} = {T₁} ≠ ∅` *and* `A_{K₂} = {T₂} ≠ ∅`. At `Σ_{r₂}`: `A_{K₁} = {T₁} ≠ ∅`. The configuration `g` reports — both slices empty — coexisted at no single index: `v₁ = ∅` held at `r₁`, `v₂ = ∅` at `r₂`, never together. This is exactly the "states that never coexisted" error, now manufactured *inside the recognizer's own evaluation*, and V2's strict-implication chain pins its single cause: one `Q`-affecting step (the `T₂`-nullify) strode between the reads. The non-`Q`-affecting `K₁`-emit moved the index and changed a slice without harming the verdict — its constituent read was already banked — which is precisely the slack V2's middle condition tolerates and V2's first condition (all reads at one index) does not.

Now run the *same* trace under MIC clause 7. A reader-side critical section pins both reads to one committed index — `Σ_n` — admitting no writer step between them. Then `v₁ = Observe_{K₁}(Σ_n) = ∅` and `v₂ = Observe_{K₂}(Σ_n) = {T₂}`, so `g(∅, {T₂}) = ⊥`: "not quiescent." This is *sound* — it is the truth about `Σ_n`, where `A_{K₂} ≠ ∅` genuinely holds. The two writer steps are not lost; they linearize *after* `r₂` once the section releases, and the verdict, retrospective about `Σ_n` (V1), simply makes no claim about the later states they produce. The drifting reader turned a true "not quiescent" into a false "quiescent"; the pinned reader recovers the sound verdict — which is the whole content of clause 7.

But soundness — V0 for a single read, V2 for several — says nothing about whether the verdict *stays* true, and here we must be honest about what a quiescence verdict can and cannot promise:

> **V1 (VerdictRetrospective).** A verdict `Q(Σ_r)` is retrospective: it asserts `Q` of `Σ_r` and of nothing later. For `r' > r`, `Q(Σ_{r'})` may differ — any step at index `j` with `r ≤ j < r'` may falsify `Q`. Extending a verdict from "held at `r`" to "holds through `r'`" requires an *additional* hypothesis the substrate does not supply: that no `Q`-falsifying step is linearized in the interval `[r, r')`. That hypothesis is a constraint on writers' linearization points relative to the observer's read — a coordination-layer obligation (extinction discipline, fair scheduling, bounded work — the coordination layer's named hypotheses), not a substrate guarantee.

V1 is the precise statement of the relationship the question asks for — "what relationship must hold between the observer's read and the writers' linearization for the verdict to be sound rather than a race." There are two distinct properties, and conflating them is the error:

- *Soundness* of the verdict is *secured* by having the observer's reads all sit at one index `r` — automatic for a single bounded access — an `Observe_K`, or an `age` read (V0, A1) — constructed by a reader-side critical section for a multi-read verdict (V2). One index is sufficient, not strictly necessary: soundness survives any interleaving step that does not affect `Q` (V2), but one index is the condition a critical section supplies. Given it, the verdict is a true statement about `Σ_r`, full stop, regardless of how many writers are "active." Activity that has linearized by `r` is counted; activity that has not is not; nothing is half-counted.
- *Durability* of the verdict — its remaining true after `r` — needs that no writer linearizes a falsifying step after `r`. This is a relationship the substrate cannot certify, because it concerns the *future* of the order, and the substrate's order carries no promise that the future is empty. To read "the system is quiescent and will stay so" off `Q(Σ_r)` is to add V1's hypothesis silently — which is precisely the smuggling a quiescence layer must refuse.

The practical reading for a quiescence recognizer: a recognizer that evaluates its quiescence condition against one snapshot — a single bounded-access read (an `Observe_K`, or an `age` read), or a multi-read sequence (a cross-type join, or any `stale` enumeration) pinned to one index by clause 7 (V2) — produces a *sound* verdict about that snapshot, never mistaking a half-applied write for a whole one and never quantifying over a state that never existed. A recognizer that instead lets its several reads drift across indices forfeits exactly this, and the verdict it returns is the race V0/V2 warn of, not a snapshot. Whether that verdict licenses *terminating* the system is the separate, conditional question a termination layer answers with its named hypotheses about who may still fire. The substrate's contribution is exactly and only the snapshot: a single canonical state, read atomically, against which the predicate has a definite truth value. That is the "reached-and-held quiescence" referent; holding it is the layer's job.

## 9. The minimal isolation contract

We can now collect the discipline an implementation must honour. The contract is the conjunction of the clauses we have been forced to by the analysis; nothing in it is a mechanism (no lock, no transaction, no scheduler), only obligations that any mechanism must meet.

> **MIC (MinimalIsolationContract).** A faithful realization of the substrate's consistency model must honour:
>
> 1. **Per-step atomicity (A0).** Each atomic transition is applied indivisibly; no observer reads a state strictly between `Σ_i` and `Σ_{i+1}`.
> 2. **Per-home allocation serialization (H0, W2, W3).** For each `(home, subspace) (d, S)`, the frontier-read-and-deposit of any two `S`-allocations to `d` are mutually exclusive. Cross-`(d,S)` allocations need no coordination (H1).
> 3. **Commit-before-acknowledge (A7).** An operation's response is produced only at or after its linearization point.
> 4. **Per-call snapshot reads (A3, A1).** Each individual *single-bounded-access* read — an `Observe_K`, and equally BH4's `age`, the one read A1 places outside the `Observe_K`-grade camp yet shows recovers one home's single-state frontier `f_d` in *one* bounded access — is evaluated against a single committed state `Σ_k`, and never witnesses a partial step. The discriminator is *access count*, not `Observe_K`-grade-ness (A1): one bounded access lands on one index, whether it reads a type's active view or a home's cross-type link frontier. The *several*-access reads — cross-type joins and `stale` (whose global active-membership filter makes it a multi-read at every member-home count, single-home included) — are clause 7's, not this clause's. (The clause's generic title is deliberate: the body now matches it, so A1's "clause-4-sound" / "free under clause 4" attribution for `age` holds literally.)
> 5. **Per-run critical section for contiguous runs (W4).** A definition's multi-atom content run holds exclusion on its `(d, s_C)` for the run's duration; no foreign content allocation to `d` interleaves.
> 6. **Registry write confinement (W6).** Registry writes occur only in the construction of `Σ_init`; the runtime relation has no registry-write step.
> 7. **Per-verdict reader snapshot (V2).** A verdict composed of two or more bounded-access reads — several `Observe_K` calls, a cross-type join, or a `stale` enumeration (A1) — must have all its constituent reads pinned to one committed index — a reader-side critical section holding the read sequence against any interleaving writer step. This exclusion is *global*, not per-home: it is *role*-dual to clauses 2/5 (writer-side ↦ reader-side) but not *scope*-dual — any writer step at any home advances the index, so pinning `p` reads to one index excludes *all* writers for the read's duration, unlike the local per-`(d,S)` exclusions of clauses 2 and 5. (Even V2's weaker sufficient condition — no `Q`-affecting step between the reads — is not per-home: type-scoped for a cross-type join, and home-scoped over the member homes for a `stale` enumeration, whose frontier constituents any same-home link emit can move.) The clause is strictly stronger than soundness alone needs — no `Q`-affecting step between the reads already suffices (V2) — and is adopted because pinning to one index discharges soundness a fortiori, without the recognizer classifying which steps affect `Q`. A *single-bounded-access* verdict — a single `Observe_K`, or an `age` read (A1) — discharges this clause for free under clause 4.
> 8. **Per-coverage-class idem=⊤ de-duplication serialization (I1a, I4).** For each coverage class `[K]` of an `idem(K) = ⊤` type, the dedup-read of the *global* active subset `A_K` and the consequent deposit form **one atomic action**, pinned to the operation's linearization index — so an `Emit_K` that misses does so against its *own* deposit's pre-state, never a stale earlier state. This is a *per-coverage-class* serialization, and since a coverage class spans every home (the dedup-read of `A_K` is global, not per-home — ASN-0128 I1) it is **non-per-home**: strictly stronger on its own axis than clause 2's per-home exclusion, and orthogonal to it (clause 2 still governs the deposit's allocation freshness). It is the one *operation*-level guarantee that resists the per-home thesis — *role*-distinct from the allocation clauses 2/5 and the reader-side clause 7. An `idem = ⊥` emit (no dedup) and any allocation discharge it trivially. Drop it and per-home MIC permits the duplicate, *and permits it regardless of home*: two coverage-equal `idem = ⊤` emits may each read a stale `A_K` and both miss, then deposit at *distinct* addresses — cross-home at their two home frontiers (no allocation collision, H1), same-home at consecutive slots `φ, φ+1` that clause 2's own deposit-spacing supplies (clause 2 orders the two deposits but never the global dedup-read that precedes them) — leaving `A_K` with *two* coverage-equal active tuples. The resulting `→_sh` execution is valid yet *not* `K`-surface-emitted *in the operative sense* (each deposit a miss against the stale `Σ` but not against its own pre-state; M1(b) draws this out, against I1a's literal clause), so ASN-0128 I1a's single-survivor does not reach it. This completes §4 instance (i)'s "wrong axis entirely": per-home order does not merely fail to *choose* the survivor, it fails to *prevent the duplicate* — on either axis, since same-home it is clause 2 itself that spaces the duplicates apart.

Two clauses are conspicuous by what they *omit*. There is no global *allocation*-serialization clause — G1 proved per-home suffices for allocation; the two non-per-home clauses the contract does carry (7, reader-side verdict pinning; 8, idem=⊤ de-duplication) are reader- and operation-level, not allocation disciplines. And there is no registry-locking clause — W6 makes runtime registry writes nonexistent, so there is nothing to lock. There is, equally, no document-registration clause: §4 scoped `K.σ` out of the conflict analysis, so document-address freshness — that two agents do not propose the same fresh `d` — is an *assumed precondition* discharged by the excluded entity-allocation layer, not an obligation MIC undertakes; provisioning fresh homes is upstream of this contract. The contract is *nearly* minimal, and we say precisely how: seven of its eight clauses are load-bearing — removing any one admits a counterexample (drop 1 and reads tear; drop 2 and same-home allocations collide; drop 3 and callers act on phantom effects; drop 4 and a single bounded-access read (an `Observe_K` or an `age`) reads across a commit; drop 5 and definition runs fragment; drop 7 and a multi-read verdict quantifies over states that never coexisted; drop 8 and two coverage-equal `idem = ⊤` emits both miss and duplicate, regardless of home). Clause 6 is the lone exception, and is *not* load-bearing: it makes explicit a guarantee the model already enforces (`W6` — no runtime step writes the registry), so dropping it admits no counterexample. We keep it not for minimality but so the contract names every consistency obligation an implementer might fear they must discharge — including the one the substrate discharges for them.

The contract earns its name by the safety it implies:

> **M1 (SafetyUnderMIC).** Under MIC, (a) no reader observes a torn effect; (b) no *single operation's* effect is observed twice, and idempotent emission admits no duplicate *under clause 8* (per-home clauses 1–7 alone permit an `idem = ⊤` duplicate *regardless of home*, §4 instance (i)) — while non-idempotent emission admits content-equal tuples *by design*, which MIC neither prevents nor intends to; (c) no writer collides with a concurrent writer's *allocation* — a sub-allocator emission `K.α`/`K.λ_sh` (document registration `K.σ` is scoped out, §4); and (d) no multi-read verdict straddles a write.
>
> *Proof.* (a) *No torn effect.* Any single emission or nullification is one step; by clause 1 (A0) the reader's state is before or after it, never within (A4). A batch is not a single effect; its constituents are each whole, and clause 4 ensures each read is one canonical snapshot (A6) — fully structurally canonical, never corrupt (though, by §2, possibly mid-batch).
>
> (b) *No duplicated effect — scoped to what is actually proved.* Two facts hold, and a third must be stated plainly so the guarantee is not overclaimed. *(i) Per-operation uniqueness.* Each state-changing operation is realized at one unique index (A1), so *its* effect appears exactly once in the order; no single operation is double-applied. *(ii) Idempotent collapse — under clause 8, and only then.* Under `idem(K) = ⊤`, the active subset holds at most one tuple per coverage class at every state a `K`-surface-emitted derivation reaches (ASN-0128 I1a, ActiveIdemUniqueness). This is the conclusion (b) wants — but it must be drawn carefully, because I1a is scoped to `K`-surface-emitted derivations and *per-home MIC clauses 1–7 do not, by themselves, place an `idem = ⊤` execution — cross-home or same-home alike — in that scope.* I1a's single-survivor rests on each `Emit_K` deposit being a genuine *miss against its own pre-state* — the *operative* sense of "`K`-surface-emitted," and one stronger than I1a's *literal* definitional clause ("every `L_K`-growing step is the deposit branch of an `Emit_K` invocation"). That literal clause the both-miss derivation *satisfies* — X's deposit is the deposit branch of X's `Emit_K`, Y's of Y's — so a naive reading would classify it `K`-surface-emitted and (wrongly) conclude no duplicate; the two readings coincide only under ASN-0128's sequential operation→step identification, where an operation's dedup-read input state *is* its step's pre-state. The dedup-read here, however, ranges over the *global* `A_K` (ASN-0128 I1), and clause 4 pins that read only to *some* committed state, never to the deposit's own linearization index. So two coverage-equal emits may each read a stale `A_K`, both miss, and both deposit at *distinct* addresses — cross-home at distinct home frontiers (no allocation collision, H1), same-home at the consecutive slots `φ, φ+1` clause 2 spaces apart (it serializes the deposits, not the global dedup-read that precedes them, §4 instance (i)); the second deposit's own pre-state already carries the first's coverage-equal tuple, so it is *not* a miss-against-its-own-pre-state — I1a's induction breaks exactly there, on either axis — and the execution, `K`-surface-emitted under the literal clause but *not* in the operative sense (§4 instance (i) draws the inference in full), carries *two* coverage-equal active tuples. The no-duplicate guarantee therefore does **not** follow from per-home MIC; it follows from **clause 8**, whose per-coverage-class serialization makes every `idem = ⊤` dedup-read-and-deposit atomic at its linearization index — so each deposit is a miss against its own pre-state, the realized derivation *is* `K`-surface-emitted in the operative sense, I1a applies, and `A_K` carries at most one tuple per coverage class. Granted clause 8, the de-duplication *mechanism* must still be stated more carefully than "a repeat is a hit," because the dedup check consults the *active* subset, not the audit slice (ASN-0128 I2, AuditSliceNotConsulted). A second `Emit_K` with coverage-equal `(F, G)` is a zero-step hit returning the incumbent address (ASN-0128 I1) *only while that incumbent is active*; if the incumbent was nullified between the two emits it sits in `L_K` but not `A_K`, so the second emit is a *miss* depositing a fresh tuple at a new address — deliberate resurrection (ASN-0128 I2; ASN-0086 R6c, RestorationByReemission), not an `A_K` duplicate. Either way — and granted clause 8 — `A_K` holds one tuple of that coverage class, never two. That same clause is what reduces two *concurrent* `idem = ⊤` coverage-equal emissions into distinct homes to operation-level *non-confluence* rather than duplication: with the dedup-read-and-deposit serialized per coverage class, first-to-commit (ASN-0128 I4 — whose single-survivor invokes exactly such a serializing authority, "ordering the two calls before either becomes a step") fixes which home keeps the lone survivor — §4's active-membership toggle, instance (i) — an order-dependence in *which* address survives. *Absent clause 8 the dependence reaches `how many`:* the both-miss interleaving leaves two — same-home no less than cross-home, clause 2 spacing the two deposits where H1 separates them across homes. (The toggle's instance (ii), a `Nullify` of the active incumbent, is non-duplicating regardless — a single emit, not a racing pair, it leaves the coverage class with one active tuple or none, by order, never two.) *(iii) But `idem(K) = ⊥` duplicates by design, and MIC does not prevent it.* Two `Emit_K` calls with identical `(F, G, K)` under `idem(K) = ⊥` produce *distinct* addresses, and *both* tuples appear in the active subset (ASN-0128 I5; ASN-0086 R2's consequence) — a reader observing `A_K` then sees two tuples of identical content. This is intended: a fresh non-idempotent emission is how the layer expresses a genuinely new instance, and no clause of MIC forbids it. In particular, commit-before-acknowledge (clause 3 / A7) does *not* suppress it: A7 fixes only that a response is produced at-or-after `lin(op)`; it neither detects nor cancels a *lost* acknowledgment, so a client that times out and retries a non-idempotent emission commits a second tuple, and A7 is indifferent to the fact. What collapses semantic repeats is idempotency (ii), not acknowledgment ordering. MIC's (b) guarantee is therefore exactly (i)+(ii). The reference-sharing principle — one permanent address, never a copy (ASN-0086) — governs *transclusion of content*, not two distinct link tuples that happen to carry equal endsets, and must not be invoked to claim more.
>
> (c) *No allocation collision.* Two allocations — sub-allocator emissions `K.α`/`K.λ_sh` — conflict only when into the same `(d, S)` (H1, H2); clause 2 serializes exactly those, so each reads a distinct frontier (H0) and deposits a fresh, unique address (W2); clause 5 extends this to whole runs (W4). Cross-home writers, by H1, never collide and need no coordination. Document registration `K.σ` falls outside this clause (§4): two agents racing the *same* caller-supplied `d` is a genuine collision, but the substrate resolves it by rejecting the loser, and *preventing* it is the office of the assumed document-address freshness the excluded entity-allocation layer supplies — not of any MIC clause.
>
> (d) *No verdict straddles a write.* The case split is by *access count* (A1), and so framed it exhausts A1's classification with no single-index read left homeless. A *single-bounded-access* verdict — a single `Observe_K`, or an `age` read of one home's frontier `f_d` (A1) — reads one canonical state by clause 4 (A3, A6, V0), the clause now covering every one-access read rather than `Observe_K` alone. A multi-read verdict — `p ≥ 2` accesses: a cross-type join, or a `stale` enumeration (single- or multi-home) — under clause 7, has its `p` constituent reads pinned to one committed index `Σ_r` (V2), so it is a statement about one coexisting state and cannot manufacture, in the recognizer's own evaluation, the "states that never coexisted" error V0/V2 warn of for reads that drift across indices. This is *soundness* of the verdict about `Σ_r`; its *durability* past `r` remains V1's separate hypothesis, which no MIC clause supplies. ∎

We can phrase the allocation half as a weakest precondition, which is the most honest summary of clause 2. For an emission into `(d, S)` with the postcondition `R ≡` "the deposited address is fresh and unique and the `S`-prefix of `d` remains a gapless interval,"

`wp(emit into (d,S), R)  ≡  (no other emission into (d,S) is realized between this emission's frontier-read and its deposit).`

In the totally-ordered model the right-hand side is *automatically true* — there is no "between" two atomic steps to host an interloper. The content of MIC clause 2 is precisely that an implementation, which does not get the total order for free, must *establish* that right-hand side per home. And `wp` makes the scope unmistakable: the precondition quantifies over emissions into *this* `(d, S)` only. It says nothing about other homes — because, by H1, there is nothing to say. The reader who wants the one-sentence form of this entire note can take it from the `wp`: **the only thing a concurrent writer must wait for is another writer reaching into the very same sub-allocator; everything else is free.** That is Nelson's owned-numbers tree and Gregory's run-to-completion loop, reconciled — the loop is one way to guarantee the `wp`, and a coarse one; per-home exclusion is the least that suffices.

## What this note does not cover

- **The scheduler and its fairness.** *Which* agent's proposal is placed at *which* index — the placement that turns a crowd of proposals into the order `𝔼` — is the implementer's and protocol layer's concern. A termination layer may name fairness as a hypothesis; we name it only as the source of `𝔼` and reason about `𝔼` itself, not its construction. No fairness, starvation-freedom, or priority property is asserted here.
- **Agent activation and rule bodies.** What makes an agent fire, and what its firing computes, are opaque — a rule-governance layer leaves rule bodies opaque. We treat a fire only as a batch of operations with linearization points; its internals are not modelled.
- **Inter-server replication / BEBE.** The substrate here is one store. Nelson left the back-end-to-back-end protocol undefined in *Literary Machines* 87.1 ("computer networks are always broken"; each server "at all times unified and operational" but no global agreement). A multi-server consistency model — how per-home orders compose across servers — is a separate note. Our G1 is the natural seam for it (per-home independence is what would let homes live on different servers), but we do not develop it.
- **The concrete concurrency-control mechanism.** MIC is a contract, not an implementation. Whether clause 2 is met by a per-home mutex, an optimistic frontier-CAS with retry, a single global loop (Gregory's choice, which over-satisfies it), or an actor-per-home is unspecified. So is the performance of any of these.
- **Predicate evaluation cost.** The cost of evaluating a verdict's predicate — a predicate-evaluation layer's territory — is not bounded here; we constrain only *when* (one snapshot) it is evaluated, not *how expensively*.

## Open Questions

What is the weakest exclusion primitive that realizes MIC clause 2 — does an optimistic frontier read-and-retry satisfy it without any held lock, and under what condition does retry terminate?

What is the weakest exclusion primitive that realizes MIC clause 7 — can a multi-read verdict be pinned to one committed index by an optimistic read-then-revalidate scheme rather than a held reader-side lock, given the substrate exposes no global version coordinate a reader could cheaply consult?

What is the weakest primitive that realizes MIC clause 8 — can per-coverage-class de-duplication serialization for `idem=⊤` emits be achieved without a global lock, e.g. by a coverage-class-keyed optimistic check-and-deposit with retry, given the dedup-read of `A_K` spans every home and the substrate exposes no per-coverage-class version coordinate?

What must an implementation guarantee so that a reader landing inside a split batch is not misled into treating a mid-batch state as settled — since canonicity (A6) cannot itself distinguish the two, must every batch needing all-or-nothing visibility run inside a critical section, or can it instead publish a snapshot-readable completion marker?

When a layer genuinely needs batch *atomicity* — a definition's content run seen whole, a retraction set applied all-or-nothing — what is the minimal additional contract that makes a multi-step batch appear atomic *to a reader*, closing the interior-prefix gap A5 leaves open even for a W4-contiguous run, without reintroducing global serialization?

What relationship between an observer's read index and writers' linearization points must a coordination layer establish to promote a sound quiescence verdict into a durable one, and is that relationship expressible as a substrate-checkable predicate rather than an external assumption?

Does the equivalence of per-home and global serialization (G1) survive across servers, so that homes resident on different servers may progress with no inter-server coordination, and what is the weakest cross-server contract that preserves cross-home uniqueness when home ownership can migrate?

Under what conditions may two allocations the model treats as same-home conflicts be proven independent after all — for instance, if a home's sub-allocator is statically partitioned among agents — thereby weakening MIC clause 2 below per-home exclusion?

What is the soundness obligation on an out-of-order retraction whose target has not yet been emitted at its linearization state — is silent rejection the right semantics, or must the substrate expose the rejection so a coordination layer can re-order rather than lose the intent?
