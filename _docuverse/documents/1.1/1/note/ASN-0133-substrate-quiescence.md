# ASN-0133: Substrate Quiescence

*The terminal state, recognizable from inside the predicate language; termination as a conditional theorem with every hypothesis named.*

ASN-0129 supplies the language a coordination system's conditions are written in, and the dynamics classes (PD0–PD2, ST/SF) that say how a condition's truth moves. ASN-0130 makes the conditions substrate artifacts. This note closes the arc: it defines what it is for such a system to be *done* — quiescence — and proves what can and cannot be guaranteed about reaching it. The shape of the answer is fixed by a fact about forward-chaining systems generally: unconditional termination is not provable; every honest treatment names conditions (extinction discipline, bounded work, fair scheduling) under which termination follows. The note's discipline is to put each hypothesis where it belongs — what the substrate guarantees unconditionally (recognizability, absorption), what a rule author can make checkable at registration (extinction, via the spelling classes), and what remains assumption (fairness, bounded work) stated as named hypotheses rather than smuggled.

**Depends:** ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework), ASN-0128 (Substrate Type Operational Semantics), ASN-0129 (Substrate Predicate Composition), ASN-0130 (Predicate Definitions as Substrate Content).

## What this note commits

- **The rule model** (RG): a minimal trigger/emission unit — a domain in QD, a Boolean PL trigger, an emission contract over the operation surface — with the action body deliberately opaque: the contract constrains outputs, never algorithms. A registry is a finite set of rules; a rule's trigger may be a registered definition address (ASN-0130), making registries themselves substrate-described.
- **Recognizability and absorption, unconditional** (Q0, Q1): quiescence — no trigger true on any domain element — is decidable at every reachable state by any observer (a single PL term for a single-view registry, a finite conjunction of PL verdicts otherwise), pure, and a fixed point of firing once reached.
- **Extinction discipline** (X-DEF, Q2–Q4) with the class-theoretic upgrade (Q-EXT): a rule whose trigger is an **SF spelling** (ASN-0129) and whose fires falsify it gets *at-most-once firing per argument* as a theorem — the falsification is permanent by the class, not by analysis of other rules. This is the Marker pattern's termination half, and the certificate that makes it registration-checkable is exactly the SF certificate ASN-0130's Open Question 4 anticipates.
- **Conditional termination** (Q5, Q5a, Q6): the termination theorem turns on a single operative hypothesis, *finitely many real fires from Σ₀* (H-RF), supplied two ways — by the bounded-work hypothesis (H-W) for any registry (Q5), or by bounded domain growth alone for an all-SF registry (Q5a). Extinction discipline is not a hypothesis of Q5's bound; it is the second, cheaper route to H-RF (Q5a), trading the cross-rule re-arm analysis for a domain-growth bound. Quiescence is then reached in finitely many steps under H-RF and the fairness hypothesis (H-FAIR). The hypotheses are named, stated, and not discharged here: H-W is provably *meta-level* (its statement quantifies over reachability, which PL deliberately cannot express); bounded domain growth is likewise reachability-quantified (a bound over all reachable states), so of Q5a's route only the SF half — at-most-once firing per argument — is registration-checkable, not the domain bound; and H-FAIR is a scheduler property this corpus has chosen not to axiomatize beyond the statement.
- **Scope parameterization** (Q7–Q9): everything above relativizes to a scope — any Boolean PL predicate — with scope quiescence carrying Q0's status (a single PL term for single-view registries), recognizability and absorption inherited per-scope, and anti-monotone nesting (outer quiescence implies inner, never conversely).
- **The falsifier accounting** (Q-FLIP): what can re-arm a trigger is not "retraction" alone but a wider falsifier inventory (ASN-0129's FP, read with PD1/PD2) — retraction, BH1-type emissions under the default view, BH4's home-traffic footprint, *and bare deposits growing an active slice* (which flip `∃`-shaped or non-monotone-verdict triggers, PD1/PD2). The folklore "no retraction ⟹ triggers flip at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery, and this note replaces it with the class-checked accounting.

## The rule model

**RG (Rule, Registry).** A *rule* is a triple `ρ = (D_ρ, T_ρ, Post_ρ)`: a domain expression `D_ρ ∈ QD`, a Boolean trigger `T_ρ : D_ρ → Bool` in PL, and an *emission contract* `Post_ρ` — a *meta-level* contract over (argument, state, emitted call set) constraining what any fire of ρ must emit, the calls drawn from the operation surface `{Emit_K, Nullify_Binary}` (ASN-0128; `Observe_K` reads, and reads are not emissions). The contract is meta-level, *not* a PL term: PL has no sort for emitted call sets (COD lists none, ASN-0129) and a PL term reads a single state, whereas the contract speaks of what a fire *emits* — so `Post_ρ` is written in the surface's emission forms, never evaluated as a PL verdict. What *is* PL is the trigger `T_ρ`, whose pre- and post-state evaluations decide extinction (Q2); the contract enters this note only through registration-time implications relating those PL verdicts to the emission forms (Q3). A *registry* `R` is a finite set of rules. A *fire* of `(ρ, x)` at Σ with `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` is: if `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊥`, a no-op (`Σ' = Σ`); otherwise the application of some emission set satisfying `Post_ρ(x, Σ, ·)` — a finite sequence of `→_sh` steps through the surface (the gated relation, ASN-0126, over extended-record states by R-TR, ASN-0128), `Σ →_sh* Σ'`. **(H-FIN, a named hypothesis on the fire itself.)** That the sequence is finite is inherited, not assumed away: each surface call (`Emit_K`, `Nullify_Binary`) is a single bounded `→_sh` step (ASN-0128), so a fire terminates exactly when the contract it discharges admits a *finite* emission set — H-FIN is the contract-level demand that it does, separate from the *registry*-level termination (Q5/Q6) of the fire *sequence*. The body that *chooses* the emissions is deliberately outside the model: two implementations producing the same emissions are indistinguishable, so every property below is a property of contracts and substrate state, never of algorithms — and because a contract may admit many emission sets, every termination claim below is read **universally over the admissible choices**: a nondeterministic body terminates iff *every* `Post_ρ`-satisfying fire sequence it can produce does. A trigger may be given as a registered definition address (`pdef`, ASN-0130) evaluated by reference (PR3) — the registry is then itself substrate content, its triggers linkable, versioned, and certified like any definition. Because evaluation-by-reference keys on *ever-registration*, not active registration (PR3), such a trigger stays evaluable — and quiescence (Q0) stays recognizable — even after its definition is de-registered: the content persists, so the verdict never becomes undecidable for want of a withdrawn endorsement. A trigger that itself *references* a since-de-registered definition is the dangling-live-reference case ASN-0130's Open Question 3 leaves open; its evaluation still computes (PR3 reads content, not registration), but it crosses a revoked endorsement, and what that should mean is settled there, not here. A registry built from inline PL triggers depends on ASN-0130 not at all — its triggers are PL terms evaluated directly (PC4/PC5), and every property below rests on ASN-0129 and the operation surface alone. The pdef-trigger option is the only place ASN-0130 enters, and there it enters with ASN-0130's own scoping: evaluation-by-reference carries its guarantees on registration-disciplined derivations (PR-DISC, ASN-0130) — the regime in which the audit-slice spelling of "ever-registered" and the standing registration coincide. A registry whose triggers are pdef addresses therefore inherits PR-DISC as a standing hypothesis, discharged the same way ASN-0130 discharges it (every `pdef` deposit enters through `register_pred`); a registry of inline triggers carries no such hypothesis. Recognizability and absorption (Q0, Q1) hold unconditionally in both cases — PR-DISC conditions only the link between a pdef-trigger's address and the definition it is taken to name, never the decidability of the trigger's PL verdict.

**Q0 (Recognizability).** Define `quiescent_R(Σ) ≡ (∀ ρ ∈ R :: (∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ)))`. The outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is *not* a PC1 quantification — `R` is a finite metalevel set of rule-triples (for an inline-trigger registry, not even substrate content), not a QD domain — but a *finite static expansion* into a PC0 conjunction, the move ASN-0129's V-IDX makes explicit for `Reg`-quantification ("Defined by static expansion… denotes the finite conjunction"). Each conjunct `(∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ))` is a PL predicate: PC1 over a QD domain, finite at every reachable state (QD-fin, ASN-0129), with `¬` by PC0. Whether the conjunction is *itself* a single PL term turns on views — PC0 conjunction requires its constituents to read the same view (PC3), and RG constrains no two triggers to a common one. *For a single-view registry* (all `T_ρ` sharing one view) the conjunction is a single PC0/PC3-conforming term, so `quiescent_R ∈ PL`. *For a heterogeneous-view registry* the merge is blocked and `quiescent_R` is a finite metalevel conjunction of separately-viewed PL predicates, not one PL term. *Either way* its value at every reachable Σ is decidable in finite time by any observer from Σ and the registry alone: finitely many PL verdicts, each pure (PC4) and terminating (PC5), conjoined at the metalevel. Purity makes every verdict observer-uniform — no agent reports, no consensus, no decision history. ∎

**Q1 (Absorption).** A quiescent state is a fixed point of firing: at any Σ with `quiescent_R(Σ)`, every fire of every `(ρ, x)` is a no-op, so `Σ' = Σ` — and the system may halt on first detection, no further monitoring required. *Proof.* Immediate from RG's no-op clause and Q0's definition. ∎ Recognizability and absorption are *unconditional* — they hold for undisciplined registries, unfair schedulers, and divergent systems alike. They say nothing about whether quiescence is reachable; that is the rest of the note.

## Extinction discipline

Reaching quiescence requires that firing *consumes* the condition that fired. Without a contract to that effect, a rule can fire forever on the same argument.

**X-DEF (ExtinctionDisciplined).** Rule ρ is *extinction-disciplined* iff for every reachable Σ and `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` with `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊤`, every fire of `(ρ, x)` yields Σ' with `T_ρ(x, Σ') = ⊥`. A registry is extinction-disciplined iff each rule is.

**Q2 (ContractOnOutputs).** Extinction discipline constrains emissions, not bodies: it is decided by `T_ρ` evaluations at the pre- and post-states — both public PL facts — so any two bodies with the same outputs are equivalent under it, and nondeterministic bodies are admissible whenever *all* their permitted outputs flip the trigger.

**Q3 (StaticCheckability).** If `Post_ρ` is *strong enough* — every emission set satisfying it at a trigger-true `(x, Σ)` produces a post-state falsifying `T_ρ(x, ·)` — then ρ's extinction discipline is verifiable at registration time, without executing any body: the implication ranges over PL-typed objects and the surface's emission forms. Sufficient, not necessary: a trivially weak contract (`Post_ρ ≡ ⊤`) certifies nothing, and a rule may be disciplined for reasons its contract is too weak to express. Failure-to-verify is not violation.

**Q4 (Locality).** Extinction discipline is per-rule: its definition mentions no other rule. Registries compose pointwise — and precisely because the property is local, it cannot alone be sufficient for termination: locally disciplined rules can re-arm *each other* without bound (H-W is doing real work).

**Q-EXT (ExtinctionByClass).** If `T_ρ` is an **SF spelling** (⊥-stable, PD0, ASN-0129), extinction discipline strengthens to *at-most-once firing per argument along any derivation*: the disciplined fire makes `T_ρ(x, ·)` false, and SF makes false permanent — no later step of any rule, on any target, re-arms it. The count of real fires of ρ is then bounded by the total growth of `[D_ρ]` alone, with no analysis of other rules' emissions. *Proof.* Immediate composition of X-DEF with PD0's ⊥-stability. ∎ This is the design rule the Marker pattern instantiates: spell the trigger as "no completion marker recorded" (`¬∃` over a grow-only audit slice — ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery, SF by the quantifier rule), and fire by emitting the marker. The check is registration-time and spelling-level — exactly the SF certificate ASN-0130's Open Question 4 anticipates; with it, "every rule in this registry is at-most-once" becomes a structural lint.

**Q-FLIP (FalsifierAccounting).** For triggers *not* in SF, what can re-arm them is the falsifier inventory ASN-0129's FP enumerates, read off with PD1/PD2: a retraction shrinking an active slice the trigger reads; a BH1-type emission moving a default-view result; a BH4-footprint change from any deposit in a watched home; and — the re-armer that is no retraction at all — a *bare deposit growing an active slice*, which flips an `∃`-shaped active-view trigger `⊥→⊤` (PD1: `(∃ x ∈ M_K :: P(x))` at view active "flips ⊥→⊤ on a K-deposit") or perturbs a non-monotone verdict atom such as `target_of`/`targets_keyed` (PD2: a term containing `targets_keyed` is "perturbed by deposits of every BH3-attached Binary type"). The deposit re-armer bites even on a `¬`-shaped, extinguishable trigger: for a BH3-attached Binary `K`, the trigger `T(s) ≡ ¬def(target_of(s, K))` is ⊤ with no active `K`-tuple from `s`, is extinguished to ⊥ by firing one tuple `s → y`, then is re-armed to ⊤ by a *second* active tuple `s → y'` that turns `target_of(s, K)` to ⊥ ("several", ASN-0128 BH3) — no retraction, no BH1, no BH4. The folklore rule "no retraction ⟹ each trigger flips at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery — a registry whose triggers read default views, age atoms, *or growing active slices* re-arms without any retraction. (SF triggers are immune: ⊥-stability (PD0) makes a falsified SF trigger permanent against every item above, deposits included — exactly why Q-EXT and Q5a demand the SF spelling.) Trigger-flip counting must be done per spelling class, and the conservative design stance falls out: *triggers in SF, termination conditions in ST*, both certifiable.

## Conditional termination

**H-FAIR (FairnessHypothesis).** A fire sequence `σ = (Σ₀, fire₁, Σ₁, …)` is *fair* iff every `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at some Σ_k is eventually either fired or removed from its domain (`x ∉ [D_ρ]_{Σ_m}`, m > k — domains can shrink under retraction, and the scheduler owes nothing to absent arguments). Fairness is a scheduler property; this corpus states it as a hypothesis and deliberately ships no scheduler note — any discipline satisfying the statement (round-robin, queue-fair, priority with aging) discharges it.

**W, H-W (BoundedWork).** `W(σ)` is the set of (rule, argument, index) triples at which a trigger is true along σ; the registry has *bounded work from Σ₀* (H-W) iff `|W(σ)| < ∞` for every σ from Σ₀. H-W is a registry-level hypothesis, and *provably meta-level*: its statement quantifies over the states reachable by fire sequences, and reachability is a fixed-point notion PL deliberately cannot express (PC6a ships no fixpoint operator; the closure admits only finite quantification). The substrate can therefore neither evaluate H-W at a state nor monitor it as a predicate — a runtime defense against its violation is necessarily bookkeeping (fire counts, thresholds), not evaluation. The structural conditions one might hope discharge it do not, and it is worth seeing why: an all-SF registry with bounded domain growth (Q5a); a *stratified* registry — `stratum : R → ℕ` with no rule's emissions re-arming a strictly lower stratum — with per-stratum bounds; acyclicity of the emission/re-arm graph — each bounds *real fires* (and so supplies H-RF, below), but none bounds `W`. H-W quantifies over *every* σ, unfair schedulers included, and an unfair scheduler that starves a persistently-true trigger drives `|W(σ)| = ∞` whatever the registry's structure — so these conditions supply the weaker H-RF, never H-W itself. None is necessary even for that.

**Q5 (RealFiresAreBounded).** Under H-W *alone*, every fire sequence from Σ₀ contains at most `|W(σ)|` real (non-no-op) fires. *Proof.* Each real fire at step k+1 witnesses `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)`, and the map real-fire ↦ `(ρ, x, k)` is injective *by the step index alone*: distinct real fires occupy distinct steps, so their triples differ in the index component — extinguished or not. Injection into a finite set bounds the count. Extinction discipline is neither used nor needed: a rule spinning on a fixed `x` would witness infinitely many `(ρ, x, k₁), (ρ, x, k₂), …`, forcing `|W(σ)| = ∞`, so H-W already forbids spinning outright. (Extinction's leverage is elsewhere — Q-EXT and Q5a — where it lets bounded domain growth supply the same finite-real-fire conclusion (H-RF, below) directly, with no `W(σ)` bound at all; it does *not* discharge H-W, which a starved SF trigger-true argument violates while real fires stay finite.) ∎

**Q5a (ExtinctionBound).** For an all-SF registry (every trigger an SF spelling), the finite-real-fire conclusion follows from bounded domain growth *directly*, with no work bound: real fires number at most `Σ_ρ |⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}|` — each argument fires each rule at most once (Q-EXT), so the only unbounded-real-fire route is unbounded *new* arguments. This supplies H-RF (below) by a route disjoint from Q5: it never mentions `W(σ)`, and it does *not* establish H-W — under an unfair scheduler a starved trigger-true argument can still drive `|W(σ)| = ∞` (its triple recurs at every step) while the real-fire count stays bounded by the display above. The W machinery, the re-arm analysis, and the stratification heuristic are all unnecessary in this case; this is the quantitative payoff of the SF design rule. The bound's two ingredients are not equal in checkability: at-most-once-per-argument is a *registration-time* fact about the SF spelling (Q-EXT), whereas `|⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}| < ∞` quantifies over all reachable states and is as meta-level as H-W — a bound on arguments rather than on trigger-true step-instances, but reachability-quantified all the same.

**H-RF (FiniteRealFires).** A fire sequence from Σ₀ has *finitely many real fires* iff its real (non-no-op) fires are finite in number. This — not H-W — is the operative hypothesis of the termination theorem below, and it must be kept distinct from H-W, which is strictly stronger. H-W bounds trigger-*true* step-instances (`|W(σ)| < ∞`); H-RF bounds only the *fires*. The two come apart precisely at starvation: an SF trigger-true argument the scheduler never fires keeps its triple `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)` at *every* step `k` (`|W(σ)| = ∞`) while contributing no real fire — so a registry can satisfy H-RF (indeed have zero real fires) yet violate H-W. Bounded domain growth, in particular, does *not* entail H-W; it entails H-RF (Q5a) and no more. Two routes therefore supply H-RF: H-W supplies it for any registry (Q5 — `|W(σ)| < ∞` bounds the real fires it injects into), and an all-SF registry with bounded domain growth supplies it with no work bound at all (Q5a). Q6 consumes H-RF and nothing stronger.

**Q6 (TerminationUnderFairness).** Under H-RF and H-FAIR, the system reaches a quiescent state in finitely many steps. *Proof.* H-RF bounds the real fires, so σ has a last real fire; no fire after it can be real — a trigger-true fire *is* a real fire and would exceed H-RF's finite count — so every later fire is on a trigger-false argument, a no-op by RG's no-op clause, leaving `Σ' = Σ`. The state is therefore constant past the last real fire (this is *why* the tail is no-ops — the exhausted real-fire budget, not quiescence, which is what we are proving). Were that constant tail state not quiescent, some `(ρ, x)` would be trigger-true at every state of the tail; H-FAIR forces it eventually fired (a real fire — contradiction with H-RF's exhaustion) or domain-removed (contradiction with the state's constancy). So quiescence is reached, in finitely many steps. The *finite*-σ case is the same H-FAIR step read at the end: a fair finite sequence cannot terminate at a non-quiescent state, for a trigger-true argument at the final state is left both unfired and unremoved, violating H-FAIR. ∎ Since H-W ⟹ H-RF (Q5) and all-SF-plus-bounded-domain-growth ⟹ H-RF (Q5a), the theorem holds in particular under H-W + H-FAIR, and under all-SF + bounded domain growth + H-FAIR. The theorem is exactly as conditional as it looks, with two hypotheses: H-RF and H-FAIR. Drop H-RF and rules can re-arm each other forever, each re-fire a fresh real fire without bound; drop H-FAIR and a trigger-true argument can be starved eternally with the system never settling. These two failure modes are genuinely separable, and neither is derivable from the other — and the starvation mode is exactly why the hypothesis is H-RF rather than H-W: a starved SF trigger-true argument drives `|W(σ)| = ∞` yet contributes no fire, so H-W is strictly stronger than what Q6 needs (and, being violated under that very scheduler, would have wrongly excluded a case Q6 settles by H-FAIR's failure instead). Extinction discipline is *not* a third independent hypothesis: it is one of the two routes to H-RF (Q5a). What it earns is a cheaper route — it replaces Q5's reachability-quantified `|W(σ)| < ∞` with at-most-once-per-argument (Q-EXT, checkable at registration from the SF spelling) plus bounded domain growth. Only the SF half is registration-checkable; bounded domain growth is itself reachability-quantified, so the move trades one meta-level assumption for a simpler one — a bound on arguments, not on trigger-true step-instances — rather than eliminating meta-level quantification. Its load-bearing roles are Q-EXT and Q5a, not Q5/Q6.

## Scope parameterization

**SC (Scope), Q7–Q9.** A *scope* is any Boolean PL predicate `S` over addresses. A rule's domain `D_ρ ∈ QD` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) or tuple-valued (`dom(Tup)` — e.g. an audit slice `L_K`, as the worked example's `ρ_R` takes), so an address-predicate cannot in general be applied to a domain element directly: over `dom(Tup)` the element `x` is a tuple, and `S(x)` is a sort error. The scope's *restriction of ρ* is therefore the QD filter `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : S(π_ρ(x))}`, where `π_ρ` is the identity on an address-valued `D_ρ` (filter body `S(x)`) and V-TUP's address projection `addr : Tup → T` on a tuple-valued `D_ρ` (filter body `S(addr(x))`, scoping each tuple by its own address). In both cases the body is a Boolean PL predicate *at `D_ρ`'s sort*: `S` directly over `dom(T)`, and the composite `S ∘ addr` over `dom(Tup)` — well-typed by WT's PC2 plain-composition rule, `addr` a V-TUP projection `Tup → T` and `S` a closed PL predicate `T → Bool`. The filtering former (ASN-0129) admits exactly such a `P : D × S → Bool` at the domain's sort over an address- *or* tuple-valued `D`, and preserves the domain's sort; so `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : S(π_ρ(x))}` is a well-typed QD filter for every rule, tuple-domained ones included. `quiescent_{R,S}(Σ)` restricts Q0's inner quantification to the elements this filter retains. Then: **Q7** — `quiescent_{R,S}` carries Q0's status exactly: the scope adds this PC1 filter to each rule's inner quantification, the outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is the same finite static expansion (V-IDX) — so `quiescent_{R,S} ∈ PL` for a single-view registry, and a finite metalevel conjunction of separately-viewed PL predicates otherwise, decidable and observer-uniform in both cases. The claim now covers tuple-domained rules — the worked example's `ρ_R` among them, whose `dom(Tup)` filter `{c ∈ L_cmt : S(addr(c))}` is well-typed by the projection above. Scope quiescence inherits Q0's recognizability, no more than Q0 itself had. **Q8** — at a scope-quiescent state, in-scope fires are no-ops (absorption relativizes); out-of-scope fires are unconstrained, and an out-of-scope emission may re-arm an in-scope trigger — *re-entry* — which is itself detectable per-state by Q7. **Q9** — quiescence is anti-monotone in the scope: `S' ⟹ S` gives `quiescent_S ⟹ quiescent_{S'}`; the converse fails whenever the larger scope holds work no smaller scope sees. Canonical tiers (per-target, per-collection, system-wide) are application vocabulary over this machinery — the substrate admits any PL predicate as a scope and commits to nothing about which scopes matter.

## Worked composition

The two illustrative classes of ASN-0129's worked trace — `cmt` (Binary, idem=⊥, comment from finding to target) and `res` (Binary, idem=⊤, resolution from target to comment) — already form a registry; we verify its termination.

*Rules.* Producer `ρ_P`: domain = `{t ∈ targets : needs_attention(t)}` — a QD filter (ASN-0129), *not* grow-only since `needs_attention` may flip either way; trigger `T_P(t) ≡ ¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))`, with `L_cmt` the audit slice (ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery) — fires by emitting one `cmt` targeting t. Folding `needs_attention` into the *domain* rather than the trigger is deliberate: it leaves `T_P` a pure negated existential (SF, below) at the cost of a non-grow-only domain, which Q5a's union bound absorbs. Resolver `ρ_R`: domain = `L_cmt` (audit slice — grow-only); trigger `T_R(c) ≡ ¬(∃ r ∈ L_res :: addr(c) ∈ coverage_G(r))` — fires by emitting one `res` targeting c.

*Class check.* With `needs_attention` moved out, both triggers are now pure negated existentials over grow-only audit slices with step-constant bodies — **SF spellings** by PD0's quantifier and Boolean rules. The move was forced: had `needs_attention` stayed a trigger conjunct, `T_P ≡ needs_attention(t) ∧ ¬(∃ …)` would *not* be SF — PD0's `∧` preserves SF only when both conjuncts are, and `needs_attention` is not ⊥-stable (it can flip `⊥→⊤` on a still-uncommented target, flipping `T_P` with it), so Q-EXT would not apply. (Spelled over *active* slices instead, neither trigger would be SF: a retraction of a resolution would re-open the comment — Q-FLIP's first falsifier — and termination would need the full H-W analysis. The audit spelling is the design choice, made checkable.)

*Extinction.* `Post_P` requires an emitted `cmt` covering t; `Post_R` an emitted `res` covering `addr(c)` — each strong enough (Q3): the emission makes the trigger's existential true, hence the trigger false. With SF, Q-EXT gives at-most-once per argument for both rules.

*Bound (Q5a).* By Q5a the real fires number at most `|⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}| + |⋃_k [D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ_k}|`. The resolver's domain is grow-only, so its union is just `|comments ever|`; the producer's domain is not, so its union is *the set of targets that ever need attention* — this is where the non-grow-only domain is paid for. Hence real fires ≤ |targets that ever need attention| + |comments ever|. Comments are emitted only by `ρ_P`, at most one per target — so the registry terminates iff the population of targets that ever need attention is bounded, which is the registry's one honest hypothesis on its environment. The classic failure mode is visible in the accounting: let the resolver's emissions also make fresh targets need attention (`needs_attention` true on addresses `ρ_R` creates, enlarging `⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}`) and the bound's right side grows with the left — locally disciplined rules, globally divergent registry, exactly the divergence Q4 warns locality cannot exclude. Stratification states the repair: `ρ_P` at stratum 0, `ρ_R` at stratum 1, legal iff resolver emissions never re-arm the producer.

*Quiescence.* `quiescent_R` here is precisely "every target needing attention is commented, every comment resolved" — two nested quantifications whose bodies are ST (negations of the SF triggers, PD0). The registry is single-view — both triggers read the audit slice — so by Q0 `quiescent_R ∈ PL`: decidable at every state, absorbing once reached (Q1), and — for this registry — reached in finitely many steps given a bounded need-attention population and any fair scheduler (Q6).

## What this note doesn't cover

- **A scheduler.** H-FAIR is stated, not constructed; scheduling disciplines, their fairness proofs, concurrency reconciliation, and violation policy (log, halt, escalate) are operational machinery this corpus deliberately leaves at the implementation layer.
- **Stochastic rule bodies.** A body that flips its trigger with probability p < 1 satisfies the deterministic contracts only operationally (retry policies, N-consecutive-clean stopping rules) — evaluator-reliability engineering, not substrate semantics.
- **The activation binding.** RG assumes rules exist; what registers them, who may, and how rule registries are themselves governed is the protocol layer above (with ASN-0130's machinery available to it).

## Open questions

1. **The SF certificate.** Q-EXT and Q5a make SF membership the load-bearing registration check for triggers, and ASN-0130 ships only the ST⁺ certificate (`pd_stable`). Should `pd_extinct` (SF certification) ship as a designated class — exactly the catalog-growth ASN-0130's own Open Question 4 raises for certificates beyond ST⁺ — closing the loop where "this registry terminates on bounded input" is a structural lint?

2. **A PL surrogate for H-W.** H-W is meta-level; is there a PL-expressible *necessary* condition — a per-state predicate whose failure certifies unbounded work in progress — strong enough to catch the common divergence patterns (mutual re-arm cycles) at runtime, even though the full hypothesis is inexpressible?

3. **Per-scope versus global work.** Q5/Q6 are stated globally; the per-scope analogs (W restricted to in-scope arguments, termination toward `quiescent_S`) appear routine, but whether a registry can have bounded work at every scope of a tier without global H-W deserves a structural answer — it is the formal version of "every document converges but the corpus doesn't."

4. **Cross-scope oscillation.** Q8's re-entry means an outer-scope fire can repeatedly un-quiesce an inner scope. What registry or scheduling discipline bounds re-entry — and whether bounded re-entry is even the right demand, versus monotone progress *across* entries — is open, and is where a future cascade/re-opening theory would live.

5. **Contract necessity.** Q3 is sufficiency only: must every extinction-disciplined rule admit a strong-enough PL contract? A rule disciplined by reasoning PL cannot express would be sound but unverifiable at registration — the same sound-but-uncertifiable gap PD0's spelling-level classes accept by design.
