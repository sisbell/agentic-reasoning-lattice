# ASN-0133: Substrate Quiescence

*The terminal state, recognizable from inside the predicate language; termination as a conditional theorem with every hypothesis named.*

ASN-0129 supplies the language a coordination system's conditions are written in, and the dynamics classes (PD0–PD2, ST/SF) that say how a condition's truth moves. ASN-0130 makes the conditions substrate artifacts. This note closes the arc: it defines what it is for such a system to be *done* — quiescence — and proves what can and cannot be guaranteed about reaching it. The shape of the answer is fixed by a fact about forward-chaining systems generally: unconditional termination is not provable; every honest treatment names conditions (extinction discipline, bounded work, fair scheduling) under which termination follows. The note's discipline is to put each hypothesis where it belongs — what the substrate guarantees unconditionally (recognizability, absorption), what a rule author can make checkable at registration (at-most-once firing — the SF spelling decidably via the spelling classes; the extinction discipline via a strong-enough contract, Q3 — effectively for the negated-existential marker pattern, a static-but-not-decidable obligation otherwise), and what remains assumption (fairness, bounded work) stated as named hypotheses rather than smuggled.

**Depends:** ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework), ASN-0128 (Substrate Type Operational Semantics), ASN-0129 (Substrate Predicate Composition), ASN-0130 (Predicate Definitions as Substrate Content).

## What this note commits

- **The rule model** (RG): a minimal trigger/emission unit — a domain in QD, a Boolean PL trigger, an emission contract over the operation surface — with the action body deliberately opaque: the contract constrains outputs, never algorithms. A registry is a finite set of rules; a rule's trigger may be a registered definition address (ASN-0130), making registries themselves substrate-described.
- **Recognizability and absorption, unconditional** (Q0, Q1): quiescence — no trigger true on any domain element — is decidable at every reachable state by any observer (a single PL term for a single-view registry, a finite conjunction of PL verdicts otherwise), pure, and a fixed point of firing once reached.
- **Extinction discipline** (X-DEF, Q2–Q4) with the class-theoretic upgrade (Q-EXT): a rule whose trigger is an **SF spelling** (ASN-0129) and whose fires falsify it gets *at-most-once firing per argument* as a theorem — the falsification is permanent by the class, not by analysis of other rules. This is the Marker pattern's termination half, and the certificate that makes it registration-checkable is exactly the SF certificate ASN-0130's Open Question 4 anticipates.
- **Conditional termination** (Q5, Q5a, Q6): the termination theorem turns on a single operative hypothesis, *finitely many real fires from Σ₀* (H-RF). The structural route that supplies it is Q5a: for an all-SF, *extinction-disciplined* registry, bounded domain growth gives H-RF directly. The bounded-work hypothesis (H-W) *would* supply H-RF too (Q5's injection), but H-W is not merely meta-level — it is **unsatisfiable** for any registry doing concurrent work: an unfair scheduler that no-op-spams a trigger-false argument while a trigger-true one persists drives `|W(σ)| = ∞`. H-W is therefore a *foil*, not a route — its sole service is to locate H-RF as the strictly weaker, attainable hypothesis. Extinction discipline is not a hypothesis of Q5's bound; with the SF spelling it is what Q5a's route requires, trading the cross-rule re-arm analysis for a domain-growth bound. Quiescence is then reached in finitely many steps under H-RF and the fairness hypothesis (H-FAIR). The hypotheses are named, stated, and not discharged here: bounded domain growth is reachability-quantified (a bound over all reachable states), so of Q5a's route the at-most-once-firing-per-argument half — resting on *both* the SF spelling (via the spelling class, decidably) and the extinction discipline (via a strong-enough contract, Q3, whose obligation reduces to a decidable match for the marker-pattern rules Q5a's route is built on) — is registration-checkable there, but not the domain bound; and H-FAIR is a scheduler property this corpus has chosen not to axiomatize beyond the statement.
- **Scope parameterization** (Q7–Q9): everything above relativizes to a scope — any Boolean PL predicate — with scope quiescence carrying Q0's status (a single PL term for single-view registries), recognizability and absorption inherited per-scope, and anti-monotone nesting (outer quiescence implies inner, never conversely).
- **The falsifier accounting** (Q-FLIP): what can re-arm a trigger is not "retraction" alone but a wider falsifier inventory (ASN-0129's FP, read with PD1/PD2) — retraction, BH1-type emissions under the default view, BH4's home-traffic footprint, *and bare deposits growing an active slice* (which flip `∃`-shaped or non-monotone-verdict triggers, PD1/PD2). The folklore "no retraction ⟹ triggers flip at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery, and this note replaces it with the class-checked accounting.

## The rule model

**RG (Rule, Registry).** A *rule* is a triple `ρ = (D_ρ, T_ρ, Post_ρ)`: a domain expression `D_ρ ∈ QD`, a Boolean trigger `T_ρ : D_ρ → Bool` in PL, and an *emission contract* `Post_ρ` — a *meta-level* contract over (argument, state, emitted call set) constraining what any fire of ρ must emit, the calls drawn from the operation surface `{Emit_K, Nullify_Binary}` (ASN-0128; `Observe_K` reads, and reads are not emissions). The contract is meta-level, *not* a PL term: PL has no sort for emitted call sets (COD lists none, ASN-0129) and a PL term reads a single state, whereas the contract speaks of what a fire *emits* — so `Post_ρ` is written in the surface's emission forms, never evaluated as a PL verdict. What *is* PL is the trigger `T_ρ`, whose pre- and post-state evaluations decide extinction (Q2); the contract enters this note only through registration-time implications relating those PL verdicts to the emission forms (Q3). A *registry* `R` is a finite set of rules. A *fire* of `(ρ, x)` at Σ with `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` is: if `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊥`, a no-op (`Σ' = Σ`); otherwise the application of some emission set satisfying `Post_ρ(x, Σ, ·)` — a finite sequence of `→_sh` steps through the surface (the gated relation, ASN-0126, over extended-record states by R-TR, ASN-0128), `Σ →_sh* Σ'`. **(H-FIN, a named hypothesis on the fire itself.)** That the sequence is finite is inherited, not assumed away: each surface call (`Emit_K`, `Nullify_Binary`) is a single bounded `→_sh` step (ASN-0128), so a fire terminates exactly when the contract it discharges admits a *finite* emission set — H-FIN is the contract-level demand that it does, separate from the *registry*-level termination (Q5/Q6) of the fire *sequence*. The body that *chooses* the emissions is deliberately outside the model: two implementations producing the same emissions are indistinguishable, so every property below is a property of contracts and substrate state, never of algorithms — and because a contract may admit many emission sets, every termination claim below is read **universally over the admissible choices**: a nondeterministic body terminates iff *every* `Post_ρ`-satisfying fire sequence it can produce does. A trigger may be given as a registered definition address (`pdef`, ASN-0130) evaluated by reference (PR3) — the registry is then itself substrate content, its triggers linkable, versioned, and certified like any definition. Because evaluation-by-reference keys on *ever-registration*, not active registration (PR3), such a trigger stays evaluable — and quiescence (Q0) stays recognizable — even after its definition is de-registered: the content persists, so the verdict never becomes undecidable for want of a withdrawn endorsement. A trigger that itself *references* a since-de-registered definition is the dangling-live-reference case ASN-0130's Open Question 3 leaves open; its evaluation still computes (PR3 reads content, not registration), but it crosses a revoked endorsement, and what that should mean is settled there, not here. A registry built from inline PL triggers depends on ASN-0130 not at all — its triggers are PL terms evaluated directly (PC4/PC5), and every property below rests on ASN-0129 and the operation surface alone. The pdef-trigger option is the only place ASN-0130 enters, and there it enters with ASN-0130's own scoping: evaluation-by-reference carries its guarantees on registration-disciplined derivations (PR-DISC, ASN-0130) — the regime in which the audit-slice spelling of "ever-registered" and the standing registration coincide. A registry whose triggers are pdef addresses therefore inherits PR-DISC as a standing hypothesis, discharged the same way ASN-0130 discharges it (every `pdef` deposit enters through `register_pred`); a registry of inline triggers carries no such hypothesis. Recognizability and absorption (Q0, Q1) hold unconditionally in both cases — PR-DISC conditions only the link between a pdef-trigger's address and the definition it is taken to name, never the decidability of the trigger's PL verdict.

**Q0 (Recognizability).** Define `quiescent_R(Σ) ≡ (∀ ρ ∈ R :: (∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ)))`. The outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is *not* a PC1 quantification — `R` is a finite metalevel set of rule-triples (for an inline-trigger registry, not even substrate content), not a QD domain — but a *finite static expansion* into a PC0 conjunction, the move ASN-0129's V-IDX makes explicit for `Reg`-quantification ("Defined by static expansion… denotes the finite conjunction"). Each conjunct `(∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ))` is a PL predicate: PC1 over a QD domain, finite at every reachable state (QD-fin, ASN-0129), with `¬` by PC0. Whether the conjunction is *itself* a single PL term turns on views — PC0 conjunction requires its constituents to read the same view (PC3), and RG constrains no two triggers to a common one. *For a single-view registry* — one whose *every* view-parameterized constituent shares one view, not the triggers `T_ρ` alone but the view-parameterized parts of each domain `D_ρ` too (a domain reading `M_K` is view-parameterized; one reading only a fixed-view base such as `L_K` leaves the view free) — the conjunction is a single PC0/PC3-conforming term, so `quiescent_R ∈ PL`. The criterion must range over domains, not triggers alone: a rule pairing an active-view domain with a view-independent trigger forces its conjunct to the active view as surely as a viewed trigger would. *For a heterogeneous-view registry* the merge is blocked and `quiescent_R` is a finite metalevel conjunction of separately-viewed PL predicates, not one PL term. *Either way* its value at every reachable Σ is decidable in finite time by any observer from Σ and the registry alone: finitely many PL verdicts, each pure (PC4) and terminating (PC5), conjoined at the metalevel. Purity makes every verdict observer-uniform — no agent reports, no consensus, no decision history. ∎

**Q1 (Absorption).** A quiescent state is a fixed point of firing: at any Σ with `quiescent_R(Σ)`, every fire of every `(ρ, x)` is a no-op, so `Σ' = Σ` — and the system may halt on first detection, no further monitoring required. *Proof.* Immediate from RG's no-op clause and Q0's definition. ∎ Recognizability and absorption are *unconditional* — they hold for undisciplined registries, unfair schedulers, and divergent systems alike. They say nothing about whether quiescence is reachable; that is the rest of the note.

## Extinction discipline

Reaching quiescence requires that firing *consumes* the condition that fired. Without a contract to that effect, a rule can fire forever on the same argument.

**X-DEF (ExtinctionDisciplined).** Rule ρ is *extinction-disciplined* iff for every reachable Σ and `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` with `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊤`, every fire of `(ρ, x)` yields Σ' with `T_ρ(x, Σ') = ⊥`. A registry is extinction-disciplined iff each rule is.

**Q2 (ContractOnOutputs).** Extinction discipline constrains emissions, not bodies: it is decided by `T_ρ` evaluations at the pre- and post-states — both public PL facts — so any two bodies with the same outputs are equivalent under it, and nondeterministic bodies are admissible whenever *all* their permitted outputs flip the trigger.

**Q3 (StaticCheckability).** If `Post_ρ` is *strong enough* — *every* emission set satisfying it at a trigger-true `(x, Σ)` produces a post-state falsifying `T_ρ(x, ·)` — then ρ is extinction-disciplined. The condition is *static*: it speaks only of PL-typed objects and the surface's emission forms, never of a running body. But *static* is not *effective* — being non-executable buys only that no body need be run, not that the obligation is decidable — and the load-bearing claims must not conflate the two. The condition carries a quantifier over `(x, Σ)`, and its scope fixes its standing against this note's own checkable/meta-level line, the line that disqualifies H-W and bounded domain growth precisely *for* quantifying over reachable states. Read at the **reachable level** — `(x, Σ)` over reachable trigger-true pairs, the reading that matches X-DEF's own "for every reachable Σ" — the condition is reachability-quantified, hence *meta-level by this note's own standard*, no more registration-evaluable than H-W: it soundly *characterizes* discipline but is no decision procedure for it. Read at the **schema level** — `(x, Σ)` over *all* states — it is instead a sound over-approximation of X-DEF (all-states ⟹ reachable-states, so a schema-valid contract is disciplined; the converse can fail, so the check may *reject* a contract disciplined only on its reachable states), but schema-validity of "every `Post_ρ`-satisfying deposit falsifies this PL trigger" is a validity question over PL — which carries PC1 quantifiers and PC2a aggregates — composed with the emission semantics, *not shown decidable here and given no checking procedure*; even this reading yields a static obligation, not a general effective check. Where the check *is* effective is the **negated-existential marker pattern**, the load-bearing case (Q-EXT, Q5a, the worked example): the trigger is `¬(∃ c ∈ L_K :: a ∈ coverage_G(c))` over a grow-only audit slice, and `Post_ρ` deposits *exactly the witness the `∃` quantifies over* — a `K`-tuple covering `a` — so the deposit grows `L_K`, the existential goes ⊤, the trigger goes ⊥, and "strong enough" is decided by a finite syntactic comparison of trigger spelling against emission form, no state quantified. There "verifiable at registration" is sound *and* effective; the general statement is the sound-but-not-effective envelope around it, and every claim below resting on Q3 (Q5a, Q6) is scoped to this pattern. The companion SF-spelling half of at-most-once is, by contrast, genuinely decidable wherever invoked — PD0's ST/SF rules are syntax-directed, like WT, the very rules ASN-0130's `certify_pd_stable` runs — so it is the extinction half alone this note must scope. Sufficient, not necessary: a trivially weak contract (`Post_ρ ≡ ⊤`) certifies nothing, and a rule may be disciplined for reasons no contract of this shape expresses. Failure-to-verify is not violation.

**Q4 (Locality).** Extinction discipline is per-rule: its definition mentions no other rule. Registries compose pointwise — and precisely because the property is local, it cannot alone be sufficient for termination: locally disciplined rules can re-arm *each other* without bound (H-W is doing real work).

**Q-EXT (ExtinctionByClass).** If `T_ρ` is an **SF spelling** (⊥-stable, PD0, ASN-0129), extinction discipline strengthens to *at-most-once firing per argument along any derivation*: the disciplined fire makes `T_ρ(x, ·)` false, and SF makes false permanent — no later step of any rule, on any target, re-arms it. The count of real fires of ρ is then bounded by the total growth of `[D_ρ]` alone, with no analysis of other rules' emissions. *Proof.* Immediate composition of X-DEF with PD0's ⊥-stability. ∎ This is the design rule the Marker pattern instantiates: spell the trigger as "no completion marker recorded" (`¬∃` over a grow-only audit slice — ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery, SF by the quantifier rule), and fire by emitting the marker. The check is registration-time and spelling-level — exactly the SF certificate ASN-0130's Open Question 4 anticipates; with it, and with the extinction half here in Q3's decidable-match case (the fire emits exactly the marker the trigger's `∃` is missing), "every rule in this registry is at-most-once" becomes a structural lint for Marker-pattern registries.

**Q-FLIP (FalsifierAccounting).** For triggers *not* in SF, what can re-arm them is the falsifier inventory ASN-0129's FP enumerates, read off with PD1/PD2: a retraction shrinking an active slice the trigger reads; a BH1-type emission moving a default-view result; a BH4-footprint change from any deposit in a watched home; and — the re-armer that is no retraction at all — a *bare deposit growing an active slice*, which flips an `∃`-shaped active-view trigger `⊥→⊤` (PD1: `(∃ x ∈ M_K :: P(x))` at view active "flips ⊥→⊤ on a K-deposit") or perturbs a non-monotone verdict atom such as `target_of`/`targets_keyed` (PD2: a term containing `targets_keyed` is "perturbed by deposits of every BH3-attached Binary type"). The deposit re-armer bites even on a `¬`-shaped, extinguishable trigger: for a BH3-attached Binary `K`, the trigger `T(s) ≡ ¬def(target_of(s, K))` is ⊤ with no active `K`-tuple from `s`, is extinguished to ⊥ by firing one tuple `s → y`, then is re-armed to ⊤ by a *second* active tuple `s → y'` that turns `target_of(s, K)` to ⊥ ("several", ASN-0128 BH3) — no retraction, no BH1, no BH4. The folklore rule "no retraction ⟹ each trigger flips at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery — a registry whose triggers read default views, age atoms, *or growing active slices* re-arms without any retraction. (SF triggers are immune: ⊥-stability (PD0) makes a falsified SF trigger permanent against every item above, deposits included — exactly why Q-EXT and Q5a demand the SF spelling, alongside the extinction discipline that supplies the falsification SF then makes permanent.) Trigger-flip counting must be done per spelling class, and the conservative design stance falls out: *triggers in SF, termination conditions in ST*, both certifiable.

## Conditional termination

**H-FAIR (FairnessHypothesis).** A fire sequence `σ = (Σ₀, fire₁, Σ₁, …)` is *fair* iff every `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at some Σ_k is eventually either fired or removed from its domain (`x ∉ [D_ρ]_{Σ_m}`, m > k — domains can shrink under retraction, and the scheduler owes nothing to absent arguments). Fairness is a scheduler property; this corpus states it as a hypothesis and deliberately ships no scheduler note — any discipline satisfying the statement (round-robin, queue-fair, priority with aging) discharges it.

**W, H-W (BoundedWork).** `W(σ)` is the set of (rule, argument, index) triples at which a trigger is true along σ; the registry has *bounded work from Σ₀* (H-W) iff `|W(σ)| < ∞` for every σ from Σ₀. H-W is a registry-level hypothesis, and *provably meta-level*: its statement quantifies over the states reachable by fire sequences, and reachability is a fixed-point notion PL deliberately cannot express (PC6a ships no fixpoint operator; the closure admits only finite quantification). The substrate can therefore neither evaluate H-W at a state nor monitor it as a predicate — a runtime defense against its violation is necessarily bookkeeping (fire counts, thresholds), not evaluation. The structural conditions one might hope discharge it do not, and it is worth seeing why. Of the three below, only the first is *proved* to supply even the weaker H-RF; the other two supply it only with an extra bound adjoined that is no structural freebie. An all-SF, extinction-disciplined registry with bounded domain growth (Q5a) bounds *real fires* — proved there, from at-most-once-per-argument plus the domain bound — and so supplies H-RF. A *stratified* registry — `stratum : R → ℕ` with no rule's emissions re-arming a strictly lower stratum — supplies H-RF only once *per-stratum* real-fire bounds are adjoined, and those are themselves a per-stratum H-RF hypothesis: a decomposition of the conclusion, not an independent structural condition that delivers it. Acyclicity of the emission/re-arm graph supplies H-RF only with a domain-growth bound adjoined — acyclicity *alone* does not, since a single rule firing on an unboundedly growing domain has a vacuously acyclic re-arm graph (nothing re-arms anything) yet unbounded real fires, exactly the divergence route Q5a's *separate* domain-growth clause exists to exclude. None of the three, however equipped, bounds `W`: H-W quantifies over *every* σ, unfair schedulers included, and an unfair scheduler that starves a persistently-true trigger drives `|W(σ)| = ∞` whatever the registry's structure — so these conditions supply at most the weaker H-RF, never H-W itself. None is necessary even for that. Sharper than meta-level unverifiability: the same starvation argument shows H-W *false*, not merely inexpressible. Whenever a reachable state holds one trigger-true argument beside one trigger-false in-domain argument, an unfair σ can no-op-spam the false argument forever — freezing the state — while the true argument's triple `(ρ, x, k)` recurs at every index `k`, so `|W(σ)| = ∞` and H-W fails. That is essentially every registry that does concurrent work, the worked example below included (a commented-but-still-needs-attention target sits trigger-false and in-domain beside an uncommented one). H-W is therefore not a usable route to H-RF but a *foil*: Q5's injection shows H-W *would* bound real fires, and that is its entire service — to exhibit H-RF as the strictly weaker, attainable hypothesis the structural route (Q5a) actually supplies.

**Q5 (RealFiresAreBounded).** Under H-W *alone*, every fire sequence from Σ₀ contains at most `|W(σ)|` real (non-no-op) fires. *Proof.* Each real fire at step k+1 witnesses `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)`, and the map real-fire ↦ `(ρ, x, k)` is injective *by the step index alone*: distinct real fires occupy distinct steps, so their triples differ in the index component — extinguished or not. Injection into a finite set bounds the count. Extinction discipline is neither used nor needed: a rule spinning on a fixed `x` would witness infinitely many `(ρ, x, k₁), (ρ, x, k₂), …`, forcing `|W(σ)| = ∞`, so H-W already forbids spinning outright. (Extinction's leverage is elsewhere — Q-EXT and Q5a — where it lets bounded domain growth supply the same finite-real-fire conclusion (H-RF, below) directly, with no `W(σ)` bound at all; it does *not* discharge H-W, which a starved SF trigger-true argument violates while real fires stay finite.) ∎

**Q5a (ExtinctionBound).** For an all-SF, *extinction-disciplined* registry (every trigger an SF spelling, every rule extinction-disciplined — equivalently, a registry of Marker-pattern rules), the finite-real-fire conclusion follows from bounded domain growth *directly*, with no work bound: real fires number at most `Σ_ρ |⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}|` — each argument fires each rule at most once (Q-EXT, which consumes *both* the SF spelling and the extinction discipline), so the only unbounded-real-fire route is unbounded *new* arguments. Both hypotheses are load-bearing: SF alone does not bound the count — an SF trigger paired with a contract that emits something *other* than its own falsifier (a `cmt`-trigger `¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))` fired by emitting a `res`, never a `cmt`) stays ⊤ on a fixed argument forever (⊥-stability permits ⊤→⊤, and no `cmt` is ever emitted to flip it) and is a real fire at *every* step — unboundedly many, against the display, though its domain `{t}` is constant. Extinction discipline forbids exactly that second route: the disciplined fire falsifies its own trigger, SF makes the falsification permanent (Q-EXT), and only the two together make at-most-once per argument a theorem. This supplies H-RF (below) by a route disjoint from Q5: it never mentions `W(σ)`, and it does *not* establish H-W — under an unfair scheduler a starved trigger-true argument can still drive `|W(σ)| = ∞` (its triple recurs at every step) while the real-fire count stays bounded by the display above. The W machinery, the re-arm analysis, and the stratification heuristic are all unnecessary in this case; this is the quantitative payoff of the SF-plus-extinction design rule. The bound's two ingredients are not equal in checkability: at-most-once-per-argument is a *registration-time* fact *for the Marker-pattern rules this clause is about* — the SF spelling decidable by the spelling class, and the extinction discipline by a strong-enough contract whose Q3 obligation reduces, for these rules, to a decidable syntactic match (each fire emits exactly the witness its SF trigger's `∃` is missing) — the two combining in Q-EXT; whereas `|⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}| < ∞` quantifies over all reachable states and is as meta-level as H-W — a bound on arguments rather than on trigger-true step-instances, but reachability-quantified all the same.

**H-RF (FiniteRealFires).** A fire sequence from Σ₀ has *finitely many real fires* iff its real (non-no-op) fires are finite in number. This — not H-W — is the operative hypothesis of the termination theorem below, and it must be kept distinct from H-W, which is strictly stronger. H-W bounds trigger-*true* step-instances (`|W(σ)| < ∞`); H-RF bounds only the *fires*. The two come apart precisely at starvation: an SF trigger-true argument the scheduler never fires keeps its triple `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)` at *every* step `k` (`|W(σ)| = ∞`) while contributing no real fire — so a registry can satisfy H-RF (indeed have zero real fires) yet violate H-W. Bounded domain growth, in particular, does *not* entail H-W; for an all-SF, extinction-disciplined registry it entails H-RF (Q5a) and no more. The structural route that supplies H-RF is therefore Q5a: an all-SF, extinction-disciplined registry with bounded domain growth, with no work bound at all. H-W would supply it too (Q5's injection — `|W(σ)| < ∞` bounds the real fires it injects into), but H-W is unsatisfiable for any registry doing concurrent work (above), so it is a *foil*, not a route: it exhibits H-RF as the strictly weaker, attainable hypothesis rather than supplying it. Q6 consumes H-RF and nothing stronger.

**Q6 (TerminationUnderFairness).** Under H-RF and H-FAIR, the system reaches a quiescent state in finitely many steps. *Proof.* H-RF bounds the real fires. If σ has *no* real fire, the constant tail is all of σ from Σ₀, and the H-FAIR argument below applies verbatim at Σ₀ (taken as the constant-tail state). Otherwise σ has a last real fire; no fire after it can be real — a trigger-true fire *is* a real fire and would exceed H-RF's finite count — so every later fire is on a trigger-false argument, a no-op by RG's no-op clause, leaving `Σ' = Σ`. The state is therefore constant past the last real fire (this is *why* the tail is no-ops — the exhausted real-fire budget, not quiescence, which is what we are proving). Were that constant tail state not quiescent, some `(ρ, x)` would be trigger-true at every state of the tail; H-FAIR forces it eventually fired (a real fire — contradiction with H-RF's exhaustion) or domain-removed (contradiction with the state's constancy). So quiescence is reached, in finitely many steps. The *finite*-σ case is the same H-FAIR step read at the end: a fair finite sequence cannot terminate at a non-quiescent state, for a trigger-true argument at the final state is left both unfired and unremoved, violating H-FAIR. ∎ Since all-SF-plus-extinction-disciplined-plus-bounded-domain-growth ⟹ H-RF (Q5a), the theorem holds in particular under all-SF + extinction-disciplined + bounded domain growth + H-FAIR. (H-W ⟹ H-RF too, by Q5's injection, so `H-W + H-FAIR` instantiates no case *beyond* `H-RF + H-FAIR`. The conjunction is not thereby *empty*: a trivial or sequential registry inhabits it — take a single rule with a single argument that starts trigger-true, which cannot be no-op-spammed while the argument stays true (firing it is the only available fire and that fire falsifies its trigger), so along *every* σ the argument is true at just one step, `|W(σ)| < ∞`, and H-FAIR holds since the argument is fired. H-W is unsatisfiable only for registries doing concurrent work; where H-W *does* hold its redundancy here is by *subsumption* under H-RF, not by emptiness — its role is to locate H-RF, never to add a case Q6 settles.) The theorem is exactly as conditional as it looks, with two hypotheses: H-RF and H-FAIR. Drop H-RF and rules can re-arm each other forever, each re-fire a fresh real fire without bound; drop H-FAIR and a trigger-true argument can be starved eternally with the system never settling. These two failure modes are genuinely separable, and neither is derivable from the other — and the starvation mode is exactly why the hypothesis is H-RF rather than H-W: a starved SF trigger-true argument drives `|W(σ)| = ∞` yet contributes no fire, so H-W is strictly stronger than what Q6 needs (and, being violated under that very scheduler, would have wrongly excluded a case Q6 settles by H-FAIR's failure instead). Extinction discipline is *not* a third independent hypothesis of Q6: it is a hypothesis of Q5a, the structural route that supplies H-RF. What it earns is a cheaper route — it replaces Q5's reachability-quantified `|W(σ)| < ∞` with at-most-once-per-argument (Q-EXT, checkable at registration for the Marker-pattern rules of Q5a's route from *both* the SF spelling, via the spelling class, and the extinction discipline, whose Q3 obligation reduces there to a decidable syntactic match) plus bounded domain growth. That at-most-once half is registration-checkable for those rules; bounded domain growth is itself reachability-quantified, so the move trades one meta-level assumption for a simpler one — a bound on arguments, not on trigger-true step-instances — rather than eliminating meta-level quantification. Its load-bearing roles are Q-EXT and Q5a, not Q5/Q6.

## Scope parameterization

**SC (Scope), Q7–Q9.** A *scope* is any Boolean PL predicate `S` over addresses. A rule's domain `D_ρ ∈ QD` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) or tuple-valued (`dom(Tup)` — e.g. an audit slice `L_K`, as the worked example's `ρ_R` takes), so an address-predicate cannot in general be applied to a domain element directly: over `dom(Tup)` the element `x` is a tuple, and `S(x)` is a sort error. The scope's *restriction of ρ* is therefore the QD filter `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : S(π_ρ(x))}`, where `π_ρ` is the identity on an address-valued `D_ρ` (filter body `S(x)`) and V-TUP's address projection `addr : Tup → T` on a tuple-valued `D_ρ` (filter body `S(addr(x))`, scoping each tuple by its own address). In both cases the body is a Boolean PL predicate *at `D_ρ`'s sort*: `S` directly over `dom(T)`, and the composite `S ∘ addr` over `dom(Tup)` — well-typed by WT's PC2 plain-composition rule, `addr` a V-TUP projection `Tup → T` and `S` a closed PL predicate `T → Bool`. The filtering former (ASN-0129) admits exactly such a `P : D × S → Bool` at the domain's sort over an address- *or* tuple-valued `D`, and preserves the domain's sort; so `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : S(π_ρ(x))}` is a well-typed QD filter for every rule, tuple-domained ones included. `quiescent_{R,S}(Σ)` restricts Q0's inner quantification to the elements this filter retains. Then: **Q7** — `quiescent_{R,S}` carries Q0's status exactly: the scope adds this PC1 filter to each rule's inner quantification, the outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is the same finite static expansion (V-IDX) — so `quiescent_{R,S} ∈ PL` for a single-view registry — now counting the scope predicate `S` among the view-parameterized constituents that must share the common view, alongside the triggers and domains Q0 already counts — and a finite metalevel conjunction of separately-viewed PL predicates otherwise, decidable and observer-uniform in both cases. The claim now covers tuple-domained rules — the worked example's `ρ_R` among them, whose `dom(Tup)` filter `{c ∈ L_cmt : S(addr(c))}` is well-typed by the projection above. Scope quiescence inherits Q0's recognizability, no more than Q0 itself had. **Q8** — at a scope-quiescent state, in-scope fires are no-ops (absorption relativizes); out-of-scope fires are unconstrained, and an out-of-scope emission may re-arm an in-scope trigger — *re-entry* — which is itself detectable per-state by Q7. **Q9** — quiescence is anti-monotone in the scope: `S' ⟹ S` gives `quiescent_S ⟹ quiescent_{S'}`; the converse fails whenever the larger scope holds work no smaller scope sees. Canonical tiers (per-target, per-collection, system-wide) are application vocabulary over this machinery — the substrate admits any PL predicate as a scope and commits to nothing about which scopes matter.

## Worked composition

The two illustrative classes of ASN-0129's worked trace — `cmt` (Binary, idem=⊥, comment from finding to target) and `res` (Binary, idem=⊤, resolution from target to comment) — already form a registry; we verify its termination.

*Rules.* Producer `ρ_P`: domain = `{t ∈ M_tgt : needs_attention(t)}` — a QD filter (ASN-0129) over the base `M_tgt`, the members of a Unary target-marking type `tgt` (one of ASN-0129's QD bases `M_K`), with filter body `needs_attention(t) ≡ is_attn(t)` a Boolean PL membership predicate (D2, ASN-0128) over a Unary attention-flag type `attn`. The producer conjunct is read at the *active* view — so `M_tgt` and `is_attn` alike denote active slices (PC3 fixes one view per term, so the domain cannot mix an audit `M_tgt` with an active `is_attn`); `T_P` below stays SF at that nominal view all the same, reading `L_cmt`, the audit slice view-fixed by name (`L_K`, ASN-0129). The filter is well-typed — `M_tgt` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) and `is_attn : T → Bool` is Boolean at that sort — so the domain is a well-formed QD expression before any SF/extinction/bound property is claimed of the rule. It is *not* grow-only: `is_attn` at the active view is not ⊥-stable (PD1 — a fresh `attn` deposit flips it `⊥→⊤`, a retraction `⊤→⊥`), so `needs_attention` may flip either way; trigger `T_P(t) ≡ ¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))`, with `L_cmt` the audit slice (ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery) — fires by emitting one `cmt` targeting t. Folding `needs_attention` into the *domain* rather than the trigger is deliberate: it leaves `T_P` a pure negated existential (SF, below) at the cost of a non-grow-only domain, which Q5a's union bound absorbs. Resolver `ρ_R`: domain = `L_cmt` (audit slice — grow-only); trigger `T_R(c) ≡ ¬(∃ r ∈ L_res :: addr(c) ∈ coverage_G(r))` — fires by emitting one `res` targeting c.

*Class check.* With `needs_attention` moved out, both triggers are now pure negated existentials over grow-only audit slices with step-constant bodies — **SF spellings** by PD0's quantifier and Boolean rules. The move was forced: had `needs_attention` stayed a trigger conjunct, `T_P ≡ needs_attention(t) ∧ ¬(∃ …)` would *not* be SF — PD0's `∧` preserves SF only when both conjuncts are, and `needs_attention` is not ⊥-stable (it can flip `⊥→⊤` on a still-uncommented target, flipping `T_P` with it), so Q-EXT would not apply. (Spelled over *active* slices instead, neither trigger would be SF: a retraction of a resolution would re-open the comment — Q-FLIP's first falsifier — and termination would need the full H-W analysis. The audit spelling is the design choice, made checkable.)

*Extinction.* `Post_P` requires an emitted `cmt` covering t; `Post_R` an emitted `res` covering `addr(c)` — each strong enough (Q3), and strong enough *effectively*: this is Q3's decidable-match case, each contract depositing exactly the witness its trigger's `∃` quantifies over (`Post_P` a `cmt` covering t, growing `L_cmt`; `Post_R` a `res` covering `addr(c)`, growing `L_res`), so "strong enough" is settled by syntactic comparison of trigger spelling against emission form, no state quantified. The emission makes the trigger's existential true, hence the trigger false. With SF, Q-EXT gives at-most-once per argument for both rules.

*Bound (Q5a).* Both of Q5a's hypotheses now hold — every trigger an SF spelling (just shown) and every rule extinction-disciplined (the contracts above, Q3) — so by Q5a the real fires number at most `|⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}| + |⋃_k [D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ_k}|`. The resolver's domain is grow-only, so its union is just `|comments ever|`; the producer's domain is not, so its union is *the set of targets that ever need attention* — this is where the non-grow-only domain is paid for. Hence real fires ≤ |targets that ever need attention| + |comments ever|. Comments are emitted only by `ρ_P`, at most one per target — so the registry terminates iff the population of targets that ever need attention is bounded, which is the registry's one honest hypothesis on its environment. The classic failure mode is visible in the accounting: let the resolver's emissions also make fresh targets need attention (`needs_attention` true on addresses `ρ_R` creates, enlarging `⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}`) and the bound's right side grows with the left — locally disciplined rules, globally divergent registry, exactly the divergence Q4 warns locality cannot exclude. Stratification states the repair: `ρ_P` at stratum 0, `ρ_R` at stratum 1, legal iff resolver emissions never **enlarge the producer's domain** — never make a fresh target need attention. The phrasing must name domain growth, not re-arming: `T_P` is an SF spelling, so ⊥-stability (PD0) already makes re-arming an *existing* target impossible outright (a falsified `T_P(t)` — `t` commented — stays false under every later deposit, and a `res` lands in `L_res`, which `T_P` never reads). The general stratification condition "no emission re-arms a strictly lower stratum" is the right demand for a *non-SF* lower stratum; specialized to this all-SF producer, re-arm is vacuously excluded and the only divergence route left open is domain growth — which is exactly what the diagnosed failure exploits and what the legality condition must forbid.

*Quiescence.* `quiescent_R` here is precisely "every target needing attention is commented, every comment resolved" — two nested quantifications whose bodies are ST (negations of the SF triggers, PD0). The registry is single-view *at the active view*: the producer's conjunct reads active (its domain's `M_tgt`/`is_attn`, the only view-parameterized constituents in the whole registry), while both triggers and the resolver's domain `L_cmt` are view-independent (audit slices fixed by name), so every view-parameterized constituent unifies at active. By Q0, then, `quiescent_R ∈ PL`: decidable at every state, absorbing once reached (Q1), and — for this registry — reached in finitely many steps given a bounded need-attention population and any fair scheduler (Q6).

## What this note doesn't cover

- **A scheduler.** H-FAIR is stated, not constructed; scheduling disciplines, their fairness proofs, concurrency reconciliation, and violation policy (log, halt, escalate) are operational machinery this corpus deliberately leaves at the implementation layer.
- **Stochastic rule bodies.** A body that flips its trigger with probability p < 1 satisfies the deterministic contracts only operationally (retry policies, N-consecutive-clean stopping rules) — evaluator-reliability engineering, not substrate semantics.
- **The activation binding.** RG assumes rules exist; what registers them, who may, and how rule registries are themselves governed is the protocol layer above (with ASN-0130's machinery available to it).

## Open questions

1. **The SF certificate.** Q-EXT and Q5a make SF membership the load-bearing *uncertified* registration check for triggers — its companion, extinction discipline, being checkable via Q3's strong-enough contract for the marker pattern, where the obligation reduces to a decidable match (a static, not-shown-decidable obligation otherwise, Q3) — and ASN-0130 ships only the ST⁺ certificate (`pd_stable`), no SF one. Should `pd_extinct` (SF certification) ship as a designated class — exactly the catalog-growth ASN-0130's own Open Question 4 raises for certificates beyond ST⁺ — closing the loop where "this registry terminates on bounded input" is a structural lint?

2. **A PL surrogate for H-W.** H-W is meta-level; is there a PL-expressible *necessary* condition — a per-state predicate whose failure certifies unbounded work in progress — strong enough to catch the common divergence patterns (mutual re-arm cycles) at runtime, even though the full hypothesis is inexpressible?

3. **Per-scope versus global work.** Q5/Q6 are stated globally; the per-scope analogs (W restricted to in-scope arguments, termination toward `quiescent_S`) appear routine, but whether a registry can have bounded work at every scope of a tier without global H-W deserves a structural answer — it is the formal version of "every document converges but the corpus doesn't."

4. **Cross-scope oscillation.** Q8's re-entry means an outer-scope fire can repeatedly un-quiesce an inner scope. What registry or scheduling discipline bounds re-entry — and whether bounded re-entry is even the right demand, versus monotone progress *across* entries — is open, and is where a future cascade/re-opening theory would live.

5. **Contract necessity.** Q3 is sufficiency only: must every extinction-disciplined rule admit a strong-enough PL contract? A rule disciplined by reasoning PL cannot express would be sound but unverifiable at registration — the same sound-but-uncertifiable gap PD0's spelling-level classes accept by design.
