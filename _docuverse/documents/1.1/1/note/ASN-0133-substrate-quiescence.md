# ASN-0133: Substrate Quiescence

*The terminal state, recognizable from inside the predicate language; termination as a conditional theorem with every hypothesis named.*

ASN-0129 supplies the language a coordination system's conditions are written in, and the dynamics classes (PD0–PD2, ST/SF) that say how a condition's truth moves. ASN-0130 makes the conditions substrate artifacts. This note closes the arc: it defines what it is for such a system to be *done* — quiescence — and proves what can and cannot be guaranteed about reaching it. The shape of the answer is fixed by a fact about forward-chaining systems generally: unconditional termination is not provable; every honest treatment names conditions (extinction discipline, bounded work, fair scheduling) under which termination follows. The note's discipline is to put each hypothesis where it belongs — what the substrate guarantees unconditionally (recognizability, absorption), what a rule author can make checkable at registration (at-most-once firing — the SF spelling decidably via the spelling classes; the extinction discipline via a strong-enough contract, Q3 — effectively for the negated-existential marker pattern, a static-but-not-decidable obligation otherwise), and what remains assumption (fairness, bounded work) stated as named hypotheses rather than smuggled.

**Depends:** ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework), ASN-0128 (Substrate Type Operational Semantics), ASN-0129 (Substrate Predicate Composition), ASN-0130 (Predicate Definitions as Substrate Content).

## What this note commits

- **The rule model** (RG): a minimal trigger/emission unit — a domain in QD, a Boolean PL trigger, an emission contract over the operation surface — with the action body deliberately opaque: the contract constrains outputs, never algorithms. A registry is a finite set of rules; a rule's trigger may be a registered definition address (ASN-0130), making registries themselves substrate-described. The substrate is *shared* — a fire sequence interleaves the registry's own fires with **environment steps** (non-registry `→_sh` transitions), so the termination hypotheses below bound *external input*, and the closed, environment-free special case collapses them to restatements of their own conclusions.
- **Recognizability and absorption, unconditional** (Q0, Q1): quiescence — no trigger true on any domain element — is decidable at every reachable state by any observer (a single PL term for *every* registry, the heterogeneous-view case paying an explicit fixed-view-base rewrite — PC3's cross-view device — not the property), pure, and a fixed point of firing once reached.
- **Extinction discipline** (X-DEF, Q2–Q4) with the class-theoretic upgrade (Q-EXT): a rule whose trigger is an **SF spelling** (ASN-0129) and whose fires falsify it gets *at-most-once firing per argument* as a theorem — the falsification is permanent by the class, not by analysis of other rules. This is the Marker pattern's termination half, and the certificate that makes it registration-checkable is exactly the SF certificate ASN-0130's Open Question 4 anticipates.
- **Conditional termination** (Q5, Q5a, Q6): the termination theorem turns on a single operative hypothesis, *finitely many real fires from Σ₀* (H-RF). The structural route that supplies it is Q5a: for an all-SF, *extinction-disciplined* registry, bounded domain growth gives H-RF directly. The bounded-work hypothesis (H-W) *would* supply H-RF too (Q5's injection), but H-W is not merely meta-level — on the shared substrate it is **unsatisfiable** for any registry that ever reaches a trigger-true argument: an unfair scheduler that no-op-spams a trigger-false argument while a trigger-true one persists, *or simply the environment holding a trigger-true argument true across its own steps*, drives `|W(σ)| = ∞`. H-W is therefore a *foil*, not a route — its sole service is to locate H-RF as the strictly weaker, attainable hypothesis. Extinction discipline is not a hypothesis of Q5's bound; with the SF spelling it is what Q5a's route requires, trading the cross-rule re-arm analysis for a domain-growth bound. Quiescence is then *reached* after finitely many real fires under H-RF and H-FAIR *when domains are grow-only* — and *held* thereafter, since all-SF's SF triggers bar re-arming of *settled* content; for a **non-grow-only** domain weak H-FAIR plus bounded growth delivers only the registry-side guarantee (finitely many real fires, registry-inert past N), and *reaching* a quiescent state — not merely *holding* one — requires the environment to eventually stop cycling unfired arguments (environment-idle, regime (i)) or a strong fairness (H-SFAIR, Q6): an environment that cycles finitely many flagged-but-unfired targets out of phase keeps every state non-quiescent while satisfying H-RF, bounded growth, and weak H-FAIR alike. The hypotheses are named, stated, and not discharged here: bounded domain growth is reachability-quantified (a bound over external input across all reachable states), so of Q5a's route the at-most-once-firing-per-argument half — resting on *both* the SF spelling (via the spelling class, decidably) and the extinction discipline (via a strong-enough contract, Q3, whose obligation reduces to a decidable match for the marker-pattern rules Q5a's route is built on) — is registration-checkable there, but not the domain bound; and H-FAIR is a scheduler property this corpus has chosen not to axiomatize beyond the statement.
- **Scope parameterization** (Q7–Q9): everything above relativizes to a scope — any Boolean PL predicate, applied to a tuple-domained rule through a *rule-chosen* scoping body (per-emitter by `addr`, per-target by the denoted-target coverage, per-source symmetrically) rather than one fixed projection — with scope quiescence carrying Q0's status (a single PL term for every registry), recognizability and absorption inherited per-scope, and anti-monotone nesting (outer quiescence implies inner, never conversely).
- **The falsifier accounting** (Q-FLIP): what can re-arm a trigger is not "retraction" alone but a wider falsifier inventory (ASN-0129's FP, read with PD1/PD2) — retraction, BH1-type emissions under the default view, BH4's home-traffic footprint, *and bare deposits growing an active slice* (which flip `∃`-shaped or non-monotone-verdict triggers, PD1/PD2). The folklore "no retraction ⟹ triggers flip at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery, and this note replaces it with the class-checked accounting.

## The rule model

**RG (Rule, Registry).** A *rule* is a triple `ρ = (D_ρ, T_ρ, Post_ρ)`: a domain expression `D_ρ ∈ QD`, a Boolean trigger `T_ρ : D_ρ → Bool` in PL, and an *emission contract* `Post_ρ` — a *meta-level* contract over (argument, state, emitted call set) constraining what any fire of ρ must emit, the calls drawn from the operation surface `{Emit_K, Nullify_Binary}` (ASN-0128; `Observe_K` reads, and reads are not emissions). The contract is meta-level, *not* a PL term: PL has no sort for emitted call sets (COD lists none, ASN-0129) and a PL term reads a single state, whereas the contract speaks of what a fire *emits* — so `Post_ρ` is written in the surface's emission forms, never evaluated as a PL verdict. What *is* PL is the trigger `T_ρ`, whose pre- and post-state evaluations decide extinction (Q2); the contract enters this note only through registration-time implications relating those PL verdicts to the emission forms (Q3). A *registry* `R` is a finite set of rules. A *fire* of `(ρ, x)` at Σ with `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` is: if `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊥`, a no-op (`Σ' = Σ`); otherwise the application of some emission set satisfying `Post_ρ(x, Σ, ·)` — a finite sequence of `→_sh` steps through the surface (the gated relation, ASN-0126, over extended-record states by R-TR, ASN-0128), `Σ →_sh* Σ'`. **(H-FIN, a named hypothesis on the fire itself.)** That the sequence is finite is inherited, not assumed away: each surface call (`Emit_K`, `Nullify_Binary`) is a single bounded `→_sh` step (ASN-0128), so a fire terminates exactly when the emission set it discharges is *finite* — and H-FIN is the contract-level demand that *every* `Post_ρ`-satisfying emission set is finite (equivalently: every admissible fire sequence terminates), not merely that the contract *admits* some finite set. The universal is forced by the very nondeterminism the next sentence turns on: a contract admitting both a finite and an infinite emission set would satisfy a bare `∃`-reading while leaving a body free to choose the infinite one and diverge — at which point the fire has no post-state `Σ'` and the sequence model is undefined — so H-FIN must demand *all* admissible sets be finite. This is separate from the *registry*-level termination (Q5/Q6) of the fire *sequence*. **(H-ATOM, fire atomicity.)** A fire's post-state `Σ'` is read as the state *immediately* after the fire's own `→_sh` steps, with *no* environment step interleaved among them — the fire is *atomic* against environment interleaving, and σ's interleaving (H-FAIR) places environment steps only *between* fires, never inside one. This must be named, not smuggled into the σ construction: X-DEF and Q2 read extinction off the fire's *own* post-state `Σ'` (`T_ρ(x, Σ') = ⊥`), so an environment step landing mid-fire could perturb that post-value for a non-monotone trigger, leaving the extinction test ill-posed. The assumption is not free in general — ASN-0128's I4 serializes *individual* `→_sh` steps, not contiguous *runs* of them — so for a *multi-step* fire H-ATOM is a genuine obligation, discharged only by the deferred scheduler (which must serialize a fire's run as a unit), and we carry it as a standing scheduler obligation rather than a substrate guarantee. But it costs the load-bearing results *nothing*: the marker-pattern fires that carry Q5a and Q6 — and every rule of the worked composition — emit exactly *one* tuple, a *single* `→_sh` step, and a one-step fire is atomic for free, exactly by I4's per-step serialization, no scheduler hypothesis needed. H-ATOM therefore enters with teeth only for registries whose contracts emit multi-tuple sets. The body that *chooses* the emissions is deliberately outside the model: two implementations producing the same emissions are indistinguishable, so every property below is a property of contracts and substrate state, never of algorithms — and because a contract may admit many emission sets, every termination claim below is read **universally over the admissible choices**: a nondeterministic body terminates iff *every* `Post_ρ`-satisfying fire sequence it can produce does. The registry is, moreover, only *one* actor on a shared substrate: other agents, other registries, and whatever feeds the docuverse its raw work all emit through the same surface. The system's evolution therefore interleaves the registry's own fires with **environment steps** — non-registry `→_sh` transitions the registry neither issues nor controls — and a *fire sequence* `σ` (made precise at H-FAIR) is an interleaving of the two from `Σ₀`. Between two fires a rule domain `[D_ρ]` may grow *or* shrink under an environment step: a fresh target flagged for attention, a slice retracted out from under a trigger. This *open* reading is the one the termination hypotheses below are built for — it is environment input, not the registry's own fire-reachable states, that the bounded-domain-growth hypothesis (Q5a) bounds. The *closed* special case, in which no environment step ever fires and the registry alone moves the state, collapses that hypothesis to a restatement of its own conclusion (Q5a): with the registry the only depositor, `⋃_k [D_ρ]` can grow only by the registry's own deposits, so bounded domain growth becomes *equivalent* to H-RF (unbounded growth forces unbounded real fires, and conversely), and the Q5a route carries no content beyond directly assuming H-RF. This is emphatically *not* auto-termination: a closed registry that grows its own domain — each fire extinguishing its own argument yet depositing a fresh trigger-true one (an SF marker rule whose contract emits both its own falsifier and a new domain member) — diverges while satisfying every Q5a hypothesis (all-SF, extinction-disciplined) *but* the domain bound. QD-fin (ASN-0129) pins each `[D_ρ]_{Σ_k}` finite *at its own state* and says nothing about the cumulative `⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}` over the generally infinite sequence — the quantity H-RF and the bound turn on — so "leaving nothing to assume" must be read as "no assumption beyond H-RF," not "no assumption." The conditionality the worked example advertises is thus a fact about *open* systems specifically. A trigger may be given as a registered definition address (`pdef`, ASN-0130) evaluated by reference (PR3) — the registry is then itself substrate content, its triggers linkable, versioned, and certified like any definition. Because evaluation-by-reference keys on *ever-registration*, not active registration (PR3), such a trigger stays evaluable — and quiescence (Q0) stays recognizable — even after its definition is de-registered: the content persists, so the verdict never becomes undecidable for want of a withdrawn endorsement. A trigger that itself *references* a since-de-registered definition is the dangling-live-reference case ASN-0130's Open Question 3 leaves open; its evaluation still computes (PR3 reads content, not registration), but it crosses a revoked endorsement, and what that should mean is settled there, not here. A registry built from inline PL triggers depends on ASN-0130 not at all — its triggers are PL terms evaluated directly (PC4/PC5), and every property below rests on ASN-0129 and the operation surface alone. The pdef-trigger option is the only place ASN-0130 enters, and there it enters with ASN-0130's own scoping: evaluation-by-reference carries its guarantees on registration-disciplined derivations (PR-DISC, ASN-0130) — the regime in which the audit-slice spelling of "ever-registered" and the standing registration coincide. A registry whose triggers are pdef addresses therefore inherits PR-DISC as a standing hypothesis, discharged the same way ASN-0130 discharges it (every `pdef` deposit enters through `register_pred`); a registry of inline triggers carries no such hypothesis. Recognizability and absorption (Q0, Q1) hold unconditionally in both cases — PR-DISC conditions only the link between a pdef-trigger's address and the definition it is taken to name, never the decidability of the trigger's PL verdict.

**Q0 (Recognizability).** Define `quiescent_R(Σ) ≡ (∀ ρ ∈ R :: (∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ)))`. The outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is *not* a PC1 quantification — `R` is a finite metalevel set of rule-triples (for an inline-trigger registry, not even substrate content), not a QD domain — but a *finite static expansion* into a PC0 conjunction, the move ASN-0129's V-IDX makes explicit for `Reg`-quantification ("Defined by static expansion… denotes the finite conjunction"). Each conjunct `(∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ))` is a PL predicate: PC1 over a QD domain, finite at every reachable state (QD-fin, ASN-0129), with `¬` by PC0. Whether the conjunction is *itself* a single PL term turns on views — PC0 conjunction requires its constituents to read the same view (PC3), and RG constrains no two triggers to a common one. What that blocks is only the *naive* merge — the one that leaves each view-parameterized atom at its native view; for a heterogeneous-view registry that merge is ill-formed. But PC3 furnishes more than the same-view constraint, and the stronger reading is the correct one. `A_K` and `L_K` are *fixed-view bases* — they denote the active and audit K-slices *at every term view* — and through them PC3's own cross-view derivations rebuild each view-parameterized read at a freely chosen common view, value intact: `members(K, audit) = ⋃(L_K, addrs_F)` and `members(K, active) = ⋃(A_K, addrs_F)` (an audit read thus rebuilds inside an active-view term and conversely), `targets_of` and `is_K` rebuild by the same `⋃`/`∃`-over-fixed-base device (V-AUD gives the audit readings directly), and a default-view collection rebuilds as `{x ∈ ⋃(A_K, addrs_F) : ¬(∃ J :: is_filtered_J(x))}` over those same fixed bases. PC3 names *exactly four* view-parameterized constituents — `members`, `targets_of`, `is_K`, `M_K` — and each rebuilds as above. View-parameterization is not, however, the whole of view-sensitivity, and the merge must account for the rest. UV (ASN-0129) rewrites *six* collection atoms under the default view: `members` and `targets_of`, already among the four, *and* the four *fixed-view* behavior collections `succs`, `sources_to`, the sequence `chain` returns, and `stale` (BH2/BH3/BH4). These four are not view-parameterized in PC3's sense — they read the active slice at *every* term view, so their audit and active readings coincide — yet under `v = default` their *results* are filtered, so a default-view read of one differs in value from its active read, and a Boolean trigger taking that read does *not* "already sit at one fixed slice." Each rebuilds over the same fixed-view bases at the chosen common view all the same. The three set-valued ones rebuild as a QD filter over the atom's own raw active reading — `{y ∈ succs(x) : ¬(∃ J :: is_filtered_J(y))}`, and symmetrically for `sources_to` and `stale` — a `℘_fin(T)`-valued domain by set-valued closure, its body the Boolean `is_filtered_J`, which UV never rewrites and which is therefore view-stable. `chain` is `Seq_fin(T)`-valued and PL has *no* sequence-to-sequence filter former, so a Boolean trigger reaches its default-view value only through one of the two sequence-eliminating primitives — V-PRIM's order-forgetting projection `elems(chain(x))`, a `℘_fin(T)` term taking the same set-valued filter, or the verdict atom `is_in_chain`, which UV never rewrites and so reads identically at every view, needing no rebuild. Everything else a Boolean trigger or a QD domain can read — the verdict/optional atoms `is_in_chain`, `tip`, `age`, `targets_keyed` (never UV-rewritten, by UV's Verdicts-and-optionals and Booleans clauses) and the fixed-view slices `A_K`/`L_K` themselves — *is* genuinely view-stable: one fixed slice, one value at every term view. So every view-sensitive part of every trigger *and* of every domain — a domain's `M_K` rebuilding as the set-valued term `⋃(A_K/L_K, addrs_F)`, a QD domain by set-valued closure — can be moved to one chosen term view, and the per-rule conjuncts, so rewritten, PC0-conjoin into a single PC3-conforming term. Hence `quiescent_R ∈ PL` for *every* registry, single-view or not: the single-view registry conjoins as written, the heterogeneous one pays an explicit fixed-view-base rewrite — a change of spelling, not of value (PC4) — but lands in PL all the same. (A genuinely non-unifiable trigger would require a *view-sensitive* read with *no* fixed-view-base rewrite; the vocabulary of ASN-0129 has none — the four view-parameterized constituents together with the four UV-rewritten fixed-view collections `succs`/`sources_to`/`chain`/`stale` exhaust the view-sensitive forms, and each rebuilds: the set-valued ones by a fixed-view filter, `chain` through `elems` or the view-stable `is_in_chain`.) Its value at every reachable Σ is therefore decidable in finite time by any observer from Σ and the registry alone — one PL term, pure (PC4) and terminating (PC5), its evaluation finitely many verdicts. Purity makes every verdict observer-uniform — no agent reports, no consensus, no decision history. ∎

**Q1 (Absorption).** A quiescent state is a fixed point of firing: at any Σ with `quiescent_R(Σ)`, every fire of every `(ρ, x)` is a no-op, so `Σ' = Σ` — and the registry may halt its firing on first detection, no further monitoring of its own activity required (a later environment step may re-arm a trigger — re-entry, Q8 — but that is fresh external input, not a fire). *Proof.* Immediate from RG's no-op clause and Q0's definition. ∎ Recognizability and absorption are *unconditional* — they hold for undisciplined registries, unfair schedulers, and divergent systems alike. They say nothing about whether quiescence is reachable; that is the rest of the note.

## Extinction discipline

Reaching quiescence requires that firing *consumes* the condition that fired. Without a contract to that effect, a rule can fire forever on the same argument.

**X-DEF (ExtinctionDisciplined).** Rule ρ is *extinction-disciplined* iff for every reachable Σ and `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` with `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊤`, every fire of `(ρ, x)` yields Σ' with `T_ρ(x, Σ') = ⊥`. A registry is extinction-disciplined iff each rule is.

**Q2 (ContractOnOutputs).** Extinction discipline constrains emissions, not bodies: it is decided by `T_ρ` evaluations at the pre- and post-states — both public PL facts — so any two bodies with the same outputs are equivalent under it, and nondeterministic bodies are admissible whenever *all* their permitted outputs flip the trigger.

**Q3 (StaticCheckability).** If `Post_ρ` is *strong enough* — *every* emission set satisfying it at a trigger-true `(x, Σ)` produces a post-state falsifying `T_ρ(x, ·)` — then ρ is extinction-disciplined. The condition is *static*: it speaks only of PL-typed objects and the surface's emission forms, never of a running body. But *static* is not *effective* — being non-executable buys only that no body need be run, not that the obligation is decidable — and the load-bearing claims must not conflate the two. The condition carries a quantifier over `(x, Σ)`, and its scope fixes its standing against this note's own checkable/meta-level line, the line that disqualifies H-W and bounded domain growth precisely *for* quantifying over reachable states. Read at the **reachable level** — `(x, Σ)` over reachable trigger-true pairs, the reading that matches X-DEF's own "for every reachable Σ" — the condition is reachability-quantified, hence *meta-level by this note's own standard*, no more registration-evaluable than H-W: it soundly *characterizes* discipline but is no decision procedure for it. Read at the **schema level** — `(x, Σ)` over *all* states — it is instead a sound over-approximation of X-DEF (all-states ⟹ reachable-states, so a schema-valid contract is disciplined; the converse can fail, so the check may *reject* a contract disciplined only on its reachable states), but schema-validity of "every `Post_ρ`-satisfying deposit falsifies this PL trigger" is a validity question over PL — which carries PC1 quantifiers and PC2a aggregates — composed with the emission semantics, *not shown decidable here and given no checking procedure*; even this reading yields a static obligation, not a general effective check. Where the check *is* effective is the **negated-existential marker pattern**, the load-bearing case (Q-EXT, Q5a, the worked example): the trigger is `¬(∃ c ∈ L_K :: a ∈ coverage_G(c))` over a grow-only audit slice, and `Post_ρ` deposits *exactly the witness the `∃` quantifies over* — a `K`-tuple covering `a` — so the deposit grows `L_K`, the existential goes ⊤, the trigger goes ⊥, and "strong enough" is decided by a finite syntactic comparison of trigger spelling against emission form, no state quantified. One subtlety attends an **idem=⊤** marker class, where `Emit_K` is a *no-op on a dedup hit* (ASN-0128 I1) and deposits nothing: "the deposit grows `L_K`" then needs a hit ruled out, and the audit-slice spelling rules it out for free. The trigger reads the *audit* slice `L_K`, so firing (`T_ρ(a, Σ) = ⊤`) certifies that *no* `c ∈ L_K` covers `a`; a dedup hit would be an *active* tuple I0-equal to the emitted witness — its `G` covering `a` by the coverage equality — and the active subset injects into the audit slice (`A_K ⊆ L_K` by ActiveSubset, ASN-0086; the dedup test reads `A_K`, ASN-0128 I2), so that hit-witness would *itself* be a `c ∈ L_K` covering `a`, contradicting the fire. Fire and dedup-hit cannot co-occur: the emit is necessarily a *miss*, deposits, and grows `L_K`. The same audit reading makes even a *born-nullified* deposit suffice — born-nullified means *in* `L_K`, absent from `A_K` (ASN-0128 I3) — so the existential, ranging over the audit slice, goes ⊤ whether or not the deposit lands active. (An idem=⊥ class never dedups, so its deposit is unconditional.) There "verifiable at registration" is sound *and* effective; the general statement is the sound-but-not-effective envelope around it, and every claim below resting on Q3 (Q5a, Q6) is scoped to this pattern. The companion SF-spelling half of at-most-once is, by contrast, genuinely decidable wherever invoked — PD0's ST/SF rules are syntax-directed, like WT, the very rules ASN-0130's `certify_pd_stable` runs — so it is the extinction half alone this note must scope. Sufficient, not necessary: a trivially weak contract (`Post_ρ ≡ ⊤`) certifies nothing, and a rule may be disciplined for reasons no contract of this shape expresses. Failure-to-verify is not violation.

**Q4 (Locality).** Extinction discipline is per-rule: its definition mentions no other rule. Registries compose pointwise — and precisely because the property is local, it cannot alone be sufficient for termination: locally disciplined rules can re-arm *each other* without bound (H-W is doing real work).

**Q-EXT (ExtinctionByClass).** If `T_ρ` is an **SF spelling** (⊥-stable, PD0, ASN-0129), extinction discipline strengthens to *at-most-once firing per argument along any derivation*: the disciplined fire makes `T_ρ(x, ·)` false, and SF makes false permanent — no later `→_sh` step re-arms it, whether another rule's fire *or an environment step*, on any target: PD0's ⊥-stability is a property of the step relation, indifferent to who issues the step. This *step-agnosticism* is load-bearing for the open model — it is exactly why Q5a's bound survives environment input. The count of real fires of ρ is then bounded by the total growth of `[D_ρ]` alone, with no analysis of other rules' emissions *or of the environment's*. *Proof.* Immediate composition of X-DEF with PD0's ⊥-stability. ∎ This is the design rule the Marker pattern instantiates: spell the trigger as "no completion marker recorded" (`¬∃` over a grow-only audit slice — ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery, SF by the quantifier rule), and fire by emitting the marker. The check is registration-time and spelling-level — exactly the SF certificate ASN-0130's Open Question 4 anticipates; with it, and with the extinction half here in Q3's decidable-match case (the fire emits exactly the marker the trigger's `∃` is missing), "every rule in this registry is at-most-once" becomes a structural lint for Marker-pattern registries.

**Q-FLIP (FalsifierAccounting).** For triggers *not* in SF, what can re-arm them is the falsifier inventory ASN-0129's FP enumerates, read off with PD1/PD2: a retraction shrinking an active slice the trigger reads; a BH1-type emission moving a default-view result; a BH4-footprint change from any deposit in a watched home; and — the re-armer that is no retraction at all — a *bare deposit growing an active slice*, which flips an `∃`-shaped active-view trigger `⊥→⊤` (PD1: `(∃ x ∈ M_K :: P(x))` at view active "flips ⊥→⊤ on a K-deposit") or perturbs a non-monotone verdict atom such as `target_of`/`targets_keyed` (PD2: a term containing `targets_keyed` is "perturbed by deposits of every BH3-attached Binary type"). The deposit re-armer bites even on a `¬`-shaped, extinguishable trigger: for a BH3-attached Binary `K`, the trigger `T(s) ≡ ¬def(target_of(s, K))` is ⊤ with no active `K`-tuple from `s`, is extinguished to ⊥ by firing one tuple `s → y`, then is re-armed to ⊤ by a *second* active tuple `s → y'` that turns `target_of(s, K)` to ⊥ ("several", ASN-0128 BH3) — no retraction, no BH1, no BH4. The folklore rule "no retraction ⟹ each trigger flips at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery — a registry whose triggers read default views, age atoms, *or growing active slices* re-arms without any retraction. (SF triggers are immune: ⊥-stability (PD0) makes a falsified SF trigger permanent against every item above, deposits included — exactly why Q-EXT and Q5a demand the SF spelling, alongside the extinction discipline that supplies the falsification SF then makes permanent.) Trigger-flip counting must be done per spelling class, and the conservative design stance falls out: *triggers in SF, termination conditions in ST*, both certifiable.

## Conditional termination

**H-FAIR (FairnessHypothesis).** A *fire sequence* `σ = (Σ₀, s₁, Σ₁, s₂, …)` interleaves, from `Σ₀`, two kinds of step: a *fire* of some `(ρ, x)` (RG) — atomic against environment interleaving (H-ATOM: environment steps fall *between* fires, never within one; free for single-step marker fires, a scheduler obligation otherwise) — and an *environment step* — any non-registry `→_sh*` transition (another agent or registry, or external input), which the scheduler neither issues nor constrains. Both advance the state through the gated relation; the registry authors only its fires. `σ` is *fair* iff for every `(ρ, x)` and *every* index `k` with `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at `Σ_k`, some later index `m > k` *discharges that occurrence* one of three ways: **real-fired** (a non-no-op fire of `(ρ, x)` at a step past `k`), **removed** from its domain (`x ∉ [D_ρ]_{Σ_m}`), or **falsified in place** (`T_ρ(x, Σ_m) = ⊥` with `x ∈ [D_ρ]_{Σ_m}` still). This is the *per-occurrence* reading, not once-per-argument: every trigger-true index incurs its own later discharge, so a single earlier discharge cannot excuse an argument that *stays* trigger-true across a later tail — the strength Q6's regime (i) and the finite-σ case both turn on, and the strength H-SFAIR's strong form must supply (below). The third discharge is not optional bookkeeping: a trigger can stop being true without its argument leaving the domain — the SF marker trigger `¬(∃ c :: x ∈ coverage_G(c))` goes ⊥ the instant *any* actor (the environment included, emitting through the same surface) deposits a covering tuple, leaving `x` in-domain but trigger-false — and a scheduler controls only its *fires*, so it cannot prevent the environment from falsifying `x` first. Without the falsification escape, *no* scheduler would satisfy H-FAIR in any environment that ever falsifies a trigger it did not itself fire, gutting the open model; with it, the obligation is one a scheduler can actually meet. *"Fired" here means real-fired*: a no-op fire discharges nothing — it neither advances the state nor consumes the trigger — and Q6 reads the discharge at this strength. A domain can shrink under a fire's retraction *or* under an environment step, and the scheduler owes nothing to absent or already-falsified arguments. Fairness binds only the *registry's* scheduling of its fires; it places no obligation on the environment, which may create, remove, and falsify trigger-true arguments as it likes. Fairness is a scheduler property; this corpus states it as a hypothesis and deliberately ships no scheduler note — any discipline satisfying the statement (round-robin, queue-fair, priority with aging) discharges it, the removal and falsification escapes absorbing exactly the environmental interference a fire-only scheduler cannot forestall.

**H-SFAIR (StrongFairnessHypothesis).** A fire sequence `σ` is *strongly fair* iff every `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at *infinitely many* indices `Σ_k` along σ is *real-fired at infinitely many indices* — GF-taken, not merely *eventually* taken once. This is the standard *infinitely-often-enabled ⟹ infinitely-often-taken* strengthening, and it differs from H-FAIR in the two *non-firing escapes* H-FAIR allows: removal and in-domain falsification. H-FAIR discharges its obligation to a `(ρ, x)` by real-firing, removal, *or* in-domain falsification, so an argument the environment removes (or, for a non-SF trigger, re-falsifies) before each scheduled fire and then re-arms satisfies H-FAIR forever without ever firing — each trigger-true occurrence answered by the following withdrawal or falsification. H-SFAIR closes both escapes for the *infinitely-recurring* arguments: one trigger-true at infinitely many indices must be *real-fired at infinitely many indices*, removals and re-falsifications notwithstanding. It is genuinely stronger — *for infinite σ*, `H-SFAIR ⟹ H-FAIR` (the per-occurrence H-FAIR above): an infinitely-recurring argument is, by H-SFAIR's infinitely-often firing, real-fired at some index past *every* one of its trigger-true indices, so *each occurrence* is discharged by a later fire — the per-occurrence obligation met after each, not merely once for the argument; and an argument trigger-true only *finitely* often is, past its last true index `K_last`, permanently not-trigger-true, so each of its finitely many occurrences (all `≤ K_last`) is discharged by the removal-or-falsification holding at every index past `K_last` — a non-firing escape, the very escapes whose absence would leave the two *incomparable*, since such an argument satisfies H-SFAIR vacuously yet, under a firing-or-removal-only H-FAIR, could violate it. The restriction to *infinite* σ is not idle: the finitely-recurring branch just discharged each occurrence by a removal-or-falsification *at some index past `K_last`*, which presupposes σ *has* an index past `K_last`. A *finite* σ whose final state is trigger-true for `(ρ, x)` — `K_last` the final index, with no later index existing — satisfies H-SFAIR *vacuously* (no argument is trigger-true at infinitely many indices in a finite sequence) yet *violates* H-FAIR (that terminal occurrence admits no later discharge of any of the three kinds), so `H-SFAIR ⟹ H-FAIR` *fails* on exactly the finite-σ case the note elsewhere gives teeth (Q6's "a fair finite sequence cannot end at a non-quiescent state"). Finite σ are governed instead by H-FAIR's own end-of-sequence obligation and lie outside H-SFAIR's vacuous reach; scoping the implication to infinite σ costs nothing downstream, since H-SFAIR is invoked in exactly one role (below), on infinite oscillating sequences. The *weak* reading struck above — *eventually* real-fired, a single fire per argument — supplies only one discharge and so fails the per-occurrence obligation (an argument fired once at index 5 but trigger-true ever after, never re-fired, leaves its index-6 occurrence undischarged); GF-taken is exactly what makes the implication go through. The gap is exactly the oscillating, non-grow-only arguments H-FAIR lets the environment starve by withdrawal or by re-falsification of a non-SF trigger. It binds only the *registry's* scheduling — like H-FAIR, it places no obligation on the environment, which may present, withdraw, and falsify arguments at will. Its *satisfiability*, though, is not H-FAIR's, and the parallel breaks in exactly the dimension that matters. H-FAIR is satisfiable against an arbitrary environment precisely because its removal and falsification escapes absorb the interference a fire-only scheduler cannot forestall; H-SFAIR *removes* the removal escape for infinitely-recurring arguments (it demands GF-taken — real-firing), and that removal makes its satisfiability environment-*conditional*. Take the withdrawal mechanism to the limit: the interleaving model admits consecutive environment steps (no turn-fairness is stated), so an admissible environment can, around each scheduler turn, *add* `xᵢ` then *remove* it — from `xᵢ` absent, one environment step makes it trigger-true, the next removes it, the scheduler's turn then acts on a pre-state with `xᵢ` absent and cannot fire it, and the environment re-adds it, repeating forever. Now `xᵢ` is trigger-true at infinitely many indices (infinitely-recurring) yet absent at *every* scheduler pre-state, so *no* discipline ever real-fires it — priority-with-aging included, since aging only orders *in-domain* arguments. Then *no* σ in this environment satisfies H-SFAIR: it is unsatisfiable by *any* scheduler here, not merely undischarged by a standard one. H-SFAIR is therefore satisfiable only under an interleaving/turn-fairness in which the scheduler eventually gets to fire any persistently- or recurrently-*presented* argument — a condition the withdraw-before-every-fire environment above violates, and one this note neither states nor derives. That full turn/serialization model is the scheduler layer's to fix and is deferred with the rest of the scheduling discipline; only the satisfiability *claim* is repaired here. Under such a turn-fairness, standard disciplines (priority with aging across recurrences) discharge it. This also tempers H-SFAIR's standing as an *independent* alternative to regime (i): Q6 offers, for a non-grow-only rule, that the environment "eventually leaves each argument in-domain and trigger-true until fired (regime (i) for the rule), or *strong* fairness (H-SFAIR)" — but because an all-SF fired argument settles permanently, H-SFAIR-satisfiability *itself* requires the environment to eventually leave each recurring argument in-domain and trigger-true at some scheduler turn, essentially regime (i)'s own condition for that rule. The "or" is thus nearer a restatement than a genuinely disjoint second route: H-SFAIR relocates the environmental concession into a fairness premise rather than dispensing with it. The corpus states it as a named hypothesis, ships no scheduler discharging it, and invokes it in exactly one role: where a non-grow-only domain lets the environment oscillate an argument's membership (Q6's non-grow-only regime, instantiated by the producer `ρ_P` of the worked composition).

**W, H-W (BoundedWork).** `W(σ)` is the set of (rule, argument, index) triples at which a trigger is true along σ; the registry has *bounded work from Σ₀* (H-W) iff `|W(σ)| < ∞` for every σ from Σ₀. H-W is a registry-level hypothesis, and *provably meta-level*: its statement quantifies over the states reachable by fire sequences, and reachability is a fixed-point notion PL deliberately cannot express (PC6a ships no fixpoint operator; the closure admits only finite quantification). The substrate can therefore neither evaluate H-W at a state nor monitor it as a predicate — a runtime defense against its violation is necessarily bookkeeping (fire counts, thresholds), not evaluation. The structural conditions one might hope discharge it do not, and it is worth seeing why. Of the three below, only the first is *proved* to supply even the weaker H-RF; the other two supply it only with an extra bound adjoined that is no structural freebie. An all-SF, extinction-disciplined registry with bounded domain growth (Q5a) bounds *real fires* — proved there, from at-most-once-per-argument plus the domain bound — and so supplies H-RF. A *stratified* registry — `stratum : R → ℕ` with no rule's emissions re-arming a strictly lower stratum — supplies H-RF only once *per-stratum* real-fire bounds are adjoined, and those are themselves a per-stratum H-RF hypothesis: a decomposition of the conclusion, not an independent structural condition that delivers it. Acyclicity of the emission/re-arm graph supplies H-RF only with a domain-growth bound adjoined — acyclicity *alone* does not, since a single rule firing on an unboundedly growing domain has a vacuously acyclic re-arm graph (nothing re-arms anything) yet unbounded real fires, exactly the divergence route Q5a's *separate* domain-growth clause exists to exclude. None of the three, however equipped, bounds `W`: H-W quantifies over *every* σ, unfair schedulers included, and an unfair scheduler that starves a persistently-true trigger drives `|W(σ)| = ∞` whatever the registry's structure — so these conditions supply at most the weaker H-RF, never H-W itself. None is necessary even for that. Sharper than meta-level unverifiability: the same starvation argument shows H-W *false*, not merely inexpressible. Whenever a reachable state holds one trigger-true argument beside one trigger-false in-domain argument, an unfair σ can no-op-spam the false argument forever — freezing the state — while the true argument's triple `(ρ, x, k)` recurs at every index `k`, so `|W(σ)| = ∞` and H-W fails. That is essentially every registry that does concurrent work, the worked example below included (a commented-but-still-needs-attention target sits trigger-false and in-domain beside an uncommented one). The concurrent-work caveat is itself a closed-model artifact: the no-op-spam argument needs a second, false argument to advance σ while the true one waits, but in the open sequence the *environment* supplies that advance for free — irrelevant deposits keep σ moving while a lone trigger-true argument waits its fair fire — so H-W fails for essentially any registry that ever reaches a trigger-true argument, single-rule registries no longer excepted (Q6's parenthetical). H-W is therefore not a usable route to H-RF but a *foil*: Q5's injection shows H-W *would* bound real fires, and that is its entire service — to exhibit H-RF as the strictly weaker, attainable hypothesis the structural route (Q5a) actually supplies.

**Q5 (RealFiresAreBounded).** Under H-W *alone*, every fire sequence from Σ₀ contains at most `|W(σ)|` real (non-no-op) fires. *Proof.* Each real fire at step k+1 witnesses `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)`, and the map real-fire ↦ `(ρ, x, k)` is injective *by the step index alone* (the index ranging over σ's positions, environment steps among them): distinct real fires occupy distinct steps, so their triples differ in the index component — extinguished or not. Injection into a finite set bounds the count. Extinction discipline is neither used nor needed: a rule spinning on a fixed `x` would witness infinitely many `(ρ, x, k₁), (ρ, x, k₂), …`, forcing `|W(σ)| = ∞`, so H-W already forbids spinning outright. (Extinction's leverage is elsewhere — Q-EXT and Q5a — where it lets bounded domain growth supply the same finite-real-fire conclusion (H-RF, below) directly, with no `W(σ)` bound at all; it does *not* discharge H-W, which a starved SF trigger-true argument violates while real fires stay finite.) ∎

**Q5a (ExtinctionBound).** For an all-SF, *extinction-disciplined* registry (every trigger an SF spelling, every rule extinction-disciplined — equivalently, a registry of Marker-pattern rules), the finite-real-fire conclusion follows from bounded domain growth *directly*, with no work bound: real fires number at most `Σ_ρ |⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}|` — each argument fires each rule at most once (Q-EXT, which consumes *both* the SF spelling and the extinction discipline), so the only unbounded-real-fire route is unbounded *new* arguments — and in the open sequence those arrive from *two* sources, the registry's own emissions and the environment's steps, so `|⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}|` bounds external input as much as internal enlargement. Both hypotheses are load-bearing: SF alone does not bound the count — an SF trigger paired with a contract that emits something *other* than its own falsifier (a `cmt`-trigger `¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))` fired by emitting a `res`, never a `cmt`) stays ⊤ on a fixed argument forever (⊥-stability permits ⊤→⊤, and no `cmt` is ever emitted to flip it) and is a real fire at *every* step — unboundedly many, against the display, though its domain `{t}` is constant. Extinction discipline forbids exactly that second route: the disciplined fire falsifies its own trigger, SF makes the falsification permanent (Q-EXT), and only the two together make at-most-once per argument a theorem. This supplies H-RF (below) by a route disjoint from Q5: it never mentions `W(σ)`, and it does *not* establish H-W — under an unfair scheduler a starved trigger-true argument can still drive `|W(σ)| = ∞` (its triple recurs at every step) while the real-fire count stays bounded by the display above. The W machinery, the re-arm analysis, and the stratification heuristic are all unnecessary in this case; this is the quantitative payoff of the SF-plus-extinction design rule. The bound's two ingredients are not equal in checkability: at-most-once-per-argument is a *registration-time* fact *for the Marker-pattern rules this clause is about* — the SF spelling decidable by the spelling class, and the extinction discipline by a strong-enough contract whose Q3 obligation reduces, for these rules, to a decidable syntactic match (each fire emits exactly the witness its SF trigger's `∃` is missing) — the two combining in Q-EXT; whereas `|⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}| < ∞` quantifies over all reachable states and is as meta-level as H-W — a bound on arguments rather than on trigger-true step-instances, but reachability-quantified all the same. *Route, not restatement — and only because the substrate is open.* In the open sequence the domain bound is *strictly stronger* than H-RF: it implies H-RF (the display above), but H-RF does not imply it — a fair scheduler facing an environment that flags infinitely many distinct targets and retracts each before its fire keeps the real-fire count at zero while `⋃_k [D_ρ]` grows unbounded. So Q5a is a genuine *route*: a sufficient condition on external input, proved *instead of* the cross-rule re-arm analysis. The **closed** special case is degenerate — with the registry the only depositor, unbounded `⋃_k [D_ρ]` needs unboundedly many deposits, hence unboundedly many real fires, so there the implication reverses (`bounded-domain-growth ⟺ H-RF`) and Q5a says nothing H-RF did not. The trade buys something exactly when there is an environment to bound.

**H-RF (FiniteRealFires).** A fire sequence from Σ₀ has *finitely many real fires* iff its real (non-no-op) fires are finite in number. This — not H-W — is the operative hypothesis of the termination theorem below, and it must be kept distinct from H-W, which is strictly stronger. H-W bounds trigger-*true* step-instances (`|W(σ)| < ∞`); H-RF bounds only the *fires*. The two come apart precisely at starvation: an SF trigger-true argument the scheduler never fires keeps its triple `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)` at *every* step `k` (`|W(σ)| = ∞`) while contributing no real fire — so a registry can satisfy H-RF (indeed have zero real fires) yet violate H-W. Bounded domain growth, in particular, does *not* entail H-W; for an all-SF, extinction-disciplined registry it entails H-RF (Q5a) and no more. The structural route that supplies H-RF is therefore Q5a: an all-SF, extinction-disciplined registry with bounded domain growth, with no work bound at all. H-W would supply it too (Q5's injection — `|W(σ)| < ∞` bounds the real fires it injects into), but H-W is unsatisfiable for essentially any registry that ever triggers (above), so it is a *foil*, not a route: it exhibits H-RF as the strictly weaker, attainable hypothesis rather than supplying it. Q6 consumes H-RF, not the stronger H-W, for its real-fire bound; the further conditions for *reaching and holding* quiescence (the environment eventually idle, or strong fairness — H-SFAIR — over non-grow-only domains, where weak fairness no longer even *reaches* it) are separate and consumed there.

**Q6 (TerminationUnderFairness).** Under H-RF and H-FAIR, the registry's own drive to fire is exhausted after finitely many real fires; past the last real fire every fire is a no-op, so any residual non-quiescence is *environment-driven*. The system reaches and *holds* a quiescent state when the environment eventually stops presenting trigger-true work — *regime (i)*; all-SF (regime (ii)) does not supply this by itself, but it makes the registry's work finite structurally (Q5a) and — for grow-only domains — does deliver *reached and held* quiescence under weak fairness, the non-grow-only domains being where an environment hypothesis remains. *Proof.* H-RF bounds the real fires, so σ has a last real fire — index N — or none, in which case read what follows at Σ₀. No fire past N is real (a trigger-true fire *is* a real fire and would exceed H-RF's count), so every fire past N is a no-op (RG): the registry moves the state no further, and *all* state change past N is environment steps. Any `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at some `Σ_m`, m ≥ N, is by H-FAIR eventually *real-fired* — impossible past N without exceeding H-RF — or *removed* from its domain *or falsified in place* (`T_ρ(x, ·) = ⊥`, `x` still in `[D_ρ]`); neither a removal nor an in-domain falsification past N can come from a fire (none is real, so none retracts or deposits), so each is an *environment* step. The discharge past N is thus a clean two-way environmental split — removed *or* falsified — real-firing having been spent. The creation side splits by epoch and must not be collapsed: an argument that *newly arises* trigger-true past N does so by an environment step — no-op fires cannot change state, and real fires are spent — whereas one *already* trigger-true at `Σ_N` was armed at some step `≤ N`, which may be a pre-N registry real fire re-arming it (the non-SF case), not an environment step. Thus past N every trigger-true argument is *removed or falsified in place* by the environment, and every one *newly arising* trigger-true past N is *created* by it; the blanket "created by the environment" is false for arguments that straddle N. Two regimes bear on *reaching and holding* quiescence (for a grow-only domain weak fairness reaches and holds it outright; for a non-grow-only one even *reaching* defers to a regime below). **(i) Environment eventually idle** (finitely many environment steps): past both N and the last environment step the state is constant, and a non-quiescent constant tail would leave a trigger-true argument forever unfired, unremoved, *and unfalsified* (a constant tail changes nothing, so none of the three discharges can occur), contradicting H-FAIR — so the tail is quiescent and absorbing. This route is general (any registry). **(ii) All-SF registry** (Q5a's case): all-SF bounds the real fires structurally (Q5a ⟹ H-RF, so N exists by Q5a's structural route rather than by assuming H-RF directly — Q5a's bounded-domain-growth hypothesis still in force) and makes every *fired* argument permanently trigger-false against every `→_sh` step (SF immunity, Q-EXT step-agnostic). When additionally every domain is *grow-only*, both *reaching* and *holding* quiescence follow under weak H-FAIR alone: a grow-only domain cannot shrink, so H-FAIR's removal escape is unavailable and each trigger-true argument is eventually *real-fired or falsified in place* — and for an SF trigger either settling is permanent (Q-EXT step-agnostic), the marker, once recorded by the rule's own fire or by an environment deposit, never un-recording. With bounded growth each of the finitely many arguments is thus eventually settled and SF-immune thereafter — no later step re-arms it — so quiescence is reached and held. When a domain is *not* grow-only the environment can remove an argument (or, for a non-SF trigger, re-falsify it) *before* it fires — discharging weak H-FAIR by removal or in-domain falsification, not firing — and re-arm it later, so *reaching* a quiescent state, not merely holding one, reacquires an environment hypothesis for that rule: the environment eventually leaves each argument in-domain and trigger-true until fired (regime (i) for the rule), or *strong* fairness (H-SFAIR) real-fires every infinitely-recurring argument. SF immunity still pays — re-arming an *already-settled* argument is a no-op, so only *unsettled* arguments can defer quiescence. The *finite*-σ case is the same H-FAIR step read at the end: a fair finite sequence cannot end at a non-quiescent state, a trigger-true argument there left unfired, unremoved, *and unfalsified*. Where neither regime applies — a non-grow-only all-SF registry under an environment that never idles — the conclusion is genuinely weaker, and not by a gap in the proof. Three obstructions to a *reached-and-held* quiescent state must be distinguished, and they do not all fail the same way: **(1)** and **(3)** obstruct *reaching* — no state along σ is ever quiescent — whereas **(2)** obstructs only *holding*, quiescent states being reached but then unsettled by the environment. Only the first is excluded by bounded growth. **(1)** An environment *alternating ever-fresh* trigger-true arguments (each removed before the next, so H-FAIR holds, and none fired, so H-RF holds with zero real fires) keeps *every* state non-quiescent — quiescence *unreached* — but this needs *unboundedly many* distinct arguments, so bounded domain growth (Q5a) excludes it outright. **(2)** An environment *oscillating one* non-grow-only argument's domain membership re-presents it with all-empty gaps between presentations — quiescent in the gaps, non-quiescent during each presentation: quiescence *is reached* (each gap state satisfies `quiescent_R`, Q0) but not *held*, the re-presentation being fresh environment input — Q8 re-entry, against which Q0 quiescence absorbs *firing* (Q1) but never the environment — a *holding* failure, not a reaching one, that survives bounded growth on its single argument. **(3)** — the case the bounded-growth reader must not miss — an environment *cycling finitely many* trigger-true arguments *out of phase*: a fixed finite set `{x₁, …, x_j}` (so bounded growth holds, `⋃_k [D_ρ] = {x₁, …, x_j}`), each `xᵢ` *removed* from its domain before its fire (the SF trigger forbids falsify-and-re-arm — falsification is permanent — so the cycling runs through domain membership: unflag before the fire, re-flag after) and re-presented, arranged so that at *every* state at least one `xᵢ` stands trigger-true — re-arming one before settling the next, leaving *no* all-empty gap. Then H-RF holds (zero real fires), bounded growth holds (the argument set is fixed and finite), weak H-FAIR holds (each argument discharged by removal, never firing) — yet *no* state is quiescent: quiescence is *not reached*, with every registry-side and bounded-growth hypothesis satisfied. The obstruction is the environment's power to *withdraw* an argument before it fires — a non-grow-only/open-model phenomenon, impossible where domains are grow-only (there removal is barred, so weak H-FAIR collapses to firing-or-in-place-falsification, each of the finitely many arguments is eventually settled, and quiescence is reached *and* held — the clean grow-only contrast of regime (ii)). H-SFAIR is exactly the closing hypothesis: it forbids case (3)'s σ (each `xᵢ` is trigger-true at infinitely many indices and never real-fired, which H-SFAIR prohibits), forcing each `xᵢ` real-fired and its trigger SF-settled, so quiescence is reached *and held*. Cases (2) and (3) are Q8's *re-entry* at top level, and all-SF stops neither; only H-SFAIR (or an eventually-idle environment, regime (i)) *reaches and holds* quiescence over a non-grow-only domain — case (2) reaching one unaided but unable to hold it, case (3) reaching none. *Reaching* a quiescent state and *holding* it are therefore both conditioned — as guarantees — on H-SFAIR-or-environment-idle for a non-grow-only registry: weak H-FAIR plus bounded growth delivers only the registry-side guarantee (finitely many real fires, registry-inert past N), and guarantees no *reached-and-held* quiescent state. What survives unconditionally is the registry-side half: past N the registry never fires for real again, so non-termination past N is the environment's doing, and Q1's absorption (a fixed point of *firing*) is the registry's standing guarantee. ∎ Since all-SF + extinction-disciplined + bounded domain growth ⟹ H-RF (Q5a), that structural package with H-FAIR makes the registry's *work* terminate, and — when its domains are grow-only — delivers *reached and held* quiescence under weak fairness outright; a non-grow-only domain defers *both reaching and holding* quiescence to regime (i) or strong fairness (H-SFAIR) for the rule that owns it. (H-W ⟹ H-RF too, by Q5's injection, so `H-W + H-FAIR` instantiates no case *beyond* `H-RF + H-FAIR`. On the open substrate the inclusion is in fact *vacuous* wherever the registry does any work at all: the environment alone can stall — hold a trigger-true argument true across arbitrarily many of its own steps before the registry's fair fire lands — so `|W(σ)| = ∞` for some σ from any Σ₀ that reaches a trigger-true argument, and H-W is satisfiable only by registries that never trigger. H-W's service is therefore *entirely* to locate H-RF as the weaker, attainable hypothesis; it adds no case Q6 settles, by subsumption where it would hold and by vacuity where it does not.) What is exactly as conditional as it looks is the *registry-side* guarantee — H-RF (structural for all-SF, via Q5a) and H-FAIR make the registry's work finite and the state past N registry-inert; *reaching and holding* a quiescent state is the environment-conditional part, free (both) under weak fairness when domains are grow-only and otherwise — for a non-grow-only domain — needing regime (i) or a strong fairness even to be *reached*. Drop H-RF and rules can re-arm each other forever, each re-fire a fresh real fire without bound; drop H-FAIR and a trigger-true argument can be starved eternally with the system never settling. These two registry-side failure modes are genuinely separable, and neither is derivable from the other. (The environment supplies a third, open-model axis drawn out in the proof — re-entry over non-grow-only domains can prevent or undo quiescence — which no registry hypothesis controls.) The starvation mode is exactly why the hypothesis is H-RF rather than H-W: a starved SF trigger-true argument drives `|W(σ)| = ∞` yet contributes no fire, so H-W is strictly stronger than what Q6 needs (and, being violated under that very scheduler, would have wrongly excluded a case Q6 settles by H-FAIR's failure instead). Extinction discipline is *not* a third independent hypothesis of Q6: it is a hypothesis of Q5a, the structural route that supplies H-RF. What it earns is a cheaper route — it replaces Q5's reachability-quantified `|W(σ)| < ∞` with at-most-once-per-argument (Q-EXT, checkable at registration for the Marker-pattern rules of Q5a's route from *both* the SF spelling, via the spelling class, and the extinction discipline, whose Q3 obligation reduces there to a decidable syntactic match) plus bounded domain growth. That at-most-once half is registration-checkable for those rules; bounded domain growth is itself reachability-quantified, so the move trades one meta-level assumption for a simpler one — a bound on arguments, not on trigger-true step-instances — rather than eliminating meta-level quantification. Its load-bearing roles are Q-EXT and Q5a, not Q5/Q6.

## Scope parameterization

**SC (Scope), Q7–Q9.** A *scope* is any Boolean PL predicate `S` over addresses. A rule's domain `D_ρ ∈ QD` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) or tuple-valued (`dom(Tup)` — e.g. an audit slice `L_K`, as the worked example's `ρ_R` takes), so an address-predicate cannot in general be applied to a domain element directly: over `dom(Tup)` the element `x` is a tuple, and `S(x)` is a sort error. The scope's *restriction of ρ* is therefore a QD filter `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : β_ρ^S(x)}` whose body `β_ρ^S` is a Boolean PL predicate *at `D_ρ`'s sort* relating the element to `S` — and the relation is the *rule's* to declare, not one fixed projection, because over `dom(Tup)` several inequivalent bodies are well-typed and they realize different tiers. For an address-valued `D_ρ` there is one natural body, `S(x)` itself: the element scoped by its own address. For a tuple-valued `D_ρ` the canonical choices are:
- **per-emitter** — `S(addr(x))`, scoping a tuple by its own address. `addr : Tup → T` is V-TUP's projection and `S ∘ addr` is well-typed by WT's PC2 plain-composition rule. This scopes a tuple by *where the tuple itself lives*; for the worked resolver (`D = L_cmt`) that is the comment's home chain — its finding's region — *not* the target the comment points at.
- **per-target** — `(∃ y ∈ addrs_G(x) :: S(y))`, scoping a tuple by its *denoted targets*. `addrs_G : Tup → ℘_fin(T)` is V-TUP's to-endset projection; the finite denoted set is a QD domain by set-valued closure, and the existential is PC1 with closed body `S(y)` — a Boolean PL predicate at `dom(Tup)`. This, *not* the `addr` body, is what a per-target scope means for a tuple rule: "elements whose target is in scope." Symmetrically **per-source** is `(∃ y ∈ addrs_F(x) :: S(y))`.
Each body is a Boolean PL predicate at the domain's sort, and the filtering former (ASN-0129) admits exactly such a `P : D × S → Bool` over an address- *or* tuple-valued `D`, preserving the sort; so `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : β_ρ^S(x)}` is a well-typed QD filter for every rule, tuple-domained ones included. The `addr` body realizes per-target scoping *only* for an address-domained rule whose elements already live in the target region; a tuple-domained rule reaches the per-target tier through the `addrs_G`/`coverage_G` body, never through `addr` — under `addr`-scoping a per-target restriction of `ρ_R` would be vacuous, no comment's home-chain address lying in its target's region. `quiescent_{R,S}(Σ)` restricts Q0's inner quantification to the elements this filter retains. Then: **Q7** — `quiescent_{R,S}` carries Q0's status exactly: the scope adds this PC1 filter to each rule's inner quantification, the outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is the same finite static expansion (V-IDX), and the scope body `β_ρ^S` simply adds whatever view-sensitive constituents `S` reads — view-parameterized atoms or the UV-rewritten fixed-view collections `succs`/`sources_to`/`chain`/`stale` alike — to the rebuild Q0 already performs (extended above to those four) — so by Q0's fixed-view-base rewrite `quiescent_{R,S} ∈ PL` for *every* registry, decidable and observer-uniform, the heterogeneous case again paying a rebuild rather than PL-membership. The claim covers tuple-domained rules — the worked example's `ρ_R` among them, whose `dom(Tup)` restriction is a well-typed QD filter for whichever scoping body it declares: per-emitter `{c ∈ L_cmt : S(addr(c))}`, or — the per-target reading a comment scope usually intends — `{c ∈ L_cmt : (∃ y ∈ addrs_G(c) :: S(y))}`. Scope quiescence inherits Q0's recognizability, no more than Q0 itself had. **Q8** — at a scope-quiescent state, in-scope fires are no-ops (absorption relativizes); out-of-scope fires are unconstrained, and an out-of-scope emission may re-arm an in-scope trigger — *re-entry* — which is itself detectable per-state by Q7. **Q9** — quiescence is anti-monotone in the scope: `S' ⟹ S` gives `quiescent_S ⟹ quiescent_{S'}`; the converse fails whenever the larger scope holds work no smaller scope sees. Canonical tiers (per-target, per-collection, system-wide) are application vocabulary over this machinery — each a scope predicate `S` paired with the SC body that carries it to a rule's domain sort (per-target reaching a tuple rule through the denoted-target body, an address rule through `S` directly) — the substrate admitting any PL predicate as a scope and committing to nothing about which scopes matter.

## Worked composition

The two illustrative classes of ASN-0129's worked trace — `cmt` (Binary, idem=⊥, comment from finding to target) and `res` (Binary, idem=⊤, resolution from target to comment) — already form a registry; we verify its termination.

*Rules.* Producer `ρ_P`: domain = `{t ∈ M_tgt : needs_attention(t)}` — a QD filter (ASN-0129) over the base `M_tgt`, the members of a Unary target-marking type `tgt` (one of ASN-0129's QD bases `M_K`), with filter body `needs_attention(t) ≡ is_attn(t)` a Boolean PL membership predicate (D2, ASN-0128) over a Unary attention-flag type `attn`. The producer conjunct is read at the *active* view — so `M_tgt` and `is_attn` alike denote active slices (PC3 fixes one view per term, so the domain cannot mix an audit `M_tgt` with an active `is_attn`); `T_P` below stays SF at that nominal view all the same, reading `L_cmt`, the audit slice view-fixed by name (`L_K`, ASN-0129). The filter is well-typed — `M_tgt` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) and `is_attn : T → Bool` is Boolean at that sort — so the domain is a well-formed QD expression before any SF/extinction/bound property is claimed of the rule. It is *not* grow-only: `is_attn` at the active view is not ⊥-stable (PD1 — a fresh `attn` deposit flips it `⊥→⊤`, a retraction `⊤→⊥`), so `needs_attention` may flip either way; trigger `T_P(t) ≡ ¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))`, with `L_cmt` the audit slice (ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery) — fires by emitting one `cmt` targeting t. Folding `needs_attention` into the *domain* rather than the trigger is deliberate: it leaves `T_P` a pure negated existential (SF, below) at the cost of a non-grow-only domain, which Q5a's union bound absorbs. Resolver `ρ_R`: domain = `L_cmt` (audit slice — grow-only); trigger `T_R(c) ≡ ¬(∃ r ∈ L_res :: addr(c) ∈ coverage_G(r))` — fires by emitting one `res` targeting c.

*Class check.* With `needs_attention` moved out, both triggers are now pure negated existentials over grow-only audit slices with step-constant bodies — **SF spellings** by PD0's quantifier and Boolean rules. The move was forced: had `needs_attention` stayed a trigger conjunct, `T_P ≡ needs_attention(t) ∧ ¬(∃ …)` would *not* be SF — PD0's `∧` preserves SF only when both conjuncts are, and `needs_attention` is not ⊥-stable (it can flip `⊥→⊤` on a still-uncommented target, flipping `T_P` with it), so Q-EXT would not apply. (Spelled over *active* slices instead, neither trigger would be SF: a retraction of a resolution would re-open the comment — Q-FLIP's first falsifier — and termination would need the full H-W analysis. The audit spelling is the design choice, made checkable.)

*Extinction.* `Post_P` requires an emitted `cmt` covering t; `Post_R` an emitted `res` covering `addr(c)` — each strong enough (Q3), and strong enough *effectively*: this is Q3's decidable-match case, each contract depositing exactly the witness its trigger's `∃` quantifies over (`Post_P` a `cmt` covering t, growing `L_cmt`; `Post_R` a `res` covering `addr(c)`, growing `L_res`), so "strong enough" is settled by syntactic comparison of trigger spelling against emission form, no state quantified. The emission makes the trigger's existential true, hence the trigger false. For `ρ_R`'s **idem=⊤** `res` the growth of `L_res` is not free of the dedup caveat but survives it (Q3): firing reads `T_R(c) = ⊤`, so no `r ∈ L_res` covers `addr(c)`, whereas a dedup hit would need an *active* — hence audit (`A_res ⊆ L_res`) — tuple covering it; the emit is therefore necessarily a miss and deposits, and even a born-nullified deposit joins `L_res` and flips the audit-read trigger. `ρ_P`'s `cmt` is idem=⊥, so it never dedups. With SF, Q-EXT gives at-most-once per argument for both rules.

*Bound (Q5a).* Both of Q5a's hypotheses now hold — every trigger an SF spelling (just shown) and every rule extinction-disciplined (the contracts above, Q3) — so by Q5a the real fires number at most `|⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}| + |⋃_k [D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ_k}|`. The resolver's domain is grow-only, so its union is just `|comments ever|`; the producer's domain is not, so its union is *the set of targets that ever need attention*. Here is the crux the open model makes honest: **no rule writes `attn` or `tgt`** — `ρ_P` emits `cmt`, `ρ_R` emits `res` — so the producer's domain grows only by *environment* deposits, and `⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}` is exactly the population of targets the environment ever flags. Bounding it is a bound on external input, the open-model hypothesis Q5a's route turns on. Hence real fires ≤ |targets the environment ever flags| + |comments ever| (comments entering `L_cmt` through `ρ_P`, one per flagged target, plus any the environment deposits directly) — so the registry's real fires are finite, its *work* terminates, iff that flagged population, and any direct environment comment traffic, is bounded: the registry's one honest hypothesis on its environment (reaching and holding quiescence adds the grow-only/fairness refinement of the second check below). Two checks place the hypothesis correctly. First, it is *load-bearing, not vacuous*: the closed, environment-free special case has `{t ∈ M_tgt : is_attn(t)}` static — `attn`/`tgt` written by no one — hence equal to its `Σ₀` value and finite by QD-fin, so the registry would terminate *unconditionally* under fairness; it is precisely the shared substrate that turns "bounded flagged population" into a real, dischargeable-or-not assumption rather than a QD-fin theorem. Second, SF immunity governs what can *defer* quiescence — reached or held — and the two rules differ by exactly the grow-only split. The resolver's domain `L_cmt` is *grow-only*: a comment cannot leave it, so H-FAIR's removal escape is unavailable and the discharge collapses to *firing or in-place falsification* — every comment is eventually resolved (by `ρ_R`'s own fire or by an environment `res`) and SF-immune thereafter — and `ρ_R` reaches *and holds* quiescence under weak fairness given bounded comments (Q6 regime (ii), grow-only). The producer's domain `{t : is_attn(t)}` is *not* grow-only — the environment writes `needs_attention` — so the environment can unflag a target before it is commented, discharging H-FAIR by removal rather than firing and deferring the comment indefinitely; `ρ_P`'s *reaching* quiescence (not merely holding it) therefore needs either that the environment eventually leave each to-be-commented target flagged until fired (regime (i) for `ρ_P`), or a strong fairness. The non-grow-only domain that Q5a's union bound "absorbs" for the *work* count is exactly where reaching-and-holding quiescence acquires its one environment/fairness hypothesis. SF immunity still pays throughout: re-flagging an *already-commented* target is a no-op, so only *uncommented* targets can defer quiescence. The classic failure mode is visible in the accounting: let the resolver's emissions also make fresh targets need attention (`needs_attention` true on addresses `ρ_R` creates, enlarging `⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}`) and the bound's right side grows with the left — locally disciplined rules, globally divergent registry, exactly the divergence Q4 warns locality cannot exclude. Stratification states the repair: `ρ_P` at stratum 0, `ρ_R` at stratum 1, legal iff resolver emissions never **enlarge the producer's domain** — never make a fresh target need attention. The phrasing must name domain growth, not re-arming: `T_P` is an SF spelling, so ⊥-stability (PD0) already makes re-arming an *existing* target impossible outright (a falsified `T_P(t)` — `t` commented — stays false under every later deposit, and a `res` lands in `L_res`, which `T_P` never reads). The general stratification condition "no emission re-arms a strictly lower stratum" is the right demand for a *non-SF* lower stratum; specialized to this all-SF producer, re-arm is vacuously excluded and the only divergence route left open is domain growth — which is exactly what the diagnosed failure exploits and what the legality condition must forbid.

*Quiescence.* `quiescent_R` here is precisely "every target needing attention is commented, every comment resolved" — two nested quantifications whose bodies are ST (negations of the SF triggers, PD0). The registry is single-view *at the active view*: the producer's conjunct reads active (its domain's `M_tgt`/`is_attn`, the only view-parameterized constituents in the whole registry), while both triggers and the resolver's domain `L_cmt` are view-independent (audit slices fixed by name), so every view-parameterized constituent already sits at active and Q0's fixed-view-base rewrite is not even called on. By Q0, then, `quiescent_R ∈ PL`: decidable at every state, absorbing once reached (Q1) — and were a heterogeneous trigger added, the rewrite would keep it in PL regardless. For this registry the *registry-side* guarantee — finitely many real fires, registry-inert past N — holds given a bounded flagged population under any weakly-fair scheduler. Reaching and holding `quiescent_R` then split by the grow-only line: the resolver (grow-only `L_cmt`) both *reaches and holds* its half under weak fairness outright, while the producer (non-grow-only domain) — whose flagged targets the environment can cycle out of phase, each unflagged before its fire — *reaches* its half (and holds it) only once the environment stops re-flagging uncommented targets (regime (i)) or under strong fairness (H-SFAIR, Q6); weak fairness plus bounded flagging leaves the producer's half possibly *unreached*, not merely unheld.

*A reached terminal state.* One concrete sequence exercises the nested quantifier and Q6's reaching claim end to end. Let the environment flag a single target and then idle: at `Σ₀`, `t ∈ M_tgt` with `is_attn(t) = ⊤` (both the `tgt`-membership and the `attn`-flag environment-deposited, neither ever retracted), and `L_cmt = L_res = ∅`. Then `[D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ₀} = {t ∈ M_tgt : is_attn(t)} = {t}` and `T_P(t, Σ₀) = ¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c)) = ⊤` (the audit slice is empty), while `[D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ₀} = L_cmt = ∅` is argument-free — so `Σ₀` is non-quiescent, `ρ_P` trigger-true on `t`. **Fire `(ρ_P, t)`**: a real fire (trigger ⊤), discharging `Post_P` by one `Emit_cmt` depositing `c` covering `t` — a *single* `→_sh` step, atomic by H-ATOM via I4. At the post-state `Σ₁`: `L_cmt = {c}`, so the existential `T_P` quantifies over is now witnessed and `T_P(t, Σ₁) = ⊥` (extinguished; SF-permanent thereafter, Q-EXT), and `[D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ₁} = {c}` with `T_R(c, Σ₁) = ¬(∃ r ∈ L_res :: addr(c) ∈ coverage_G(r)) = ⊤` — non-quiescent, `ρ_R` trigger-true on `c`. **Fire `(ρ_R, c)`**: real fire, one `Emit_res` depositing `r` covering `addr(c)`, again single-step. At `Σ₂`: `T_R(c, Σ₂) = ⊥` (extinguished, SF-permanent). Now *evaluate* `quiescent_R(Σ₂)` — the nested `(∀ ρ ∈ R :: ∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_{Σ₂} :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ₂))`. For `ρ_P`: `[D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ₂} = {t}` (the environment never unflagged `t`, so `t` is *in-domain but trigger-false* — discharged by in-place falsification, not removal) and `¬T_P(t, Σ₂) = ⊤`. For `ρ_R`: `[D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ₂} = {c}` and `¬T_R(c, Σ₂) = ⊤`. Both conjuncts hold, so `quiescent_R(Σ₂) = ⊤` — the quantifier computes exactly "every flagged target commented, every comment resolved" and returns ⊤. Two real fires, then quiescence *reached* at `Σ₂`; with the environment idle past `Σ₀` it is also *held* — both settled triggers SF-immune against every later `→_sh` step — the finite, reached-and-held sequence Q6 promises, the resolver's grow-only half (regime (ii)) needing only weak fairness for it. (Had the environment instead unflagged `t` between `Σ₀` and `ρ_P`'s scheduled fire, `t` would leave `[D_{ρ_P}]` — H-FAIR's removal escape — and `ρ_P`'s half could go *unreached*: the non-grow-only producer's one environment hypothesis, made concrete.)

## What this note doesn't cover

- **A scheduler.** H-FAIR is stated, not constructed; scheduling disciplines, their fairness proofs, the serialization of multi-step fires that discharges H-ATOM (single-step marker fires needing none — atomic for free by ASN-0128's I4), concurrency reconciliation, and violation policy (log, halt, escalate) are operational machinery this corpus deliberately leaves at the implementation layer.
- **Stochastic rule bodies.** A body that flips its trigger with probability p < 1 satisfies the deterministic contracts only operationally (retry policies, N-consecutive-clean stopping rules) — evaluator-reliability engineering, not substrate semantics.
- **The activation binding.** RG assumes rules exist; what registers them, who may, and how rule registries are themselves governed is the protocol layer above (with ASN-0130's machinery available to it).
- **An environment model.** Environment steps are admitted abstractly — arbitrary non-registry `→_sh` transitions interleaved into σ; *which* external workloads supply bounded input (the dischargeable form of Q5a's hypothesis) and *whether* an environment eventually idles (Q6 regime (i)) are workload/protocol questions above this layer, not properties the substrate constrains.

## Open questions

1. **The SF certificate.** Q-EXT and Q5a make SF membership the load-bearing *uncertified* registration check for triggers — its companion, extinction discipline, being checkable via Q3's strong-enough contract for the marker pattern, where the obligation reduces to a decidable match (a static, not-shown-decidable obligation otherwise, Q3) — and ASN-0130 ships only the ST⁺ certificate (`pd_stable`), no SF one. Should `pd_extinct` (SF certification) ship as a designated class — exactly the catalog-growth ASN-0130's own Open Question 4 raises for certificates beyond ST⁺ — closing the loop where "this registry terminates on bounded input" is a structural lint?

2. **A PL surrogate for H-W.** H-W is meta-level; is there a PL-expressible *necessary* condition — a per-state predicate whose failure certifies unbounded work in progress — strong enough to catch the common divergence patterns (mutual re-arm cycles) at runtime, even though the full hypothesis is inexpressible?

3. **Per-scope versus global work.** Q5/Q6 are stated globally; the per-scope analogs (real fires restricted to in-scope arguments, termination toward `quiescent_S`) appear routine, but whether a registry can settle at every scope of a tier (per-scope H-RF, under bounded per-scope input) without the corpus settling globally (global H-RF) deserves a structural answer — it is the formal version of "every document converges but the corpus doesn't," and the open substrate is exactly where it bites: an environment feeding unboundedly many internally-bounded scopes.

4. **Cross-scope oscillation.** Q8's re-entry means an outer-scope fire can repeatedly un-quiesce an inner scope. What registry or scheduling discipline bounds re-entry — and whether bounded re-entry is even the right demand, versus monotone progress *across* entries — is open, and is where a future cascade/re-opening theory would live.

5. **Contract necessity.** Q3 is sufficiency only: must every extinction-disciplined rule admit a strong-enough PL contract? A rule disciplined by reasoning PL cannot express would be sound but unverifiable at registration — the same sound-but-uncertifiable gap PD0's spelling-level classes accept by design.
