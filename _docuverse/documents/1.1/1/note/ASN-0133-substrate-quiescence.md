# ASN-0133: Substrate Quiescence

*The terminal state, recognizable from inside the predicate language; termination as a conditional theorem with every hypothesis named.*

ASN-0129 supplies the language a coordination system's conditions are written in, and the dynamics classes (PD0–PD2, ST/SF) that say how a condition's truth moves. ASN-0130 makes the conditions substrate artifacts. This note defines what it is for such a system to be *done* — quiescence — and proves what can and cannot be guaranteed about reaching it under named hypotheses: extinction discipline, bounded work, fair scheduling.

**Depends:** ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework), ASN-0128 (Substrate Type Operational Semantics), ASN-0129 (Substrate Predicate Composition), ASN-0130 (Predicate Definitions as Substrate Content).

## The rule model

**RG (Rule, Registry).** A *rule* is a triple `ρ = (D_ρ, T_ρ, Post_ρ)`: a domain expression `D_ρ ∈ QD`, a Boolean trigger `T_ρ : D_ρ → Bool` in PL, and an *emission contract* `Post_ρ` — a *meta-level* contract over (argument, state, emitted call set) constraining what any fire of ρ must emit, the calls drawn from the operation surface `{Emit_K, Nullify_Binary}` (ASN-0128; `Observe_K` reads, and reads are not emissions). It is not a PL term — PL has no sort for emitted call sets (COD lists none, ASN-0129) — so `Post_ρ` is written in the surface's emission forms; the PL part is the trigger `T_ρ`, read at a single state, which the contract relates to those emission forms only as a registration-time obligation. A *registry* `R` is a finite set of rules. A *fire* of `(ρ, x)` at Σ with `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` is: if `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊥`, a no-op (`Σ' = Σ`); otherwise the application of some emission set satisfying `Post_ρ(x, Σ, ·)` — a finite sequence of `→_sh` steps through the surface (the gated relation, ASN-0126, over extended-record states by R-TR, ASN-0128), `Σ →_sh* Σ'`. **(H-FIN, fire finiteness.)** *Every* `Post_ρ`-satisfying emission set is finite — equivalently, every admissible fire's *step run* (its own `→_sh` steps) terminates. The demand is universal over the contract's admissible choices. **(H-ATOM, fire atomicity.)** A fire's post-state `Σ'` is read *immediately* after the fire's own `→_sh` steps, with *no* environment step interleaved among them — environment steps fall *between* fires (H-FAIR), never within one. The body that *chooses* the emissions is deliberately outside the model (Q2). The registry is, moreover, only *one* actor on a shared substrate: other agents, other registries, and whatever feeds the docuverse its raw work all emit through the same surface. The system's evolution therefore interleaves the registry's own fires with **environment steps** — non-registry `→_sh` transitions the registry neither issues nor controls — and a *fire sequence* `σ` (made precise at H-FAIR) is an interleaving of the two from `Σ₀`. Between two fires a rule domain `[D_ρ]` may grow *or* shrink under an environment step. The termination hypotheses below bound *external input*, not the registry's own fire-reachable states.

**Triggers: inline or by reference.** A trigger may be an *inline* PL term or a *registered definition address*. A trigger given as a `pdef` address (ASN-0130), evaluated by reference (PR3), makes the registry itself substrate content. Because evaluation-by-reference keys on *content* — *ever-registration*, not active registration (PR3) — such a trigger stays evaluable, and quiescence (Q0) stays recognizable, even after its own definition or any definition it references is de-registered: expansion still computes and the verdict stays a decidable PL term whatever the registration status of self or referent. A pdef-trigger registry inherits PR-DISC (ASN-0130) as a standing hypothesis, discharged the way ASN-0130 discharges it (every `pdef` deposit enters through `register_pred`): pdef-triggers require PR-DISC for `expand` to terminate, so Q0's PL-membership is conditional on it for them. An inline-trigger registry carries no such premise — its triggers are PL terms by construction (PC4/PC5), every property below resting on ASN-0129 and the operation surface alone.

**Q0 (Recognizability).** Define `quiescent_R(Σ) ≡ (∀ ρ ∈ R :: (∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ)))`. The outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is *not* a PC1 quantification — `R` is a finite metalevel set of rule-triples (for an inline-trigger registry, not even substrate content), not a QD domain — but a *finite static expansion* into a PC0 conjunction, the move ASN-0129's V-IDX makes explicit for `Reg`-quantification ("Defined by static expansion… denotes the finite conjunction"). Each conjunct `(∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ))` is a PL predicate: PC1 over a QD domain, finite at every reachable state (QD-fin, ASN-0129), with `¬` by PC0. Whether the conjunction is *itself* a single PL term turns on views — PC0 conjunction requires its constituents to read the same view (PC3), and RG constrains no two triggers to a common one. What that blocks is only the *naive* merge — the one that leaves each view-parameterized atom at its native view; for a heterogeneous-view registry that merge is ill-formed. But PC3 furnishes more than the same-view constraint: `A_K` and `L_K` are *fixed-view bases* — denoting the active and audit K-slices at every term view — and through them PC3's cross-view derivations (its `⋃`/`∃`-over-fixed-base device, V-AUD supplying the audit readings directly) rebuild each of its *exactly four* view-parameterized constituents — `members`, `targets_of`, `is_K`, `M_K` — recovering their *audit* and *active* values, the rebuild equations being PC3's (ASN-0129). That `⋃`/`∃`-over-fixed-base rebuild carries the audit and active values only, the two views UV leaves unfiltered. View-parameterization is not, however, the whole of view-sensitivity, and the merge must account for the rest. UV (ASN-0129) rewrites *six* collection atoms under the default view: `members` and `targets_of`, already among the four, *and* the four *fixed-view* behavior collections `succs`, `sources_to`, the sequence `chain` returns, and `stale` (BH2/BH3/BH4). The four behavior collections are not view-parameterized in PC3's sense — they read the active slice at *every* term view, so their audit and active readings coincide — yet under `v = default` their *results* are filtered, so a default-view read of one differs in value from its active read, and a Boolean trigger taking that read does *not* "already sit at one fixed slice." The three set-valued behavior atoms (`succs`, `sources_to`, `stale`) rebuild their default value as a QD filter (by set-valued closure) over the atom's own raw active reading, its body `¬filtered` — UV's `filtered`, the disjunction `(∃ J ∈ Φ, J ≠ K_queried :: is_filtered_J(·))` over every BH1 type, each `is_filtered_J` view-stable because UV never rewrites it — the filter being precisely UV's own default-view rewrite (ASN-0129) recast as a PL term, uniformly with the `members`/`targets_of` rebuild below. The two view-parameterized collections `members`/`targets_of`, and with them the domain base `M_K`, carry their default value the same way: the UV filter `{· : ¬filtered(·)}` (UV's `filtered`, ASN-0129) wrapped around the active rebuild — for `members`, `{x ∈ ⋃(A_K, addrs_F) : ¬filtered(x)}`; for `targets_of(x, ·)`, the source-matched `{y ∈ ⋃({c ∈ A_K : x ∈ addrs_F(c)}, addrs_G) : ¬filtered(y)}` (D3's active reading, ASN-0128, under the same filter — `x` matched against the *source* `addrs_F`, the *targets* `addrs_G` collected) — both view-parameterized *and* UV-rewritten, they take the PC3 `⋃`/`∃`-over-fixed-base rebuild for the audit and active values and this UV filter for the default. `chain` is `Seq_fin(T)`-valued and PL has *no* sequence-to-sequence filter former, so a Boolean trigger that consumes `chain`'s *default-view value* must first eliminate the sequence through V-PRIM's order-forgetting projection `elems(chain(x))` — a `℘_fin(T)` term, which then carries its default value through the same set-valued filter as the other collections. The verdict atom `is_in_chain` is *not* a second route to that value: UV never rewrites it, so — evaluated against the *unrewritten* active walk — it reads identically at every view, needing no rebuild precisely because it never reads the filtered chain. Everything else a Boolean trigger or a QD domain can read — the two view-stable Booleans `is_in_chain` and `is_filtered` (BH1's read-filter), the verdict/optional atoms `tip`, `target_of`, `age`, `targets_keyed` (all never UV-rewritten, by UV's Verdicts-and-optionals and Booleans clauses, and none among PC3's four view-parameterized atoms) and the fixed-view slices `A_K`/`L_K` themselves — *is* genuinely view-stable: one fixed slice, one value at every term view. So every view-sensitive part of every trigger *and* of every domain can be moved to one chosen term view by the rebuilds just classified, and the per-rule conjuncts, so rewritten, PC0-conjoin into a single PC3-conforming term. Hence `quiescent_R ∈ PL` for *every* registry, single-view or not: the single-view registry conjoins as written, the heterogeneous one pays an explicit fixed-view-base rewrite — a change of spelling, not of value (carried by PC3's fixed-view rebuild equations and UV's default-view definition) — but lands in PL all the same. Its value at every reachable Σ is therefore decidable in finite time by any observer from Σ and the registry alone — one PL term, pure (PC4) and terminating (PC5), its evaluation finitely many verdicts. ∎

**Q1 (Absorption).** A quiescent state is a fixed point of firing: at any Σ with `quiescent_R(Σ)`, every fire of every `(ρ, x)` is a no-op, so `Σ' = Σ` — and the registry may halt its firing on first detection, no further monitoring of its own activity required (a later environment step may re-arm a trigger — re-entry, Q8 — but that is fresh external input, not a fire). *Proof.* Immediate from RG's no-op clause and Q0's definition. ∎ Recognizability and absorption are *unconditional* relative to the dynamics hypotheses the termination results below name — fairness, finite real fires, bounded domain growth — holding for undisciplined registries, unfair schedulers, and divergent systems alike. They say nothing about whether quiescence is reachable.

*Heterogeneous rewrite, worked.* The case Q0's rewrite is built for is the *heterogeneous-view* registry, where the per-rule conjuncts read incompatible views and the merge is not the naive one. Take `R' = {ρ_walk, ρ_mark}` over types `sup` (Binary, BH2 determinate-walk), `retired` (Unary, BH1 read-filter — the shipped S1), `mark` (Unary, idem=⊥), `obs` (Unary). Both domains are audit slices, view-independent by name — ρ_walk takes `L_sup`, ρ_mark takes `L_obs` — so all view-sensitivity lives in the triggers, and they split. **ρ_walk reads a default-view collection:** `T_walk(c) ≡ (∃ s ∈ addrs_F(c) :: succs(s) ≠ ∅)`, the `succs` at view `default` — "some source of `c` still shows a successor after retired versions are hidden." **ρ_mark reads an audit slice:** `T_mark(c) ≡ ¬(∃ m ∈ L_mark :: (∃ a ∈ addrs_F(c) :: a ∈ coverage_F(m)))` — "`c`'s source carries no mark." Then `quiescent_{R'} = (∀ c ∈ L_sup :: ¬T_walk(c)) ∧ (∀ c ∈ L_obs :: ¬T_mark(c))`. The right conjunct is wholly audit/view-independent (domain `L_obs`, trigger `L_mark`, V-TUP projections `addrs_F`/`coverage_F`); the left is audit throughout *except* the one default-view `succs`. PC0-conjoining demands a common view (PC3), so the lone default-view `succs` must be rebuilt to the audit view the rest of the term carries. The rebuild replaces `succs(s)` at default by its UV equation as a QD filter over the raw reading, `{y ∈ succs(s) : ¬is_filtered_retired(y)}` (UV's default-view rewrite recast as a term, set-valued closure with the view-stable Boolean `is_filtered_retired` as body; `Φ = {retired}` and `retired ≠ sup`, so UV's `filtered` is just `is_filtered_retired`). ρ_walk's conjunct becomes `(∀ c ∈ L_sup :: ¬(∃ s ∈ addrs_F(c) :: {y ∈ succs(s) : ¬is_filtered_retired(y)} ≠ ∅))`, wholly at audit view, and PC0-conjoins with ρ_mark's into one audit-view PL term.

*Value-preservation, at one state.* Let `Σ*` have `L_sup = {c₀}` with `addrs_F(c₀) = {s₀}` and one *active* `sup`-edge `s₀ → y₀` (so `succs(s₀) = {y₀}` at active), `y₀` carrying an active `retired` mark (`is_filtered_retired(y₀) = ⊤`), and `L_obs = {b₀}` with `addrs_F(b₀) = {a₀}`, `a₀` covered by an active `mark`. *Default-view read:* `succs(s₀)` at default `= {y ∈ {y₀} : ¬is_filtered_retired(y₀)} = ∅`, so `T_walk(c₀) = ⊥` and `¬T_walk(c₀) = ⊤`. *Rewritten audit-view term:* `{y ∈ succs(s₀) : ¬is_filtered_retired(y)} = {y ∈ {y₀} : ¬⊤} = ∅`, identical `⊥` for `T_walk`. The two spellings agree, and the agreement is not vacuous: the *naive* same-view merge, reading `succs(s₀)` at active *without* the filter, would see `{y₀} ≠ ∅` and compute `T_walk(c₀) = ⊤`, `¬T_walk = ⊥`, the wrong value. The retired successor `y₀` is precisely where default and active part, and the rebuild's filter is what carries the default-view verdict faithfully into the audit-view term. With `T_mark(b₀) = ¬⊤ = ⊥`, `¬T_mark(b₀) = ⊤`, so `quiescent_{R'}(Σ*) = ⊤ ∧ ⊤ = ⊤` — one PL term, one common view, value intact across the heterogeneous rebuild.

## Extinction discipline

Reaching quiescence requires that firing *consumes* the condition that fired. Without a contract to that effect, a rule can fire forever on the same argument.

**X-DEF (ExtinctionDisciplined).** Rule ρ is *extinction-disciplined* iff for every reachable Σ and `x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ` with `T_ρ(x, Σ) = ⊤`, every fire of `(ρ, x)` yields Σ' with `T_ρ(x, Σ') = ⊥`. A registry is extinction-disciplined iff each rule is.

**Q2 (ContractOnOutputs).** Extinction discipline constrains emissions, not bodies: it is decided by `T_ρ` evaluations at the pre- and post-states — both public PL facts — so any two bodies with the same outputs are equivalent under it, and nondeterministic bodies are admissible whenever *all* their permitted outputs flip the trigger.

**Q3 (StaticCheckability).** If `Post_ρ` is *strong enough* — *every* emission set satisfying it at a trigger-true `(x, Σ)` produces a post-state falsifying `T_ρ(x, ·)` — then ρ is extinction-disciplined. The condition is *static*: it speaks only of PL-typed objects and the surface's emission forms, never of a running body. **The Marker pattern.** Where the static check is also *effective* is the **Marker pattern**: the trigger is the negated existential `¬(∃ c ∈ L_K :: a ∈ coverage_G(c))` over a grow-only audit slice `L_K`, and `Post_ρ` deposits *exactly the witness the `∃` quantifies over* — a `K`-tuple covering `a` — so the deposit grows `L_K`, the existential goes ⊤, the trigger goes ⊥, and "strong enough" is decided by a finite syntactic comparison of trigger spelling against emission form, no state quantified. One subtlety attends an **idem=⊤** marker class, where `Emit_K` is a *no-op on a dedup hit* (ASN-0128 I1) and deposits nothing: "the deposit grows `L_K`" then needs a hit ruled out, and the audit-slice spelling rules it out for free. The trigger reads the *audit* slice `L_K`, so firing (`T_ρ(a, Σ) = ⊤`) certifies that *no* `c ∈ L_K` covers `a`; a dedup hit would be an *active* tuple I0-equal to the emitted witness — its `G` covering `a` by the coverage equality — and the active subset injects into the audit slice (`A_K ⊆ L_K` by ActiveSubset, ASN-0086; the dedup test reads `A_K`, ASN-0128 I2), so that hit-witness would *itself* be a `c ∈ L_K` covering `a`, contradicting the fire. Fire and dedup-hit cannot co-occur: the emit is necessarily a *miss*, deposits, and grows `L_K`. The same audit reading makes even a *born-nullified* deposit suffice — born-nullified means *in* `L_K`, absent from `A_K` (ASN-0128 I3) — so the existential, ranging over the audit slice, goes ⊤ whether or not the deposit lands active. (An idem=⊥ class never dedups, so its deposit is unconditional.) For the Marker pattern, then, "verifiable at registration" is sound *and* effective. **Not effective in general.** Outside it, the strong-enough condition quantifies over `(x, Σ)`: read over *reachable* trigger-true pairs (matching X-DEF's "for every reachable Σ") it is reachability-quantified — meta-level, no decision procedure, a sound characterization only — and read over *all* states it is a sound over-approximation of X-DEF but a PL-validity question (PC1 quantifiers, PC2a aggregates, composed with the emission semantics) this note neither shows decidable nor equips with a procedure; either reading is a static obligation, not a general effective check. The companion SF-spelling half of at-most-once is, by contrast, genuinely decidable wherever invoked — PD0's ST/SF rules are syntax-directed, like WT, the very rules ASN-0130's `certify_pd_stable` runs — so it is the extinction half alone this note must scope. Sufficient, not necessary: a trivially weak contract (`Post_ρ ≡ ⊤`) certifies nothing, and a rule may be disciplined for reasons no contract of this shape expresses. Failure-to-verify is not violation.

**Q4 (Locality).** Extinction discipline is per-rule: its definition mentions no other rule. Registries compose pointwise — and precisely because the property is local, it cannot alone be sufficient for termination: locally disciplined rules can re-arm *each other* without bound.

**Q-EXT (ExtinctionByClass).** If `T_ρ` is an **SF spelling** (⊥-stable, PD0, ASN-0129), extinction discipline strengthens to *at-most-once firing per argument along any derivation*: the disciplined fire makes `T_ρ(x, ·)` false, and SF makes false permanent — no later `→_sh` step re-arms it, whether another rule's fire *or an environment step*, on any target: PD0's ⊥-stability is a property of the step relation, indifferent to who issues the step. This *step-agnosticism* is exactly why Q5a's bound survives environment input in the open model. The count of real fires of ρ is then bounded by the total growth of `[D_ρ]` alone, with no analysis of other rules' emissions *or of the environment's*. *Proof.* Immediate composition of X-DEF with PD0's ⊥-stability. ∎ The **Marker pattern (Q3)** is the construction that secures both halves at registration: its trigger is an SF spelling (SF by PD0's quantifier rule) and its emit-the-witness contract is the extinction half in Q3's decidable-match case. The check is registration-time and spelling-level.

**Q-FLIP (FalsifierAccounting).** For triggers *not* in SF, what can re-arm them is the falsifier inventory ASN-0129's FP enumerates, read off with PD1/PD2: a retraction shrinking an active slice the trigger reads; a BH1-type emission moving a default-view result; a BH4-footprint change from any deposit in a watched home; and — the re-armer that is no retraction at all — a *bare deposit growing an active slice*, which flips an `∃`-shaped active-view trigger `⊥→⊤` (PD1: `(∃ x ∈ M_K :: P(x))` at view active "flips ⊥→⊤ on a K-deposit") or perturbs a non-monotone verdict atom such as `target_of`/`targets_keyed` (PD2: a term containing `targets_keyed` is "perturbed by deposits of every BH3-attached Binary type"). The deposit re-armer bites even on a `¬`-shaped, extinguishable trigger: for a BH3-attached Binary `K`, the trigger `T(s) ≡ ¬def(target_of(s, K))` is ⊤ with no active `K`-tuple from `s`, is extinguished to ⊥ by firing one tuple `s → y`, then is re-armed to ⊤ by a *second* active tuple `s → y'` that turns `target_of(s, K)` to ⊥ ("several", ASN-0128 BH3) — no retraction, no BH1, no BH4. The folklore rule "no retraction ⟹ each trigger flips at most once" is therefore unsound against the shipped view machinery — a registry whose triggers read default views, age atoms, *or growing active slices* re-arms without any retraction. (SF triggers are immune: ⊥-stability (PD0) makes a falsified SF trigger permanent against every item above, deposits included — exactly why Q-EXT and Q5a demand the SF spelling, alongside the extinction discipline that supplies the falsification SF then makes permanent.) Trigger-flip counting must be done per spelling class, and the conservative design stance falls out: *triggers in SF, termination conditions in ST*, both certifiable.

## Conditional termination

**H-FAIR (FairnessHypothesis).** A *fire sequence* `σ = (Σ₀, s₁, Σ₁, s₂, …)` interleaves, from `Σ₀`, two kinds of step: a *fire* of some `(ρ, x)` (RG) — atomic against environment interleaving (H-ATOM) — and an *environment step* — any non-registry `→_sh*` transition (another agent or registry, or external input), which the scheduler neither issues nor constrains. Both advance the state through the gated relation; the registry authors only its fires. `σ` is *fair* iff for every `(ρ, x)` and *every* index `k` with `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at `Σ_k`, some later index `m > k` *discharges that occurrence* one of three ways: **real-fired** (a non-no-op fire of `(ρ, x)` at a step past `k`), **removed** from its domain (`x ∉ [D_ρ]_{Σ_m}`), or **falsified in place** (`T_ρ(x, Σ_m) = ⊥` with `x ∈ [D_ρ]_{Σ_m}` still). This is the *per-occurrence* reading, not once-per-argument: every trigger-true index incurs its own later discharge, so a single earlier discharge cannot excuse an argument that *stays* trigger-true across a later tail. The third discharge — in-domain falsification — covers a trigger that stops being true without its argument leaving the domain: the SF marker trigger `¬(∃ c :: x ∈ coverage_G(c))` goes ⊥ the instant *any* actor (the environment included, emitting through the same surface) deposits a covering tuple, leaving `x` in-domain but trigger-false — and a scheduler controls only its *fires*, so it cannot prevent the environment from falsifying `x` first. *"Fired" here means real-fired* — a *trigger-true* fire (RG's non-no-op), keyed on the trigger alone and so decoupled from both state-change and trigger-consumption: an idem=⊤ dedup-hit emission leaves `Σ' = Σ` (ASN-0128 I1), and a fire of a non-extinction-disciplined rule need not falsify its own trigger. A no-op fire (trigger-false by RG) discharges nothing. The real-fired discharge is *effective* — it consumes the occurrence's trigger — only under extinction discipline, which forces the post-state falsification (X-DEF); a *vacuous* real fire, advancing neither, would re-arm its trigger at once, but H-RF excludes any loop of them, each iteration being a real fire. A domain can shrink under a fire's retraction *or* under an environment step, and the scheduler owes nothing to absent or already-falsified arguments. Fairness is a scheduler property; any discipline satisfying the statement (round-robin, queue-fair, priority with aging) discharges it, the removal and falsification escapes absorbing exactly the environmental interference a fire-only scheduler cannot forestall.

**H-SFAIR (StrongFairnessHypothesis).** A fire sequence `σ` is *strongly fair* iff every `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at *infinitely many* indices along σ is *real-fired at infinitely many indices* — the standard *infinitely-often-enabled ⟹ infinitely-often-taken* strengthening. It differs from H-FAIR in closing, for the *infinitely-recurring* arguments, the two non-firing escapes H-FAIR allows (removal and in-domain falsification): an argument the environment removes — or, for a non-SF trigger, re-falsifies — before each scheduled fire and then re-arms satisfies H-FAIR forever without firing, whereas H-SFAIR demands it be fired. It is genuinely stronger: for infinite σ, `H-SFAIR ⟹ H-FAIR` — each occurrence of an infinitely-recurring argument discharged by a later fire, each occurrence of a finitely-recurring one by the removal-or-falsification holding permanently past its last true index. The implication is scoped to infinite σ: a finite σ ending trigger-true satisfies H-SFAIR vacuously yet violates H-FAIR's end-of-sequence obligation (Q6). Like H-FAIR, it constrains only the *registry's* scheduling, not the environment.

*Read through Q-EXT: the regime form.* In the all-SF, extinction-disciplined regime over a *non-grow-only* domain, Q-EXT caps each argument at *one* real fire — a single fire settles its SF trigger permanently — so H-SFAIR's consequent, *real-fired at infinitely many indices*, is unsatisfiable for every `(ρ, x)`. The implication therefore collapses to the negation of its antecedent — not to "each recurring argument is fired infinitely often," which Q-EXT forbids — giving the **regime form** of H-SFAIR: **in this regime H-SFAIR holds iff *no* `(ρ, x)` is trigger-true at infinitely many indices.**

*Satisfiability is environment-conditional.* H-FAIR is satisfiable against an arbitrary environment because its removal and falsification escapes absorb interference a fire-only scheduler cannot forestall; the regime form of H-SFAIR is not. The interleaving model admits consecutive environment steps (no turn-fairness is stated), so an admissible environment can, around each scheduler turn, *add* `xᵢ` then *remove* it: `xᵢ` is then trigger-true at infinitely many indices yet absent at *every* scheduler pre-state, and no discipline ever fires it — priority-with-aging included, aging ordering only *in-domain* arguments. So the regime form is satisfiable only under a turn-fairness in which the scheduler eventually fires any recurrently-*presented* argument — a condition this note neither states nor derives. That turn-fairness is *joint* — scheduler aggression together with an environment that leaves each recurrently-presented argument in-domain at some scheduler turn — so H-SFAIR is a *distinct* sufficient condition for reach-and-hold, overlapping regime (i) in outcome but not a sub-form of it secured by scheduling alone: it trades environment idle for environment turn-fairness, and (with bounded growth) reaches-and-holds even against an environment that never idles — e.g. endless `dom(Σ.C)` deposits, which no `[D_ρ]` or `T_ρ` reads (V-DOC/QD-audit, ASN-0129).

**H-RF (FiniteRealFires).** A fire sequence from Σ₀ has *finitely many real fires* iff its real (non-no-op) fires are finite in number.

**W (Work).** `W(σ)` is the set of (rule, argument, index) triples at which a trigger is true along σ. The load-bearing quantity is the *per-σ* count `|W(σ)|` — Q5 below bounds any single σ's real fires by it, with no registry-level hypothesis. The registry-level universal `|W(σ)| < ∞` for *every* σ from Σ₀ (H-W) holds iff every reachable state is quiescent — the contrapositive: from a reachable non-quiescent Σ with `(ρ, x)` trigger-true, extend σ by environment `K.α` (content) deposits. A literal stutter `Σ →_sh* Σ` is *not* available — every `→_sh` step strictly grows one of `dom(Σ.M)`, `dom(Σ.C)`, `dom(Σ.L)` (the gated step effects, ASN-0126), so no nontrivial run returns to Σ, and the one other way to hold the state fixed, a no-op fire, needs an available trigger-*false* `(ρ', x')` (RG), which a non-quiescent state need not offer. The `K.α` tail sidesteps both: `dom(Σ.C)` carries no QD base and no membership atom (V-DOC/QD-audit, ASN-0129), so neither `[D_ρ]` nor `T_ρ` reads it — both are preserved at every later index, so `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)` for all `k` and `|W(σ)| = ∞` — so it restates the conclusion, not a hypothesis toward it.

**Q5 (RealFiresAreBounded).** For *every* fire sequence σ from Σ₀, with no registry-level hypothesis, the real (non-no-op) fires number at most `|W(σ)|`. *Proof.* Each real fire at step k+1 witnesses `(ρ, x, k) ∈ W(σ)`, and the map real-fire ↦ `(ρ, x, k)` is injective *by the step index alone* (the index ranging over σ's positions, environment steps among them): distinct real fires occupy distinct steps, so their triples differ in the index component — extinguished or not. The injection bounds the count. ∎ This *per-σ injection* is the meaningful content: it bites exactly on the σ's with `|W(σ)| < ∞`, and is vacuous (≤ ∞) on a σ in which a rule spins on a fixed `x` — which witnesses infinitely many `(ρ, x, k₁), (ρ, x, k₂), …`. Extinction discipline is neither used nor needed: its leverage is elsewhere — Q-EXT and Q5a, where it lets bounded domain growth supply the same finite-real-fire conclusion (H-RF, above) directly, with no `W(σ)` bound at all.

**Q5a (ExtinctionBound).** For an all-SF, *extinction-disciplined* registry (every trigger an SF spelling, every rule extinction-disciplined — instantiated by Marker-pattern rules (Q3), the registration-checkable instance), the finite-real-fire conclusion follows from bounded domain growth *directly*, with no work bound: real fires number at most `Σ_ρ |⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}|` — each argument fires each rule at most once (Q-EXT, which consumes *both* the SF spelling and the extinction discipline), so the only unbounded-real-fire route is unbounded *new* arguments — and in the open sequence those arrive from *two* sources, the registry's own emissions and the environment's steps, so `|⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}|` bounds external input as much as internal enlargement. SF alone does not bound the count — an SF trigger paired with a contract that emits something *other* than its own falsifier (a `cmt`-trigger `¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))` fired by emitting a `res`, never a `cmt`) stays ⊤ on a fixed argument forever (⊥-stability permits ⊤→⊤, and no `cmt` is ever emitted to flip it) and is a real fire at *every* step — unboundedly many, against the display, though its domain `{t}` is constant. Extinction discipline forbids exactly that second route: the disciplined fire falsifies its own trigger, SF makes the falsification permanent (Q-EXT), and only the two together make at-most-once per argument a theorem. This supplies H-RF (above) by a route disjoint from Q5 — it never mentions `W(σ)`. The bound's two ingredients are not equal in checkability: at-most-once-per-argument is a *registration-time* fact for these Marker-pattern rules (Q-EXT), whereas `|⋃_k [D_ρ]_{Σ_k}| < ∞` quantifies over all reachable states and is as meta-level as H-W — a bound on arguments rather than on trigger-true step-instances, but reachability-quantified all the same. In the open sequence the domain bound is *strictly stronger* than H-RF: it implies H-RF (the display above), but H-RF does not imply it — a fair scheduler facing an environment that flags infinitely many distinct targets and retracts each before its fire keeps the real-fire count at zero while `⋃_k [D_ρ]` grows unbounded. So Q5a is a genuine *route*: a sufficient condition on external input, proved *instead of* the cross-rule re-arm analysis. The **closed** special case is degenerate — with the registry the only depositor, unbounded `⋃_k [D_ρ]` needs unboundedly many deposits, hence unboundedly many real fires, so there the implication reverses (`bounded-domain-growth ⟺ H-RF`) and Q5a says nothing H-RF did not.

**Q6 (TerminationUnderFairness).** *Registry-side, unconditional.* Under H-RF and H-FAIR, the registry's own drive to fire is exhausted after finitely many real fires: σ has a last real fire (index N) or none, and past N every fire is a no-op. The registry moves the state no further — *all* state change past N is environment steps, so any residual non-quiescence is *environment-driven* — and Q1's absorption (a fixed point of *firing*) is the registry's standing guarantee, holding whatever the environment does and under every regime hypothesis below.

*Reaching and holding, by hypothesis package.* Whether σ additionally *reaches and holds* a quiescent state turns on what is assumed beyond H-RF + H-FAIR:
- **+ regime (i)** (the environment eventually stops presenting trigger-true work — idles): reached and held, for *any* registry.
- **+ Q5a's package** (all-SF, extinction-disciplined, bounded growth) **with grow-only domains, under weak H-FAIR**: reached and held — the structural route (regime (ii)), no environment-idle hypothesis.
- **+ Q5a's package, non-grow-only**: all-SF makes the registry's *work* finite, but *reaching* quiescence defers to an environment hypothesis for each non-grow-only rule — regime (i) for that rule directly, or strong fairness (H-SFAIR).

*Proof.* H-RF bounds the real fires, so σ has a last real fire — index N — or none, in which case read what follows at Σ₀. No fire past N is real (a trigger-true fire *is* a real fire and would exceed H-RF's count), so every fire past N is a no-op (RG): the registry moves the state no further, and *all* state change past N is environment steps. Any `(ρ, x)` trigger-true at some `Σ_m`, m ≥ N, is by H-FAIR eventually *real-fired* — impossible past N without exceeding H-RF — or *removed* from its domain *or falsified in place* (`T_ρ(x, ·) = ⊥`, `x` still in `[D_ρ]`); neither a removal nor an in-domain falsification past N can come from a fire (none is real, so none retracts or deposits), so each is an *environment* step. The discharge past N is thus a clean two-way environmental split — removed *or* falsified — real-firing having been spent. Two regimes bear on *reaching and holding* quiescence. **(i) Environment eventually idle** (finitely many environment steps): past both N and the last environment step the state is constant, and a non-quiescent constant tail would leave a trigger-true argument forever unfired, unremoved, *and unfalsified* (a constant tail changes nothing, so none of the three discharges can occur), contradicting H-FAIR — so the tail is quiescent and absorbing. This route is general (any registry). **(ii) All-SF registry** (Q5a's case): all-SF bounds the real fires structurally (Q5a ⟹ H-RF, so N exists by Q5a's structural route rather than by assuming H-RF directly — Q5a's bounded-domain-growth hypothesis still in force) and makes every *fired* argument permanently trigger-false against every `→_sh` step (SF immunity, Q-EXT step-agnostic). When additionally every domain is *grow-only*, both *reaching* and *holding* quiescence follow under weak H-FAIR + bounded growth: a grow-only domain cannot shrink, so H-FAIR's removal escape is unavailable and each trigger-true argument is eventually *real-fired or falsified in place* — and for an SF trigger either settling is permanent (Q-EXT step-agnostic), the marker, once recorded by the rule's own fire or by an environment deposit, never un-recording. With bounded growth each of the finitely many arguments is thus eventually settled and SF-immune thereafter — no later step re-arms it — so quiescence is reached and held. When a domain is *not* grow-only the environment can remove an argument (or, for a non-SF trigger, re-falsify it) *before* it fires — discharging weak H-FAIR by removal or in-domain falsification, not firing — and re-arm it later, so *reaching* a quiescent state, not merely holding one, reacquires an environment hypothesis for that rule — that the environment eventually leave each argument in-domain and trigger-true until fired (regime (i) for the rule) — supplied directly, or by strong fairness (H-SFAIR). SF immunity still pays — re-arming an *already-settled* argument is a no-op, so only *unsettled* arguments can defer quiescence. The *finite*-σ case is the same H-FAIR step read at the end: a fair finite sequence cannot end at a non-quiescent state, a trigger-true argument there left unfired, unremoved, *and unfalsified*. The non-grow-only all-SF case under a never-idling environment is where the conclusion is genuinely weaker. The non-grow-only case has a definite structure: three obstructions to a *reached-and-held* quiescent state, not all failing the same way. **(1)** and **(3)** obstruct *reaching* — no state along σ is ever quiescent — whereas **(2)** obstructs only *holding*, quiescent states being reached but then unsettled by the environment; only the first is excluded by bounded growth. **(1)** An environment *alternating ever-fresh* trigger-true arguments (each removed before the next, so H-FAIR holds, and none fired, so H-RF holds with zero real fires) keeps *every* state non-quiescent — quiescence *unreached* — but this needs *unboundedly many* distinct arguments, so bounded domain growth (Q5a) excludes it outright. **(2)** An environment *oscillating one* non-grow-only argument's domain membership re-presents it with all-empty gaps between presentations — quiescent in the gaps, non-quiescent during each presentation: quiescence *is reached* (each gap state satisfies `quiescent_R`, Q0) but not *held*, the re-presentation being fresh environment input — Q8 re-entry, against which Q0 quiescence absorbs *firing* (Q1) but never the environment — a *holding* failure, not a reaching one, that survives bounded growth on its single argument. **(3)** an environment *cycling finitely many* trigger-true arguments *out of phase*: a fixed finite set `{x₁, …, x_j}` (so bounded growth holds, `⋃_k [D_ρ] = {x₁, …, x_j}`), each `xᵢ` *removed* from its domain before its fire (the SF trigger forbids falsify-and-re-arm — falsification is permanent — so the cycling runs through domain membership: unflag before the fire, re-flag after) and re-presented, arranged so that at *every* state at least one `xᵢ` stands trigger-true — re-arming one before settling the next, leaving *no* all-empty gap. Then H-RF holds (zero real fires), bounded growth holds (the argument set is fixed and finite), weak H-FAIR holds (each argument discharged by removal, never firing) — yet *no* state is quiescent: quiescence is *not reached*, with every registry-side and bounded-growth hypothesis satisfied. The obstruction is the environment's power to *withdraw* an argument before it fires — a non-grow-only/open-model phenomenon, impossible where domains are grow-only (regime (ii): removal barred). H-SFAIR closes case (3): by its regime form (above), case (3)'s σ is excluded — its every `xᵢ` is trigger-true at infinitely many indices, which the regime form forbids. With bounded growth the arguments are finitely many, so each is then trigger-true at only finitely many indices, their trigger-true indices admitting a maximum `N′`; past `N′` no argument is trigger-true — `quiescent_R` holds — and every settled SF trigger is immune to re-arming, so quiescence is reached *and held*. Cases (2) and (3) are *re-entry* at top level, and all-SF stops neither; only a further hypothesis — regime (i) (environment idle) directly, or strong fairness (H-SFAIR) — *reaches and holds* quiescence over a non-grow-only domain, case (2) reaching one unaided but unable to hold it, case (3) reaching none. ∎ Drop H-RF and rules can re-arm each other forever, each re-fire a fresh real fire without bound; drop H-FAIR and a trigger-true argument can be starved eternally with the system never settling. These two registry-side failure modes are genuinely separable, and neither is derivable from the other. (The environment supplies a third, open-model axis drawn out in the proof — re-entry over non-grow-only domains can prevent or undo quiescence — which no registry hypothesis controls.)

## Scope parameterization

**SC (Scope), Q7–Q9.** A *scope* is any Boolean PL predicate `S` over addresses. A rule's domain `D_ρ ∈ QD` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) or tuple-valued (`dom(Tup)` — e.g. an audit slice `L_K`, as the worked example's `ρ_R` takes), so an address-predicate cannot in general be applied to a domain element directly: over `dom(Tup)` the element `x` is a tuple, and `S(x)` is a sort error. The scope's *restriction of ρ* is therefore a QD filter `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : β_ρ^S(x)}` whose body `β_ρ^S` is a Boolean PL predicate *at `D_ρ`'s sort* relating the element to `S` — and the relation is the *rule's* to declare, not one fixed projection, because over `dom(Tup)` several inequivalent bodies are well-typed and they realize different tiers. The one standing constraint on that declaration is *S-monotonicity*: `β_ρ^S` must be monotone in `S` — `S` occurring only positively — so that `S' ⟹ S` yields `β_ρ^{S'} ⟹ β_ρ^S` pointwise. The three canonical bodies below satisfy it by construction (`S` appears once, positively, under at most an existential), so the constraint costs the vocabulary nothing; Q9 is where it does its work. For an address-valued `D_ρ` there is one natural body, `S(x)` itself: the element scoped by its own address. For a tuple-valued `D_ρ` the canonical choices are:
- **per-emitter** — `S(addr(x))`, scoping a tuple by its own address. `addr : Tup → T` is V-TUP's projection and `S ∘ addr` is well-typed by WT's PC2 plain-composition rule. This scopes a tuple by *where the tuple itself lives*; for the worked resolver (`D = L_cmt`) that is the comment's home chain — its finding's region — *not* the target the comment points at.
- **per-target** — `(∃ y ∈ addrs_G(x) :: S(y))`, scoping a tuple by its *denoted targets*. `addrs_G : Tup → ℘_fin(T)` is V-TUP's to-endset projection; the finite denoted set is a QD domain by set-valued closure, and the existential is PC1 with closed body `S(y)` — a Boolean PL predicate at `dom(Tup)`. The domain here *must* be the finite denoted set `addrs_G(x)`, never the infinite coverage `⋃{t : y ≼ t, y ∈ addrs_G(x)}` those targets close upward to: that upward closure is non-finite (`coverage_G(x)` is membership-testable but not enumerable), so it is not a QD domain (QD-fin), and `(∃ y ∈ coverage_G(x) :: …)` would be ill-formed. A coverage-*flavored* per-target scope — keyed to a region beneath each target rather than to the exact denoted addresses — stays available, but as the scope predicate `S` itself carrying a coverage *membership* test (V-PRIM, decidable) evaluated at each finite `y ∈ addrs_G(x)`, never as a quantification over `coverage_G(x)`. This, *not* the `addr` body, is what a per-target scope means for a tuple rule: "elements whose target is in scope." Symmetrically **per-source** is `(∃ y ∈ addrs_F(x) :: S(y))`.
Each body is a Boolean PL predicate at the domain's sort, and the filtering former (ASN-0129) admits exactly such a `P : D × S → Bool` over an address- *or* tuple-valued `D`, preserving the sort; so `{x ∈ [D_ρ]_Σ : β_ρ^S(x)}` is a well-typed QD filter for every rule, tuple-domained ones included. The `addr` body realizes per-target scoping *only* for an address-domained rule whose elements already live in the target region; a tuple-domained rule reaches the per-target tier through the `addrs_G` body, never through `addr` — under `addr`-scoping a per-target restriction of `ρ_R` would be vacuous, no comment's home-chain address lying in its target's region. `quiescent_{R,S}(Σ)` restricts Q0's inner quantification to the elements this filter retains. Then: **Q7** — `quiescent_{R,S}` carries Q0's status exactly: the scope adds this PC1 filter to each rule's inner quantification, the outer `∀ ρ ∈ R` is the same finite static expansion (V-IDX), and the scope body `β_ρ^S` simply adds whatever view-sensitive constituents `S` reads to the rebuild Q0 already performs — so by Q0's fixed-view-base rewrite `quiescent_{R,S} ∈ PL` for *every* registry, decidable and observer-uniform, the heterogeneous case again paying a rebuild rather than PL-membership. The claim covers tuple-domained rules — the worked example's `ρ_R` among them, whose `dom(Tup)` restriction is a well-typed QD filter for whichever scoping body it declares: per-emitter `{c ∈ L_cmt : S(addr(c))}`, or — the per-target reading a comment scope usually intends — `{c ∈ L_cmt : (∃ y ∈ addrs_G(c) :: S(y))}`. Scope quiescence inherits Q0's recognizability, no more than Q0 itself had. **Q8** — at a scope-quiescent state, in-scope fires are no-ops (absorption relativizes); out-of-scope fires are unconstrained, and an out-of-scope emission may re-arm an in-scope trigger — *re-entry* — which is itself detectable per-state by Q7. **Q9** — for the S-monotone scoping bodies SC admits (its standing constraint), quiescence is anti-monotone in the scope: `S' ⟹ S` shrinks each filtered domain — `{x ∈ [D_ρ] : β_ρ^{S'}(x)} ⊆ {x ∈ [D_ρ] : β_ρ^S(x)}` by `β_ρ`'s S-monotonicity — and quiescence over the smaller domain is the weaker demand, so `quiescent_S ⟹ quiescent_{S'}`; the converse fails whenever the larger scope holds work no smaller scope sees. Without S-monotonicity the nesting inverts: a body with `S` under negation — `β_ρ^S(x) ≡ ¬S(addr(x))` — *grows* the filtered domain as `S` shrinks, so for the everywhere-true scope `S` and everywhere-false `S'` (whence `S' ⟹ S`) the `S`-filtered domain is empty (`quiescent_S` vacuously ⊤) while the `S'`-filtered domain is all of `[D_ρ]` (`quiescent_{S'}` possibly ⊥), inverting the implication — exactly the body SC's S-monotonicity excludes. Canonical tiers (per-target, per-collection, system-wide) are application vocabulary over this machinery — each a scope predicate `S` paired with the SC body that carries it to a rule's domain sort (per-target reaching a tuple rule through the denoted-target body, an address rule through `S` directly) — the substrate admitting any PL predicate as a scope and committing to nothing about which scopes matter.

## Worked composition

The two illustrative classes of ASN-0129's worked trace — `cmt` (Binary, idem=⊥, comment from finding to target) and `res` (Binary, idem=⊤, resolution from target to comment) — already form a registry; we verify its termination.

*Rules.* Producer `ρ_P`: domain = `{t ∈ M_tgt : needs_attention(t)}` — a QD filter (ASN-0129) over the base `M_tgt`, the members of a Unary target-marking type `tgt` (one of ASN-0129's QD bases `M_K`), with filter body `needs_attention(t) ≡ is_attn(t)` a Boolean PL membership predicate (D2, ASN-0128) over a Unary attention-flag type `attn`. The producer conjunct is read at the *active* view — so `M_tgt` and `is_attn` alike denote active slices (PC3 fixes one view per term, so the domain cannot mix an audit `M_tgt` with an active `is_attn`); `T_P` below stays SF at that nominal view all the same, reading `L_cmt`, the audit slice view-fixed by name (`L_K`, ASN-0129). The filter is well-typed — `M_tgt` is address-valued (`dom(T)`) and `is_attn : T → Bool` is Boolean at that sort — so the domain is a well-formed QD expression before any SF/extinction/bound property is claimed of the rule. It is *not* grow-only: `is_attn` at the active view is not ⊥-stable (PD1 — a fresh `attn` deposit flips it `⊥→⊤`, a retraction `⊤→⊥`), so `needs_attention` may flip either way; trigger `T_P(t) ≡ ¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c))`, with `L_cmt` the audit slice (ASN-0086's typed-relation machinery) — fires by emitting one `cmt` targeting t. Folding `needs_attention` into the *domain* rather than the trigger is deliberate: it leaves `T_P` a pure negated existential (SF, below) at the cost of a non-grow-only domain, which Q5a's union bound absorbs. Resolver `ρ_R`: domain = `L_cmt` (audit slice — grow-only); trigger `T_R(c) ≡ ¬(∃ r ∈ L_res :: addr(c) ∈ coverage_G(r))` — fires by emitting one `res` targeting c.

*Class check.* With `needs_attention` moved out, both triggers are now pure negated existentials over grow-only audit slices with step-constant bodies — **SF spellings** by PD0's quantifier and Boolean rules. The move was forced: had `needs_attention` stayed a trigger conjunct, `T_P ≡ needs_attention(t) ∧ ¬(∃ …)` would *not* be SF — PD0's `∧` preserves SF only when both conjuncts are, and `needs_attention` is not ⊥-stable (it can flip `⊥→⊤` on a still-uncommented target, flipping `T_P` with it), so Q-EXT would not apply. (Spelled over *active* slices instead, neither trigger would be SF: a retraction of a resolution would re-open the comment — Q-FLIP's first falsifier — and termination would need the full Q-FLIP falsifier analysis. The audit spelling is the design choice, made checkable.)

*Extinction.* `Post_P` requires an emitted `cmt` covering t; `Post_R` an emitted `res` covering `addr(c)` — each strong enough (Q3), and strong enough *effectively*: this is Q3's decidable-match case (Q-EXT), each contract depositing exactly the witness its trigger's `∃` quantifies over (`Post_P` a `cmt` covering t, growing `L_cmt`; `Post_R` a `res` covering `addr(c)`, growing `L_res`). The emission makes the trigger's existential true, hence the trigger false. For `ρ_R`'s **idem=⊤** `res` the growth of `L_res` is not free of the dedup caveat but survives it (Q3): firing reads `T_R(c) = ⊤`, so no `r ∈ L_res` covers `addr(c)`, whereas a dedup hit would need an *active* — hence audit (`A_res ⊆ L_res`) — tuple covering it; the emit is therefore necessarily a miss and deposits, and even a born-nullified deposit joins `L_res` and flips the audit-read trigger. `ρ_P`'s `cmt` is idem=⊥, so it never dedups. With SF, Q-EXT gives at-most-once per argument for both rules.

*Bound (Q5a).* Both of Q5a's hypotheses now hold — every trigger an SF spelling (just shown) and every rule extinction-disciplined (the contracts above, Q3) — so by Q5a the real fires number at most `|⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}| + |⋃_k [D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ_k}|`. The resolver's domain is grow-only, so its union is just `|comments ever|`; the producer's domain is not, so its union is *the set of targets that ever need attention*. Here is the crux the open model makes honest: **no rule writes `attn` or `tgt`** — `ρ_P` emits `cmt`, `ρ_R` emits `res` — so the producer's domain grows only by *environment* deposits, and `⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}` is exactly the population of targets the environment ever flags. Bounding it is a bound on external input, the open-model hypothesis Q5a's route turns on. Hence real fires ≤ |targets the environment ever flags| + |comments ever| (comments entering `L_cmt` through `ρ_P`, one per flagged target, plus any the environment deposits directly) — so the registry's real fires are finite, its *work* terminates, *if* that flagged population, and any direct environment comment traffic, is bounded — a *sufficient* condition, not necessary, instantiating Q5a's open-model asymmetry: bounding `⋃_k [D_{ρ_P}]` bounds environment flags, not registry fires. Q5a's closed-case degeneracy, made concrete: were the registry the only depositor — `attn`/`tgt` then unwritten entirely (the crux) — `{t ∈ M_tgt : is_attn(t)}` would be static at its `Σ₀` value, finite by QD-fin, and the registry would terminate *unconditionally* under fairness; only the shared substrate makes the bounded-flagged-population assumption necessary here. (That is the *work* bound, distinct from *reaching and holding* `quiescent_R`.) This registry cannot diverge *of its own accord* — it has no *internal* divergence route — and the reason is structural: by the crux no rule writes `attn`/`tgt`, and `res` is no retraction, so a `ρ_R` deposit moves neither `M_tgt` nor `is_attn` and cannot enlarge `[D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ_k}` — the two rules' domains and emissions are *type-isolated*. Re-arm of an *existing* target is independently barred: `T_P` is an SF spelling, so ⊥-stability (PD0) makes a falsified `T_P(t)` (`t` commented) stay false under every later deposit, and a `res` lands in `L_res`, which `T_P` never reads. Both *internal* divergence routes — re-arm and producer-domain growth via `ρ_R`'s emissions — are closed here by the registry's own types; what they do *not* close is the crux's environment-driven growth of `[D_{ρ_P}]` — the bounded-flagged-population hypothesis above. Divergence thus remains reachable — an unboundedly-flagging environment whose flags outrun retraction drives unboundedly many real `ρ_P` fires — just never of the registry's own making. Q4's warning that locally disciplined rules can re-arm each other is here *vacuous by type isolation* — no fire of either rule enlarges the other's domain — and the general cross-rule coupling discipline, where a *non-SF* lower rule makes re-arm a live route, is Open Question 4's cascade/re-opening theory, left there.

*Quiescence.* `quiescent_R` here is precisely "every target needing attention is commented, every comment resolved" — two nested quantifications whose bodies are ST (negations of the SF triggers, PD0). The registry is single-view *at the active view*: the producer's conjunct reads active (its domain's `M_tgt`/`is_attn`, the only view-parameterized constituents in the whole registry), while both triggers and the resolver's domain `L_cmt` are view-independent (audit slices fixed by name), so every view-parameterized constituent already sits at active and Q0's fixed-view-base rewrite is not even called on. By Q0, then, `quiescent_R ∈ PL`: decidable at every state, absorbing once reached (Q1). For this registry the *registry-side* guarantee — finitely many real fires, registry-inert past N — holds given a bounded flagged population and bounded direct comment traffic, under any weakly-fair scheduler. The two rules' halves then fare differently: the resolver (grow-only `L_cmt`) both *reaches and holds* its half under weak fairness outright, while the producer (non-grow-only domain) — whose flagged targets the environment can cycle out of phase, each unflagged before its fire — *reaches* its half (and holds it) only once the environment stops re-flagging uncommented targets (regime (i)) or under strong fairness (H-SFAIR); weak fairness plus bounded flagging leaves the producer's half possibly *unreached*, not merely unheld.

*A reached terminal state.* One concrete sequence exercises the nested quantifier and Q6's reaching claim end to end. Let the environment flag a single target and then idle: at `Σ₀`, `t ∈ M_tgt` with `is_attn(t) = ⊤` (both the `tgt`-membership and the `attn`-flag environment-deposited, neither ever retracted), and `L_cmt = L_res = ∅`. Then `[D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ₀} = {t ∈ M_tgt : is_attn(t)} = {t}` and `T_P(t, Σ₀) = ¬(∃ c ∈ L_cmt :: t ∈ coverage_G(c)) = ⊤` (the audit slice is empty), while `[D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ₀} = L_cmt = ∅` is argument-free — so `Σ₀` is non-quiescent, `ρ_P` trigger-true on `t`. **Fire `(ρ_P, t)`**: a real fire (trigger ⊤), discharging `Post_P` by one `Emit_cmt` depositing `c` covering `t` — a *single* `→_sh` step (I5: an idem=⊥ `Emit_cmt` invokes one `K.λ_sh` step), trivially atomic by H-ATOM. At the post-state `Σ₁`: `L_cmt = {c}`, so the existential `T_P` quantifies over is now witnessed and `T_P(t, Σ₁) = ⊥` (extinguished; SF-permanent thereafter, Q-EXT), and `[D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ₁} = {c}` with `T_R(c, Σ₁) = ¬(∃ r ∈ L_res :: addr(c) ∈ coverage_G(r)) = ⊤` — non-quiescent, `ρ_R` trigger-true on `c`. **Fire `(ρ_R, c)`**: real fire, one `Emit_res` depositing `r` covering `addr(c)`, again single-step. At `Σ₂`: `T_R(c, Σ₂) = ⊥` (extinguished, SF-permanent). Now *evaluate* `quiescent_R(Σ₂)` — the nested `(∀ ρ ∈ R :: ∀ x ∈ [D_ρ]_{Σ₂} :: ¬T_ρ(x, Σ₂))`. For `ρ_P`: `[D_{ρ_P}]_{Σ₂} = {t}` (the Σ₀ occurrence discharged by real-firing at Σ₁; `t` remains in-domain-but-false at Σ₂ — the environment never unflagged it — SF-permanent thereafter) and `¬T_P(t, Σ₂) = ⊤`. For `ρ_R`: `[D_{ρ_R}]_{Σ₂} = {c}` and `¬T_R(c, Σ₂) = ⊤`. Both conjuncts hold, so `quiescent_R(Σ₂) = ⊤` — the quantifier computes exactly "every flagged target commented, every comment resolved" and returns ⊤. Two real fires, then quiescence *reached* at `Σ₂`; with the environment idle past `Σ₀` it is also *held* — both settled triggers SF-immune against every later `→_sh` step — the finite, reached-and-held sequence Q6 promises, the resolver's grow-only half (regime (ii)) needing only weak fairness for it. (Had the environment instead unflagged `t` between `Σ₀` and `ρ_P`'s scheduled fire, `t` would leave `[D_{ρ_P}]` — H-FAIR's removal escape — and `[D_{ρ_P}]` would go empty, so `ρ_P`'s half would be *reached* vacuously in that gap (Q0 quiescent on the empty domain). A later re-flag re-arms it, so what a *single* flagged target lets the environment deny is *holding*, never *reaching* — case (2)'s one-argument oscillation, the non-grow-only producer's one environment hypothesis made concrete. *Reaching* the producer's half fails only under case (3), which needs ≥2 flagged targets cycled out of phase, as the plural-target statement above has it.)

## What this note doesn't cover

- **A scheduler.** H-FAIR is stated, not constructed; scheduling disciplines, their fairness proofs, the serialization of multi-step fires that discharges H-ATOM, the turn/serialization model H-SFAIR's satisfiability needs, concurrency reconciliation, and violation policy (log, halt, escalate) are operational machinery this corpus deliberately leaves at the implementation layer.
- **Stochastic rule bodies.** A body that flips its trigger with probability p < 1 satisfies the deterministic contracts only operationally (retry policies, N-consecutive-clean stopping rules) — evaluator-reliability engineering, not substrate semantics.
- **The activation binding.** RG assumes rules exist; what registers them, who may, and how rule registries are themselves governed is the protocol layer above (with ASN-0130's machinery available to it).
- **An environment model.** Environment steps are admitted abstractly — arbitrary non-registry `→_sh` transitions interleaved into σ; *which* external workloads supply bounded input (the dischargeable form of Q5a's hypothesis) and *whether* an environment eventually idles (Q6 regime (i)) are workload/protocol questions above this layer, not properties the substrate constrains.

## Open questions

1. **The SF certificate.** Q-EXT and Q5a make SF membership the load-bearing *uncertified* registration check for triggers — its companion, extinction discipline, being checkable via Q3's strong-enough contract for the Marker pattern, where the obligation reduces to a decidable match (a static, not-shown-decidable obligation otherwise, Q3) — and ASN-0130 ships only the ST⁺ certificate (`pd_stable`), no SF one. Should `pd_extinct` (SF certification) ship as a designated class — exactly the catalog-growth ASN-0130's own Open Question 4 raises for certificates beyond ST⁺ — closing the loop where "this registry terminates on bounded input" is a structural lint?

2. **A runtime divergence detector.** The distinct-argument bound (Q5a) is meta-level — reachability-quantified, PL-inexpressible; is there a PL-expressible *necessary* condition — a per-state predicate whose failure certifies unbounded work in progress — strong enough to catch the common divergence patterns (mutual re-arm cycles) at runtime, even though the full bound is inexpressible?

3. **Per-scope versus global work.** Q5/Q6 are stated globally; the per-scope analogs (real fires restricted to in-scope arguments, termination toward `quiescent_S`) appear routine, but whether a registry can settle at every scope of a tier (per-scope H-RF, under bounded per-scope input) without the corpus settling globally (global H-RF) deserves a structural answer — it is the formal version of "every document converges but the corpus doesn't," and the open substrate is exactly where it bites: an environment feeding unboundedly many internally-bounded scopes.

4. **Cross-scope oscillation.** Q8's re-entry means an outer-scope fire can repeatedly un-quiesce an inner scope. What registry or scheduling discipline bounds re-entry — and whether bounded re-entry is even the right demand, versus monotone progress *across* entries — is open, and is where a future cascade/re-opening theory would live.

5. **Contract necessity.** Q3 is sufficiency only: must every extinction-disciplined rule admit a strong-enough PL contract? A rule disciplined by reasoning PL cannot express would be sound but unverifiable at registration — the same sound-but-uncertifiable gap PD0's spelling-level classes accept by design.
