# ASN-0086: Typed Relations on Address Sets

*Drawing the link model forward into a relational vocabulary*

ASN-0043 establishes the link as a primitive: an addressed, owned, typed connection between spans of content. We now adopt a different vocabulary for the same structure. Where ASN-0043 speaks of *links* and *endsets*, we speak of *tuples* and *typed relations*. The two vocabularies describe one object — a standard-triple link `(F, G, Θ)` at address `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` is a tuple in a typed relation indexed by `Θ` — but predicates compose more cleanly over relations than over endsets, and several substrate-level guarantees become easier to state in this form.

We are looking for what a relation algebra over the link store affords. The answer is structural properties on the typed-relation substrate, partitioned by status: R0–R5 are derived lemmas from ASN-0043; R6a/R6b/R6c (and R6c-Corollary) are the substantive lemmas carrying the *active/audit distinction* (well-definedness of `A_K^Σ` is folded into the Definition of ActiveSubset itself — one line of set-difference accounting against `Σ.L` — rather than being numbered as a separate R-claim). The conceptual contribution sits in the *active/audit distinction* itself: two coherent views (`L_K`, the audit trail; `A_K`, the operational currently-in-effect set) co-exist over the same link store, with retractions cleanly mediating between them. The substantive properties carrying that distinction are R6a (RetractionStability — nullification persists across state transitions), R6b (SingleDepthRetraction — the predicate does not recurse), and R6c (RestorationByReemission — once retracted, always out of every future active subset). Their joint content is the substrate's own contribution: a self-referential retraction relation (made possible by R5) computed against a monotone audit trail (R3), yielding a usable active subset that ASN-0043's link model alone does not articulate. Alongside the active/audit distinction, this note makes a second structural commitment: the *sibling-frontier emission discipline*, a hypothesis on the substrate primitive (detailed below) under which the supplementary lemma R0a (FlatLinkDomain) establishes `dom(Σ.L)` as a tumbler-prefix antichain. R0a is Setup-free in its dependence on the foundation ASNs but discipline-conditional on the implementation's emission policy — the substrate primitive in isolation admits emissions at strict prefix-extensions of existing link addresses, which the discipline forbids; the udanax-green substrate adopts the discipline as evidence of realizability. The conditionality propagates to Nullify's single-tuple-scope guarantee. On top of these we define three operations (Emit_K, Observe, Nullify) and prove R7a (no `Σ.L`-affecting transition lies outside class (iii)); the relational layer is then *defined* to commit `Emit_K` as its sole state-affecting class-(iii) emission, and the consequence (the legacy alias *NullifyIsEmit*, sometimes labeled R7) is that all relational-layer state change reduces to `Emit_K`. (Document allocation and content emission, the other two primitive transitions in `→`, are inherited from ASN-0036 and are not reductions of `Emit_K`; the scope of the reduction is the link store `Σ.L` and the typed relations indexed over it.)


## The Two Foundational Sets

*Three-field tag convention.* Each R-claim and named operation below carries a headline tag of the form `[setup: req|free, discipline: req|free|N/A, stipulation: req|free|N/A]`, where the three fields summarize *direct* hypothesis use (the claim's own proof cites the hypothesis at a specific step), with an optional parenthetical for indirect dependencies inherited through other R-claims. The four non-foundation hypotheses each appear and are characterized at their first consumption site: the *Setup hypothesis* (globally `s_C`-resident content) and the *Subspace-distinctness axiom* (`s_C ≠ s_L`) below, the *Sibling-frontier emission discipline* before R0a, the *Unit-depth retraction discipline* before Nullify; R7b's stipulation appears at R7. A claim with all three fields `free`/`N/A` is unconditionally derived from the foundation ASNs alone.

**Setup hypothesis.** We work in systems satisfying ASN-0043 (and therefore ASN-0036 and ASN-0034). We additionally assume globally `s_C`-resident content:

`(A a ∈ dom(Σ.C) :: subspace_I(a) = s_C)`

Under this hypothesis the disjointness between content and tuple addresses (R4 below) holds substrate-wide as a structural property, not merely within the slice scoped to `s_C` that L14 (DualPrimitive, ASN-0043) supplies in its stated form.

*Maintenance protocol — Setup as external constraint on class-(ii) emission policy.* Setup is not derivable from ASN-0036's class-(ii) emission primitive, which permits content emission at any T4-valid `c` with `zeros(c) = 3` regardless of `subspace_I(c)`; Setup therefore stands as an external commitment on the implementation's class-(ii) emission policy. Tightening ASN-0036's class-(ii) primitive to require `subspace_I(c) = s_C` would discharge it at every reachable state.

**Subspace-distinctness hypothesis.** We additionally assume that the content and link subspace identifiers are distinct first-element-field values:

`s_C ≠ s_L`.

This is adopted as an explicit hypothesis of this note, parallel to the `s_C`-resident content hypothesis above. The distinctness is *presupposed* by ASN-0043's L0/L0a partition and L14's scoped disjointness (both collapse — L0a's slice to all of `dom(Σ.C)`, L14's disjointness to an unconditional rather than scoped form — if `s_C = s_L`), but ASN-0043 does not state it as one labeled fact. ASN-0086 promotes it to a single citable clause; consumption sites are R0 Step 4 (L14a and L14 preservation bullets), R4, and Nullify's no-content-address-under-`a` argument (direct), plus R5 via R0 (indirect). A formal derivation of `s_C ≠ s_L` from the foundation ASNs would discharge this hypothesis at every consumption site; pending such a derivation, it remains an explicit commitment of ASN-0086.

**Definition — zero-count depth.** The *zero-count depth* of a tumbler `t` relative to its prefix `s ≼ t` is `zeros(t) − zeros(s)`. By T4 (HierarchicalParsing, ASN-0034), each zero is a field separator, so the difference counts the new T4 field separators introduced between `s` and `t`. *Terminology note.* The name is chosen to avoid clashing with T4b's "element field" (the `E` projection — the fourth field, after the third zero, in T4b's NUDE parse). "Zero-count depth" is a generic measure over any T4-conforming tumbler; "element field" remains reserved throughout this note for T4b's `E` projection. Step (b) of the lemma below uses this directly: a child-spawn `(d, k')` with `k' ≥ 1` produces a child whose zero count exceeds `zeros(d)` by `k' − 1` (TA5 postcondition (d)), so the zero-count depth of the spawn relative to `d` is exactly `k' − 1`.

**Definition — allocator-tree depth.** The *allocator-tree depth* of an allocator A relative to a document `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` is the number of T10a child-spawn pairs `(·, k')` with `k' ∈ {1, 2}` on the L1c witness chain from `d` to A's base address (i.e., to A's first emission). This is *not* the same as zero-count depth: a `(·, 1)` child-spawn opens a new allocator without introducing a new zero (TA5 with `k' = 1` gives `zeros` unchanged), so it advances the allocator hierarchy by one level without advancing zero-count depth. Concretely, the allocator opened by the single child-spawn `(d, 2)` lives at allocator-tree depth 1 and produces outputs at zero-count depth 1 relative to `d`; an allocator additionally opened by `(d.0.s_X, 1)` for `X ∈ {C, L}` lives at allocator-tree depth 2 but still produces outputs at zero-count depth 1 relative to `d` (its outputs have one *additional component within T4b's element field `E`* — `#E = 2` instead of `#E = 1` — but the same zero count).

**Lemma — SharedDepthOneAllocator.** *Naming convention for unnumbered lemmas.* Throughout this note, named-but-unnumbered lemmas in the Setup section (`SharedDepthOneAllocator`, `FramePreservation`, etc.) are foundational tools that R-numbered claims invoke by name. They are LEMMA-typed in the Properties Introduced table without an R-number, with citation form "the *SharedDepthOneAllocator* lemma" (italicized name) at every consumption site; this is the uniform citation convention. R-numbered claims (R0, R0a, …) carry the load-bearing R-results of this note, while named lemmas carry Setup-level structural tools whose role is to be cited rather than independently consumed in proofs. The Properties Introduced table includes a Consumed-at column tracking downstream citations for each named lemma so the dependency direction is visible alongside the R-claim dependencies. *Allocator-naming convention.* Throughout this note, `A_x` denotes the allocator whose *first emission* is `x`; subscripts are first-emission-named uniformly, not parent-named. Under each document address `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, there exists exactly one allocator at allocator-tree depth 1 below `d` *whose outputs sit at zero-count depth 1 relative to `d`* — written `A_{d.0.1}` (its first emission being `d.0.1`) and enumerating the positions of the first component of T4b's element field (`E₁`) appended to `d.0.`; this is the allocator opened by the `(d, 2)` child-spawn — and this allocator is *shared across all subspaces*: its enumeration `d.0.1, d.0.2, d.0.3, …` indexes subspace identifiers, with position `s_C` landing in the content subspace and position `s_L` in the link subspace by L0 (SubspacePartition, ASN-0043). *Depth-notation caveat.* The other admissible child-spawn from `d`, namely `(d, 1)`, *also* opens an allocator at allocator-tree depth 1 (one child-spawn from `d`); but its outputs sit at zero-count depth 0 relative to `d` (the `(·, 1)` spawn introduces no new zero, per TA5(d), ASN-0034 — `zeros(inc(d, 1)) = zeros(d) + 0 = 2`), extending `d`'s rightmost T4 field (the document D field) rather than opening a new field. The uniqueness in the lemma's headline is therefore over allocators at allocator-tree depth 1 *and* zero-count depth 1; without the zero-count qualifier, there are two depth-1 allocators (one per `k' ∈ {1, 2}`), not one. The subspace-specific allocators at allocator-tree depth 2 (the content allocator `A_{d.0.s_C.1}` and the link allocator `A_{d.0.s_L.1}`, rooted respectively at `d.0.s_C.1` and `d.0.s_L.1` — the first emissions of the child-spawns `(d.0.s_C, 1)` and `(d.0.s_L, 1)` — and enumerating sibling streams by T10a.7) are mutually *independent*. Their outputs share `A_{d.0.1}`'s zero-count depth (the `(·, 1)` child-spawns preserve `zeros`, so outputs sit at zero-count depth 1 relative to `d` in both cases); the depth-2 allocators are distinguished from `A_{d.0.1}` by carrying one *additional* component within T4b's element field `E` (#E = 2 vs #E = 1), not by zero-count depth.

*Proof.* In three steps.

*(a) The only T10a-admissible child-spawns from `d` are `(d, 1)` and `(d, 2)`.* Document addresses have `zeros(d) = 2` by S7d (DocumentAllocationDiscipline, ASN-0036). T10a (AllocatorDiscipline, ASN-0034) licenses child-spawn via `inc(·, k')` with `k' > 0`, and TA5 (HierarchicalIncrement, ASN-0034), postcondition (d), gives the resulting child's zero count: `zeros(inc(d, k')) = zeros(d) + (k' - 1) = 2 + (k' - 1)` for `k' ≥ 1`. T4 (HierarchicalParsing, ASN-0034) caps every tumbler's zero count at 3, so admissible child-spawns from `d` require `2 + (k' - 1) ≤ 3`, i.e., `k' ∈ {1, 2}`. Both `(d, 1)` and `(d, 2)` are T10a-admissible at `d`; no further child-spawn pair from `d` exists in T10a's vocabulary.

*(b) Only `(d, 2)` introduces a new zero, and only `(d, 2)` opens an allocator at zero-count depth 1.* By TA5 (HierarchicalIncrement, ASN-0034), postcondition (d), `(d, 1)` yields a child with `zeros = 2 + (1 - 1) = 2` — no new zero is introduced, and the resulting tumbler `inc(d, 1)` stays at zero-count depth 0 relative to `d` (it extends `d`'s rightmost T4 field rather than opening a new one; for the document-level tumbler `d` with three fields N/U/D under T4b's NUDE projection, this is the D field). `(d, 2)` yields a child with `zeros = 2 + (2 - 1) = 3` — exactly one new zero is introduced, placing the spawn one T4 field separator deeper than `d`, i.e., at zero-count depth 1. The spawn-parameter-to-depth correspondence is in bijection on `k' ∈ {1, 2}`: `k' = 1` keeps the depth, `k' = 2` deepens by one level; no other `k'` is available at `d`. Therefore `(d, 2)` is the *unique* child-spawn pair from `d` whose admissibility opens an allocator at zero-count depth 1.

*(c) T10a's at-most-once child-spawn discipline on `(d, 2)` gives uniqueness.* By T10a (AllocatorDiscipline, ASN-0034) — whose axiom binds each parent to a single `inc(·, k')` with `k' > 0` per child-spawn — each child-spawn pair fires at most once across the entire reachability chain. Applied to `(d, 2)`, this entails that the allocator at allocator-tree depth 1 *and* zero-count depth 1 opened by `(d, 2)` exists at most once under `d`. Combined with (b), there is exactly one such allocator under `d`; we name it `A_{d.0.1}`. A per-subspace splitting of `A_{d.0.1}` (e.g., separate per-subspace depth-1 allocators rooted at distinct depth-1 positions opening separate zero-count-depth-1 allocators) would require a second child-spawn pair from `d` opening an additional allocator at zero-count depth 1, which by (b) does not exist in the spawn family (only `(d, 2)` introduces a new zero); equivalently, re-firing `(d, 2)` would contradict T10a's at-most-once axiom. The `(d, 1)` spawn, although it also opens a depth-1 allocator in the allocator-tree sense, opens it at zero-count depth 0 (extending the D field) rather than at zero-count depth 1, so it does not contribute another candidate for `A_{d.0.1}`'s slot.

*Subspace-routing and allocator-tree-depth-2 independence.* `A_{d.0.1}`'s enumeration produces successive first-post-`d` `E₁` values `1, 2, 3, …`, which by L0 (SubspacePartition, ASN-0043) are the subspace identifiers `s_1, s_2, s_3, …` (with `s_C` and `s_L` among them by the substrate's standard convention); subspace-internal allocation therefore lives one level deeper in the allocator hierarchy, at the allocator-tree-depth-2 child-spawns `(d.0.s_C, 1)`, `(d.0.s_L, 1)`, etc. (Their outputs remain at zero-count depth 1 relative to `d` — the `(·, 1)` child-spawns preserve `zeros` — and so the "deeper" here is allocator-tree depth, not zero-count depth.) Each allocator-tree-depth-2 spawn is admitted by T2 and at-most-once by T10a but the spawn pairs are *mutually distinct* (distinct parent tumblers `d.0.s_C ≠ d.0.s_L` by L0 + the subspace-distinctness axiom `s_C ≠ s_L`, named above), so T10a's at-most-once on each pair imposes no joint constraint — the allocator-tree-depth-2 allocators evolve independently. ∎

*Status.* The shared-depth-1 / independent-depth-2 structure is therefore a *derived lemma* from L0 + T10a + TA5 (HierarchicalIncrement) postcondition (d) + T4 + S7d, not a separate model commitment. The conclusion is unconditional given the foundation ASNs.

This corollary is consumed at R0 Step 2 Case A's L1c chain construction (which walks the shared `A_{d.0.1}` from position 1 through the sibling sweep to position `s_L`, then opens the depth-2 link allocator at `(d.0.s_L, 1)`) and at the worked sketch's concrete L1c chain for `a₁` (which exhibits the same structure with `s_C = 1`, `s_L = 2`). The udanax-green implementation realizes the corollary directly: `findisatoinsertmolecule` (granf2.c:158–181) maintains a single granfilade tree rooted at each document address and dispatches subspace-internal allocation by atomtype parameter — bytes and links share the depth-1 enumeration and differ only in which depth-2 sub-allocator (content versus link) is engaged.

*Reconciliation with Nelson's design.* The link-model design intent occasionally referenced in the literature — that bytes are ordered by V-stream position while links are ordered by permanent arrival — is *not* in tension with the shared-depth-1 corollary: V-stream ordering and arrival ordering are properties of the *depth-2-and-deeper* enumerations within their respective subspaces (`A_{d.0.s_C.1}` and `A_{d.0.s_L.1}`), which the corollary keeps independent. The shared `A_{d.0.1}` enumerates only subspace identifiers, not the within-subspace ordering. The implementation evidence (Gregory's `findisatoinsertmolecule`) and the design intent (Nelson's per-subspace ordering principles) agree once the level distinction is drawn.

**State transition relation.** We write `Σ → Σ'` for the substrate's *dom-extending* one-step transition relation — the transitions whose effect on `(Σ.C, Σ.M, Σ.L)` is to add a fresh key to exactly one of the three stores. The primitive dom-extending transitions are exactly the substrate-level emissions inherited from the underlying ASNs and lifted here: (i) document allocation (ASN-0036, S7a, S7d) extends `dom(Σ.M)` with a new document address; (ii) content emission (ASN-0036, S0–S3) extends `dom(Σ.C)`; (iii) `Emit_K` as defined later in this note (which composes the underlying ASN-0043 link-store extension) extends `dom(Σ.L)` by one address. Every dom-extending transition in `→` is one of (i)–(iii); the substrate exposes no removal, replacement, or in-place mutation transition that touches `(dom(Σ.C), dom(Σ.M), dom(Σ.L))` (consistent with S0, L12, and T8 across the underlying ASNs). The operations defined later in this note (Observe, Nullify) either compose `Emit_K` (Nullify is `Emit_R` with a designated argument shape, per R7) or leave Σ unchanged (Observe). R0 through R7 are claims about `→` so defined; in particular, R0's existential `(E Σ' extending Σ, …)` is discharged by exhibiting an `Emit_K` transition.

*Scoping note — arrangement modifications and the broader transition relation `↦`.* ASN-0036 admits a further class of state-changing transitions: *arrangement modifications* that extend `dom(Σ.M(d))` for an existing `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` (e.g., INSERT and related editing operations on a document's arrangement). These transitions affect `Σ.M`'s value but not `dom(Σ.M)` itself, and so are not dom-extending in the sense of `→` as defined here. We name the *broader transition relation* — the union of dom-extending `→` with these arrangement-modifying transitions — `↦`, with arrangement-modifying steps written explicitly as `Σ ↦ Σ'` when their distinction from `→` matters; every `→`-step is an `↦`-step but not conversely. R0–R7 are stated against `→` (dom-extending only); the lift to `↦` is collected in R6c-Corollary, which extends R6c's persistence-of-nullification conclusion from `⊑` (reflexive-transitive closure of `→`, defined below) to `⊑̂` (reflexive-transitive closure of `↦`, defined alongside `⊑`). The arrangement-modifying steps live in a parallel transition vocabulary whose L-invariant preservation is handled in ASN-0036 (and the editing-operation ASNs that extend it). *Arrangement-modification frame (definitional on `(Σ.C, dom(Σ.M))`; `Σ.L`-identity from L12 / L12a applied to ASN-0036's class).* The substrate-level frame on every arrangement-modifying transition is `(Σ'.C, dom(Σ'.M), Σ'.L) = (Σ.C, dom(Σ.M), Σ.L)` — only `Σ.M`'s pointwise values change (specifically, `Σ.M(d)` for the document `d` being edited); the content store, the document-allocation domain, and the entire link store are held identical as ordered components. The frame's `(Σ.C, dom(Σ.M))` components are *definitional* in the same sense as the class (i)/(ii)/(iii) frames above: ASN-0036's class definition specifies which substrate components an arrangement-modifying transition may touch — `Σ'.C = Σ.C` (no content emission is part of the transition class) and `dom(Σ'.M) = dom(Σ.M)` (the transition operates on an *existing* `d`, not as an allocation event) — and these are inherited from ASN-0036's class without re-derivation. The frame's `Σ.L` component, by contrast, is *not* directly entailed by ASN-0036's class definition: ASN-0036's state vocabulary is `(Σ.C, Σ.M)`, and `Σ.L` is introduced only in ASN-0043, so the `Σ'.L = Σ.L` clause is not part of ASN-0036's own definitional vocabulary. Instead the `Σ.L`-identity *follows from* L12 (LinkImmutability, ASN-0043) and L12a (LinkStoreMonotonicity, ASN-0043) applied to ASN-0036's transition class: L12a constrains `dom(Σ.L)` to be non-decreasing across any state transition, while L12 forces existing link-store entries to persist with unchanged values; since arrangement modifications by ASN-0036's class definition emit no link (no class-(iii) sub-step is part of the transition), `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L)` (no addition under L12a) and the pointwise values are preserved (under L12), yielding `Σ'.L = Σ.L` as a function. S9 (TwoStreamSeparation, ASN-0036) is the parallel statement at ASN-0036's signature level — recording that arrangement modifications do not affect the content store — and is consistent with the same frame from ASN-0036's side; every reference in this note to "arrangement modifications leave `Σ.L` invariant" cites L12 / L12a applied to ASN-0036's class definition, and every reference to "leave `Σ.C` invariant" cites the ASN-0036 class definition itself (or equivalently S9). The link-store-only claims R0–R7 are stated and proved against `→` as defined above; they are unaffected by arrangement modifications, because such transitions leave `Σ.L` and `dom(Σ.M)` untouched (by the citations just stated) and therefore preserve every L-invariant trivially.

**Frame conditions on the primitive transitions.** The abstract substrate model commits, *definitionally*, to the following frame conditions on each primitive-transition class — these are part of what each class *is* at the `(Σ.C, Σ.M, Σ.L)` level of abstraction, not consequences derivable from the underlying ASNs' invariants:

- (i) *Document allocation:* `dom(Σ'.M) = dom(Σ.M) ∪ {d}` for a fresh document address `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)`; `Σ'.C = Σ.C` and `Σ'.L = Σ.L`.
- (ii) *Content emission:* `dom(Σ'.C) = dom(Σ.C) ∪ {c}` for a fresh content address `c ∉ dom(Σ.C)`; `Σ'.M = Σ.M` and `Σ'.L = Σ.L`.
- (iii) *Link emission (`Emit_K`):* `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L) ∪ {a}` for a fresh link address `a ∉ dom(Σ.L)` with `Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K)` *and* `Σ'.L(a') = Σ.L(a')` for every `a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` (value-preservation at existing keys is part of the Frame definition, not a derived consequence) — equivalently stated as a functional update `Σ'.L = Σ.L ⊕ {a ↦ (F, G, K)}`; `Σ'.C = Σ.C` and `Σ'.M = Σ.M`.

These commitments are at the substrate-model interface and constrain only the visible values of `Σ.C, Σ.M, Σ.L` after the transition. Concrete implementations may maintain auxiliary backing structures (separate index trees, shared B-tree pages, POOM entries) and may write to several such structures per logical transition without violating any frame condition above; the implementation's `docreatelink` in udanax-green, for instance, writes to the granfilade orgl tree, the home document's POOM, and the spanfilade for each link emission, but the abstract values `Σ.C, Σ.M, Σ.L` move only as (iii) prescribes. In particular, R0's role below is to discharge the existential "there exists a `→` step extending `dom(Σ.L)` by a fresh address satisfying L0/L1/L1a/L1b/L1c/L3" — *not* to derive the frame conditions on `Σ.C` and `Σ.M`, which are part of (iii)'s definition. R0 Step 4 invokes the frame definitionally when setting `Σ'.C := Σ.C` and `Σ'.M := Σ.M`.

*Sole sourcing.* Throughout this note, when a transition's effect on a held-identical substrate component is invoked, the source depends on whether the held-identical component sits in the defining ASN's state vocabulary. *For class (i)/(ii)/(iii) `→`-transitions*, all three held-identical components are in this note's own substrate-model vocabulary `(Σ.C, Σ.M, Σ.L)`, and the Frame conditions are definitional sources directly; L12 (LinkImmutability), L12a (LinkStoreMonotonicity), and S9 (TwoStreamSeparation) are *consistency consequences* of those Frame definitions, not independent inputs. *For arrangement-modifying `↦ \ →`-transitions*, the held-identical `Σ.C` and `dom(Σ.M)` components are in ASN-0036's class-definition vocabulary and are definitional from ASN-0036 directly; the held-identical `Σ.L` component, however, is *not* in ASN-0036's state vocabulary (ASN-0036's signature is `(Σ.C, Σ.M)`) and the `Σ_arr.L = Σ.L` clause therefore follows from L12 (LinkImmutability) + L12a (LinkStoreMonotonicity) applied to ASN-0036's arrangement-modifying transition class (which by definition emits no link), as elaborated in the *Arrangement-modification frame* paragraph above. R7a's proof discharges from the class-(iii) Frame alone (Step 3, below). Where this note cites L12 / L12a / S9 in any other context (i.e., other than the arrangement-modifying `Σ.L`-identity), the citation is a label for a consistency property of the substrate, not a load-bearing derivation step.

*Status — model definition, not derivation.* The three classes (i), (ii), (iii) jointly *define* the dom-extending transition vocabulary of ASN-0086's substrate model. They are not derived from L12, L12a, S0, S9, T8, or any other invariant in the foundation ASNs (which permit broader transition vocabularies — ASN-0034's underlying `→_{0034}` admits allocator-state-only transitions that have no class-(i)/(ii)/(iii) projection, and the udanax-green substrate's `findisatoinsertmolecule` writes to multiple backing structures per logical class-(iii) deposit). ASN-0086 selects (i)/(ii)/(iii) as the abstraction at which the relational layer factors cleanly into `Emit_K`, `Observe_K`, and `Nullify`; alternative coarsenings remain admissible (Appendix A.1 traces the precise projection from `→_{0034}` to `→`, with finer or coarser substrate-level abstractions also conformant under stated conditions). ASN-0086's commitment to (i)/(ii)/(iii) is parallel in standing to its other model-level commitments — the Setup hypothesis (class-(ii) emission policy, *Maintenance protocol* above) and the sibling-frontier discipline (class-(iii) emission policy, named below) — each of which constrains a primitive-transition class beyond what the underlying ASNs entail.

**Lemma — FramePreservation.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* Let `↝ ∈ {→, ↦}` be either of this note's transition relations and `Σ ↝ Σ'` a transition. For any substrate-level predicate `P` whose free variables draw only from substrate components `X` for which the transition's frame asserts `Σ'.X = Σ.X` (equal as functions, hence identical in domain, range, and pointwise value), the propositions `P(Σ)` and `P(Σ')` are the same proposition:

`(A Σ ↝ Σ', P : free(P) ⊆ X ∧ Σ'.X = Σ.X :: P(Σ) ⟺ P(Σ'))`

*Justification.* By definitional identity of inputs: substituting `Σ'.X` for `Σ.X` in `P` leaves the proposition unchanged, since the two are equal as functions / values. Preservation holds by input-substitution, not by re-verification of `P`'s content.

*Specializations used below.* Five frame-cases instantiate the lemma:
- *(a) Class-(iii) frame on `→`.* Class-(iii) steps hold `Σ'.C = Σ.C` and `Σ'.M = Σ.M`. Every predicate whose free variables draw only from `(Σ.C, Σ.M)` is preserved.
- *(b) Class-(i) frame on `→`.* Class-(i) steps hold `Σ'.C = Σ.C` and `Σ'.L = Σ.L`. Every predicate whose free variables draw only from `(Σ.C, Σ.L)` is preserved.
- *(c) Class-(ii) frame on `→`.* Class-(ii) steps hold `Σ'.M = Σ.M` and `Σ'.L = Σ.L`. Every predicate whose free variables draw only from `(Σ.M, Σ.L)` is preserved.
- *(d) Arrangement-modification frame on `↦ \ →`.* Arrangement-modifying steps hold `Σ'.C = Σ.C`, `Σ'.L = Σ.L`, and `dom(Σ'.M) = dom(Σ.M)` (Scoping note above). Every predicate whose free variables draw only from `(Σ.C, Σ.L, dom(Σ.M))` is preserved.
- *(e) Scope-orthogonality to endset content under any frame.* The free variables admitted by this specialization are *exactly* `(Σ.C, Σ.M, dom(Σ.L))` — i.e., `P` may consult the content and arrangement components in full, and may consult the *domain* of the link store, but may *not* consult the endset values `Σ.L(a)` stored at those addresses. The tumbler algebra is pre-state structure (T1, T3 of ASN-0034) and contributes no state-dependence. The `→`-admissibility relation *is* state-dependent, but its state-dependence is exactly through `dom(Σ.L)` and `dom(Σ.M)`, not through `Σ.L`'s pointwise values: an address `a` is class-(iii) admissible at `Σ` iff `a ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, `home(a) ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, and a T10a-conforming L1c witness chain exists for `a` on `Σ` — and the L1c chain's existence depends on the allocator history reachable from `dom(Σ.M)`, not on what endsets any `a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` happens to store. Consequently `→`-admissibility is itself a predicate over `(dom(Σ.L), dom(Σ.M))` and falls within the admitted free-variable scope. A transition that *only* alters endset content (extending `dom(Σ.L)` with a witnessed-admissible new address whose admissibility R0 separately discharges, or — for class-(i)/(ii) frames — leaves `dom(Σ.L)` fixed) therefore leaves `P` unchanged: the components `P` consults all transform compatibly under input-substitution per the lemma.

**Substrate emission primitive (for `Emit_K`).** The substrate admits, as its primitive emission for the link store, *emit-at-any-L1c-conforming-fresh-address*: for any state `Σ` and any address `a` jointly satisfying (1) `a ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, (2) L0/L1/L1a/L1b at `a` (i.e., `a.E₁ = s_L`, `zeros(a) = 3`, `home(a) ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, `#E(a) ≥ 2`), and (3) the existence of a T10a-conforming producer chain witnessing `a`'s reachability per L1c on `Σ`, there exists a `→` step `Σ → Σ'` of class (iii) with `Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K)` for any `(F, G, K)` satisfying L3. The L1c chain is required to *exist as a conformance witness* on `Σ`; it is not required to be operationally re-traversed by the emission, and intermediate addresses along the chain are not required to be in `dom(Σ.L)` or `dom(Σ.C)`. *Allocator-state commitment (load-bearing for R0 Step 2 Case A).* The atomic class-(iii) step implicitly discharges T10a's child-spawn admissibility at each intermediate spawn pair on the L1c witness chain in one indivisible action; allocator state has no observable evolution apart from the emissions whose witness chains it supports. This is a load-bearing design commitment of the substrate model, on which R0 Step 2 Case A's sibling sweep through `A_{d.0.1}` from position 1 to position `s_L` depends — the sweep traverses intermediate positions as *witness*, not as substrate-level deposits. The commitment matches Nelson's ghost-element design (*Literary Machines* 4/23: "the address population of tumbler-space is also an abstraction, since things may be addressed even though nothing is there to represent them in storage") and is realized by udanax-green's `findisatoinsertmolecule` (granf2.c:158–181), which selects the next-available address by structural query of `dom(Σ.L)` alone. Without this commitment, every intermediate position in Case A's sweep would have to be paired with a class-(ii) `Σ.C` deposit (or a separate per-allocator activation step), changing R0's atomicity and the bijection of `→` with substrate-level emissions stated in A.1.

*Coarsening of ASN-0034's transition relation.* ASN-0086's `→` is a coarsening of ASN-0034's underlying transition relation under which maximal sequences of T1 (sibling-increment) and T2 (child-spawn) ASN-0034-transitions with no exposed effect on `(dom(Σ.C), dom(Σ.M), dom(Σ.L))` collapse to `→`-stutter, and each substrate-level dom-extension projects to one `→`-step. The L1c witness chain traversed by Step 2 is one such collapsed sequence. See Appendix A.1 for the precise projection, the admissibility of alternative (finer or coarser) abstractions, and the udanax-green substrate's bijection with this note's `→`-steps.

*Breadth of the primitive vs. the discipline R0a names.* The primitive is permissive — it admits, for example, `a' = a₁.1` for an existing `a₁ ∈ dom(Σ.L)` (set `a' = a₁.1` with `s_L`, `zeros = 3`, `home = home(a₁)`, `#E ≥ 2` satisfied, and the L1c chain extended from `a₁`'s chain by one child-spawn at `(a₁, 1)` — the only fresh child-spawn pair, with `k' = 1` so no zeros constraint). The primitive then permits the step `Σ → Σ'` with `Σ'.L(a') = (F, G, K)`, after which `a₁ ≼ a'` and `a' ≠ a₁`. R0a's antichain conclusion is therefore *not* a property of the substrate primitive in isolation — the primitive admits emissions that falsify it. R0a holds only under a stricter implementation discipline, which we name and characterize next, and which the substrate primitive's R0 witness already constructs (Step 2's Case A and Case B both choose sibling-frontier addresses, never prefix-extensions). The substrate primitive is what R0 *discharges* (existence of *some* fresh-address emission); the discipline is what R0a *additionally hypothesizes*.

**Definition — Sibling-frontier discipline (on class-(iii) `→`-transitions).** The *sibling-frontier discipline* on `→` is the implementation hypothesis that every class-(iii) transition (every Emit_K) deposits the fresh link address at an address constructed by R0 Step 2: either the first link sited under a document (Step 2 Case A: `a = d.0.s_L.1`) or the next sibling of an existing link in the document's link-allocator enumeration (Step 2 Case B: `a = incⁱ(b, 0)` for the least `i ≥ 1` with `incⁱ(b, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, where `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` has `home(b) = home(a)`). Equivalently: the discipline never invokes the substrate primitive at an address that is a strict prefix-extension of an existing link address; every emission lands at a sibling-frontier position within the relevant document's link-allocator enumeration. The discipline is a hypothesis on the implementation's choice of which substrate-primitive-permissible address to actually deposit at; it is not entailed by the primitive. R0 Step 2's construction witnesses that the discipline is *realizable* on every state with `dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅` — sibling-frontier addresses always exist — but realizability is not adoption. Concrete adoption requires a binding implementation rule. The udanax-green substrate adopts the discipline directly: the `LINKATOM` branch of `findisatoinsertmolecule` (granf2.c:170–175) deposits every link at `lowerbound + 1` (a sibling of the previous link, via `tumblerincrement(·, 0, 1, ·)`) or at `docaddr` extended by two positions (Case A), and never invokes any increment that would place a link as a child of another link.

*Citation form.* Downstream references invoke this definition as "the sibling-frontier discipline" (R0a's hypothesis, Nullify's single-tuple-scope argument, R7b's relational-layer commitment, Appendix B's failure modes) and as "discipline-conditional" in claim tags `[Setup-free, discipline-conditional]`. R0a additionally introduces the discipline-restricted reachability relation `→_D` (transitions whose class-(iii) steps respect this definition) as the quantifier range of its sibling-stream invariant; that relation refers back to this single definition.

**Definition — Unit-depth retraction discipline (on class-(iii) `→`-transitions emitting `R`-typed tuples).** The *unit-depth retraction discipline* is the implementation hypothesis that every retraction emission deposits an `R`-typed tuple whose to-endset is of the unit-depth form `{(b, δ(1, #b))}` for some target `b ∈ A_rel^Σ` — equivalently, that every `L_R^Σ` tuple was produced by a `Nullify(Σ, d_retr, b)` call (Definition of Nullify below). The discipline is *realizable* on every state — Nullify constructs the unit-depth tuple explicitly — but it is not entailed by the substrate emission primitive, which admits broader-coverage to-endsets. The udanax-green substrate scopes retractions through Nullify alone, so it adopts the discipline; callers that bypass Nullify and invoke `Emit_R` directly with a broader-coverage to-endset opt out of it. The discipline is orthogonal to the sibling-frontier discipline: the unit-depth retraction discipline scopes the *to-endset shape* of `R`-typed emissions, the sibling-frontier discipline scopes the *address* at which any class-(iii) emission deposits. Downstream references invoke this definition as "the unit-depth retraction discipline" and as "retraction-discipline-conditional" in claim tags; consumption sites are Emit_K's *A_K^{Σ'} membership* note (regime (i)), Consequence R6c(e), and Nullify's discipline assumption.

**Definition — Extension.** `Σ' extends Σ`, written `Σ ⊑ Σ'`, is the reflexive-transitive closure of `→`:

`Σ ⊑ Σ' ≡ Σ →* Σ'`

By the frame conditions of (i)–(iii) — each primitive transition extends exactly one of `Σ.C`, `Σ.M`, `Σ.L` at a fresh key and leaves the other two components unchanged — `Σ ⊑ Σ'` entails `dom(Σ.C) ⊆ dom(Σ'.C)`, `dom(Σ.M) ⊆ dom(Σ'.M)`, `dom(Σ.L) ⊆ dom(Σ'.L)`, with `Σ'.C|_{dom(Σ.C)} = Σ.C`, `Σ'.M|_{dom(Σ.M)} = Σ.M`, `Σ'.L|_{dom(Σ.L)} = Σ.L`. The phrase "Σ' extending Σ" used throughout this note (and lifted from ASN-0043 invariant restatements such as L9, L11b) is this relation.

**Definition — BroadExtension.** In parallel, `Σ ⊑̂ Σ'` is the reflexive-transitive closure of `↦` (the broader transition relation introduced in the Scoping note above):

`Σ ⊑̂ Σ' ≡ Σ ↦* Σ'`

Every `Σ ⊑ Σ'` entails `Σ ⊑̂ Σ'` (since `→ ⊆ ↦`); `⊑̂` additionally collects sequences that include arrangement-modifying steps, which `⊑` excludes. By the arrangement-modification frame (above) every arrangement-modifying step holds `Σ.C` and `Σ.L` identical and changes only `Σ.M`'s pointwise values (specifically `Σ.M(d)` for the edited document `d`); composed with class-(i)/(ii)/(iii) frames, `Σ ⊑̂ Σ'` entails `dom(Σ.C) ⊆ dom(Σ'.C)`, `dom(Σ.M) ⊆ dom(Σ'.M)`, `dom(Σ.L) ⊆ dom(Σ'.L)`, and `Σ'.L|_{dom(Σ.L)} = Σ.L` (link store extension only). R6c is stated against `⊑`; its corollary lifts the conclusion to `⊑̂`, drawing on the link-component identity that arrangement-modifying steps preserve.

**Definition — AddressUniverse.** The substrate's address universe at state Σ is

`A^Σ = dom(Σ.C) ∪ dom(Σ.L)`

By L14 (DualPrimitive, ASN-0043) and the setup hypothesis, `A^Σ` is the entirety of stored-entity addresses at Σ; no third category exists.

**Definition — Partition.** Define:

`A_doc^Σ = dom(Σ.C)` &nbsp; — content addresses
`A_rel^Σ = dom(Σ.L)` &nbsp; — relation-tuple addresses

We claim `A^Σ = A_doc^Σ ⊔ A_rel^Σ` (disjoint union). The disjointness is R4 below.

*Notation.* All three sets are state-dependent — they grow as the substrate evolves. Where the ambient state is unambiguous, we drop the superscript and write `A`, `A_doc`, `A_rel`.

**Definition — TypeCatalog.** The set of *admissible types* is

`T_admissible = {K ∈ Endset : K ≠ ∅}`

— non-empty endsets, eligible to serve as a link's type endset by L3 (NEndsetStructure, ASN-0043). For each state Σ, the *type catalog at Σ* is the subset actually in use:

`T_cat^Σ = {Θ ∈ T_admissible : (E a ∈ dom(Σ.L) :: |Σ.L(a)| = 3 ∧ Σ.L(a).e₃ = Θ)}`

By L4 (EndsetGenerality, ASN-0043) and L9 (TypeGhostPermission, ASN-0043), `T_admissible` is unconstrained by content existence: type endsets may reference any tumbler addresses, including ghosts. We require only that type-equality is decidable by endset comparison — which it is, by L8 (TypeByAddress).

Type indices in what follows range over `T_admissible`, not `T_cat^Σ`. `T_cat^Σ` is descriptive — the snapshot of which type-endset values literally appear at the type slot in state Σ — but is not constitutive: `L_K^Σ` (below) is well-defined for any `K ∈ T_admissible` and is simply empty when no `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` has a stored type-slot endset coverage-equivalent to `K`. This avoids the bootstrap circularity that would arise if `K ∈ T_cat^Σ` were required as a precondition for introducing a genuinely new type via emission.

*Asymmetry between `T_cat^Σ` and `L_K^Σ`.* `T_cat^Σ` is defined by *literal endset equality* at the type slot (`Σ.L(a).e₃ = Θ`), while `L_K^Σ` (below) is defined by *coverage-equivalence* (`coverage(Σ.L(a).e₃) = coverage(K)`). Two coverage-equivalent but literally-distinct endsets `Θ₁ ≠ Θ₂` with `coverage(Θ₁) = coverage(Θ₂)` would appear as separate elements of `T_cat^Σ` yet collapse into a single coverage-class slice `L_{[Θ₁]}^Σ = L_{[Θ₂]}^Σ`. The asymmetry is deliberate: `T_cat^Σ` records the *literal type-slot values stored at Σ*, while `L_K^Σ` is the *coverage-class slice* over which active-subset and retraction machinery operate. Statements that need the coverage-class semantics use `L_K^Σ`-membership (e.g., `L_R^Σ ≠ ∅`) rather than `T_cat^Σ`-membership; statements about literally-stored type-slot values (e.g., "the system has emitted a tuple whose type-slot endset is literally `R`") use `T_cat^Σ`.

For the rest of this development we restrict attention to standard-triple links — those with `|Σ.L(a)| = 3`. Higher-arity links (L3, NEndsetStructure, ASN-0043) exist in `dom(Σ.L)` but are not members of any `L_K`; they admit an analogous construction with additional slot positions, which we do not pursue here.


## The Typed Relation

**Definition — TypeEquivalence.** Two admissible types are *type-equivalent* iff they cover the same address set:

`K ~ K' ≡ coverage(K) = coverage(K')`

This is L8's (TypeByAddress, ASN-0043) notion of `same_type`, lifted from links to type endsets themselves. The quotient `T_admissible / ~` is the set of *coverage classes*; the equivalence class of `K` is written `[K]`.

**Definition — TypedRelation.** For each `K ∈ T_admissible` and state Σ, the *typed relation of type K at Σ* is

`L_K^Σ = {(a, F, G) : a ∈ dom(Σ.L) ∧ |Σ.L(a)| = 3 ∧ Σ.L(a).e₁ = F ∧ Σ.L(a).e₂ = G ∧ coverage(Σ.L(a).e₃) = coverage(K)}`

Each member is a triple of (tuple-address, from-endset, to-endset). The pair `(F, G)` is the *relational content* of the tuple; `a` is the *tuple address*. Membership at the type slot is by coverage-equivalence, not by literal endset value: a tuple stored with third endset `K'` belongs to `L_K^Σ` whenever `K' ~ K`, so `L_K^Σ = L_{K'}^Σ` whenever `K ~ K'`. The substrate's standard-triple link store at state Σ is therefore the disjoint union over coverage classes:

`L^Σ = ⨆_{[K] ∈ T_admissible / ~} L_K^Σ`

We will show (R1) that this disjoint union is well-defined: each tuple address belongs to exactly one coverage-class slice. Note that `L^Σ` collects only the arity-3 links; higher-arity links in `dom(Σ.L)` are outside its scope, as noted above. Where ambient state is clear we drop the superscript and write `L_K`, `L`.

*Rationale for coverage-equivalence.* L8 (TypeByAddress, ASN-0043) defines link type-equality through coverage of the third endset, not through endset-value identity. Taking `L_K^Σ` to use literal endset equality at the type slot would make `L_K` a strict refinement of L8's equivalence: two tuples whose type endsets cover the same address set via differently structured spans would lie in distinct `L_K`'s here yet be "same type" under L8 — and the active-subset machinery would silently miss retractions whose type endset was coverage-equivalent to `R` but not literally equal. The coverage-equivalence definition aligns `L_K` with L8 and renders all coverage-equivalent type endsets interchangeable for retraction-relation membership and active-subset computation. (This matches the convention of every endset-comparison primitive in the substrate stack, which projects through `coverage(·)` rather than comparing raw endset values.)

**Definition — TupleAddress.** Define `addr : L^Σ → A_rel^Σ` by `addr(a, F, G) = a`.

*Remark — relation to ℘(A) × ℘(A).* A generic mathematical typed relation is a subset of `℘(A) × ℘(A)` — a set of address-pair-pairs distinguished only by content. Our typed relation is richer: each tuple carries an address that participates in the relation's identity. The projection `(a, F, G) ↦ (coverage(F), coverage(G))` recovers the address-pair view, but it loses information that the substrate retains (R0, R1).


## Tuple Identity (R0, R1, R2)

A generic mathematical relation distinguishes its members only by content: two tuples with identical (F, G) are the same tuple. The substrate's relations do not work that way. Each tuple emission allocates a fresh address (R0), the address-to-pair binding is a function (R1), and the binding is permanent (R2).

**R0 — TupleAddressFreshness.** *[setup: req, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* For any state Σ with `dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅` and any `(F, G, K) ∈ Endset × Endset × T_admissible`, there exists a state Σ' with Σ → Σ' that emits a tuple with content (F, G) of type K at a fresh address:

`(A Σ : dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅ :: (A F, G ∈ Endset, K ∈ T_admissible :: (E Σ' extending Σ, a : a ∉ dom(Σ.L) :: a ∈ dom(Σ'.L) ∧ Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K))))`

*Proof.* We construct the witness in four steps. Let `LS(d) = {a ∈ T : d ≼ a ∧ T4-valid(a) ∧ zeros(a) = 3 ∧ a.E₁ = s_L ∧ #E(a) ≥ 2}` denote the well-formed link-subspace addresses sited under document `d` (combining the T4 element-level constraint, the L0 first-element-field marker `s_L`, and the L1b depth requirement).

(Step 1 — locate a home document.) By precondition, `dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅`; pick any `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`. By L1a (LinkScopedAllocation, ASN-0043), every link address `a'' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` satisfies `home(a'') = N(a'').0.U(a'').0.D(a'') ∈ dom(Σ.M)` — its document-level prefix is an allocated document. We will exhibit a fresh link address with `home = d`.

(Step 2 — constructively exhibit a reachable fresh address sited under `d`, with its T10a-conforming chain.) We case-split on whether `d` already has any link allocations under `Σ`. Both cases produce a concrete `a ∈ LS(d) \ dom(Σ.L)` together with its T10a-conforming chain from `d` (seed `s = d` is T4-valid by S7d, ASN-0036, which fixes `zeros(d) = 2` for every document tumbler). The construction mirrors the L1c-conformance argument used by L9 (TypeGhostPermission, ASN-0043) in its own emission proof, narrowed here to the link store and ground sequentially in the allocator's actual history at `Σ`.

*Case A — `d` has no prior link allocations under `Σ`* (`{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d} = ∅`). Set `a = d.0.s_L.1`. The L1c chain from `d` to `a` describes a walk through `A_{d.0.1}`, the unique allocator-tree-depth-1 allocator under `d` (single shared across all subspaces under `d`, per the *SharedDepthOneAllocator* lemma in Setup): (i) `t₁ = inc(d, 2)` → `d.0.1` — at zero-count depth 1, at position 1 of T4b's element-field component `E₁` (an enumeration index within `A_{d.0.1}`, not yet committed to any subspace label); the underlying spawn pair is `(d, 2)`, admissible because `zeros(d) = 2 ≤ 2`. If `A_{d.0.1}` has not yet emitted any address under `Σ` (e.g., `d` has no content allocations either), this is the spawn event that creates `A_{d.0.1}`; if `A_{d.0.1}` already exists under `Σ` from prior content emissions, the chain navigates to its already-emitted base. L1c asserts the existence of a conforming chain to `a`, not the re-issuance of every spawn that chain traverses — and likewise here, the chain witnesses `a` as a legal allocator output, not as a re-emission. (ii) Sibling sweep `inc(·, 0)` within `A_{d.0.1}`, advancing from `A_{d.0.1}`'s base `d.0.1` (its first emission — last-component value 1, equivalently T10a.7 enumeration index 0, at zero-count depth 1) to `d.0.s_L` (last-component value `s_L`, equivalently T10a.7 enumeration index `s_L − 1`, at the same depth), applied `s_L − 1` times — each step is a `k = 0` sibling advance within `A_{d.0.1}`, unconditionally T4-preserving (TA5a, ASN-0034) and consistent with T10a's at-most-once discipline because sibling enumeration is the allocator's own monotone advance, not a fresh spawn. *Terminology note on "enumeration index" vs. "last-component value".* T10a.7 (EnumerationInjectivity, ASN-0034) names an allocator's domain `{tₙ : n ≥ 0}` indexed from zero, so the base address `t₀` carries enumeration index 0. The numerals `1, 2, …, s_L` appearing in the sweep above are *last-component values* (the rightmost-component digit of each sibling output) — they index from one, with `1` denoting `A_{d.0.1}`'s base. Throughout this note, "enumeration index" is reserved for T10a.7's index-from-zero; sibling-sweep traversals and concrete tumbler digit values are stated as "last-component value" to avoid the off-by-one ambiguity. Both phrasings refer to the same sibling stream — the equivalence `last-component value N ⟺ T10a.7 enumeration index N − 1` is a direct consequence of `A_{d.0.1}`'s base being `d.0.1` (last-component value 1, T10a.7 index 0). *T4-validity chain induction.* *Base:* the seed `d.0.1 = inc(d, 2)` is T4-valid — `d` is T4-valid (S7d, ASN-0036) with `zeros(d) = 2`, and the spawn `(d, 2)` is T4-admissible by step (a) of the *SharedDepthOneAllocator* lemma, with `zeros(d.0.1) = 2 + (2 - 1) = 3` (TA5(d), ASN-0034) within the T4 zero-count cap. *Step:* assume `d.0.k` is T4-valid for some `k ∈ {1, …, s_L − 1}`; the sibling step `d.0.(k+1) = inc(d.0.k, 0)` preserves T4-validity unconditionally by TA5a (ASN-0034), so `d.0.(k+1)` is T4-valid. By induction over `k = 1, 2, …, s_L − 1`, every intermediate `d.0.k` in the sweep is T4-valid, and in particular the sweep's endpoint `d.0.s_L` is T4-valid — discharging the T4-validity hypothesis of step (iii)'s child-spawn `(d.0.s_L, 1)` below. *Witness, not material traversal.* The intermediate positions `d.0.1, d.0.2, …, d.0.s_L − 1` need not have been emitted into `dom(Σ.C)` or `dom(Σ.L)` for this sweep to be admissible — the chain *witnesses* `a` as a legal allocator output, not a sequence of substrate-level deposits. The substantive commitment licensing this reading is the sparse-allocator interpretation of the substrate emission primitive (above, *Allocator-state commitment*), under which the atomic class-(iii) step implicitly discharges T10a's child-spawn admissibility at each intermediate spawn pair on the L1c witness chain in one indivisible action, with no per-position deposit required. Without that interpretation, the sweep would have to be paired with deposits at every intermediate position; with it, the sweep is a chain-of-witness, not a chain-of-emissions. The udanax-green `findisatoinsertmolecule` (granf2.c:158–181) realizes this directly — it computes the next-available address by structural query of `dom(Σ.L)` and the granfilade tree, depositing only at the final position. The `s_L`-th element of `A_{d.0.1}`'s enumeration lands in the link subspace by L0 (SubspacePartition, ASN-0043), which identifies link-resident addresses by their `E₁` component being `s_L`; nothing in the construction privileges `A_{d.0.1}`'s base as "the content position" — `A_{d.0.1}` enumerates positions whose subspace identity is fixed by L0 and the substrate's `(s_C, s_L)` convention, not by ordinal precedence within `A_{d.0.1}`. *Remark on the precondition.* The hypothesis `dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅` is necessary: by L1a (LinkScopedAllocation), every link address lives under some allocated document, so before any document exists, no link can be allocated. **R0a — FlatLinkDomain (conditional on the sibling-frontier discipline).** *[setup: free, discipline: req, stipulation: N/A]* *Discipline-restricted reachability relation.* Write `Σ_0 →_D* Σ` for "`Σ` is reachable from `Σ_0` via finitely many `→`-transitions each of whose class-(iii) steps respects the sibling-frontier emission discipline (named above at *Implementation discipline — sibling-frontier link emission*)" — i.e., `→_D*` is the reflexive-transitive closure of `→` restricted at class (iii) to disciplined emissions, with class-(i) and class-(ii) steps unconstrained. Under the discipline (every class-(iii) `→`-transition along the reachability chain is `→_D`-admissible), `dom(Σ.L)` is a tumbler-prefix antichain at every reachable state:

`(A Σ : (E Σ_0 : dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅ ∧ Σ_0 →_D* Σ) :: (A a, a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) :: a ≼ a' ⟹ a = a'))`

The discipline-restricted quantifier range — `Σ` is `→_D*`-reachable from a Σ_0 with empty link store — is *not* a stylistic abbreviation but the substantive scope of the claim: the universal does *not* range over arbitrary `→`-reachable states. By the canonical statement at *Breadth of the primitive vs. the discipline R0a names* above, the substrate primitive in isolation admits class-(iii) emissions falsifying the antichain, so R0a fails as a substrate-level theorem on `→`-reachability without the `→_D` restriction; it holds for any implementation that adopts the discipline and *only* for such implementations (the udanax-green substrate is one example — cited in the Remark below).

*Proof.* Direct induction on the antichain conclusion does not go through: the inductive step that adds a fresh link sharing a home document with an existing link must invoke T10a.2 (NonNestingSiblingPrefixes, ASN-0034) to obtain prefix-incomparability between the two, and T10a.2's hypothesis requires both addresses to lie in the *same* sibling stream — information strictly stronger than antichain (antichain alone does not pin either address to `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` for any `j`). We therefore prove the antichain conclusion in three stages, separating the unconditional cross-home case from the discipline-conditional same-home case. Stage 1 (the *cross-home sub-argument*) discharges the cross-home case *unconditionally* (zero-count additivity + L1, no discipline appeal). Stage 2 strengthens the induction invariant to the *sibling-stream invariant* and proves it *under the discipline*; this is where the discipline-conditionality is concentrated. Stage 3 composes Stages 1 and 2 into the antichain corollary.

*Stage 1 — Cross-home sub-argument (no cross-home prefix-comparability).* *[setup: free, discipline: free — this stage holds unconditionally for any pair `a, a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with distinct homes, regardless of whether the chain reaching Σ respects the sibling-frontier discipline, stipulation: N/A]* Let `a, a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `d = home(a) ≠ home(a') = d'`. Suppose, toward contradiction, that `a ≼ a'`. Then `a' = a · w` for some suffix `w` (the digits appended to `a` to obtain `a'`). Zero counts add along concatenation: `zeros(a') = zeros(a) + zeros(w)`. By L1 (LinkElementLevel, ASN-0043), `zeros(a) = zeros(a') = 3`, so `zeros(w) = 0` — `w` contains no zero positions. By L1a (LinkScopedAllocation, ASN-0043), `home(·) = N(·).0.U(·).0.D(·)` — the prefix of the link extending through the document-field `D(·)` and ending *just before* the third zero. Since `a ≼ a'`, the positions `1..#a` of `a'` agree pointwise with all of `a`; the remaining positions `#a + 1 .. #a'` of `a'` are `w`, which contains no zeros. Therefore every zero of `a'` sits at a position `≤ #a`, and the three zeros of `a'` are *exactly* the three zeros of `a`, at the same positions. In particular, `a'`'s third zero sits at the position of `a`'s third zero — call this position `p₃`, with `p₃ ≤ #a`. The `home` prefix has length `p₃ − 1` (the positions up to and including `D(·)`, which immediately precedes the third zero). Since `p₃ − 1 < p₃ ≤ #a`, the prefix of `a'` of length `p₃ − 1` agrees pointwise with the prefix of `a` of length `p₃ − 1` (by `a ≼ a'` applied at positions `1..#a`); equivalently, `N(a') = N(a)`, `U(a') = U(a)`, and `D(a') = D(a)` — the three NUDE field-components delimited by `a'`'s first three zeros coincide with those of `a` position-by-position. Therefore `home(a') = N(a').0.U(a').0.D(a') = N(a).0.U(a).0.D(a) = home(a) = d`, contradicting `d' ≠ d`. The reverse direction `a' ⊀ a` (under `d ≠ d'`) is covered by the same argument with roles swapped: instantiating the derivation at `(a', a)` yields the symmetric contradiction. So under `d ≠ d'`, neither `a ≼ a'` nor `a' ≼ a` holds.

*Stage 2 — Sibling-stream invariant induction.* *[setup: free, discipline: req — the inductive step requires the class-(iii) emitted address to come from R0 Step 2's construction (Case A or Case B), which the discipline guarantees, stipulation: N/A]*

*Sibling-stream invariant statement.* For every state Σ reachable from Σ_0 with `dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅` via finitely many discipline-respecting `→`-transitions (`Σ_0 →_D* Σ`),

`(A Σ : (E Σ_0 : dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅ ∧ Σ_0 →_D* Σ) :: (A a ∈ dom(Σ.L) :: (E j ≥ 0 :: a = incʲ(home(a).0.s_L.1, 0))))`

i.e., every link address lies in its home document's link-subspace sibling stream, anchored at the Case A base `home(a).0.s_L.1`.

*Induction.* The sibling-stream invariant is proved by induction on `→_D`-chain length. *Base* (`Σ_0`): `dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅` makes the invariant vacuously true.

*Step*: assume the sibling-stream invariant at Σ; consider Σ → Σ'. By Frame conditions (above), only class (iii) transitions extend `dom(Σ.L)`; classes (i) and (ii) leave `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L)`, preserving the invariant by induction. For class (iii), the discipline hypothesis on this step constrains the fresh address `a` to be constructed by R0 Step 2 (Case A or Case B), not just any substrate-primitive-permissible address. So `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L) ∪ {a}`. By IH, every `a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` already satisfies the invariant at Σ' (the invariant is purely a relation between `a'` and `home(a') ∈ dom(Σ.M) ⊆ dom(Σ'.M)`, both held by the frame); it suffices to exhibit a witnessing `j` for the fresh `a` itself. Let `d = home(a) ∈ dom(Σ.M)` (Step 1).

*Sub-case A — `a` constructed via Step 2 Case A* (`a = d.0.s_L.1`). Set `j = 0`; then `a = inc⁰(d.0.s_L.1, 0) = d.0.s_L.1`, witnessing the invariant for `a` at Σ' directly.

*Sub-case B — `a` constructed via Step 2 Case B* (`a = incⁱ(b, 0)` for the least `i ≥ 1` with `incⁱ(b, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, where `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` has `home(b) = d`). By IH applied to `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` (with `home(b) = d`), there exists `k ≥ 0` with `b = incᵏ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)`. Composing, `a = incⁱ(b, 0) = incⁱ(incᵏ(d.0.s_L.1, 0), 0) = incⁱ⁺ᵏ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)`. The composition is well-defined and the equality of iterated forms holds: by TA5a (IncrementPreservesT4, ASN-0034), `inc(·, 0)` is unconditionally T4-preserving for `k = 0`, so the seed `d.0.s_L.1` (T4-valid by R0 Step 2 Case A's discharge) admits an unbroken sequence of k=0 increments, with every intermediate `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` for `j ∈ {0, …, i+k}` remaining T4-valid and signature-valid (TA5-SigValid, ASN-0034) as a legal input to the next step. By TA5(c) for `k = 0` (chain-prefix-preservation, ASN-0034), each sibling step preserves all positions but the rightmost, so all `i + k` increments operate within the *same* depth-level enumeration anchored at `d.0.s_L`, and iterated function application `incⁱ ∘ incᵏ = incⁱ⁺ᵏ` is unambiguous on that single sibling-stream. Setting `j = i + k` (with `j ≥ 1 ≥ 0`) witnesses the invariant for `a` at Σ'.

In both sub-cases the sibling-stream invariant extends to Σ', completing the induction.

*Stage 3 — Antichain corollary, composing Stages 1 and 2.* Let Σ be a reachable state under the discipline and `a, a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `a ≼ a'`. Let `d = home(a)` and `d' = home(a')` (L1a at Σ). *Same-home case (`d = d'`):* by Stage 2's sibling-stream invariant (just proved), both `a` and `a'` lie at `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` and `incʲ'(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` for some `j, j' ≥ 0` — both are siblings in the depth-2 link allocator rooted at `d.0.s_L.1` (the allocator `A_{d.0.s_L.1}` opened by R0 Step 2 Case A's step-(iii) child-spawn `(d.0.s_L, 1)`; in the worked example this is `A_{a₁}`, whose base is its first emission). By T10a.7 (EnumerationInjectivity, ASN-0034), `A_{d.0.s_L.1}`'s enumeration coincides with the sibling stream `{incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0) : j ≥ 0}` — its `j`-th emission is `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)`, with `j ≠ j'` distinguishing distinct enumeration outputs — so `a` and `a'` are members of the *same* allocator's domain, discharging T10a.2's same-allocator precondition. By T10a.1 (UniformSiblingLength, ASN-0034) they have equal length, by T10a.8 (UniformSiblingZeroCount, ASN-0034) they share `zeros = 3`, and by T10a.2 (NonNestingSiblingPrefixes, ASN-0034) any two distinct siblings of the same allocator are prefix-incomparable; so `a ≼ a' ⟹ a = a'`. *Cross-home case (`d ≠ d'`):* discharged unconditionally by Stage 1 — `a ≼ a'` is impossible when `home(a) ≠ home(a')`, so the implication `a ≼ a' ⟹ a = a'` holds vacuously. Either way, R0a's antichain conclusion holds. The discipline-conditionality of R0a is concentrated entirely in Stage 2's induction (which licenses the same-home case); Stage 1's cross-home sub-argument needs no discipline appeal. ∎

*Failure modes — necessity of the discipline.* The R0a hypothesis is *necessary*, not just sufficient: a single non-disciplinary class-(iii) transition permanently breaks the antichain (by L12 + L12a: no removal transition exists, so the bad pair persists forever), and Nullify's single-tuple-scope fails at every subsequent state. See Appendix B for the full case analysis with a concrete instantiation against the Worked Sketch, and for the operational summary on partial discipline.

*Remark — substrate evidence (discipline held in practice).* The flat-link-domain invariant matches the link-allocation behavior of the udanax-green implementation: the `LINKATOM` branch of `findisatoinsertmolecule` (granf2.c:170–175) deposits every link at `lowerbound + 1` (a sibling of the previous link, via `tumblerincrement(·, 0, 1, ·)`) or at `docaddr` extended by two positions (the first link sited at depth +2 under the document), and never invokes any increment that would place a link as a child of another link. This is evidence that the sibling-frontier discipline is *held* by the concrete implementation — i.e., the discipline-hypothesis of R0a is empirically satisfied for udanax-green-trajectory systems — but it is not a derivation of the discipline from the substrate primitive. The substrate emission primitive's permission to deposit at any L1c-conforming-fresh-address (above) is broader than the discipline: in particular, a hypothetical alternative emission policy that deposited at `a' = a₁.1` (a strict prefix-extension of an existing link) would satisfy the primitive's conditions (1)/(2)/(3) yet would not respect the discipline. R0a's conditional form makes the dependence visible. Future work that elevates the discipline to a substrate-level guarantee — e.g., by tightening Emit_K's specification to require prefix-incomparability with `dom(Σ.L)` as a postcondition, or by tightening the substrate primitive to forbid emissions at descendants of existing link addresses — would strengthen R0a from discipline-conditional to substrate-level. Under such a strengthening, the sibling-frontier hypothesis above becomes redundant and R0a holds unconditionally.

**R0a-Cor1 — ContiguousPrefixUnderDiscipline.** *[setup: free, discipline: req, stipulation: N/A]* Under R0a's hypothesis (i.e., for every state Σ `→_D*`-reachable from a Σ_0 with `dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅`) and every `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, there exists `J_d^Σ ∈ ℤ_{≥-1}` such that

`(A Σ : (E Σ_0 : dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅ ∧ Σ_0 →_D* Σ) :: (A d ∈ dom(Σ.M) :: (E J_d^Σ ∈ ℤ_{≥-1} :: {a ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a) = d} = {incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0) : 0 ≤ j ≤ J_d^Σ})))`

(with `J_d^Σ = -1` denoting the empty set when no link is homed at `d`). The reachability quantifier is `→_D*`, not `→*` — R0a-Cor1 inherits R0a's discipline-restricted scope rather than ranging over arbitrary `→`-reachable states. The R0a sibling-stream invariant supplies the `⊆` direction (every homed link lies at some `incʲ`); R0a-Cor1 strengthens this with the `⊇` direction — the occupied positions form a contiguous prefix `{0, 1, …, J_d^Σ}` of the stream, with no skipped indices.

*Proof.* By induction on the `→_D`-chain length (the discipline-restricted reachability inherited from R0a's hypothesis), parallel to R0a's induction. Class-(iii) `→`-steps that *fail* the discipline are outside `→_D` and so outside the chains over which the induction ranges; the Sub-case B argument below explicitly invokes R0a's invariant, which holds only along `→_D*`-reachable chains. *Direction of the strengthening.* R0a's sibling-stream invariant supplies the `⊆` direction — every homed link lies at some `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` — and is inherited unchanged here. The induction below proves the `⊇` direction: that the set of occupied indices forms a contiguous prefix `{0, 1, …, J_d^Σ}` with no skipped indices. The induction is therefore over the *strengthened* (contiguous-prefix) invariant, not over R0a's sibling-stream invariant alone — the IH below carries the contiguous-prefix property, which is what licenses Sub-case B's bound on `k` and its identification of `i = J_{d_new}^Σ - k + 1`.

*Base.* `dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅`, so `J_d^{Σ_0} = -1` for every `d ∈ dom(Σ_0.M)` (the empty set on the left, the vacuous range on the right).

*Step.* Assume the invariant at Σ; consider Σ → Σ'. Classes (i) and (ii) leave `dom(Σ.L)` unchanged (Frame), preserving the invariant. For class (iii) emitting fresh `a` at home `d_new` via R0 Step 2:

*Sub-case A* — `a = d_new.0.s_L.1 = inc⁰(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0)`; Case A's hypothesis `{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d_new} = ∅` gives `J_{d_new}^Σ = -1`. The post-state homed set is `{a} = {inc⁰(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0)} = {incʲ : 0 ≤ j ≤ 0}`; set `J_{d_new}^{Σ'} = 0`.

*Sub-case B* — `a = incⁱ(b, 0)` for `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `home(b) = d_new`, where `i ≥ 1` is the least with `incⁱ(b, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)`. By IH, the existing homed set is `{incʲ(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0) : 0 ≤ j ≤ J_{d_new}^Σ}` with `J_{d_new}^Σ ≥ 0` (Case B's `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` requires the IH'd set to be non-empty). Hence `b = incᵏ(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0)` for some `k ∈ {0, …, J_{d_new}^Σ}`. The IH's exact form gives both: (i) every `incᵏ⁺¹(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0), …, incᴶ_{d_new}^Σ(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0)` is in `dom(Σ.L)`; (ii) `incᴶ_{d_new}^Σ⁺¹(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)` (no index `> J_{d_new}^Σ` lies in the IH'd set). The least `i ≥ 1` with `incⁱ(b, 0) = incᵏ⁺ⁱ(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)` is therefore `i = J_{d_new}^Σ - k + 1`, yielding `a = incᴶ_{d_new}^Σ⁺¹(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0)`. The post-state homed set is `{incʲ(d_new.0.s_L.1, 0) : 0 ≤ j ≤ J_{d_new}^Σ + 1}`; set `J_{d_new}^{Σ'} = J_{d_new}^Σ + 1`.

For every `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` with `d ≠ d_new`, `{a' ∈ dom(Σ'.L) : home(a') = d} = {a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d}` (no new link is homed elsewhere); the invariant holds at Σ' with `J_d^{Σ'} = J_d^Σ`. In both sub-cases the strengthened invariant extends to Σ', completing the induction. ∎

*Use.* R0a-Cor1 licenses the seed-independence argument in `Emit_K`'s Definition (below): under the discipline, Case B's choice of `b` ranges only over the contiguous prefix `{inc⁰, …, incᴶ_d^Σ}`, and the least-`i` selection lands the fresh emission at the *global* next index `J_d^Σ + 1` regardless of which `b` is chosen. The corollary is otherwise dormant — it is consumed exactly at the `Emit_K`-as-function argument.

**R0a-Cor2 — DepthTwoLinkAddresses.** *[setup: free, discipline: req, stipulation: N/A]* Under R0a's hypothesis, every link address in `dom(Σ.L)` has an element field (T4b's `E` projection) of length exactly 2:

`(A Σ : (E Σ_0 : dom(Σ_0.L) = ∅ ∧ Σ_0 →_D* Σ) :: (A a ∈ dom(Σ.L) :: #E(a) = 2))`

(Here `#E(a)` is the length of the element-field projection — equivalently, the number of components in `E(a)` past `a`'s home document, matching the convention used in the worked sketch — e.g., `E(a₁) = [2, 1]`, `#E(a₁) = 2` at the concrete instantiation — and the threshold of L1b (LinkElementFieldDepth, ASN-0043), which admits `#E ≥ 2`.)

*Proof.* By R0a's sibling-stream invariant, every `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` lies on the form `a = incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` for `d = home(a)` and some `j ≥ 0`. The base case `j = 0` gives `a = d.0.s_L.1` with element field `E(a) = [s_L, 1]`, so `#E(a) = 2` directly. Each subsequent sibling step `inc(·, 0)` operates within the depth-2 link allocator `A_{d.0.s_L.1}` and, by TA5(c) (chain-prefix-preservation, ASN-0034), preserves every position of `E` other than the rightmost — in particular preserving `#E`. Hence `#E` is constant across the entire sibling stream at value 2. ∎

R0a-Cor2 narrows L1b's `#E ≥ 2` admission to `#E = 2`, matching the udanax-green implementation; relaxation to deeper-sited links is discussed in Open Questions.

**R1 — AddressInjectivity.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* The map `addr : L → A_rel` is an injection:

`(A (a, F, G), (a', F', G') ∈ L : a = a' :: F = F' ∧ G = G' ∧ both belong to the same coverage-class slice L_{[K]})`

*Proof.* `Σ.L` is a partial function `T ⇀ Link` (ASN-0043, Definition of LinkStore). Function-ness gives uniqueness of value: if `a = a'`, then `Σ.L(a) = Σ.L(a')`, and that single value determines the triple `(F, G, K'')` stored at `a`. Therefore `F = F'`, `G = G'`, and the third endset `K''` is unique. Since `coverage(·)` is a pure function on endset values, `coverage(K'')` is a single fixed address set, so the coverage class `[K'']` is unique — whence both members of `L` lie in the same `L_{[K'']}`. ∎

**R2 — TupleAddressPermanence.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* Once allocated, a tuple address resolves permanently to the same relational content:

`(A Σ → Σ', a ∈ dom(Σ.L), (F, G, K) = Σ.L(a) :: a ∈ dom(Σ'.L) ∧ Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K))`

*Proof.* Direct from L12 (LinkImmutability, ASN-0043): for every state transition, every existing link address persists with its value unchanged. ∎

*Consequences.* Each bullet is tagged by type — [COROLLARY] for a theorem-level implication of the R-claim and prior R-claims/L-invariants; [POLICY] for a higher-layer convention not entailed by the substrate; [ARCHITECTURE] for a structural design observation about the substrate's shape. The typology is fixed once here and applied uniformly in subsequent Consequences sections (R3, R4, R5, R6c).

(a) *[COROLLARY]* *Distinct emissions are distinguishable even when content matches.* Two agents independently filing tuples with identical `(F, G)` under identical `K` produce distinct addresses (R0 produces a fresh address regardless of value). By L11b (NonInjectivity, ASN-0043), value-level coincidence is permitted; by R1, address-level identity nevertheless distinguishes them. The substrate does not silently merge them.

(b) *[COROLLARY]* *Counting is well-defined.* `|{(a, F, G) ∈ L_K : pattern matches (F, G)}|` is a number, not an equivalence-class size, because the elements counted are distinct addresses (R1).

(c) *[COROLLARY]* *Audit references are stable forever.* An address written into the to-set of any tuple in cycle 1 still resolves to the same emission in cycle N, after the substrate has grown by orders of magnitude (R2). The reference does not need re-validation.

(d) *[POLICY]* *Idempotency on emit is policy, not substrate guarantee.* The substrate accepts duplicate emissions — R0 produces a fresh address regardless of whether identical content already exists. Higher layers wishing at-most-once semantics check `(E (a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ :: F, G match)` before calling Emit. This is a layer above the substrate's primitive.


## Append-Only Slices (R3)

**R3 — TypedSliceMonotonicity.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* Each typed relation grows monotonically:

`(A Σ → Σ', K ∈ T_admissible :: L_K^Σ ⊆ L_K^{Σ'})`

where `L_K^Σ` denotes the typed relation evaluated at state `Σ`.

*Proof.* Let `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ`. By Definition of `L_K^Σ` (membership at the type slot is by coverage-equivalence, not by literal endset value), `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `Σ.L(a) = (F, G, K'')` for some `K'' ∈ T_admissible` satisfying `coverage(K'') = coverage(K)`. By L12a (LinkStoreMonotonicity, ASN-0043), `dom(Σ.L) ⊆ dom(Σ'.L)`; by R2, `Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K'')` — the literal value stored at `a` is preserved exactly. The membership test for `L_K^{Σ'}` is `coverage(Σ'.L(a).e₃) = coverage(K)`, i.e., `coverage(K'') = coverage(K)`, which holds by the choice of `K''`. Therefore `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'}`. ∎

*Consequences.* (Typology per R2's Consequences key.)

(a) *[COROLLARY]* *One-directional audit stability.* "A tuple of type K with this `(F, G)` existed at some point" stays true once true. Histories do not rewrite themselves.

(b) *[COROLLARY]* *Retractions are themselves auditable.* When we introduce the retraction type `R` (R6), `L_R` is one of the typed slices and R3 applies to it as well. Every nullification leaves an entry in `L_R` that persists.

(c) *[COROLLARY]* *Historical replay is well-defined.* `L_K` at past cycle `n` is a prefix of `L_K` at any cycle `m ≥ n`; "what was the substrate at cycle n?" is computable from the current substrate and a cycle-cutoff predicate. No separate snapshot mechanism is required.

(d) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *No information loss.* No compaction, no garbage collection, no archive tier removes tuples from `L_K`. The substrate's reliability for downstream agents — that an emission in cycle 3 is still observable in cycle 30 — is exactly R3.


## Subspace Disjointness (R4)

**R4 — TupleAddressDisjointness.** *[setup: req, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* Under the setup hypothesis, tuple addresses and document-content addresses are disjoint:

`A_doc^Σ ∩ A_rel^Σ = ∅`

*Proof.* By the setup hypothesis, every content address is `s_C`-resident, so the slice `dom(Σ.C)|_{s_C}` (L0a, ContentSubspaceScope, ASN-0043) coincides with `dom(Σ.C)`. L14 (DualPrimitive, ASN-0043) asserts `dom(Σ.L) ∩ dom(Σ.C)|_{s_C} = ∅`. Substituting, `dom(Σ.L) ∩ dom(Σ.C) = ∅`, i.e., `A_doc^Σ ∩ A_rel^Σ = ∅`. ∎

*Remark on the underlying structural mechanism.* L14's derivation in ASN-0043 rests on three facts: link addresses have `subspace_I = s_L` (L0, SubspacePartition), the `s_C`-resident content slice has `subspace_I = s_C` (L0a, ContentSubspaceScope), and the subspace-distinctness axiom (stated above near the Setup hypothesis) fixes `s_C ≠ s_L` as distinct first-element-field subspace identifiers. T3 (CanonicalRepresentation, ASN-0034) makes tumblers that disagree in any component distinct as values, so addresses with distinct first-element-field components are distinct tumblers. T7 (SubspaceDisjointness, ASN-0034) packages this for element-level addresses, but its applicability requires `zeros(a) = zeros(a') = 3` on both addresses — which L1 (LinkElementLevel, ASN-0043) supplies for link addresses and the analogous ASN-0036 element-level constraint supplies for `s_C`-resident content. R4 invokes only the consolidated L14, deferring this chain to ASN-0043's derivation.

*Remark on L14's scoped form.* L14 supplies disjointness scoped to its hypothesis on which subspace each content address inhabits; here the setup hypothesis is what discharges that scoping globally. Where future work admits `s_L`-resident content, R4 would be replaced by L14 in its native scoped form, and the consequences below would apply slice by slice rather than substrate-wide.

*Consequences.* (Typology per R2's Consequences key.)

(a) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Predicates are typeable.* A predicate like `is_classified(d, K)` has signature `A_doc × T_cat → Bool`; a predicate like `is_active(τ)` (R6) has signature `A_rel → Bool`. No predicate has an ambiguous signature; categorical confusion at the address level is impossible.

(b) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Retraction is well-typed.* Only `A_rel` addresses are valid arguments to Nullify (R6). "Retracting a document" is not directly expressible — the to-set of an `L_R` tuple must contain a tuple address, not a document address. Document removal from active consideration is done via classifier tuples (e.g., `L_retired`) targeting the document; the document's `A_doc` address is never disturbed.

(c) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Lifecycle separation.* Documents have mutable bodies (arrangements `Σ.M` change per ASN-0036); tuples never mutate (R2). The address-level structure permits these to diverge without interference.


## Self-Reference (R5)

**R5 — TupleSelfTargeting.** *[setup: req (indirect via R0), discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* A tuple's from-set or to-set may reference tuple addresses. Specifically, for any state Σ and any `a ∈ A_rel^Σ`, the unit-depth span `(a, δ(1, #a))` is well-formed and may appear in the from-set or to-set of an emitted tuple, with `a` in its coverage. (The Worked Sketch below, Step 1, instantiates R5: the retraction tuple's to-set references the link address `a₁` directly.)

*Justification (positive permission).* We argue in two stages.

(Stage 1 — the construct is permitted.) L4(c) (EndsetGenerality, ASN-0043) explicitly states that endset spans may reference addresses in the link subspace `s_L` — i.e., addresses of other links. L13 (ReflexiveAddressing, ASN-0043) establishes that for any address `b`, the unit-depth span `(b, δ(1, #b))` is well-formed with coverage `{t : b ≼ t}` ⊇ `{b}`. Specializing to `b = a` with `a ∈ A_rel^Σ`, the span `(a, δ(1, #a))` is well-formed by L13 and is span-target-admissible by L4(c). R0's construction discharges invariant-preservation for an emission carrying such a span as an endset component: R0 Step 4 verifies each L-invariant against an arbitrary well-formed triple `(F, G, K)`, and the verification chain does not condition on what addresses appear inside `F` or `G` — so taking `F` or `G` to contain `(a, δ(1, #a))` with `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` is admissible by the same step-by-step check. (The L14a-preservation bullet in R0 Step 4 uses the Setup hypothesis to exclude the *fresh emitter address* from `ran(Σ.M)`; that dependence carries through into R5's appeal to R0's emission, so R5 is Setup-required even though Stage 2's check against L14a is orthogonal to span targets.)

(Stage 2 — no invariant opposes the construct.) L4(c) (EndsetGenerality, ASN-0043) is the only L-invariant that constrains endset target addresses; it permits link-subspace targets directly. No other invariant on the ASN-0043 / ASN-0034 / ASN-0036 stack constrains endset content (the remaining invariants are properties of `Σ.L`'s shape, the addressing structure, or `(Σ.C, Σ.M)` — all orthogonal to which addresses an endset's spans may target). Therefore non-opposition follows from L4(c) alone, and the construct is admissible. ∎

*Modal note.* R5 is a positive admissibility lemma: it asserts that a specific substrate-level construction is exhibitable (Stage 1, via L4(c) + L13 + R0) and is not opposed by any other invariant (Stage 2's exhaustive check). The classification is therefore LEMMA, not META — Stage 1 constructs the witness and Stage 2 verifies non-opposition, both substantive proof steps that R5's content depends on. The modality differs from R0–R4 only in surface phrasing: those state existence directly ("the substrate exhibits the property"); R5 states existence indirectly ("the construction is admissible — i.e., exhibitable and non-opposed"). The construction is exhibited concretely by the worked example in ASN-0043 Step 2, where a meta-link of exactly this form appears, confirming the permission is exercised within ASN-0043's own examples.

*Consequences.* Several constructs that would otherwise require out-of-band machinery collapse into the relational primitive. (Typology per R2's Consequences key.)

(a) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Retraction.* A tuple in a designated relation `L_R` whose to-set contains the address of the tuple being nullified. *Dependency chain — existence of the post-state with the retraction tuple:* R5 admits the unit-depth self-targeting span `(a, δ(1, #a))` as a to-set component (Stage 1's L4(c) + L13 chain establishes the span as well-formed and span-target-admissible at the link subspace); R0 at a caller-supplied home `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)` then emits the retraction triple `(∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))}, R)` by its Step 4 invariant-preservation argument, witnessing a state `Σ'` with the retraction tuple at a fresh address in `dom(Σ'.L)`. Mutation becomes Emit; `L_K` is never modified (R3).

(b) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Resolution.* A tuple in `L_resolution` whose to-set contains a comment-tuple's address. *Dependency chain — existence of the post-state with the resolution tuple:* R5 admits the to-span targeting the comment-tuple's address (a relational address in `A_rel^Σ`, admissible by the same L4(c) + L13 chain as (a)); R0 at a caller-supplied home then emits the resolution triple, witnessing a state with the resolution recorded at a fresh `A_rel` address. Comment lifecycle is uniformly substrate-tracked; "this comment is closed" is an ordinary observation, not a flag stored elsewhere.

(c) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Agent provenance.* A tuple whose from-set contains an agent's address and whose to-set contains the emitted tuple. *Dependency chain — existence of the post-state with the provenance tuple:* R5 admits both the from-set's agent-address span and the to-set's emitted-tuple-address span as `A_rel^Σ`-targeting endset components (each unit-depth span discharges via L4(c) + L13, and R5's Stage 1 argument does not distinguish from-slot vs. to-slot — both inherit the same admissibility); R0 at a caller-supplied home emits the provenance triple, witnessing a state with the attribution recorded. Every emission has an attributable emitter as a substrate fact, with no separate metadata channel.

(d) *[COROLLARY for substrate-derivable half; POLICY for conventionally-derivable half]* *Higher-order predicates.* The substrate's Observe machinery uniformly answers any predicate expressible as a query over `L_K`, but the questions split by what each requires as input. *Substrate-derivable from Σ alone [COROLLARY]:* "Has τ been retracted?" reduces to `τ ∈ nullified(Σ)` (Definition of `nullified`), and "What tuples target τ?" to Observe filtered for tuples whose endset coverage contains τ — both decided directly against `Σ.L`. *Conventionally derivable [POLICY]:* "Who emitted τ?" is *not* answered by Σ.L alone — `Emit_K` writes `(F, G, K)` to a fresh address with no implicit emitter slot (Definition of `Emit_K`), so provenance has no substrate-level meaning. By consequence (c) above, the emitter includes its own address in the from-set of an `L_provenance` tuple targeting τ; once that convention is in force, "who emitted τ?" reduces to Observe over `L_provenance`. Predicates over documents face the same split (e.g., "who authored d?" is conventional in exactly the same way). The substrate's contribution is uniform evaluation machinery; *which* relations exist to evaluate against is a caller obligation upstream.

Without R5, each construct would require its own layer that predicates could not see and that the audit trail (R3) would not preserve. R5 collapses such layers into the relational structure.


## The Active Subset (R6a, R6b, R6c)

R0–R5 are derivable from ASN-0043. The *conceptual* contribution of this section — added here, not present in Nelson's link model — is the *active/audit distinction* that the Definition of ActiveSubset (below) makes available: two coherent views (`L_K`, the audit trail accumulating monotonically per R3; `A_K`, the operational currently-in-effect set obtained by excluding `nullified(Σ)`) co-existing over the same link store. The substantive lemmas carrying that distinction are R6a (RetractionStability — nullification persists across state transitions), R6b (SingleDepthRetraction — the predicate does not recurse through the retraction relation), and R6c (RestorationByReemission — once retracted, always out of every future active subset). The construction is made possible by R5 (a self-referential retraction relation can exist) and R3 (the retraction relation accumulates monotonically, providing the audit trail against which the active subset is computed). Well-definedness of `A_K^Σ` is folded into the Definition itself (below) — set-difference between two sets each fixed by `Σ.L`, one line of accounting — rather than carried as a numbered R-claim.

**Definition — RetractionType.** Fix a designated coverage class `[R]` reserved for retraction, represented by any `R ∈ T_admissible` whose coverage selects the conventional retraction address set. The corresponding typed relation `L_R^Σ` is the *retraction relation at state Σ*. By L9 (TypeGhostPermission, ASN-0043), `R` need not refer to anything stored — its coverage is an address set, chosen by convention — and `L_R^Σ` is well-defined as a coverage-class slice regardless of whether any literal representative endset has yet been stored. Before the first retraction emission, `L_R^Σ = ∅`; after the first such emission, `L_R^Σ ≠ ∅`. The definition of `L_R^Σ` does not depend on which case applies, and it is stated in coverage-class terms rather than `T_cat^Σ`-membership terms — `T_cat^Σ` is literal-endset-valued (per the asymmetry noted above), so `T_cat^Σ`-membership is not the right indicator of "has any retraction been emitted yet?" — that question is exactly `L_R^Σ ≠ ∅`. By coverage-equivalence (Definition of TypedRelation), any emission with a type endset `R'` satisfying `coverage(R') = coverage(R)` contributes to `L_R^Σ` and to `nullified(Σ)` — callers are not required to use a canonical span structure for `R`, only its canonical coverage.

**Convention — RetractionDirectionality.** *[Substrate-level convention; consumed by Definition of Nullified.]* For the retraction coverage class `[R]`, the to-set carries the retraction's targets — addresses whose tuples are being withdrawn from the active subset — and the from-set is reserved for *attribution-bearing endset content* (e.g., the retractor's own address under the agent-provenance pattern of R5 Consequence (c)) or is left empty for unattributed retractions. The asymmetry is *not* a substrate-level entailment of L4–L8: L4 (EndsetGenerality) and L5 (EndsetSetSemantics) treat from- and to-slots symmetrically, L6 (SlotDistinction) gives them only positional identity, and L7 (DirectionalFlexibility) explicitly defers directional interpretation to the type. The convention names how the retraction type `R` exercises L7: the from-/to-slot asymmetry is *contributed by the retraction type's intended use*, not derived from the substrate's link model. Symmetric and reverse-direction conventions are equally well-formed at the L-invariant level (the substrate would not reject `Emit_R(Σ, d, {(a, δ(1, #a))}, ∅)` — a from-set retraction — as malformed); ASN-0086 adopts the to-set-as-target convention to match Nelson's link-model design intent for retraction and the udanax-green retraction-tuple shape.

**Definition — Nullified.** *[Inherits Convention — RetractionDirectionality.]* The set of *nullified* tuple addresses at state `Σ` is

`nullified(Σ) = {a ∈ A_rel^Σ : (E (b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ :: a ∈ coverage(G'))}`

The existential checks `coverage(G')` only — the to-set's coverage — and does not inspect `coverage(F')`. This is the Convention exercised at the substrate-level predicate: retraction targets are in `G'` by Convention; an `Emit_R` call whose to-span coverage misses `a` does not nullify `a`, regardless of what its from-set covers. A reformulation under the reverse convention would replace `G'` with `F'` here; a symmetric convention would replace it with `coverage(F') ∪ coverage(G')`. Both alternatives are well-formed but adopt a different directional reading of the retraction type; the present Definition is bound to the to-set convention. By R5, `coverage(G')` may include `A_rel^Σ` addresses, so `nullified(Σ)` is well-defined as a subset of `A_rel^Σ`.

*Operational scope of the `A_rel^Σ` filter.* The `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` filter scopes `nullified(Σ)` to relational addresses, while `coverage(G')` is a subset of `T` (not of `A_rel^Σ`) and may also contain content addresses (`A_doc^Σ`), ghost tumblers (addresses without stored representatives, admitted by L9), or higher-arity link addresses (where `|Σ.L(a)| > 3`). Retractions whose `coverage(G')` lies entirely outside `A_rel^Σ ∩ {a : |Σ.L(a)| = 3}` — e.g., a tuple emitted via `Emit_R(Σ, d', ∅, {(c, δ(1, #c))})` for `c ∈ A_doc^Σ`, or a tuple whose to-span covers only a higher-arity link — are *well-formed* `Emit_R` calls (`Emit_R`'s precondition imposes no `A_rel^Σ` constraint on the to-set's coverage) but *operationally inert* for the active-subset machinery defined here: they contribute their `(b, F', G')` triple to `L_R^Σ` (preserved by R3) yet contribute nothing to `nullified(Σ) ∩ {a : |Σ.L(a)| = 3}`, and hence leave every `A_K^Σ` (defined only over standard-triple links) unchanged. This asymmetry is structural — between *syntactic admissibility* (`Emit_R` permits any to-span) and *operational effect* (active-subset machinery is scoped to standard-triple `L_K`'s) — and is handled in two complementary ways: `Nullify`'s precondition P1 (`a ∈ A_rel^Σ`) confines that *named operation* to the operationally effective regime; other `Emit_R` calls with crafted spans remain syntactically permitted but operationally a no-op for `A_K`. The higher-arity case is flagged in the Open Questions section (proposing `A_K^{(n)}` machinery as a possible extension); the content-targeting case faces the same asymmetry but has no `A_K`-side counterpart at the relational layer at all — content's operational presence is governed by arrangements (`Σ.M`, ASN-0036), not by relational retraction.

**Definition — ActiveSubset.** For each `K ∈ T_admissible`, the *active subset of type K at state Σ* is

`A_K^Σ = {(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ : a ∉ nullified(Σ)}`

*Well-definedness.* `A_K^Σ = L_K^Σ \ {(a, F, G) : a ∈ nullified(Σ)}` is computable from `Σ.L` alone, with no auxiliary state — `L_K^Σ` is fixed by `Σ.L` (Definition of TypedRelation), and `nullified(Σ)` is fixed by `L_R^Σ`, itself a slice of `Σ.L` (Definition of Nullified). The definition names the active subset, distinct from `L_K`, as an addressable view over the same link store, so that "what is currently in effect" and "what has ever existed" are two coherent observations distinguished only by the set-difference. The well-definedness check is one line (set-difference between two sets each fixed by `Σ.L`); the substantive properties of the active/audit distinction are carried by R6a (the distinction is stable across state transitions), R6b (the distinction does not recurse through `L_R`), R6c (re-emission, not retraction-of-retraction, is the restoration mechanism), and R6c-Corollary (the distinction extends across arrangement-modifying transitions).

**R6a — RetractionStability.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* Once a tuple's address is nullified, it stays nullified across all future state transitions:

`(A Σ → Σ', a ∈ A_rel^Σ : a ∈ nullified(Σ) :: a ∈ nullified(Σ'))`

*Proof.* Recall that `coverage : Endset → ℘(T)` is a pure function on endset values, fixed by the substrate model (ASN-0043, Definition of coverage): given an endset value `E`, `coverage(E)` is determined entirely by `E` and the tumbler-order relation `≼`, which itself is state-independent (T1, ASN-0034). The codomain is `℘(T)` — the full tumbler space — not the state-dependent address universe `A^Σ`; coverage may include addresses outside `dom(Σ.C) ∪ dom(Σ.L)` (L9, TypeGhostPermission, ASN-0043). In particular, `coverage(E)` does not depend on the state Σ in which `E` is evaluated.

Suppose `a ∈ nullified(Σ)`. By Definition of `nullified(Σ)`, this entails `a ∈ A_rel^Σ = dom(Σ.L)`, and there exist `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` and `(b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ` with `a ∈ coverage(G')`. By the coverage-equivalence membership criterion of `L_R^Σ`, the literal value stored at `b` in Σ is `Σ.L(b) = (F', G', R'')` for some `R'' ∈ T_admissible` with `coverage(R'') = coverage(R)` — the third entry need not equal `R` literally; only its coverage must. We exhibit the same witness at Σ': by L12a (LinkStoreMonotonicity, ASN-0043) applied to `a ∈ A_rel^Σ`, `a ∈ dom(Σ.L) ⊆ dom(Σ'.L) = A_rel^{Σ'}`, discharging the `a ∈ A_rel^{Σ'}` predicate required by Definition of `nullified(Σ')`. By R3 (applied to the type slice indexed by `R`), `L_R^Σ ⊆ L_R^{Σ'}`, so `(b, F', G') ∈ L_R^{Σ'}`. By R2, `b ∈ dom(Σ'.L)` with `Σ'.L(b) = (F', G', R'')` — the literal stored value is preserved exactly, so in particular the from- and to-endsets `F'` and `G'` are preserved; the proof requires only this preservation of `G'`, not any literal equality at the type slot. Since `coverage` is a pure function on endset values, `coverage(G')` is a single fixed set, and `a ∈ coverage(G')` is a state-independent proposition once `G'` has been fixed. Therefore `a ∈ nullified(Σ')`. ∎

**R6b — SingleDepthRetraction.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* The retraction predicate is *single-depth*: `nullified(Σ)` checks only whether some tuple in `L_R` directly targets `a`, regardless of whether that retracting tuple is itself nullified.

*Justification.* By the Definition's quantifier range over `L_R^Σ` (the audit slice, not the active subset `A_R^Σ`), `a ∈ nullified(Σ)` is witnessed by any retraction tuple in `L_R^Σ` whose to-coverage contains `a`, regardless of that witness's own active-subset status. Deciding `a ∈ nullified(Σ)` therefore requires only one level of existential check, with no further evaluation of `nullified(·)` on the witness.

This is a property of *logical depth* within a single state, distinct from R6a's property of *temporal persistence* across state transitions. R6a says "nullification persists as Σ evolves"; R6b says "the predicate does not iterate through the retraction relation." Even within a single fixed state at which no transition has yet occurred, R6b already determines `nullified(Σ)`'s value, while R6a is vacuous at a single state.

To see the distinction concretely, consider two emissions at the same state. (1) Emit `(b, F', G_a, R)` with `a ∈ coverage(G_a)` — call this *direct retraction of a*. After this emission at state `Σ_1`, `(b, F', G_a) ∈ L_R^{Σ_1}`, so `a ∈ nullified(Σ_1)`, and also `b ∈ A_R^{Σ_1}` (b is not yet retracted). (2) Emit `(c, F'', G_b, R)` with `b ∈ coverage(G_b)` — *second-order retraction targeting b*. After this emission at state `Σ_2`, `(c, F'', G_b) ∈ L_R^{Σ_2}`, so `b ∈ nullified(Σ_2)`, and now `b ∉ A_R^{Σ_2}`. Question: is `a ∈ nullified(Σ_2)`? By Definition of `nullified(Σ_2)`, the existential at `a` is witnessed by `(b, F', G_a) ∈ L_R^{Σ_2}` — which holds by R3 applied to step (1)'s tuple. The existential succeeds *whether or not* `b ∈ A_R^{Σ_2}`; only `b ∈ L_R^{Σ_2}` is checked. So `a ∈ nullified(Σ_2)` regardless of `b`'s active-subset status. This is R6b. R6a is the separate claim that `a` having been in `nullified(Σ_1)` guarantees `a ∈ nullified(Σ_2)` independently of step (2); both properties hold here, but R6b would still answer the question even if R6a were absent. ∎

**R6c — RestorationByReemission (dom-extending, on `⊑`).** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* Once retracted, a tuple stays out of every active subset at any state reachable from Σ through dom-extending transitions — i.e., under the `⊑` relation:

`(A Σ, K, (a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ : a ∈ nullified(Σ) : (A Σ' : Σ ⊑ Σ' :: (a, F, G) ∉ A_K^{Σ'}))`

*Scope of this statement.* R6c is the dom-extending half of the persistence guarantee. The relation `Σ ⊑ Σ'` is the reflexive-transitive closure of the dom-extending `→`, so the formal quantifier above ranges over `→`-reachability only; ASN-0036's arrangement-modifying transitions live in a parallel transition vocabulary outside `→` (Scoping note above). The user-facing reading "once retracted, always out of every future active subset" is the *broad* sense — persistence across both dom-extending and arrangement-modifying successors — and is the content of R6c-Corollary below, which lifts the conclusion from `⊑` to `⊑̂`. The split between R6c (this statement) and R6c-Corollary (the lift) tracks the two scopes precisely: R6c's `⊑`-statement is the substantive proof step, and R6c-Corollary derives the broader `⊑̂`-statement by an additional pointwise-preservation step on arrangement-modifying transitions.

*Proof.* We first show `a ∈ nullified(Σ')` by induction on the length `n` of the `→`-chain `Σ = Σ_0 → Σ_1 → … → Σ_n = Σ'` witnessing `Σ ⊑ Σ'` (Definition of Extension). *Base* (`n = 0`): `Σ' = Σ` and `a ∈ nullified(Σ)` by hypothesis. *Step* (`n = k+1`): assume `a ∈ nullified(Σ_k)`. Apply R6a to the single step `Σ_k → Σ_{k+1}` to obtain `a ∈ nullified(Σ_{k+1}) = nullified(Σ')`. *Audit-slice persistence by a parallel induction.* We show `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'}` by induction on the same chain length `n`. *Base* (`n = 0`): `L_K^{Σ'} = L_K^Σ` and `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ` by hypothesis. *Step* (`n = k+1`): assume `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ_k}`. Apply R3 (TypedSliceMonotonicity) to the single step `Σ_k → Σ_{k+1}` to obtain `L_K^{Σ_k} ⊆ L_K^{Σ_{k+1}}`, hence `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ_{k+1}} = L_K^{Σ'}`. (Equivalently, by R3 at each step and transitivity of `⊆`, `L_K^Σ ⊆ L_K^{Σ'}` holds for the whole chain.) By Definition of `A_K`, since `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'}` and `a ∈ nullified(Σ')`, `(a, F, G) ∉ A_K^{Σ'}`. ∎

**R6c-Corollary — RestorationByReemission on the broader transition relation.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* R6c lifts unchanged from `⊑` to `⊑̂` (broader transition relation `↦` and its reflexive-transitive closure `⊑̂` defined in the State transition relation and BroadExtension sections above):

`(A Σ, K, (a, F, G) ∈ L_K^Σ : a ∈ nullified(Σ) : (A Σ'' : Σ ⊑̂ Σ'' :: (a, F, G) ∉ A_K^{Σ''}))`

*Proof.* We show the stronger pointwise statement that arrangement-modifying transitions preserve `A_K` exactly; the corollary then follows by induction on the `↦`-chain composing arrangement-modifying and dom-extending steps in any order. *Step 1:* `L_R^Σ` is the coverage-class slice of `Σ.L` indexed by `R` (Definition of TypedRelation), and so depends only on `Σ.L`. *Step 2:* `nullified(Σ) = {a ∈ A_rel^Σ : (E (b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ :: a ∈ coverage(G'))}` (Definition of `nullified`); its inputs are `A_rel^Σ = dom(Σ.L)`, `L_R^Σ`, and the state-independent pure function `coverage` (R6a's proof) — so `nullified(Σ)` depends only on `Σ.L`. *Step 3:* `A_K^Σ = L_K^Σ \ {(a, F, G) : a ∈ nullified(Σ)}` (Definition of ActiveSubset); both `L_K^Σ` (Definition of TypedRelation) and `nullified(Σ)` (Step 2) depend only on `Σ.L`, so `A_K^Σ` does too. *Step 4:* by the arrangement-modification frame stated in the Scoping note above (frame inherited from ASN-0036's class definitions, with the `Σ.L`-identity for arrangement-modifying transitions following from L12 / L12a applied to that transition class — since ASN-0036's state vocabulary is `(Σ.C, Σ.M)` and `Σ.L` is introduced in ASN-0043, the `Σ_arr.L = Σ.L` clause is a consequence of L12 / L12a's pointwise- and domain-preservation guarantees applied to the arrangement-modifying transition class ASN-0036 defines, not a direct entailment of ASN-0036's own state vocabulary), every arrangement-modifying transition leaves `Σ.L` identical — `Σ_arr.L = Σ.L` — and changes only `Σ.M`'s pointwise values. Substituting `Σ_arr.L = Σ.L` through Steps 1–3 yields `L_R^{Σ_arr} = L_R^Σ`, `nullified(Σ_arr) = nullified(Σ)`, and `A_K^{Σ_arr} = A_K^Σ`. The post-arrangement-modification active subset is therefore pointwise identical to the pre-modification one. Now induct on the `↦`-chain `Σ = Σ_0 ↦ Σ_1 ↦ … ↦ Σ_n = Σ''`: each `Σ_k ↦ Σ_{k+1}` is either a dom-extending `→` step (covered by R6c on `⊑`) or an arrangement-modifying step (pointwise preserves `A_K` by Steps 1–4 just discharged); in either case `(a, F, G) ∉ A_K^{Σ_{k+1}}` follows from `(a, F, G) ∉ A_K^{Σ_k}`. ∎

To "restore" content, emit a fresh tuple with the desired value (R0). The new tuple receives a fresh address; the retracted tuple keeps its address (R2) and stays out of `A_K` (R6a).

*Consequences.* (Typology per R2's Consequences key.)

(a) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Operational vs. historical views.* `A_K` is the operational view ("what is currently in effect"); `L_K` is the audit view ("what has ever existed"). Both are computed from `Σ.L` by the same observation machinery, differing only in whether `nullified(Σ)` is excluded. Operational and historical queries use the same observation primitive but specify different views.

(b) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Mutation as set-difference.* `A_K^Σ = L_K^Σ \ {(a, F, G) : a ∈ nullified(Σ)}`. Computed live; no flag, no cache, no version field anywhere in the architecture.

(c) *[ARCHITECTURE]* *Quiescence is operational, not historical.* "Every public predicate over `A_K` holds" is the convergence condition. It does not require historical agreement; it requires the current substrate to satisfy every public check.

(d) *[COROLLARY, stipulation-conditional]* *All visible state-transforming relational-layer operations reduce to `Emit_K` — anticipating R7 below.* Stated here in R6's vocabulary because the active subset already places Nullify within the relational-layer operation set. The reduction rests on R7a (proven below) together with the Definition of the relational layer (which commits `Emit_K` as the layer's sole state-affecting primitive); the substrate itself admits broader class-(iii) deposits outside that commitment. With the scope made explicit: file a comment, close it, retract a citation, retire a document (by classifier tuple), revive it — each is one or two `Emit_K` calls (varying `K`). The substrate's response (`A_K` shifts, predicates flip) is uniform across the lot. The qualifier "state-transforming" is essential: Observe is also a relational-layer operation but leaves `Σ` unchanged. Document allocation and content emission remain separate primitive transitions in `→`; the reduction here is scoped to *state-transforming* operations of the relational layer.

(e) *[COROLLARY]* *`A_K` is not monotone; `L_K` is.* R3 (TypedSliceMonotonicity) makes the audit slice monotone — `Σ ⊑̂ Σ' ⟹ L_K^Σ ⊆ L_K^{Σ'}` — but the same is *not* true of the active subset: a single retraction emission strictly shrinks `A_K` at every type whose tuple address it covers (witnessed by R6c's set-difference: `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ ∩ (L_K^{Σ'} \ A_K^{Σ'})` for the retracted tuple), and a subsequent re-emission of the same `(F, G)` strictly grows `A_K` again at a *different* address (R0's fresh-address guarantee). The Worked Sketch exhibits both directions concretely: `A_K^{Σ_0} = {(a₁, F₁, G₁)}` shrinks to `A_K^{Σ_1} = ∅` under Step 1's Nullify, and grows to `A_K^{Σ_2} = {(a₂, F₁, G₁)}` under Step 2's re-emission. Neither `⊆` nor `⊇` holds in general between `A_K^Σ` and `A_K^{Σ'}` for `Σ ⊑̂ Σ'`; the active subset is therefore *not* a monotone function of `Σ` under either inclusion direction, while the audit slice `L_K` is monotone under `⊆`. Predicates and observation views over `A_K` must accommodate non-monotone evolution as a substrate-level fact, not assume monotonicity inherited from `L_K`'s audit semantics. *Regime-(ii) strengthening (retraction-discipline-conditional).* Under the unit-depth retraction discipline (regime (i)), the non-monotonicity of `A_K` requires a *retraction step* between `Σ` and `Σ'`: a class-(iii) `Emit_K` step alone strictly grows both `L_K` and `A_K` together (the fresh `(a, F, G)` is in `A_K^{Σ'}` per the regime-(i) analysis at Emit_K's Definition). If the discipline is dropped (regime (ii) admitted), `A_K` can fail to grow even on a non-retraction class-(iii) emission: a fresh `(a, F, G)` ∈ L_K^{Σ'} can fall in `nullified(Σ')` immediately upon emission via a pre-existing crafted-span retraction, so `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'} \ A_K^{Σ'}` at the same step that allocates it. The strengthened non-monotonicity statement is therefore: under regime (i), `A_K` is monotone along class-(iii) Emit_K steps and only shrinks at class-(iii) Nullify steps; under regime (ii), `A_K` can fail to grow at any class-(iii) emission whose home is covered by a crafted retraction-span. Observe operations expecting `A_K` to capture every recently-emitted tuple must adopt regime (i) as a caller-level commitment, or treat regime (ii)'s effect on coverage as in scope.


## Three Operations

The six properties yield three operations that suffice to span all visible substrate change.

**Definition — Emit_K.** *[setup: free (Definition core); discipline: req (Definition binds R0 Step 2's construction); stipulation: N/A]* Emit is a state-transforming operation with signature

`Emit_K : Σ × dom(Σ.M) × Endset × Endset → Σ' × A_rel^{Σ'}`

*Postcondition (address-construction discipline).* The returned address `a` is constructed by R0 Step 2 — *Case A* `a = d.0.s_L.1` when `home(d)` is unused (i.e., `{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d} = ∅`); *Case B* `a = incⁱ(b, 0)` for the least fresh `i ≥ 1` (where `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` has `home(b) = d`) otherwise. This postcondition is part of the signature: `Emit_K` is the sibling-frontier-disciplined subset of the substrate emission primitive, not the primitive in full. (The substrate primitive admits a strictly broader set of class-(iii) addresses — including strict prefix-extensions of existing link addresses — which `Emit_K` excludes by construction.)

*Scope of `Emit_K`'s contract — substrate-primitive bypass is outside it.* Callers that bypass the discipline by invoking the substrate emission primitive directly at a prefix-extension of an existing link address have *not* invoked `Emit_K` and are not covered by R0a or its corollaries (R0a-Cor1, R0a-Cor2): the resulting transition is a class-(iii) `→`-step at the substrate level — L12, L12a, and the Frame conditions admit it — but it lies outside `Emit_K`'s contract, since `Emit_K`'s postcondition binds the address to R0 Step 2's sibling-frontier construction and a prefix-extension address fails that postcondition. Such a bypass invocation is admitted by R7a (which is categorical over any-layer class-(iii) `→`-steps) but is excluded by R7b (which scopes the relational layer to `Emit_K` invocations only); from the substrate's perspective the deposit is a well-formed class-(iii) emission, but from the relational-layer-as-committed perspective the deposit is not a relational-layer operation and the discipline-dependent guarantees of R0a / Nullify / `A_K`-stability that flow from R7b's commitment no longer apply at the post-state. The failure modes of such bypasses are catalogued in Appendix B.

The defining precondition is `K ∈ T_admissible`. Given input state `Σ`, a *caller-supplied home document* `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, and finite endsets `F, G ∈ Endset`, `Emit_K(Σ, d, F, G)` deposits a fresh tuple under `d` at an address constructed by *R0 Step 2's sibling-frontier construction* applied at that `d`: emit at the Case A address (`a = d.0.s_L.1` when `{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d} = ∅`) or the Case B address (`a = incⁱ(b, 0)` for the least `i ≥ 1` with `incⁱ(b, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, where `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` has `home(b) = d`). The returned `(Σ', a)` satisfies `a ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, `a ∈ dom(Σ'.L)`, `home(a) = d`, and `Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K)`. All other components of Σ are held in frame: `Σ'.C = Σ.C` and `Σ'.M = Σ.M`. By R2, the binding `Σ'.L(a) = (F, G, K)` is permanent across all subsequent transitions. (The precondition `dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅` of R0 is enforced by the signature itself: a parameter `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` cannot be supplied unless the document-allocation domain is non-empty.)

*Case B's seed-independence — `Emit_K` is a function of `(Σ, d, F, G)`, not a relation.* Case B's selection of `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `home(b) = d` is non-deterministic in form — many such `b` may exist when `d` has accumulated multiple link allocations under `Σ` — but the resulting fresh address `a` is *independent of which `b` is chosen*. Under R0a's sibling-stream invariant (above), every `a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `home(a') = d` lies on the single sibling stream `d.0.s_L.1, inc(d.0.s_L.1, 0), inc²(d.0.s_L.1, 0), …` anchored at the Case A base. Choosing any `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` with `home(b) = d` gives `b = incᵏ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` for some `k ≥ 0` (R0a's invariant), and Case B's least-`i` selection yields `a = incⁱ(b, 0) = incⁱ⁺ᵏ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)`. Let `j = i + k`. By the definition of the least-`i` choice — the smallest `i ≥ 1` with `incⁱ(b, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)` — the resulting `j` is exactly the least `j ≥ k + 1` with `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)`; combined with R0a-Cor1 (ContiguousPrefixUnderDiscipline) applied at Σ — which gives `{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : home(a') = d} = {incʲ'(d.0.s_L.1, 0) : 0 ≤ j' ≤ J_d^Σ}` for a contiguous-prefix bound `J_d^Σ ≥ 0`, with `k ≤ J_d^Σ` (since `b = incᵏ(d.0.s_L.1, 0) ∈ dom(Σ.L)` forces `k` into Cor1's prefix range), and therefore every `incʲ'(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` for `j' ∈ {0, …, J_d^Σ}` — in particular for `j' ∈ {0, …, k}` — is in `dom(Σ.L)`, while `incᴶ_d^Σ⁺¹(d.0.s_L.1, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)` — this `j` is in fact the least `j ≥ 0` (equivalently the least `j ≥ 1`, since `j ≥ k + 1 ≥ 1`) with `incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0) ∉ dom(Σ.L)`, namely `j = J_d^Σ + 1`. So `a = incʲ(d.0.s_L.1, 0)` is fixed by `Σ` and `d` alone — the next-frontier index `j` is determined by `dom(Σ.L)`'s sibling-stream extent under `d`, with no dependence on which prior link `b` the caller (or implementation) happens to pick as the seed. The Case B definition's apparent choice of `b` is therefore a presentation device for the construction, not a genuine non-determinism in the operation's output; `Emit_K` returns a unique `(Σ', a)` for each input `(Σ, d, F, G)` with `dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅` and `K ∈ T_admissible`, and is a function in the standard sense.

*Why `d` is a caller parameter, not a substrate-internal choice.* Two independent considerations fix the home document as an explicit input rather than a substrate-chosen one. *Design-level (the link model).* In Nelson's link design, every link is *owned by* a specific document — its home — and the home-document choice is a relational fact about what document the link belongs to, settled at emission time by the caller's intent. The substrate is not free to assign homes; assignment is a caller obligation. *Implementation-level (the udanax-green code path).* The `MAKELINK` / `docreatelink` path takes the home document address as an explicit input parameter (the function's `docaddr` argument), routes allocation through that document's link enumerator (granfilade orgl tree) and POOM, and never selects the home internally. Promoting `d` into the signature aligns the abstract operation with both the design intent and the concrete code-path interface; an earlier "pick any `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`" wording at this level would have understated the determinacy of the abstraction by treating the home as substrate-internal. R0's existential quantification over `d` (`dom(Σ.M) ≠ ∅ ⟹ (E d ∈ dom(Σ.M) :: …)`) is preserved as a *foundational existence claim* — the substrate admits *some* emission whenever a home document exists — but `Emit_K`'s operational definition selects a specific `d` per call.

The address-returning convention in the rest of this note — `Emit_K(d, F, G) → A_rel` — is a metonym: the state is the ambient one, `d` is the caller-supplied home document, and `Σ'` is the post-emission state in which the returned address resides.

*Why the construction is bound into the definition.* The substrate emission primitive (above) admits a strictly broader set of addresses than R0 Step 2 — including strict prefix-extensions of existing link addresses, which would falsify R0a's antichain (see "Breadth of the primitive vs. the discipline R0a names" above). If `Emit_K` were defined as "any primitive-permissible emission," a single non-disciplinary call would break R0a, and Nullify's single-tuple-scope argument — which relies on R0a's reachable-state antichain holding at the post-state — would fail. Binding `Emit_K` to R0 Step 2's construction makes every `Emit_K`-induced trace respect the sibling-frontier discipline by definition: R0a holds unconditionally on traces whose class-(iii) transitions are all `Emit_K` calls (the discipline hypothesis of R0a is discharged at every such step), and Nullify's single-tuple-scope argument goes through without further discipline assertions. The broader substrate primitive remains documented above as the abstract emission interface; this note's `Emit_K` operation selects the sibling-frontier-disciplined subset of that primitive.

*A_K^{Σ'} membership of the fresh emission — conditional on retraction discipline.* The Definition above guarantees `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'}` for the fresh emission (coverage-equivalence membership applied to the literal stored triple) but is silent on `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}`, which turns on whether `a ∈ nullified(Σ')`. Two regimes are operationally relevant. (i) *Nullify-only discipline.* If every `L_R^Σ` tuple is the result of a `Nullify` call (below) — i.e., every retraction has a unit-depth to-span of the form `{(b, δ(1, #b))}` for some target `b ∈ A_rel^Σ` — then each retraction's coverage `{t : b ≼ t}` intersected with `A_rel^{Σ'}` reduces to `{b}` by R0a's antichain on `dom(Σ'.L)`. The fresh `a` produced by Emit_K is, by R0 Step 2's sibling-frontier construction and R0a's reachable-state antichain at Σ', prefix-incomparable with every `b ∈ A_rel^Σ` (the discipline keeps `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L) ∪ {a}` an antichain, so no existing link is a prefix of `a`). Therefore `a` is not in `coverage(G')` for any `(_, _, G') ∈ L_R^{Σ'}`, hence `a ∉ nullified(Σ')`, and `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}`. (ii) *Crafted-span retractions admitted.* The substrate primitive does not preclude callers from emitting retractions with broader-coverage to-spans — e.g., a subtree-broad retraction `Emit_R(Σ, d_retr, ∅, {(d, δ(1, #d))})` whose coverage is `{t : d ≼ t}`, which intersected with `A_rel^Σ` covers every link sited under `d` (and propagates to every link subsequently emitted under `d`, since R3 preserves the retraction tuple). If any `L_R^Σ` tuple has such a crafted span, a fresh `a` emitted under a covered home falls within `coverage(L_R^Σ)` immediately upon emission: `a ∈ nullified(Σ')` at the very step that allocates `a`, and `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'} \ A_K^{Σ'}` — the audit slice records the emission, but the active subset excludes it from emission onward. The Nullify definition below scopes that operation to the unit-depth-span form (regime (i)); whether the wider crafted-span form (regime (ii)) is admitted is a discipline-level property of caller retraction practice, not an Emit_K guarantee. A_K membership of a fresh emission is therefore conditional on which regime is in force.

**Definition — Observe_K.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* For `K ∈ T_admissible`, a pattern `(F̂, Ĝ) ∈ ℘_fin(A) × ℘_fin(A)`, and a view selector, Observe is a pure read with signature

`Observe_K : Σ × ℘_fin(A) × ℘_fin(A) × View → ℘_fin(L_K^Σ)`

where `View ∈ {hist, oper}` selects between `L_K^Σ` (audit) and `A_K^Σ` (operational). It returns

`{(a, F, G) ∈ view : F̂ ⊆ coverage(F) ∧ Ĝ ⊆ coverage(G)}`

with `view = L_K^Σ` if `View = hist` and `view = A_K^Σ` if `View = oper`. Observe leaves Σ unchanged.

*Rationale for the match relation.* `F̂ ⊆ coverage(F)` is the *minimal* substrate-level match relation, in two senses. First, every substrate computes `coverage(·)` (ASN-0043, Definition of coverage) as the canonical endset-to-address projection, and subset-containment of finite address sets is universally available — no auxiliary primitive is required. Second, the relation answers the canonical substrate-level question: "does this tuple's from-endset cover every address in the requested pattern?" This is exactly the question for which subset-on-coverage is the affirmative answer; richer queries (span-prefix containment, regex over a content projection, type-equality at the address level via L8) post-compose with Observe rather than parameterizing it. Observe is therefore fixed as the substrate-level primitive that returns "tuples whose endsets cover the requested address sets"; layered query languages obtain other relations by filtering Observe's output.

**Definition — Nullify.** *[setup: req (no-content-under-`a` argument), discipline: req (single-tuple scope, retraction unit-depth), stipulation: N/A]* Nullify has four preconditions: (P0) `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)` — the caller-supplied home document for the retraction tuple itself; (P1) `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` — the target tuple's address; (P2) `|Σ.L(a)| = 3` (i.e., `a` is the address of a standard-triple link); and (P3) `a` has no strict prefix-extension in `dom(Σ.L)`:

`(A a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : a ≼ a' :: a' = a)`

(Equivalently, no other link address in `dom(Σ.L)` is a tumbler-prefix-extension of `a`.)

*Discipline assumption.* Nullify's *single-tuple-scope* behavior (below) — the property that exactly one tuple address, namely `a` itself, is contributed to `nullified(Σ')` — is conditional on the sibling-frontier emission discipline of R0a. The discipline ensures that the fresh emitter address `b` produced by Nullify's internal `Emit_R` step is prefix-incomparable with `a` at the post-state, which together with P3 at the prior state yields the single-tuple-scope conclusion. The substrate emission primitive in isolation admits emissions at strict prefix-extensions of existing link addresses, which would falsify the conclusion.

Under these preconditions and the discipline assumption, Nullify is the composition

`Nullify(Σ, d_retr, a) ≡ Emit_R(Σ, d_retr, ∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))})`

That is, emit a tuple into the retraction relation with empty from-set and a unit-depth to-span targeting `a`, with the retraction itself homed at the caller-supplied `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`. *Dependency chain — existence of the post-state Σ' with the retraction tuple:* R5 admits the unit-depth self-targeting span `(a, δ(1, #a))` as a to-set component (Stage 1's L4(c) + L13 chain, applied at `a ∈ A_rel^Σ`); R0 at `d_retr` then emits the retraction triple `(∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))}, R)` by its Step 4 invariant-preservation argument, depositing a fresh emitter address `b` with `Σ'.L(b) = (∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))}, R)` — this is the Emit_R call's substrate witness. By PrefixSpanCoverage (ASN-0043), `coverage({(a, δ(1, #a))}) = {t : a ≼ t}`, which contains `a`. Let `(Σ', _) = Nullify(Σ, d_retr, a)`. By Definition of `nullified`, `a ∈ nullified(Σ')`. By R6a, `a` remains nullified thereafter.

*Why `d_retr` is a caller parameter, not `home(a)`.* The substrate places no constraint on which `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` houses a retraction tuple: `nullified(Σ)` is computed from `L_R^Σ`'s endset coverage, which is independent of the retraction tuple's own home. An earlier formulation specialized `d_retr := home(a)` for well-definedness — `home(a)` is determined by `a`'s tumbler structure and persists in `dom(Σ.M)` by L1a + L12b — but that choice is a *convention*, not a substrate requirement: any `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` satisfies Emit_R's home precondition, and the resulting `nullified(Σ')` is the same regardless of which `d` is picked. Two reasons make explicit caller-supply the right interface. *Design-level (Nelson's link model).* Retractions are themselves authored objects: in Nelson's design, every link — including a retraction — is owned by the document of its creator, not by the document of the link it retracts. Defaulting to `home(a)` would home the retraction at the *target's* document, contradicting authorship-based home assignment. *Symmetry with `Emit_K`.* `Emit_K`'s signature already exposes the home document as an explicit parameter for the same design and implementation reasons (above); Nullify, being `Emit_R` with a designated to-span shape (R7), inherits the same interface obligation. The `home(a)` shorthand remains available as a caller-side default for the case where retractor and target genuinely share a document — e.g., a document retracting its own internal classification — but is no longer baked into the operation's signature.

*Single-tuple scope, from P3 + R0 Step 2 + R0a. [Discipline-conditional.]* The to-span's coverage `{t : a ≼ t}` is in principle the entire prefix-subtree of `a` within `T`; restricted to `A_rel^Σ = dom(Σ.L)`, however, P3 gives that the only link address with `a` as a prefix is `a` itself: by P3, `{a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : a ≼ a'} = {a}`. Since the class-(iii) `→` step taken by `Emit_R` only adds the fresh emitter address `b` (which by R0 satisfies `b ∉ dom(Σ.L)` and which is not `a` by R0 Step 2's construction yielding `b ≠ a` and by R0a's reachable-state antichain property at Σ' giving `a ⊀ b`), we have `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L) ∪ {b}` with `b` prefix-incomparable with `a`, so `{a' ∈ dom(Σ'.L) : a ≼ a'} = {a}` as well. Consequently `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^{Σ'} = {a}`, and Nullify's `→` step contributes exactly `a` to `nullified(Σ')` — never a sub-tree of `A_rel`.

*Why no content address lies under `a`.* The unrestricted coverage `{t : a ≼ t}` ranges over all of `T`, so one must also account for tumbler positions in `A_doc^{Σ'}`. Under Setup, every `c ∈ A_doc^{Σ'}` is `s_C`-resident, hence has first element-field `c.E₁ = subspace_I(c) = s_C`; the link address `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` has `a.E₁ = subspace_I(a) = s_L` (L0, SubspacePartition, ASN-0043). By T3 (CanonicalRepresentation, ASN-0034), tumblers that disagree at any element-field position are distinct, and tumbler-prefix-extension preserves the first element-field exactly (extension appends digits past `a`'s end without rewriting any prior position). So `a ≼ c` would force `s_L = a.E₁ = c.E₁ = s_C`, contradicting the subspace-distinctness axiom (`s_C ≠ s_L`). Therefore `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_doc^{Σ'} = ∅`, and the *unrestricted* intersection `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A^{Σ'} = {a}` as well — Nullify's single-tuple scope is not merely a filtering artifact of `nullified(Σ')`'s `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` predicate but a structural consequence of subspace-distinctness on the substrate's address universe.

Under the sibling-frontier discipline, P3 is automatic at every reachable state; it is stated as an explicit precondition to keep Nullify's contract usable for systems where the discipline is not yet a global guarantee.

*Crafted-span retractions.* Nothing in `Emit_R`'s definition prevents a caller from emitting a retraction with a broader-coverage to-span — e.g., `Emit_R(Σ, d', ∅, {(d, δ(1, #d))})` (for some home `d' ∈ dom(Σ.M)` of the retraction tuple itself and a document address `d` referenced in the to-span), whose coverage is `{t : d ≼ t}`, intersected with `A_rel^Σ` is *every link sited under `d`*. This is a deliberate subtree-broad retraction, not Nullify in the sense of this definition. The substrate permits it; this note's `Nullify` covers only the single-tuple form, which is the typical case for retracting individual relational assertions. Higher-layer policies that wish to authorize or forbid broader retractions can do so by predicates over the emitted `L_R` tuples (e.g., requiring the to-span's `(seed, δ)` to be a unit-depth span at a known tuple address).

The arity-3 restriction matches this note's scope. `A_K^Σ` is defined only over standard-triple links (Definition of `L_K^Σ`), so the active-subset effect of Nullify is meaningful only on arity-3 addresses. Nullifying a higher-arity address (`|Σ.L(a)| > 3`) would be a well-formed Emit_R, and would deposit `a` into `nullified(Σ')`, but no `A_K^{Σ'}` would feel the effect under the present definitions; extending the active-subset machinery to multi-arity relations `A_K^{(n),Σ}` is left to the open question on higher-arity links.

**R7a — NoExtraClassAffectsL.** *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: N/A]* No `Σ.L`-affecting transition exists outside class (iii) — i.e., for any state-affecting transition `Σ ↝ Σ'` with `Σ.L ≠ Σ'.L`, that transition is a class-(iii) `→`-step at the substrate level. The claim is categorical, ranging over any-layer operations.

*Proof.* The argument is directly from the Frame conditions on `→` (above), which are definitional: each class's frame fully specifies how that class affects `Σ.L`. Suppose for contradiction some operation `Op` takes a transition `Σ ↝ Σ'` with `Σ.L ≠ Σ'.L` and `Op` is not a class-(iii) `→`-step. *Case (a): `Op` modifies an existing entry,* i.e., `(E a ∈ dom(Σ.L) ∩ dom(Σ'.L) :: Σ'.L(a) ≠ Σ.L(a))`. Classes (i) and (ii) hold `Σ'.L = Σ.L`; class (iii) extends at a fresh key and explicitly holds `Σ'.L(a') = Σ.L(a')` for every `a' ∈ dom(Σ.L)` per the Frame, so no in-place modification is admissible. *Case (b): `Op` removes an entry or extends the domain without class (iii),* i.e., either `dom(Σ'.L) ⊊ dom(Σ.L)` or `dom(Σ'.L) ⊋ dom(Σ.L)` via a transition not of class (iii). Classes (i) and (ii) hold `Σ'.L = Σ.L` (no domain change in either direction); class (iii) extends by exactly one fresh key (no removal, and the extension *is* a class-(iii) step). No class (iv) exists, so both sub-cases are excluded. L12 and L12a are consistency consequences of the same Frame, mentioned only for cross-reference. ∎

**Definition — relational layer.** The relational layer's operations are `{Emit_K, Observe_K, Nullify}`, with Nullify a definitional alias for `Emit_R(Σ, d_retr, ∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))})` (Step 2 below unfolds it). The layer commits to `Emit_K` — the sibling-frontier-disciplined subset of the substrate emission primitive — as its sole state-affecting class-(iii) emission; the legacy label R7b names this commitment. The commitment is definitional, not derived: the substrate primitive admits broader class-(iii) deposits that the relational layer excludes by adoption. `Observe_K` is the only state-preserving operation, taking no `→`-step.

*Consequence — R7 (legacy alias *NullifyIsEmit*).* *[setup: free, discipline: N/A, stipulation: req (via the Definition above)]* Under this Definition, R7a implies that all `Σ.L`-affecting relational-layer state change is class-(iii) `→`-steps and therefore (by the layer's commitment to `Emit_K`) reduces to `Emit_K` calls; `Nullify` is `Emit_R` with three argument positions fixed (Step 2 below). The reduction's positive content rests on the Definition's commitment; the negative space ("no `Σ.L`-affecting transition outside class (iii)") rests on R7a alone.

*Proof of the reduction (Steps 1–2 are preparatory).*

(Step 1 — enumerate the relational-layer operations.) The Definition above fixes the operation set as `{Emit_K, Observe_K, Nullify}`. `Emit_K`'s signature is `Σ × dom(Σ.M) × Endset × Endset → Σ' × A_rel^{Σ'}` with the post-state satisfying `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L) ∪ {a}`; by the Frame conditions on class (iii), `Emit_K` takes a class-(iii) `→`-step. `Observe_K`'s signature is `Σ × ℘_fin(A) × ℘_fin(A) × View → ℘_fin(L_K^Σ)` and takes no `→`-step. `Nullify`'s signature is `Σ × dom(Σ.M) × A_rel^Σ → Σ' × A_rel^{Σ'}` with the post-state arising from an internal `Emit_R` step.

(Step 2 — unfold `Nullify` to `Emit_R`.) By Definition of Nullify, for any Σ, `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, and `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` satisfying P0–P3,

`Nullify(Σ, d_retr, a) ≡ Emit_R(Σ, d_retr, ∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))})`

This is `Emit_K` instantiated at three argument positions: the type slot `K` is fixed at `R`, the from-set `F` is fixed at `∅`, and the to-set `G` is fixed at the unit-depth span set `{(a, δ(1, #a))}`. The `→`-step taken by `Nullify` is the class-(iii) `→`-step of its internal `Emit_R` call. `Nullify` is a definitional shorthand for an `Emit_R` call, not a distinct primitive.

Combining: by R7a, any `Σ.L`-affecting transition is a class-(iii) `→`-step; by the Definition's commitment to `Emit_K`, every relational-layer-initiated class-(iii) step is an `Emit_K` call. The relational layer's state-affecting operation set therefore reduces to `{Emit_K}` (with `Nullify` as alias); "Update" and "mutate" do not exist *at the relational layer as committed*. The substrate itself admits broader class-(iii) emissions outside the commitment's scope (cataloged at the substrate emission primitive's definition and in Appendix B's failure modes). ∎


## Weakest-Precondition Analysis

The operations' postconditions admit explicit weakest-precondition (wp) computations in three operationally-relevant cases — Nullify's single-tuple scope, Emit_K's membership of the fresh tuple in the active subset, and the second-order Nullify of a retractor. The Nullify and Emit_K cases are non-trivial (their wp surfaces discipline-conditionality that is otherwise embedded across the prose); the second-order Nullify case is trivial-by-design and motivates R6b. All three cases use the standard wp notation `wp(S, R)`: the weakest predicate over the prior state Σ that guarantees the post-state Σ' satisfies R after S executes.

*Case 1 — wp(Nullify(Σ, d_retr, a), "single-tuple scope holds at Σ'").* The "single-tuple scope" postcondition is `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^{Σ'} = {a}` (the to-span's `A_rel`-intersection at Σ' is exactly `a`, with no other link address falling within the prefix-subtree of `a`). Working backward through Nullify's definition `Nullify(Σ, d_retr, a) ≡ Emit_R(Σ, d_retr, ∅, {(a, δ(1, #a))})`:

`wp(Nullify(Σ, d_retr, a), single-tuple scope at Σ') ≡ P0(Σ, d_retr) ∧ P1(Σ, a) ∧ P2(Σ, a) ∧ P3(Σ, a) ∧ SFD(Σ) ∧ NoCraftedSpanReachesFreshEmitter(Σ, d_retr)`

where the conjuncts are: *P0–P3* — Nullify's stated preconditions (P0: `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`; P1: `a ∈ A_rel^Σ`; P2: `|Σ.L(a)| = 3`; P3: `(A a' ∈ dom(Σ.L) : a ≼ a' :: a' = a)`). *SFD(Σ)* — the sibling-frontier emission discipline holds along the `→`-chain reaching Σ, so R0a's reachable-state antichain on `dom(Σ.L)` is in force; the conjunct discharges P3's *automaticity* and also forces the internal Emit_R's fresh emitter `b` to be prefix-incomparable with `a` at Σ' (so `b ∉ {t : a ≼ t}`). *NoCraftedSpanReachesFreshEmitter* — no `(b', F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ` has a to-span coverage that places the would-be fresh emitter `b` of the internal Emit_R in the dom-extension's nullified frontier; in regime (i) (unit-depth retraction discipline, named at Nullify's Definition above), this conjunct is vacuously true because every prior retraction is itself unit-depth and its coverage `{t : b' ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^Σ = {b'}` covers only `b'`, leaving the fresh `b` unaffected. *Strengthened form under both disciplines* — if the sibling-frontier and unit-depth retraction disciplines hold along the chain, the wp simplifies to P0 ∧ P1 ∧ P2 ∧ P3 alone; SFD and NoCraftedSpanReachesFreshEmitter become automatic and need not be checked at the call site. This is the operational form Nullify callers in the udanax-green trajectory verify.

*Case 2 — wp(Emit_K(Σ, d, F, G), "(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}").* The Definition of `Emit_K` guarantees `(a, F, G) ∈ L_K^{Σ'}` for the fresh emission unconditionally (R0's invariant-preservation argument at Step 4 places the triple into `L_K^{Σ'}` by coverage-equivalence membership). The active-subset clause `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}` adds the extra obligation `a ∉ nullified(Σ')`. Backing the postcondition through Emit_K's behavior:

`wp(Emit_K(Σ, d, F, G), (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}) ≡ d ∈ dom(Σ.M) ∧ K ∈ T_admissible ∧ SFD(Σ) ∧ NoCraftedSpanReachesD(Σ, d)`

where *SFD(Σ)* is the sibling-frontier discipline reaching Σ (so the fresh `a` lands at the discipline-permitted next sibling-frontier address, prefix-incomparable with every `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)` by R0a's antichain); and *NoCraftedSpanReachesD* — no `(b, F', G') ∈ L_R^Σ` has a to-span coverage that contains the fresh sibling-frontier address Emit_K is about to deposit under `d` (in particular, no subtree-broad retraction `Emit_R(_, _, ∅, {(d, δ(1, #d))})` or any covering span). *Regime simplification.* Under the unit-depth retraction discipline (regime (i) at Emit_K's *A_K^{Σ'} membership* note), every `L_R^Σ` tuple has unit-depth coverage `{a' : a' = b}` for some already-allocated `b ∈ dom(Σ.L)`; the discipline together with R0a's antichain at Σ' forces the fresh `a` ∉ ⋃ coverages, so `NoCraftedSpanReachesD` is automatic and the wp simplifies to `d ∈ dom(Σ.M) ∧ K ∈ T_admissible ∧ SFD(Σ)`. Under regime (ii) (crafted-span retractions admitted), the wp retains the explicit `NoCraftedSpanReachesD` conjunct — and a fresh emission whose home falls within a pre-existing crafted-span retraction's coverage will land in `L_K^{Σ'} \ A_K^{Σ'}` immediately upon allocation, breaking the postcondition.

*Case 3 — wp(Nullify(Σ, d_meta, b₁) where b₁ ∈ L_R^Σ, "a₁ remains in nullified(Σ')").* Here `b₁` is a retractor of `a₁` already in `L_R^Σ` — the second-order retraction targeted at the retractor. The postcondition `a₁ ∈ nullified(Σ')` is meant to assert that nullifying the retractor `b₁` does not "un-retract" the originally retracted `a₁`. Working backward through `nullified(Σ')`'s Definition (existential over `L_R^{Σ'}`, not `A_R^{Σ'}`):

`wp(Nullify(Σ, d_meta, b₁), a₁ ∈ nullified(Σ')) ≡ a₁ ∈ nullified(Σ)`

The wp simplifies to the prior-state predicate `a₁ ∈ nullified(Σ)` alone, with *no additional conjunct on `b₁`'s second-order retraction*. The simplification proceeds from R3 (which gives `L_R^Σ ⊆ L_R^{Σ'}`, preserving every prior retraction tuple into the post-state) together with R2 (which preserves the literal endset values of those tuples), so the witnessing retraction `(b₁, ∅, {(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))}) ∈ L_R^Σ` carries forward to `L_R^{Σ'}`, and coverage purity (R6a's proof) preserves `a₁ ∈ coverage({(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))})` invariantly. The fact that `b₁ ∈ nullified(Σ')` (i.e., `b₁ ∉ A_R^{Σ'}`) does not enter the wp — Definition of `nullified` quantifies over `L_R^{Σ'}` (the audit slice), not over the active retraction subset, so the witness's own nullified status is operationally irrelevant. *Why this case is trivial-by-design.* This trivial wp is the operational signature of R6b's single-depth retraction design: if `nullified(Σ')` instead recursed through the retraction relation (e.g., by quantifying over `A_R^{Σ'}`), the wp would have to add a conjunct ruling out the recursive un-retraction, breaking the trivial form. The Definition's quantifier-range choice (`L_R^Σ`, not `A_R^Σ`) is exactly what makes the second-order Nullify wp-trivial; relaxing that choice would impose substantial preconditions on the second-order operation. The Worked Sketch's Step 6 exhibits this concretely — at Σ_6, `b₁ ∈ nullified(Σ_6)` *and* `a₁ ∈ nullified(Σ_6)`, with the witness `(b₁, ∅, {(a₁, …)}) ∈ L_R^{Σ_6}` carrying forward by R3 even after `b₁`'s own retraction.

*Summary.* The three wp computations expose the discipline-conditional structure of `Nullify` and `Emit_K`'s postconditions explicitly: under both the sibling-frontier and unit-depth retraction disciplines, both operations have minimal-precondition wp's matching their stated signatures; relaxing either discipline introduces visible additional wp conjuncts (`SFD` and `NoCraftedSpanReachesD`/`NoCraftedSpanReachesFreshEmitter`) that callers would have to discharge per-call. The second-order Nullify's trivial-by-design wp motivates R6b's single-depth quantifier-range choice.


## Worked Sketch

We illustrate the structure of a retraction cycle in the relational vocabulary, building on the ASN-0043 worked example.

*Setup.* Let `K ∈ T_admissible` be any content-classifying type with `K ∈ T_cat^{Σ_0}`. Suppose at state `Σ_0` the substrate contains:

`L_K^{Σ_0} = {(a₁, F₁, G₁)}` &nbsp; — one classification tuple
`L_R^{Σ_0} = ∅` &nbsp; — no retractions yet

By Definition, `A_K^{Σ_0} = L_K^{Σ_0} = {(a₁, F₁, G₁)}` and `nullified(Σ_0) = ∅`. Let `d = home(a₁) ∈ dom(Σ_0.M)`, so `a₁ ∈ LS(d)` (recall `LS(d) = {a ∈ T : d ≼ a ∧ T4-valid(a) ∧ zeros(a) = 3 ∧ a.E₁ = s_L ∧ #E(a) ≥ 2}` from R0's proof). We further note that, by L1c (LinkAllocatorConformance, ASN-0043) and the T10a-chain construction in R0 Step 2 (Case B), subsequent allocations within `LS(d)` need not nest under `a₁` — the typical configuration, since allocator outputs within a document's link subtree are siblings or successors of existing link addresses under the discipline, not unconditional prefix-extensions.

*Step 1: Nullify a₁.* `Σ_0 → Σ_1` via `Nullify(Σ_0, d, a₁) = Emit_R(Σ_0, d, ∅, {(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))})` — the retractor here happens to share `a₁`'s home document, so the caller supplies `d_retr = d`; a different caller homed at `d' ∈ dom(Σ_0.M)` with `d' ≠ d` would supply `Nullify(Σ_0, d', a₁)` instead, with identical effect on `nullified(Σ_1)`. This emission's to-set `{(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))}` references the link address `a₁` — a direct instance of R5 (TupleSelfTargeting): the to-set of an `L_R` tuple refers to another link's address. R5's permission claim is exercised concretely here. We invoke not R0's bare existential — which would expose neither the home choice nor any prefix-relation constraint at its interface — but R0's *Step 2 Case B construction* applied at the caller-supplied home `d`, whose preconditions are met since `a₁ ∈ dom(Σ_0.L)` with `home(a₁) = d`. The construction (taking `b = a₁`) yields `b₁ = incⁱ(a₁, 0)` for the least `i ≥ 1` with `incⁱ(a₁, 0) ∉ dom(Σ_0.L)`. By Case B's chain extension, `home(b₁) = home(a₁) = d`, so `b₁ ∈ LS(d)`. By T10a.2 (NonNestingSiblingPrefixes, ASN-0034), `b₁` and `a₁` are distinct siblings of the allocator producing `a₁` and are therefore prefix-incomparable; in particular `a₁ ⊀ b₁`. The to-set `{(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))}` then covers `{t : a₁ ≼ t}` (by PrefixSpanCoverage, ASN-0043), which contains `a₁` and its tumbler-prefix extensions but does not contain `b₁`. The post-state has `Σ_1.L(b₁) = (∅, {(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))}, R)`. Now:

- `L_K^{Σ_1} = {(a₁, F₁, G₁)}` &nbsp; — unchanged (R3 preserves `L_K`; the emission targets `L_R`)
- `L_R^{Σ_1} = {(b₁, ∅, {(a₁, δ(1, #a₁))})}`
- `nullified(Σ_1) = {a₁}` &nbsp; — `a₁` is in the to-set's coverage
- `A_K^{Σ_1} = ∅` &nbsp; — `a₁` is excluded from the active subset

The audit predicate `(a₁, F₁, G₁) ∈ L_K` remains true forever (R3); the operational predicate `(a₁, F₁, G₁) ∈ A_K` flips to false at `Σ_1`.

*Step 2: Restore by re-emission.* To restore the classification, we do *not* attempt to nullify the retraction (which by R6b would be ineffective — single-depth checking ignores it). Instead, `Σ_1 → Σ_2` via `Emit_K(d, F₁, G₁)` — re-using the same home document `d` as `a₁` — allocating fresh `a₂ ∉ dom(Σ_1.L)` (R0) and setting `Σ_2.L(a₂) = (F₁, G₁, K)`. Now:

- `L_K^{Σ_2} = {(a₁, F₁, G₁), (a₂, F₁, G₁)}` &nbsp; — two tuples with identical content (L11b, R0)
- `nullified(Σ_2) = {a₁}` &nbsp; — unchanged (R6a; `a₂` is not targeted by any `L_R` tuple)
- `A_K^{Σ_2} = {(a₂, F₁, G₁)}` &nbsp; — the new tuple is active

The relational content `(F₁, G₁)` is again present in `A_K`, but at a different tuple address. Provenance and audit cleanly distinguish the two emissions: `a₁` is the historical record, `a₂` is the current assertion.

*Concrete instantiation.* The schematic sketch admits a fully concrete instantiation, with every address resolved to a specific tumbler and every set-theoretic claim verified by direct inspection. Fix:

Set `a₂ = 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.3` — the third emission of the depth-2 allocator `A_{a₁}`.

*L-invariant verification at the concrete `a₂`.* `a₂ = 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.3` is the second sibling-frontier Case B emission in `A_{a₁}`, structurally identical to `b₁`'s pattern in Step 1 with the enumeration index advanced to `j = 2`; L-invariants discharge identically to `b₁` above with the substitutions: L11a's freshness witness is `a₂ ∉ dom(Σ_1.L) = {a₁, b₁}` (R0 Step 2 Case B's least-`i` selection at `i = 2`); L1c's chain extends `a₁`'s witness by *two* sibling steps within `A_{a₁}`, with the chain prefix through `(d.0.2, 1)` forced by T10a's at-most-once axiom on that child-spawn pair (the same axiom invocation used at the worked example's later steps); L4(c) discharges against `F₁ = {(c₁, δ(1, 8))}` and `G₁ = {(c₂, δ(1, 8))}` (content-address targets at `c₁, c₂ ∈ A_doc^{Σ_1}`, also L4-admissible); L-fin gives `|dom(Σ_2.L)| = 3`. *Step 3 (concrete) — cross-document retraction with `d_retr ≠ home(a)`.* The schematic Step 1 placed the retraction tuple at `d_retr = home(a₁) = d` — the typical case where the retractor shares the target's home. The Nullify signature, however, admits any `d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)` (per *"Why `d_retr` is a caller parameter, not `home(a)`"* above); we exhibit a concrete instance with a *third* document `d'` distinct from `d`, retracting `a₂` from elsewhere in the substrate. Extend the worked-sketch setup with:

- `d' = 1.0.1.0.2` — a second document address, also depth-5 with zeros at positions 2 and 4 (so `zeros(d') = 2`), T4-valid; assume `d' ∈ dom(Σ_2.M)` was allocated via a class-(i) `→` step at some prior point in the construction (the precise step is orthogonal to the relational-layer behavior under exhibition here).

*Precondition checks for `Nullify(Σ_2, d', a₂)`.* The Nullify signature requires P0–P3 to hold at the call site (Definition of Nullify, above). We discharge each at Σ_2.

- *(P0) `d' ∈ dom(Σ_2.M)`* — by hypothesis above (`d'` was allocated via a prior class-(i) `→` step), `d' ∈ dom(Σ_2.M)`. ✓
- *(P1) `a₂ ∈ A_rel^{Σ_2}`* — `A_rel^{Σ_2} = dom(Σ_2.L) = {a₁, b₁, a₂}` by the Step 2 (concrete) construction; `a₂` is its third element. ✓
- *(P2) `|Σ_2.L(a₂)| = 3`* — `Σ_2.L(a₂) = (F₁, G₁, K)` by Step 2's emission, a standard triple of arity 3. ✓
- *(P3) `(A a' ∈ dom(Σ_2.L) : a₂ ≼ a' :: a' = a₂)`* — `dom(Σ_2.L) = {a₁, b₁, a₂}`. Pairwise prefix-comparison: `a₁ = 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1` and `a₂ = 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.3` are siblings in the depth-2 allocator `A_{a₁}` (both with `home = d`, differing only at position 8); by T10a.2 (NonNestingSiblingPrefixes, ASN-0034) they are prefix-incomparable, so `a₂ ⊀ a₁` and `a₁ ⊀ a₂`. Therefore no element of `dom(Σ_2.L) \ {a₂}` strictly prefix-extends `a₂`, and `a₂ ≼ a₂` by reflexivity is the only solution. ✓

All four preconditions discharge. We invoke Nullify:

`Σ_2 → Σ_3` via `Nullify(Σ_2, d', a₂) = Emit_R(Σ_2, d', ∅, {(a₂, δ(1, 8))})`. The retraction tuple is homed at `d' ≠ d = home(a₂)`. R0 Step 2 Case A applies at `d'` (since `{a'' ∈ dom(Σ_2.L) : home(a'') = d'} = ∅` — no prior link is homed under `d'`), yielding the Case A base `b₂ = d'.0.s_L.1 = 1.0.1.0.2.0.2.1`. Verification: `zeros(b₂) = 3`, `E(b₂) = [2, 1]`, `b₂.E₁ = 2 = s_L`, `#E(b₂) = 2`, `home(b₂) = 1.0.1.0.2 = d'`, T4-valid; `b₂ ∈ LS(d')`. Crucially, `b₂` is sited under a *different* depth-1 allocator `A_{d'.0.1}` than `a₁`, `b₁`, `a₂` (which sit under `A_{d.0.1}`'s allocator-tree-depth-2 link allocator `A_{a₁}`); the prefix-incomparability of `b₂` with `{a₁, b₁, a₂}` is structural — `b₂` shares `d`'s and `d'`'s first four positions (`1.0.1.0`) but diverges at position 5 (`1` for `d`-rooted addresses, `2` for `d'`-rooted). By Prefix (PrefixRelation, ASN-0034) — `s ≼ t` iff `#s ≤ #t` and `s` agrees with `t` pointwise on positions `1..#s` — neither direction of the prefix relation holds between `b₂` and any of `a₁`, `b₁`, `a₂`: all four are length-8 tumblers, so prefixhood in either direction would require pointwise agreement at all 8 positions; but the equal-length pairs disagree at position 5 (`b₂[5] = 2`, while `a₁[5] = b₁[5] = a₂[5] = 1`), so the pointwise-agreement requirement fails and the relation does not hold either way. Hence `b₂` is prefix-incomparable with each of `{a₁, b₁, a₂}`.

## Open Questions

- What invariants must hold between `L_K` and the arrangements `Σ.M` when relational predicates depend on whether the from-set or to-set content is currently visible in some document?
- Should multi-arity links (`|Σ.L(a)| > 3`) define multiple binary projections, or be regarded directly as elements of higher-arity typed relations `L_K^{(n)} ⊆ A_rel × ℘(A)^n`?
- Under what conditions is `Nullify(b)` for `b ∈ L_R` operationally meaningful, given that R6b makes single-depth checking ignore the second-order retraction?
- What ordering, if any, must the substrate guarantee on Observe results — by emission cycle, by tuple address, or unordered as set semantics suggest?
- Must Emit be atomic with respect to concurrent Observe, and if so, what is the consistency model under which `A_K` transitions are observed?
- What guarantees does the substrate provide about the cardinality bound of `nullified(Σ)` relative to `dom(Σ.L)` — is unbounded retraction permitted, or must some structural ratio hold?
- Should the sibling-frontier discipline on which R0a is conditional be elevated to a substrate-level guarantee — e.g., by tightening Emit_K's specification to require prefix-incomparability with `dom(Σ.L)` as a postcondition, or by tightening the substrate emission primitive to forbid emissions at strict prefix-extensions of existing link addresses? Either tightening would make R0a unconditional and would discharge Nullify's P3 precondition (above) automatically; the design tradeoff is between substrate primitiveness and the structural guarantees the primitive can deliver without auxiliary implementation contracts.
- Should the sibling-frontier discipline be relaxed to admit deeper-sited link addresses (`#E ≥ 3`), aligning with Nelson's foundational design intent — in which the address space admits depth-N link addresses as a general principle of recursive sub-link allocation? R0a-Cor2 records the present narrowing: under the discipline, every link address sits at `#E = 2` strictly, matching the udanax-green implementation (`findisatoinsertmolecule` never produces depth-3 link addresses) but narrower than L1b's `#E ≥ 2` admission. A relaxed discipline would have to (a) redefine R0a's sibling-stream invariant over a tree of allocators (depth-2 base links spawning depth-3 sub-allocators, and so on) rather than a single sibling stream, (b) re-derive R0a's antichain conclusion within each sub-stream rather than substrate-wide, and (c) re-derive Nullify's single-tuple-scope guarantee under the broader prefix-incomparability checks the tree structure entails — a depth-2 link `a₁` would no longer be guaranteed prefix-incomparable with its depth-3 children, so P3 at `a₁` would no longer suffice to confine `nullified(Σ')` to `{a₁}` even within the disciplined regime. The design tradeoff is between operational tractability (single sibling stream, uniform depth, simple antichain) and design expressiveness (depth-N sub-links as the recursive address space natively admits).
- Under L14's native scoped form — i.e., dropping the globally `s_C`-resident-content Setup hypothesis stated at the head of the note — what is the precise slice-wise statement of R0, R4, and R5 on the `s_C`-resident content slice, and what auxiliary preservation argument is required to lift each across the slice boundary? Concretely: (a) R0's Step 4 L14a-preservation step is currently discharged against `dom(Σ.C)` as a whole via Setup; the slice-wise reformulation would have to discharge it against `dom(Σ.C) ∩ {a : subspace_I(a) = s_C}` and establish that emissions outside the slice cannot break L14a within it. (b) R4's `A_doc^Σ ∩ A_rel^Σ = ∅` would need to be restated as a disjointness between `A_rel^Σ` and the `s_C`-resident sub-universe `A_doc^{Σ, s_C}`, with non-`s_C`-resident content addresses occupying a separate sub-universe whose disjointness from `A_rel^Σ` is a separate question (and would require an additional subspace-distinctness obligation per such alternative subspace). (c) R5's Stage-1 appeal to R0's emission construction inherits R0's slice-wise reformulation; whether the to-set admissibility argument depends on the slice restriction at any point is a question for the reformulated proof. The interaction with R0a is also worth tracing: R0a is Setup-free, so its slice-wise statement is identical to its global statement, but R0's slice-wise reformulation may change which addresses are reachable as `a' = home(a').0.s_L.1` bases in the antichain corollary.
- Can higher layers extend the type catalog `T_cat` dynamically without coordination, given L9 (TypeGhostPermission), and what happens when two layers independently choose colliding type addresses?


## Appendix A — Substrate-level commentary on `→`

### A.1 — Coarsening of ASN-0034's transition relation

ASN-0086's `→` is the equivalence-class projection of ASN-0034's `→_{0034}` under the equivalence "compose all maximal sequences of T1 (sibling-increment) and T2 (child-spawn) ASN-0034-transitions that have no exposed effect on the substrate-level abstract values `(dom(Σ.C), dom(Σ.M), dom(Σ.L))`". Equivalently, an ASN-0034 sequence collapses to a single ASN-0086-`→` step iff exactly one of `dom(Σ.C)`, `dom(Σ.M)`, `dom(Σ.L)` extends by exactly one fresh key across the sequence and the other two are held identical; sequences with no such effect (pure allocator activation, e.g., a child-spawn that prepositions an allocator without depositing) collapse into the *same* equivalence class as the surrounding state, becoming `→`-stutter rather than a distinct `→`-step. The L1c witness chain traversed by R0 Step 2 — `(d, 2)` followed by `s_L − 1` sibling sweeps followed by `(d.0.s_L, 1)` — is one such collapsed sequence: its only exposed effect on `(dom(Σ.C), dom(Σ.M), dom(Σ.L))` is the single fresh `dom(Σ.L)` extension at `a`, and ASN-0086 projects it to one `→`-step.

*This coarsening is one admissible choice, not the unique substrate-level abstraction over ASN-0034.* A coarser abstraction — e.g., one that also fuses adjacent class-(i)/(ii)/(iii) emissions whenever a higher layer treats their composition as atomic — would also be conformant in the sense of preserving every R-claim in this note (the R-claims quantify over `→`-states, and any coarsening that respects the frame conditions of (i)/(ii)/(iii) inherits the claims). A *finer* abstraction — e.g., one that exposes per-allocator activation transitions distinct from class (iii)'s deposit — would require additional invariant-preservation arguments at the newly visible intermediate states and is not within the scope of this note's R-claims as stated (R0's existence claim, in particular, would need a finer-grained witness construction that exhibits an intermediate allocator-activation step before the deposit step). The choice taken here — collapse all allocator-state-only sequences into stutter, and treat each `(dom(Σ.C) ∪ dom(Σ.M) ∪ dom(Σ.L))`-extending op as one `→`-step — is the abstraction at which `Emit_K`, `Observe`, and `Nullify` factor cleanly as relational-layer primitives. Implementations that expose intermediate allocator-state transitions remain conforming to ASN-0086 provided that their externally-observable `(dom(Σ.C), dom(Σ.M), dom(Σ.L))`-extending steps are in bijection with this note's `→`-steps; the udanax-green substrate is one such implementation (its `findisatoinsertmolecule` performs allocator state-update and link-store deposit within a single externally atomic action, satisfying the bijection trivially).

## Appendix B — Failure modes: necessity of the sibling-frontier discipline

The R0a hypothesis is *necessary*, not just sufficient: a single non-disciplinary class-(iii) transition permanently breaks the antichain, and any operation that relies on R0a (notably Nullify) loses its substrate guarantee from that point onward. We state the failure modes explicitly.

*(a) One non-disciplinary class-(iii) transition is sufficient to break the antichain permanently.* Suppose at some reachable Σ the prior `→`-chain has respected the discipline (so `dom(Σ.L)` is an antichain by R0a applied to the prefix). Suppose Σ → Σ' is a class-(iii) transition that invokes the substrate emission primitive at a strict prefix-extension of some `a₁ ∈ dom(Σ.L)` — concretely, at `a' = a₁.1` (admissible by the substrate primitive per "Breadth of the primitive vs. the discipline R0a names" in the main body, which records the L1c witness chain — extension of `a₁`'s chain by one child-spawn `(a₁, 1)`, `k' = 1` having no zeros constraint — and verifies L0/L1/L1a/L1b at `a'`). *Concrete instantiation, building on the Worked Sketch.* With the Worked Sketch's values (`s_L = 2`, `d = 1.0.1.0.1`, `a₁ = 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1`), the non-disciplinary deposit lands at `a' = a₁.1 = 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1.1`. The same substrate-primitive checks instantiate concretely: `a' ∉ dom(Σ.L)` (fresh — the worked sketch's `dom(Σ.L)` contains `a₁` only, and `a' ≠ a₁` by their length difference 8 vs. 9); `a'.E₁ = 2 = s_L`; `zeros(a') = 3` at positions 2, 4, 6 (appending the non-zero digit `1` adds no new zero); `home(a') = 1.0.1.0.1 = d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` (the prefix ending just before `a'`'s third zero, of length 5); `#E(a') = 3 ≥ 2` (element field `E(a') = [2, 1, 1]`). The substrate primitive accepts this deposit; the discipline forbids it (R0 Step 2 Case B at seed `a₁` would yield `incⁱ(a₁, 0)` — a sibling, not a child — never the prefix-extension `a₁.1`). Then `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L) ∪ {a'} = {a₁, a'} = {1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1, 1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1.1}`, which contains the pair `(a₁, a')` with `a₁ ≼ a'` (`a'` extends `a₁` by one digit) and `a₁ ≠ a'`; the antichain conclusion fails at Σ'. By L12 (LinkImmutability) and L12a (LinkStoreMonotonicity), `{a₁, a'} ⊆ dom(Σ'.L) ⊆ dom(Σ''.L)` for every Σ'' reachable from Σ' via any further `→`-chain — the substrate exposes no removal transition (Frame conditions on `→` in the main body), so the bad pair persists in `dom(Σ''.L)` forever. The antichain failure is therefore *permanent*: no subsequent disciplinary behavior can repair it. Consequently the sibling-stream invariant fails at Σ' (the new `a' = a₁.1` cannot be expressed as `incʲ(home(a').0.s_L.1, 0)` for any `j`, since the sibling stream at depth 2 enumerates extensions of `home(a').0.s_L`, not extensions of `a₁`), and the discipline hypothesis of R0a is *never again* satisfied along any `→`-chain extending Σ'.

*(b) Nullify's single-tuple-scope fails immediately at any post-Σ state where the discipline is broken, regardless of which `a` is being nullified.* Let Σ' be a state reached by an `→`-chain with at least one non-disciplinary class-(iii) step (so by (a), some pair `a₁ ≼ a'` with `a₁ ≠ a'` sits in `dom(Σ'.L)`). Consider `Nullify(Σ', d_retr, a)` for any `a ∈ A_rel^{Σ'}`. Two failure cases arise. *Case (b.i) — nullifying the broken party at `a₁`:* if `a = a₁`, P3 fails at Σ' directly — `{a'' ∈ dom(Σ'.L) : a₁ ≼ a''} ⊇ {a₁, a'}` with `a' ≠ a₁` — so Nullify's precondition is unmet at the call site. *Case (b.ii) — nullifying an unrelated `a` ≠ a₁`:* P3 may hold for `a` at Σ' (if `a` is prefix-incomparable with every other link), so the call is admissible; but Nullify's internal `Emit_R` step invokes R0 Step 2 Case B with seed `b ∈ dom(Σ'.L)` of matching home. *If* the seed chosen is `b = a₁` and Case B's least-`i` selection yields `incⁱ(a₁, 0)` that happens to coincide with no existing link, the freshness witness goes through; but in general the broken pair `{a₁, a'}` perturbs the sibling-stream invariant for *all* links homed at `home(a₁)`, and R0a's post-state antichain conclusion (which the single-tuple-scope argument in the main body relies on) is no longer available at Σ'. The argument that the fresh emitter address `b` is prefix-incomparable with `a` no longer follows from R0a, so single-tuple-scope is not guaranteed even when P3 holds at the call's target. In short, the discipline failure at any point along the chain compromises Nullify's substrate guarantee at every subsequent state, regardless of whether the call's target is the directly broken party.

*Operational summary.* Partial discipline (early-disciplined, late-non-disciplined) gives no graceful degradation: R0a's antichain and Nullify's single-tuple-scope are *both* guaranteed everywhere on disciplinary chains, and *neither* is guaranteed at any state reached by a chain containing one or more non-disciplinary steps. Implementations whose substrate primitive does not enforce the discipline must therefore carry an external invariant — typically at the `Emit_K` interface, as udanax-green does via `findisatoinsertmolecule`'s sibling-frontier behavior — to keep R0a and Nullify usable.
