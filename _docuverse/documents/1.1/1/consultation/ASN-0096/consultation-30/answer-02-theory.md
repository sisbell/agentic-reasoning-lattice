## Question 2 [theory]

> Was the type endset designed as an annotation (where visibility is not part of the link's utility) while content endsets F/G are endpoints whose visibility constitutes the link's utility — and does a content endset whose coverage happens to consist entirely of ghost addresses still count as an endpoint for vitality purposes?

# Type Endset as Annotation; F/G as Endpoints

## Part 1: Yes — the type endset was designed as annotation

Nelson is explicit that the type endset's utility comes from address identity, not from anything visible at that address:

> "What the 'type' designation points to is completely arbitrary. This is because of the way we will be searching for links. The search mechanism does not actually look at what is stored under the 'type' it is searching for; it merely considers the type's address." (4/44–4/45)

> "Indeed, there is no need for the presence of elements at the addresses specified. Link types may be ghost elements." (4/45)

The type *is* its address. Visibility there contributes nothing to the link's purpose; the type endset is a categorizing mark — annotation by address-coincidence.

## Part 2: Yes — F/G are endpoints whose content matters

The from-set and to-set are framed as actually connecting bytes:

> "A Xanadu link is not between points, but between spans of data. Thus we may visualize it as a strap between bytes." (4/42)

And survivability is keyed to whether content remains *at each end*:

> "Links between bytes can survive deletions, insertions and rearrangements, **if anything is left at each end**." (4/43)

That "if anything is left at each end" rule treats F/G as valued for what they reach — the content endpoints constitute the link's connective utility, not just its searchable signature.

## Part 3: Ghost-only F/G coverage and vitality — partial answer

Nelson does permit F/G to point at ghost addresses, but the case he discusses is *hierarchical*:

> "It is possible to link to a node, or an account, even though there is nothing stored in the docuverse corresponding to them... these elements are virtually present in tumbler-space, since links may be made to them which embrace all the contents below them... A link to or search of an account or node will find any of the documents under it." (4/23)

So an F/G whose stated coverage is a bare node-address still functions as an endpoint **because it embraces descendant content** — vitality flows from the subordinate documents, not from the ghost address itself.

The narrower case in the question — an F/G whose ghost coverage has **no** descendants and never did, so there is no content beneath to embrace — is not directly addressed in the curated sources. The "if anything is left at each end" phrasing reads as a *deletion-survival* rule (presupposing content was there) rather than a categorical statement about initial-creation. The symmetry pitch on 4/44 ("symmetrical with the other endsets") could be read either as collapsing the type/content distinction or merely as describing structural uniformity while leaving the functional asymmetry intact.
