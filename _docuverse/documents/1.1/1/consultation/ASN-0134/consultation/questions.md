# Sub-Questions — Substrate Consistency and Isolation Model

**Inquiry:** The substrate's transition relation is sequential — atomic steps in a single total order (ASN-0047), with the operation surface Emit_K / Nullify_Binary / Observe_K defined step-by-step (ASN-0128). But the coordination layers built on it assume many agents act at once — predicates are evaluated by any observer (ASN-0129), quiescence must be recognizable while writers may still be active (ASN-0133 Q0), a definition's content run must be allocated contiguously even as other agents allocate (ASN-0130), and the runner that produced this corpus runs many workers against one store. What consistency does the substrate actually guarantee when concurrent agents touch the operation surface? What is the unit of atomicity — a single →_sh step (the gated transition relation, ASN-0126), a typed emission, or a whole fire/batch — and what does an Observe_K see relative to an Emit_K or Nullify_Binary that is in flight — only committed state, or can it witness a partial effect? How do independently-proposed operations become positions in the one sequential order — is there a linearization point, is ordering per-home-independent or globally serialized, and may two agents allocating in the same home or writing the same registry interleave their effects? Which of the substrate's invariants — dense contiguous allocation chains (ASN-0093), unique addresses, append-only monotonicity, the audit/active-slice coincidence (ASN-0086) — are preserved by the model itself versus only by the serialization, and what is the minimal contract an implementation must honor so that a reader never observes a torn or duplicated effect and a writer never collides with a concurrent writer's allocation? What does a quiescence verdict (ASN-0133 Q0) mean if it is read while a write is in flight — is the verdict taken at a consistent snapshot, and what relationship must hold between the observer's read and the writers' linearization for the verdict to be sound rather than a race?

1. [theory] When two users act on the same shared store at the same moment, what is the smallest unit of change that must take effect all-at-once — so that no reader ever sees half of one person's edit?
2. [theory] If one user is reading content while another is writing it, must the reader see only changes that have fully landed, or may the reader witness a change still in progress?
3. [theory] When many independent edits are proposed at once, what does the design guarantee about the single order in which they ultimately count — does every observer agree on that one order?
4. [theory] Must edits to two unrelated documents be free to take effect independently, or does the design require even unrelated changes to fall into one shared sequence?
5. [theory] When two users each try to give new content its own permanent identity at the same time, what guarantees that no two pieces of content ever end up sharing one identity?
6. [theory] If two users add content to the very same document at the same instant, what must the design promise about whether their additions can be tangled together or must remain cleanly separated?
7. [theory] Which permanence guarantees — that content once placed is never moved or overwritten — hold purely from the nature of the operations themselves, regardless of how simultaneous actions are reconciled?
8. [theory] When a writer's change and a reader's view of the same shared content meet, what must hold so the reader never sees that change counted twice nor counted as a broken fragment?
9. [theory] If someone asks "is everyone done editing?" while another user is mid-edit, what must that answer be taken against — a single frozen view of the store — for the answer to be trustworthy rather than accidental?
10. [theory] What is the minimal promise the design demands of any faithful realization, so that a writer's act of claiming space for new content can never silently overwrite space another writer claimed at the same time?

20. [evidence] Is ordering globally serialized by the one event loop with no per-document independence, or could the design support per-home-document independent commit — and does any code path assume a single total order over operations touching distinct POOMs?
