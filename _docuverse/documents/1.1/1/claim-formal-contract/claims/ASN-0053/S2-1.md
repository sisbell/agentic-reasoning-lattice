**S2** (*EmptyDistinction*). The empty set of positions is not the denotation of any span. Every well-formed span denotes a non-empty set.

We are proving that the denotation map from spans to sets of positions never produces the empty set: for every well-formed span, the set of positions it denotes contains at least one element. Equivalently, ∅ has no preimage under denotation — there is no span whose meaning is "no positions."

Fix a well-formed span. By construction it is given by a start position s together with a length ℓ, and it denotes the half-open interval of positions

  ⟦s, ℓ⟧ = [s, s ⊕ ℓ) = { p : s ≤ p < s ⊕ ℓ },

where ⊕ advances a position by a length. Well-formedness imposes two conditions on this data: by TA-strict the length is strictly positive, ℓ > 0; and the span fits within the stream, k ≤ #s, where k is the end offset s ⊕ ℓ.

The argument has a single step. Because ℓ > 0 and k ≤ #s, T12 gives strict monotonicity of advancement, namely s ⊕ ℓ > s. Hence the lower endpoint s satisfies both s ≤ s (trivially) and s < s ⊕ ℓ (just shown), so s lies in the half-open interval:

  s ∈ [s, s ⊕ ℓ).

Therefore the denotation contains at least the position s, and so ⟦s, ℓ⟧ ≠ ∅. Since the span was an arbitrary well-formed span, no well-formed span denotes ∅. ∎

This is the distinction that matters downstream: when two disjoint spans are intersected, the result is "no span" — the empty set, which by the above is the denotation of no span at all — rather than a zero-width span. A zero-width span would require ℓ = 0, which TA-strict forbids; the empty intersection therefore falls outside the image of denotation entirely.
