Proves that any two spans stand in exactly one of five mutually exclusive relationships — separated, adjacent, proper overlap, containment, or equal — determined entirely by comparing their start and reach boundary points under the total order T1. The disjoint cases (separated, adjacent) are those where the position sets share nothing; the overlapping cases (proper overlap, containment, equal) are those where the intersection is nonempty. Exhaustiveness follows by a case split on the four boundary values, with symmetry eliminating the need to treat α and β separately.
