**S7a (DocumentScopedAllocation).** Every Istream address is allocated under the tumbler prefix of the document that created it. That is, for every `a ∈ dom(Σ.C)`, the document-level prefix of `a` — the tumbler `N(a).0.U(a).0.D(a)` obtained by truncating the element field, where `N(a)`, `U(a)`, `D(a)` are the partial projections supplied by T4b (UniqueParse, ASN-0034) — identifies the document whose owner performed the allocation that placed `a` into `dom(C)`.

This allocation is an event of T10a's discipline (ASN-0034), not an informal act: the document owner baptises element addresses through an *element allocator* `A_element` — a node of the global allocator tree `𝒯`, spawned under the document's own allocator by T10a's child-spawning rule — whose domain holds exactly the element addresses issued under that document's prefix. Consequently the element allocator is bound *per address*, the existential nested inside the universal: for every `a ∈ dom(Σ.C)` there exists a T10a-conforming element allocator `A_element` — the one spawned under `a`'s own home document — exhibiting two memberships of distinct types, `A_element ∈ 𝒯`, the element allocator being a node of the tree, and `a ∈ dom(A_element)`, the address lying in that node's domain — the element-level counterpart of S7d's document-level allocation assertion. Symbolically this is `(A a ∈ dom(Σ.C) :: (E A_element ∈ 𝒯 :: a ∈ dom(A_element)))`, each address resolved by the allocator of *its* home document. The order of binding is essential: the reversed nesting `(E A_element ∈ 𝒯 :: (A a ∈ dom(Σ.C) :: a ∈ dom(A_element)))` — a single allocator whose one domain would hold every stored address — is not what is meant, and would be false the moment content is allocated under two documents, since each `dom(A_element)` holds *exactly* the element addresses issued under one document's prefix and so cannot contain another document's.

These two memberships are precisely what secures the well-definedness of the projections the prefix `N(a).0.U(a).0.D(a)` names — for T4b's four partial functions are not total, and we may not write `U(a)` or `D(a)` until we have placed `a` in their domains. T4b's four projections share `dom(N)`, the T4-valid subset of `T`, as the outer domain from which every field's presence is carved, and T4b's postcondition warns explicitly that outside that subdomain none of `N, U, D, E` is assigned a value: any consumer applying `U` or `D` must carry T4-validity as a precondition. We discharge it from the allocation discipline itself. T10a.4 (T4PreservationUnderDiscipline, ASN-0034) asserts that for every allocator `A ∈ 𝒯` and every `t ∈ dom(A)`, `t` is T4-valid; instantiating it at `A := A_element` and `t := a` — licensed by exactly the two memberships `A_element ∈ 𝒯` and `a ∈ dom(A_element)` just established — places `a ∈ dom(N)`. The element-level projections `U` and `D` impose a second domain condition beyond T4-validity, the zero-count thresholds `dom(U) = {t ∈ dom(N) : zeros(t) ≥ 1}` and `dom(D) = {t ∈ dom(N) : zeros(t) ≥ 2}`; this S7b (ElementLevelIAddresses) supplies, its axiom `zeros(a) = 3` for every `a ∈ dom(Σ.C)` giving `zeros(a) ≥ 2` and so placing `a` in `dom(U)` and `dom(D)` together. Both conditions T4b imposes on a consumer of `U` and `D` are thus met, and `N(a)`, `U(a)`, `D(a)` are well-defined at every `a ∈ dom(Σ.C)`.

Nelson's baptism principle establishes it: "The owner of a given item controls the allocation of the numbers under it." A document owner baptises element addresses under that document's prefix, so the home document is ascertainable from the address alone.
