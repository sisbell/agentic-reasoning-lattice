Proves that every I-address structurally encodes the tumbler of the document that allocated it: the origin function, obtained by truncating the element field to its three-field document-level prefix, is well-defined, uniquely discriminates allocating documents, and is invariant across all system states in which the address exists. Because attribution is computed from the address components alone — not from any mutable annotation or registry — it is unseverable: when content is transcluded into a foreign document, origin still traces to the creator, making the two-stream separation between where content is read and where it was allocated structurally explicit.
