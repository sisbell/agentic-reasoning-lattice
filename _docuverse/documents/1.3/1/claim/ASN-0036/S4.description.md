Proves that two independently written I-addresses are always distinct, even when their stored values are identical — fresh allocation always produces a new address, never reuses an existing one. This guarantees that address equality is the structural test for shared content: when two document positions map to the same I-address, that is transclusion (common origin); when they map to different I-addresses with equal values, that is coincidence. The content store is value-oblivious; identity lives in the address, not the content.
