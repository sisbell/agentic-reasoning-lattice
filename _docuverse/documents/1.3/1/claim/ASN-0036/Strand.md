**Strand (StrandDefinition).** A conventional system merges content and arrangement — "the file" IS the content IS the arrangement. Editing overwrites; saving destroys the prior state. Nelson rejected this explicitly: "Virtually all of computerdom is built around the destructive replacement of successive whole copies of each current version." The two-component model is his alternative: an edit modifies the arrangement `Σ.M(d)` while the content store `Σ.C` remains invariant. The separation of the two components is the premise of the model; the invariants the remainder of this ASN derives are the conditions that separation must satisfy.

We call this paired state the *strand*: the two-component object `(Σ.C, Σ.M)` — an immutable content store `Σ.C` woven together with the family of mutable arrangements `Σ.M(d)` that reference it. Every invariant in this ASN is a statement about a strand: some constrain its content component, others its arrangement component, and the structural-attribution results connect the two.

*Formal Contract:*
- *Definition:* The *strand* is the pair `(Σ.C, Σ.M)`, where `Σ.C : T ⇀ Val` is the content store — a partial function from Istream addresses to content values — and `Σ.M` maps each document `d` to its arrangement `Σ.M(d) : T ⇀ T`, a partial function from Vstream positions to Istream addresses. The strand is the combined state object on which the invariants of this ASN are imposed: its content component is held immutable across state transitions while its arrangement component is freely mutable. The definition introduces no constraint of its own beyond pairing an immutable content store with a mutable family of arrangements.
