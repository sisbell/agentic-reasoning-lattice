**Bridge2 (Genesis Coverage — forward requirement on activation discipline).** Every address inhabiting an activated allocator's domain at system genesis is a seed element of the baptismal registry:

  `allocated(Σ_init) ⊆ B₀`

This is not a consequence of B₀ conf., which stipulates only finiteness, contiguity, and T4 for the seed; nor is it a consequence of ASN-0034 in isolation, which fixes `allocated(s₀)` but does not name B₀. The activation-discipline ASN must arrange the alignment by constraining which allocators are activated at genesis and how their initial domains relate to the seed set chosen for ASN-0040.

Under Bridge1 and Bridge2, the inclusion `allocated(Σ) ⊆ Σ.B` is preserved at every transition. ASN-0034's non-extending transitions leave `allocated` fixed; allocator extensions, by Bridge1, are baptismal operations that add the same element to both sets; B0a forbids any non-baptismal enlargement of Σ.B, so Σ.B never grows in a way that would let `allocated` outpace it. Iterating across a finite transition sequence from genesis, the registry side of the inclusion is non-contracting by B0★ (Multi-step Irrevocability) and the allocator side is non-contracting by T8 (AllocationPermanence) extended along the same sequence; both directions remain monotone, so the per-transition preservation argument lifts to every reachable state. By induction from genesis (with Bridge2 securing the base case `allocated(Σ_init) ⊆ Σ_init.B`, since `B₀ ⊆ Σ_init.B`), `allocated(Σ) ⊆ Σ.B` in every reachable state. Without either bridge requirement, the inclusion is unjustified.

The reverse inclusion `Σ.B ⊆ allocated(Σ)` requires that every baptized address have a corresponding activated allocator, which in turn requires that the parent prerequisite — must a parent position be baptized before children can be baptized beneath it — be enforced. That question is deferred to the Open Questions. Under enforcement of the parent prerequisite (and the bridge above) the two sets coincide and the names become interchangeable across the two ASNs; without that enforcement, Σ.B may admit addresses whose parents lack an activated allocator and the reverse inclusion fails.

The lenses differ in emphasis regardless of which inclusion is in force: ASN-0034 decomposes the allocated set by allocator (each A contributes domₛ(A)), making per-allocator forward-ordering visible; ASN-0040 treats the set as monolithic, making per-namespace contiguity (B1) and cross-namespace disjointness (B7) visible. The seed-set inclusion `B₀ ⊆ Σ_init.B` is the requirement that every seed address be admitted to the registry at genesis, consistent with ASN-0034's `allocated(s₀) = {t₀}` extended to a non-singleton root configuration by B₀ conf.; whether each seed simultaneously inhabits some `domₛ(A)` at Σ_init is a question of the activation discipline rather than of the registry definition.

We state B0 (Irrevocability) below as a stand-alone obligation rather than as a corollary of T8 (AllocationPermanence). The corollary route via T8 would chain three inclusions: (i) `Σ.B ⊆ allocated(Σ)` — the conditional reverse inclusion above, requiring parent-prerequisite enforcement; (ii) `allocated(Σ) ⊆ allocated(Σ')` — T8 directly; and (iii) `allocated(Σ') ⊆ Σ'.B` — the forward inclusion above, requiring the cross-ASN bridge axiom. Composing the three yields `Σ.B ⊆ Σ'.B`. Two of the three links — (i) and (iii) — rest on assumptions outside ASN-0034, so the corollary derivation is not unconditional even when T8 holds.

A second corollary route is internal to ASN-0040: B0a unconditionally implies B0. B0a's partition of Op forces `op(Σ).B = Σ.B ∪ {next(Σ.B, p, d)}` in the baptismal branch and `op(Σ).B = Σ.B` in the Σ.B-frame branch, so `Σ.B ⊆ op(Σ).B` holds in both — and therefore `Σ.B ⊆ Σ'.B` for every transition Σ → Σ', which is exactly B0. We list B0 as a primitive label rather than as a labelled corollary of B0a for two reasons. First, B0 is the Kripke-style monotonicity statement on Σ.B alone — the form cited by the inductive proofs of B1 and B10 and by textual references throughout this ASN; chaining each citation through B0a's partition would re-derive the monotonicity step at every use site and obscure it. Second, B0 is the registry analogue of T8 in ASN-0034 and is best presented with the same labelled primitive status, so that cross-ASN comparisons (registry vs. allocator monotonicity) can be drawn term-for-term. The Properties Introduced table accordingly records B0 as derivable from B0a within ASN-0040 but retained as a primitive label for proof legibility.
