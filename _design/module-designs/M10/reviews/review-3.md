Reviewing M10 against the decomposition, the engine-composition contract, ASN-0134, and the M1–M8 interfaces as given. The design is careful and mostly faithful (A7 falls out of call order, clause-6 by one-snapshot-per-read, the editlink read/write asymmetry honors both upstream signatures, the no-`Clone` idempotency cache, the Lampson-hint framing of disposition). Every dispatch arm I checked typechecks against the upstream signatures, the op→note coverage is complete, and the boundary (no per-store logic, M9 parallel, latent composite) is held. One load-bearing problem remains.

## Revision list

1. **[DEFECT] The `Stores` factory resolution (Conflicts-resolved #6) is internally inconsistent and leaves the write path unwireable.** The design asserts M3/M5/M7 keep `Namespace::new`/`Vstream::new`/`LinkStore::new` *unpublished with private fields* — "so M10 cannot construct them" — then hands construction to "the binary/engine, which owns the handles' construction." But under Rust's crate model the binary (`binary → skep-engine → store crates`) is an *external dependent* of each store crate exactly as M10 (`skep-operation`) is; it has no construction privilege M10 lacks. So if those constructors are genuinely private, **the binary cannot implement `Stores` either**, and the entire write path is unbuildable; if they are public (the digests are merely silent on construction), then M10's stated justification is false and it could call them directly. The factory pattern is fine, but its premise is self-undermining. Fix: state as an explicit **build precondition** that M3/M5/M7 must expose engine-facing constructors for their transact-driving handles (e.g. `Namespace::new(Arc<Kernel<W>>)`, `Vstream::new(&Kernel<W>)`, `LinkStore::new(&Kernel<W>, Arc<TypeRegistry>)`) — absent from their dependent-facing interfaces — and re-justify the injected `Stores` by *decoupling/testability*, not by "M10 cannot construct them." As written, the factory's sole implementor cannot be written.

2. **[SHARPENING] `FaultSite` drops the offending document `Address` that the upstream errors deliberately carry.** `RetrieveError::DocNotRegistered(Address)`, `DeletionsError::DocNotRegistered(Address)`, `CompareError::DocNotRegistered(Address)`, and `FindError::DocNotRegistered(Address)` all ride on **multi-document** ops (RetrieveV's spec-set, ShowDeletions' two docs, Compare/FindDocsContaining's region-sets), so lowering them to a bare `RejectCode::DocNotRegistered` with an empty `FaultSite` leaves the client unable to tell *which* document was unregistered — the exact thing the upstream variant exists to localize. Add `addr: Option<Address>` to `FaultSite` (or populate `detail`) and carry it in those variants' `lower`, matching the operand/region/index localization the table already threads for COMPARE.

3. **[SHARPENING] The disposition table is inconsistent on the "unregistered-vs-wrong-tier" conflated codes.** `SourceNotRegistered` is classed `Reorder` (optimistic), yet `NotAnAccount` — which M3 documents as "covers both unregistered AND non-account," the identical conflation — falls through to `Permanent` by *silent default* and is never named alongside the explicitly-conservative `NotArranged`/`fork`-`NotOwner` cases. Either class `NotAnAccount` `Reorder` to match `SourceNotRegistered`, or list it among the conservatively-`Permanent` codes; either way name it in §5 so every conflated code's treatment is on the page rather than implicit.

4. **[SHARPENING] §8's "M10 echoes `req.id` into the response" is imprecise.** `Response` carries no `ReqId` field and `execute` returns a bare `Response`; the id↔response pairing is the *transport's* (it still holds the `Request` it passed in). Say so, so a builder doesn't hunt for a nonexistent id field on `Response` (or on the rebuilt cached response in step (a)).

5. **[SHARPENING] Name the idempotency cache's concurrency/reuse limits and pin the lock choice to the stated `Total` invariant.** The "reentrant, may be called concurrently" claim invites two cases the cache doesn't handle: *concurrent* same-`ReqId` requests both miss step (a) and both commit (only *sequential* retries dedup — acceptable under "best-effort," but worth stating), and a client reusing a `ReqId` across op-kinds gets the first op's `Cached` ack for the second (silently wrong-shaped — store `OpKind` in `Cached` and verify on `idem_get`). Separately, "`execute` is Total: always yields a Response" requires a non-poisoning lock (`parking_lot::Mutex`) or explicit `PoisonError` handling, else a panic-poisoned `std::sync::Mutex` makes a later `.lock()` panic and breaks totality.
