## What this is

ASN-0040 defines the **address-allocation and commitment subsystem** of the substrate: the mechanism that turns an arithmetically-possible tumbler address into a permanent system fact ("baptism"), governs how the set of live addresses grows, and guarantees that growth is monotone, ordered, gap-free, and globally unique — unconditionally across namespaces, and within a namespace under serialized commitment. It is the *write/growth law* over the tumbler space — not the content store, not authorization, both of which it deliberately excludes.

## Design commitments

These are forced by the note; downstream design cannot violate them.

- **The registry is append-only and irrevocable.** Once an address is baptized it is baptized forever (B0/B0★). No deletion, no overwrite, no reuse of a freed address. This is the single most load-bearing constraint — it permits the entire append-only/journal family of implementations and forbids any compaction that reclaims addresses.
- **Baptism is the *only* way the registry grows, one address at a time, atomically.** Every operation in the system is either *a* baptism (adds exactly one address) or leaves the registry untouched (B0a, B4, Bop). There is no side-channel that adds addresses. *As specified*, growth is one address per operation; bulk allocation — baptizing a contiguous range of positions in one step — is an explicit open extension (a note Open Question), not forbidden, but any such path must preserve B1 contiguity and B4 atomicity.
- **Allocation is deterministic — `next` is a pure function of registry state.** Given the registry and a namespace `(p,d)`, the next address is fixed (B2). No randomness, no *authoritative* counter independent of the registry (the evidence confirms udanax-green keeps "no session-local counter"), no clock. The per-namespace counter recommended below is a *derived hint* over registry state, not a competing source of truth. This makes allocation memoizable, replayable, and property-testable against the spec.
- **Children of a namespace form a contiguous, gap-free prefix `c₁…cₘ` (B1), so a namespace's entire allocation state is one integer — the high-water mark (B2).** You cannot baptize the 5th child before the first four. This is the compression that the whole implementation should exploit: per-namespace state is a *count*, not a *set*.
- **Distinct namespaces never collide, unconditionally (B7).** Cross-namespace uniqueness is structural, requiring no coordination — *provided both namespaces clear the full B6 gate* (B7's proof leans on B6(ii)'s `d∈{1,2}` in its unequal-length-parents case, not only on the T4 parent of B6(i)). Within B6, the "pure-trailing-zero" aliasing example shows B6(i) is the specifically anti-aliasing hinge: drop it alone and `([1,0],1)` and `([1],2)` denote the same stream; the other clauses are about T4-preservation of the stream.
- **The four-level hierarchy (node→user→document→element) is baked in.** Depth is restricted to `{1,2}` and zeros to `≤3` (B6). `d=1` stays at a level, `d=2` descends one. This is not negotiable at this layer.
- **Membership in the registry is independent of content (B3 — ghost elements).** A baptized address may store nothing yet remain a permanent, ordered, addressable anchor for children and links. "Baptized" and "has bytes" are orthogonal axes.
- **Baptism imposes no parent-baptized precondition.** `Bop`'s sole precondition is B6 (depth validity); a position may be baptized beneath a parent that is itself unbaptized — a virtual anchor. This is what lets empty-start genesis work (the first baptism's parent is an unbaptized virtual position). The *only* prerequisite waived is parent-baptized-ness, not parent-validity: B6(i) still requires that (virtual) parent to satisfy T4, so the virtual root must be a T4-valid address (e.g. `[1]`), never the all-zeros tumbler. It is deliberately *all* this layer commits to about ordering prerequisites: whether ownership additionally requires a parent baptized first is deferred to the ownership model.
- **Same-namespace commits must be serialized; cross-namespace ones need not be.** B8's same-namespace uniqueness holds only *under a single authority* (B-Seq); cross-namespace uniqueness is free (B7). The *requirement* to serialize within a namespace is forced; the *granularity* (global vs per-namespace) is a builder choice (below).

Conventional, not forced: the choice to store counters vs explicit sets (the contiguity that *enables* counters is forced; the representation is not); the content of the seed `B₀`; the global-serialization granularity of B-Seq (the reference implementation's discipline, looser disciplines remain sound).

## What must be built

Described functionally — what each must *do*.

- **A durable baptismal registry.** Records which addresses are live; monotone; survives restart. Answers two queries: *is `t` baptized?* and *how many children does namespace `(p,d)` hold?*
- **A next-address allocator.** Given `(p,d)` and current registry, deterministically yield the next address. With the contiguity result this is "read the count `m`, return `c₍ₘ₊₁₎`."
- **An atomic baptism operation.** Evaluate `next` and adjoin it as one indivisible, durable step — no observable state between "computed" and "committed."
- **Tumbler arithmetic, both directions.** Forward (`inc(p,d)`, `inc(t,0)`, `zeros`, `sig`, T4 check) from ASN-0034, *plus* decomposition — given an arbitrary `t`, recover `(parent, depth, ordinal)`. Decomposition is what lets the counter representation answer membership; B6(i) (T4 parents) is what makes it unambiguous.
- **A validity gate (B6).** A stateless precondition check — T4 parent, `d∈{1,2}`, zeros budget — rejecting invalid baptisms before they touch the registry.
- **Per-namespace occupancy bookkeeping.** The high-water-mark counter per active `(p,d)` — the sufficient statistic.
- **A serialization mechanism.** Linearize commits at least within each namespace.
- **Genesis/seed initialization.** Establish `B₀` (or an empty-start convention) and check conformance.
- **Recovery.** Rebuild in-memory state from the durable record on load.

Note what is *not* here: content storage and authorization. Keep both out of this subsystem.

## Implementation approaches

This is the heart. Per component, with tradeoffs and a recommendation.

**Registry representation.**
- *Explicit set of baptized tumblers* (persistent ordered set / trie keyed by address). Direct denotation; membership is a lookup; mirrors the udanax-green granfilade, which stores each address as a tree node. Cost: O(total addresses) entries; `next` finds the per-namespace max by navigating to the `(p,d)` subtree — exactly udanax-green's `findpreviousisagr` "query-and-increment" — not a full range scan and not a separate counter. A per-namespace counter is an O(1) optimization over that navigation, not a requirement of this design.
- *Per-namespace high-water-mark counters* — a sparse map `(p,d) → m`. This *is* the B1+B2 compression. Because a count `m` can *only* denote the prefix `{c₁…cₘ}`, the counter form makes B1 contiguity an invariant *of the representation* — a gap is literally unrepresentable — rather than one to enforce. `next` is an integer increment plus arithmetic; membership is "decompose `t`, compare its ordinal to `m`"; size is O(active namespaces), not O(addresses); and it separates registry from content for free (ghosts cost nothing). The count `m` and the tumbler ordinal it tracks are *unbounded* (B9 — no architectural cap on a namespace's children), so they must be arbitrary-precision, never a fixed-width machine int. The map records *namespace children only* — addresses of the form `cₙ ∈ S(p,d)`. A seed root that belongs to no sibling stream has no `(p,d)` key and no decomposition, so a counter-only registry would answer "not baptized" for it: the note's own `B₀ = {[1]}` is exactly this case (`[1] ∉ S(p,d)` for every `(p,d)`), and a counter-only map cannot even represent the note's example state `B₁ = {[1], [1,0,1]}`. Any such non-child seed roots — exactly the length-1 node roots, since decomposition presupposes length ≥ 2 — must therefore be retained in a small explicit auxiliary set, consulted alongside the counters. **Pick the counters — plus that auxiliary set — as the primary representation for baptism.** The counters' only demand is tumbler decomposition, which is cheap (read the tail; the second-to-last component being zero distinguishes `d=2` from `d=1`); and "decomposition alone suffices" holds under empty-start genesis — and, more generally, any seed with no length-1 roots — where every baptized address is a namespace child, so the auxiliary set stays empty. Under an explicit seed containing non-child roots — the "pre-provisioned roots" alternative below — the auxiliary set is load-bearing.
- *Hierarchical tree* mirroring the address hierarchy, each parent carrying up to two child-counters and links to child nodes — the granfilade's hierarchy plus per-node counters the granfilade does not itself keep (udanax keeps no counters, deriving each max by `findpreviousisagr` navigation). **Pick this when the broader system needs subtree/range queries** ("links embrace all contents below a position"), where locality of children under parents pays off. For baptism *alone*, counters suffice.

A persistent, structurally-shared map gives near-free versioned snapshots: each baptism yields a new version sharing structure with the old, enabling MVCC-style reads (consistent membership snapshots for readers while a writer advances a namespace).

**Durability and recovery.**
- **Append-only journal (WAL) of baptismal commits, recovered by replay.** This fits *the note's* commitments exactly: B4 atomicity is one atomic durable append; B0 monotonicity makes the log purely additive (never needing deletion-compaction); recovery is replay. It also honors the note's own finality model — an address is "real only when committed to the persistent store" — by making the durable append *be* the commit, so the commit point and the durability point coincide. **This is the default.** Note that it is *stronger* durability than udanax-green provides, not a match for it: udanax keeps no journal and never replays (see checkpoint-restore next).
- **Checkpoint-restore — udanax-green's actual mechanism.** Hold baptisms in the in-memory registry and persist the whole structure *lazily* — only when idle or on clean exit — rebuilding from the last checkpoint on restart. There is no journal and no replay: a crash discards every baptism since the last checkpoint *uniformly* (never producing divergent branches), resuming from a consistent but possibly stale on-disk state. Simplest to implement and contention-free on the write path, but it accepts a crash-loss window of all post-checkpoint baptisms — weaker than the note's "real only when committed" finality, which the WAL above meets.
- **What to log (for the WAL):** the `(p,d)` event (compact, but replay must do arithmetic) vs the full resulting address (self-describing, lets recovery re-derive each counter as max-ordinal-per-namespace and detect divergence/corruption). **Log the resulting address** — robustness and debuggability outweigh the bytes.
- **Snapshot + log (checkpointing) to bound replay** — the replay-bounding refinement of the WAL: periodically persist the counter map and truncate the log tail so recovery replays only the post-snapshot segment. The log stays per-append durable, so this loses nothing a plain WAL would not; it trades only snapshot frequency against replay length. This is a *hybrid* of the two options above — a durable snapshot base, as in checkpoint-restore, plus a short replayable tail — not udanax's mechanism, which keeps no per-append log at all.
- **Lampson discipline:** keep authoritative state and recomputable *hint* clearly separated. The **in-memory counter map** is always a pure hint — rebuilt from the latest snapshot plus replay of the *post-snapshot* log tail, with the tail winning over it on any mismatch. In the default WAL regime (no truncation) there is no durable snapshot to protect, so the whole map recomputes by full replay and the log is authoritative outright — the snapshot-as-pure-hint, recover-by-full-replay framing holds only here. Once you checkpoint *and truncate the log behind a snapshot*, that **snapshot** becomes authoritative durable state — the recovery base, not a discardable hint — and only the post-snapshot tail remains a replayable hint over it, so "log wins on mismatch" then applies to that tail versus the in-memory map, never to the snapshot. This keeps the fast path (in-memory counters) cheap and the rare path (recovery) correct.

**Allocation (`next`).** With counters, `next` is a counter read plus arithmetic — no scan. The reference implementation's "stateless query-and-increment" (`next = current max + 1` over the shared structure) is the same idea without the per-namespace cache; maintaining the per-namespace count *is* the optimization that turns its tree-max query into O(1). Determinism (B2) means `next` is trivially memoizable and is the natural oracle for property tests.

**Atomicity and concurrency.**
- *Single serializing writer* (udanax-green's run-to-completion, single-threaded, single persistent-store writer): baptisms queue and execute to completion; satisfies B-Seq (its grounding case), though it serializes more than B-Seq strictly requires. Dead simple, trivially correct, no locks, easy recovery. Cost: a global write bottleneck.
- *Per-namespace serialization.* Because B7 makes distinct namespaces independent, shard by namespace: each has its own lock or atomic increment; cross-namespace baptisms run in parallel. This relaxes B-Seq to per-namespace while preserving B8 (same-namespace uniqueness needs only same-namespace order). **Pick this for multi-owner concurrency** — the common case (independent owners in disjoint namespaces) becomes contention-free. This assumes a *single writer per namespace*, so B-Seq still holds within each one; the harder case — coexisting replicas allocating in the *same* namespace, on divergent branches outside B-Seq's single-authority scope — is the note's unresolved concurrency question (what cross-replica baptism ordering preserves global uniqueness without centralized coordination), which per-namespace sharding does not answer.
- *Make the journal append the linearization point.* Order same-namespace commits by their append order; derive the counter from it. A single shared log gives a total order (simple, easy recovery, but more order than B7 requires and a write bottleneck); per-namespace log segments give parallelism matching B7's independence at the cost of merge-on-recovery. **Start with one shared log + a sharded in-memory counter map; split the log per namespace only if write throughput demands it.** The one hazard to engineer carefully: "advance counter" and "append record" must commit together — make the durable append the commit, and treat the counter as the recomputable hint.

**Validity gate (B6).** A pure, stateless predicate at the operation boundary. No storage. Reject invalid depth/parent before any state change — "rare case correct."

**Genesis.** *Empty-start under a T4-valid virtual root.* A note-conforming empty-start inserts no seeds and baptizes the first child beneath a *virtual, unbaptized* parent — which B6(i) still requires to satisfy T4 — e.g. `[1]`, whose first child is `[1,0,1]`; the first real address appears on the first operation. (udanax-green's literal all-zeros root is *not* note-conforming: a zero final component fails T4, so the digest's own B6(i) gate would reject the first baptism — the reference implementation bootstraps only because it performs no B6-style structural check on parents.) This is the simplest path, makes "load an empty log" and "fresh genesis" the same code path, and keeps the counter-map registry *complete* on its own — every address ever baptized is a namespace child (the virtual root is never itself baptized), so no auxiliary seed-root set is needed. *Explicit conforming seed `B₀`* (finite, contiguous-prefix children, T4 seeds) is the alternative when roots must be pre-provisioned; if any seed root belongs to no sibling stream, the auxiliary set above must carry it. Recovery's load path subsumes both.

## Guarantees to uphold

- **Permanence / irrevocability (B0).** *By construction* if the store is append-only and no code path deletes from the registry. Active part: never expose a registry-delete; keep deletion confined to the content layer.
- **Cross-namespace uniqueness (B7/B8).** *By construction*, contingent on the **full B6 gate** holding on both namespaces — not T4-on-parents alone: B7's proof uses B6(ii)'s `d∈{1,2}` (which bounds the parent-length gap to ≤1) in its unequal-length-parents case as well as B6(i). Within that gate, B6(i) is the *specifically anti-aliasing* clause — drop it alone and `([1,0],1)`/`([1],2)` denote the same stream — while the remaining clauses secure stream T4-preservation. Enforce all of B6.
- **Same-namespace uniqueness (B8).** *Requires active enforcement* — serialization of commits within a namespace. This is the one uniqueness guarantee that is not free; the single-writer or per-namespace lock is precisely what buys it.
- **Contiguity (B1).** *By construction* if `next` always returns `c₍hwm+1₎` and baptism is the sole growth path. Active part: any bulk/batch path you add must preserve gap-freeness.
- **T4 validity of every address (B10).** *By construction* given conforming seeds plus the B6 gate.
- **Atomicity (B4).** *Requires active enforcement* in the durability layer — a partial commit (counter advanced but record not durable, or vice versa) breaks it. This is the main correctness obligation of the storage code.
- **Determinism and finiteness (B2/B_fin).** *By construction* (no entropy in allocation; finite seed plus one-at-a-time growth).
- **Unbounded extent (B9).** *By construction, contingent on the representation.* No architectural limit caps how many children a namespace may hold — for any `M` some reachable state has `hwm ≥ M` (Nelson: "Each integer has no upper limit"). This holds automatically *only if* the high-water-mark count and the tumbler ordinal are arbitrary-precision; a fixed-width counter silently violates it.
- **Content-independence (B3).** *By construction* if the registry and content store are kept separate and "baptized" is never inferred from "has bytes."

## How it fits

- **Leans on the tumbler algebra (ASN-0034)** for the address space `T`, its total order, field structure (T4), permanent allocation (T8), strict increase (T9), the `inc`/TA5 increment laws, `zeros`/`sig`, the Prefix relation, and the transition framework (`𝒮`, `Σ`, `→*`, AllocatedSet, NoDeallocation). Baptism *is* the growth law over that algebra and additionally needs the decomposition direction (`t → (p,d,n)`).
- **Hands addresses to the content store**, which keys bytes by baptized addresses but is otherwise independent (ghosts). Keep this boundary sharp.
- **Hands anchors to the link/span subsystem**, whose links "embrace all contents below" a position — the reason ghosts must be addressable. A spanfilade/POOM-style index sits *above* the registry and, in the reference design, is a *derived* secondary index rather than a second source of truth.
- **Defers to the ownership model (Tumbler Ownership)** for *who* may baptize. B-Seq's "single baptismal authority" is an ownership notion; the open question "must a parent be baptized first?" resolves there, not here.
- **Relates to the foundation allocator `allocated(s)`.** The note distinguishes `s.B` (baptism's committed registry) from the foundation's `allocated(s)` (the allocator's realized domain). The reference implementation *unifies* allocator and permanent registry in one append-only structure (the granfilade is both), so the two coincide by construction; a layered design that feeds a distinct registry is the alternative. The layered path carries an alignment obligation the unified one sidesteps: every allocator-extension transition must be matched by a baptism, and the seed must cover the genesis allocator domain, or `allocated(s) ⊆ s.B` fails and addresses become allocated-but-unbaptized. (Note: the reference implementation *also* runs an ephemeral, per-session overlay for access-control and rollback of created-but-unwritten entries — that overlay is *not* the baptismal registry and should not be confused with it.)

## Decisions for the builder

Genuine implementation choices the note leaves open (distinct from its spec-level open questions).

- **Registry representation:** per-namespace counters (recommended for baptism), explicit address set, or hierarchical tree (choose if the system needs subtree/range queries). Driven largely by whether you need fast arbitrary-`t` membership (favors a set/trie) or only `next` plus per-namespace queries (favors counters).
- **Journal contents:** log the `(p,d)` event or the full resulting address (recommended) — robustness vs compactness.
- **Serialization granularity:** single global writer (simplest) vs per-namespace (concurrent, leveraging B7, and assuming a single writer per namespace). And whether the journal append *is* the linearization point. The shared-namespace, multi-replica case — divergent branches outside B-Seq's single-authority scope — is the note's open concurrency question, not settled here.
- **Checkpoint policy and crash-loss window:** how often to persist the counter-map hint and truncate the log, and how many uncommitted baptisms you tolerate losing on crash (the reference implementation accepts losing the in-memory tail, uniformly, with no divergence).
- **Finality model.** The note equates "committed to persistent store" with "baptized = irrevocable" (B0). The reference implementation has a *reserve-then-confirm* shape — an entry can be structurally inserted into the in-memory granfilade yet rolled back if no content is ever written. That rollback (`deleteversion`, gated by the session overlay's created/modified flags) discards the in-memory granfilade entry *before* any lazy persistent checkpoint — it is driven by the overlay, not housed in it, and the granfilade is append-only only with respect to content *delete*, not to created-but-unconfirmed versions. A rolled-back entry therefore never reaches the store, so the reference's *persistent* baptisms remain irrevocable — consistent with the note's "real only when committed to persistent store," not a divergence from B0. The genuine decision is whether *you* introduce a pre-finality reserve window of your own: baptism final at journal-append (matching the note's clean model) vs a two-phase "reserve, then confirm" with rollback of unconfirmed entries before they become durable. The latter adds semantics beyond this note and must still honor B1 contiguity (a rolled-back reservation cannot leave a gap).
- **Genesis:** empty-start under a T4-valid virtual root (e.g. `[1]`) vs an explicit pre-baptized seed set.
- **Key encoding for the counter map:** the canonical byte encoding of `(parent, depth)` — flat keyed map vs trie over the hierarchy — trading lookup speed against locality for subtree operations. Every tumbler integer is unbounded (Nelson: "Each integer has no upper limit"; B9), not just the child ordinal — so every component, the parent's included, must be variable-length in both journal records and the map, never a fixed-width integer.
