## What this is

ASN-0129 defines the substrate's **predicate language (PL)** — the closed algebra (Boolean composition, finite quantification, value-composition, aggregation) that turns ASN-0128's per-type atomic read predicates into the substrate's complete query/condition language, together with its type discipline, its purity/termination guarantees, and a dynamics theory describing how a predicate's truth moves as the substrate changes. PL is the substrate's *extension language*: every protocol-level trigger, termination condition, and gate is written in it.

## Design commitments

(forced unless marked conventional)

- **Composition is the only extension mechanism.** Builders extend the substrate by registering types and composing predicates in a fixed, closed algebra — never by injecting read-path code. The substrate evaluates terms of this algebra and runs no foreign code. This is the keystone: it is *why* purity, termination, and the structural-read boundary hold for every predicate anyone will ever write. Architecturally, the closed algebra **is** the sandbox, and it needs no runtime guard for two reasons working together: it is *closed under safe formers*, so every term is provably pure, terminating, and structural by induction; and it *lacks the unsafe formers* — no fixpoint/recursion operator, no foreign-code injection, no content read — with the substrate running nothing foreign on top. (Those safety properties are the *proven* part: purity (PC4), termination (PC5), the no-fixpoint grammar fact (PC6a), structural-reads-only. *Inexpressibility* proper belongs to the ceiling, where only the no-fixpoint grammar fact is proven and the reach/self-emit exclusions below are conjectured.)
- **The atomic vocabulary is generated from the registry, not authored.** Available atoms are a pure function of the registration records — *shape and attached behaviors only*: the generator branches on those two, never on `idem`, which is emit-side and contributes no read atom (its sole read-side consequence, I1a's at-most-one-active-tuple, is a property of states, not a predicate former). Registering a type mechanically yields its template family; there is no hand-written per-type predicate code.
- **The vocabulary is static at runtime** (V-STAT). It changes only at construction-time registration, never via a transition. Consequence: typing, expansion, and stability analysis run once at construction and stay valid for every reachable state.
- **Predicates are pure functions of state** (PC4), **total at every reachable state** (PC5 — every ⊥‑adjoined *atom* is built total, returning ⊥ as a *value*, and PC2's guard is the only thing that eliminates that ⊥; atom totality, not the guard alone, is what keeps PC4/PC5 universal). A term reads only the link store, the arrangement-store *domain*, the registry, plus its arguments and one view — no content bytes, no side effects, no emission-order or memo dependence. Same inputs → same result, always.
- **Every predicate terminates at every state** (PC5), and the grammar ships no fixpoint/recursion operator (PC6a). All iteration is either a finite fold over a finite domain or an atom's internal iteration behind a proven bound.
- **Reads are structural only.** No predicate touches the content store (value *or* domain) or dereferences an arrangement binding. PL asks "what does the link store assert," never "what bytes are here."
- **The expressive ceiling is fixed and registry-pinned** (PC6). PL is *exactly* what feedback-free, syntax-directed evaluation over the read base computes — PC6 is a **theorem**, and the grammar ships no fixpoint and no mutually-recursive-definition operator (PC6a, **proven**). That *no PL term computes* transitive closure or the self-emit test is a different and **stronger** claim — a **conjecture** (C-reach, C-emit respectively), each stronger than its *own* grammar baseline, deliberately not promoted to a theorem. **C-reach**'s baseline is PC6a: it *implies* the no-fixpoint fact (any fixpoint former would compute reach) and additionally rules out every indirect counting/order encoding, which is why it is left unproven while the trivial grammar fact is discharged. **C-emit**'s baseline is a *separate* grammar fact read off V — the vocabulary supplies no term *spelling* the self-emit test (no frontier atom, no home-grouping atom, `inc(·,0)` excluded); self-emit is frontier arithmetic, not closure, so no-fixpoint has no bearing on it, and the conjecture is stronger only because it additionally rules out the two indirect routes V *does* admit (`age`, reflected-`L_dom`). The missing invariance proofs for both, plus a third conjecture (PC6's parity candidate), are the note's OQ6. Either way the *builder's* stance is settled and needs only the relevant grammar fact: reachability is computed at agent time by unrolling, self-emit is checked on the emitting surface — neither is a PL term handed to the predicate (read) layer. The ceiling moves only when the registry does.
- **Well-typing is decidable and construction-time** (WT) — a bottom-up pass over the finite syntax tree decides it, reading no state.
- **One view per term** (PC3), fixed at the top level — audit / active / default. *Forced:* the three views are three distinct questions over the same Σ (ever-held / currently-holds / currently-holds-as-presented). *Conventional:* that the view is a single top-level tag rather than a per-constituent parameter — cross-view readings are reconstructible *inside* any term through the fixed-view slices, so first-class per-atom view syntax is an OQ1 deferral, not a missing capability.
- **Partiality is explicit** (PC2). ⊥ is a verdict with meaning, eliminated only through an explicit binder guard behind a definedness test — never a silent undefined evaluation. The guard's else-branch `c_default` is *any* PL term of the result codomain, not merely a constant: a state-reading else is what keeps the value codomains (`T`, `ℕ`) as expressive as the Boolean one.
- **Counting is set-semantics** (PC2a): count counts distinct elements of the view-selected domain, never occurrences; cross-type totals are sums of per-type counts.
- **Ordinal extrema are per-home-scoped** (PC2a): `max_{T1}`/`min_{T1}` recover emission order *only within one home's chain*; cross-home T1 comparison is well-defined but *temporally meaningless*, so any "latest"/recency trigger built on extrema inherits the per-home scope.
- **Conventional, not forced:** the specific aggregates that ship (count, T1-extrema, union-fold); and — importantly — *which compositions ship as named atoms*. AM's directional keying and BH3's reverse-lookup opt-in are naming/exposure conventions, not capability fences: the algebra expresses forward *and* reverse lookup for every type whether or not the named atom is exposed. "What BH3 withholds is the atom, never the question." The default-view rewrite is *not* on this list: its per-codomain content — collections rewritten, verdicts/Booleans/traversal/membership not — is *forced*, not chosen here (a rewrite touching verdicts, membership, or traversal would let a presentation mark change query topology, the layer confusion UV exists to prevent), UV "extending ASN-0128's committed collection rule rather than inventing a second one"; what is at most conventional is inherited from 0128 — that a presentation-filtering default view exists at all.

## What must be built

- **A vocabulary generator** — walks the registry and emits the available atoms: the core family for every type, behavior families per attached behavior, the cross-type join (`targets_keyed`) when any type attaches reverse-lookup — paired with its sole consumer, the per-class map lookup `·[K]` (V-PRIM), the only eliminator of the `Map_fin` codomain — plus the fixed primitive/projection/residence vocabulary. Each entry carries the signature the type checker needs and binds to the base read the evaluator dispatches to. Build every ⊥‑adjoined atom *total*: it returns ⊥ as a value, never a partial or undefined result (`age(a) = ⊥` exactly off the active K‑tuples, the test doubling as an activeness check). State-dependent definedness — a retraction turning a defined term undefined — would poison PC4/PC5's universal claims and the dynamics classification with them; the total re-typing is what lets PC2's guard discipline apply uniformly at composition time.
- **A representation for composed terms and domain expressions** — the (mutually recursive) syntactic structures of the algebra: atoms, connectives, quantifiers, value-composition with its guard (the guard is PL's *only* conditional former), aggregates, filters, reflected domains (the domain→term direction, address-valued domains only), set-valued terms in domain position (the term→domain direction — *every* `℘_fin(T)`-valued PL term is itself a domain expression; without this closure aggregation reaches the bases but not the algebra's own set results, leaving `count(targets_of(x))` and `out_capped` untypable), and the one view tag.
- **A type checker** — a syntax-directed, state-free pass deciding well-typing, assigning codomains/sorts, handling binder-guard narrowing, the tuple sort, and the Reg instance-wise rule — and enforcing the two QD↔PL bridge rules and the `Reg` restriction. Both bridge directions fire: a `℘_fin(T)`-valued term types as a domain at `dom(T)` (term→domain), and only *address-valued* domains reflect back into term position (QD-refl, domain→term), so the tuple-valued slices `A_K`/`L_K` are domains-only and never term values, while `Reg` is narrower still — admissible only under a quantifier binder or as the variable-free `count(Reg)`, never filtered, folded, or compared (the vocabulary has no class-valued operation, not even class equality).
- **A Reg-expander** (V-IDX) — statically expands class-quantified terms into a finite conjunction/disjunction over registered classes, rejecting instances that apply an absent behavior atom. *No* constructible registry attaches a behavior family at every class (R-C1 plus `R`'s empty-behavior record guarantee every registry holds a class lacking every family), so a class-indexed behavior atom under a `Reg` binder is *never* well-formed — which makes `targets_keyed(s)[K]` the designed (and only) route to per-class BH3 data under `Reg`: it reads the bound class's verdict via the class-unindexed join and `·[K]`, without the body ever applying an atom the class may lack.
- **An evaluator** — syntax-directed, single-pass, pure, terminating; dispatches atom leaves to base reads, combinators to Boolean/value ops, domains to filter-select or reflected-enumeration, and folds to their passes — short-circuiting for quantifiers (∃ at first witness, ∀ at first counterexample), full single-pass for `count`/union-fold — against a pinned state and a fixed view, passing that view *only* to the view-parameterized atoms (`members`/`targets_of`/`is_K`/`M_K`), never to the fixed-view slices or the behavior atoms (see the view machinery).
- **A read-base adapter** — exposes the substrate's read surface at *atom granularity*: pattern queries and slice enumeration over typed link tuples, link-store domain enumeration, the BH4 home-chain read route (frontier arithmetic over `dom(Σ.L)`'s homed segments serving `age`/`stale` — a *distinct* mechanism from slice enumeration, the third base read route PC4 names, resolving `home(a')` out of the stored record rather than recomputing it from the address, which is why home-grouping is no atom), document-store membership (only), registry lookup, *both* V-TUP per-tuple regimes — the denotation projections (`addr`/`addrs_F`/`addrs_G`) *and* the coverage-membership tests (`coverage_F`/`coverage_G`) — and the address/set/ℕ primitives, keeping span arithmetic *inside* each call. The two V-TUP regimes are not symmetric: the projections are the genuinely irreducible pair, since coverage fixes the denoted set only up to its ≼-minimal elements (I0a's separating pair), so `addrs_F`/`addrs_G` cannot be recovered from the coverage tests; the coverage tests run the other way — derivable from `addrs + ≼` (`t ∈ coverage(e) ⟺ ∃ y ∈ addrs(e): y ≼ t`, PrefixSpanCoverage), a route that uses only the prefix test — so coverage is admitted as a primitive for regime parity, not out of necessity. The PC6 converse still needs a *per-tuple* coverage test — the admitted primitive or its `addrs + ≼` equivalent — because atoms conflate per-tuple structure.
- **The view machinery** — the three lenses, with active computed as audit-minus-nullified and the default-view presentation rewrite applied elementwise to collection-valued results. The lens is *not* uniform across atoms: only `members`/`targets_of`/`is_K`/`M_K` rebind with the term view; `A_K`/`L_K` are fixed-view (active/audit *by name*, at every term view), and the behavior atoms (BH1–BH4) always read the *active* slice and have **no** audit reading — at term-view `audit`, `succs`/`tip`/`age`/… still read active state. Cross-view readings are nonetheless reconstructible *within* a term through the fixed-view slices (`⋃(L_K, addrs_F)` rebuilds audit `members` inside an active-view term), so a per-atom view *parameter* is an OQ1 deferral, not a missing capability — don't invent one.
- **A dynamics certifier** (PD0–PD2) — a second static pass computing each term's read footprint and stability class (⊤-stable / ⊥-stable / neither), so the protocol layer can validate triggers and termination conditions and skip re-evaluation safely.

## Implementation approaches

**Term representation & evaluation — interpret first, compile if hot.**
The algebra is tiny and its control tree is fixed by syntax independent of state (PC6a), so two approaches both work and the spec makes the choice low-risk:
- *Tree-walking interpreter.* Walk the AST, dispatch per node against the pinned state and view. This is PC6 made literal — the control tree *is* the syntax tree — and is trivially correct. Start here.
- *Compile-once to a pure evaluation closure.* Because typing and structure are state-independent (WT / V-STAT / PC6a), do all structural work once at construction and emit a specialized evaluator over `(state, view)`. This is the make-the-common-case-fast option when triggers fire after every step. It gives up nothing — you are caching a *recomputable* structure (a hint), and purity makes the closure freely shareable across threads.

Skip bytecode/VM — the combinator set is bounded and small; it buys nothing.

**Vocabulary generation — eager, built once.**
The registry is static (V-STAT), so build the full vocabulary table at construction by walking the registry: per-type template families, the conditional global join, the fixed primitives. There is no reason to resolve atoms lazily; this mirrors the spec's own treatment of `V_atom` as a fixed set.

**The read base — append-only journal plus indexes-as-hints.**
The link store is grow-only (PD0 leans on "no step removes an address or rewrites a stored value"), which is exactly the shape an append-only journal fits — and matches udanax-green's append-only granfilade. Commit to it:
- *Authoritative store:* an append-only journal of link writes, recovered by replay on load; the registry is the small authoritative side-table. Stored values are immutable, so journal entries are never rewritten.
- *Indexes as hints (recomputable on a miss).* Slice enumeration, pattern matching, and the active/audit distinction are all served by secondary structures derivable from the journal — never authoritative duplicate state. Two carry the load:
  - a *per-type slice index* (type → its tuples) for `M_K`/`A_K`/`L_K` enumeration and every PC1/PC2a fold;
  - a *symmetric span-intersection index* over **both** endsets of all links, answering coverage-keyed forward *and* reverse pattern queries. This is the spanfilade approach, and the udanax-green evidence is decisive: Green matches endsets by span *intersection* (not exact value) and answers to-keyed (reverse) queries for every link from one symmetrically-built index *with no per-type opt-in*. That is precisely the note's "reverse lookup is the atom, never the question" — build the symmetric index once and let BH3's opt-in govern only whether the *named* `sources_to`/`target_of` atom is exposed, never whether the index supports the query.
- *Recovery:* rebuild indexes by replaying the journal; optionally snapshot for fast startup, but the journal is the source of truth. The two content-side exclusions rest on *different* grounds. The content-store exclusion (`dom(Σ.C)` has no read at all — value *or* domain) is a **layer boundary**: no PL contract consults `dom(Σ.C)`, and content is reached only through arrangement (V→I) reads, which are ASN-0127's territory — an agent-time operation, not a substrate predicate. (The Green evidence is supporting color for why no occupancy check was ever needed *anywhere*: its append-only store makes "address exists ⇒ content exists" a construction invariant on the emit/link-creation path, so even there nothing checks occupancy.) The residence exclusion is separate: `is_doc` is *membership-only* because Green's granfilade is a pure point-lookup store whose reads never enumerate document keys, matching the note's no-document-census.

**Active slice & nullification — compute from two authoritative slices, cache as a hint.**
Active = audit minus nullified, and nullified is a prefix/subtree cover read off the retraction slice (FP: active footprint is `L_K ∪ L_R`). Rather than maintain active as authoritative derived state, test active-membership by "in the audit slice AND not covered by any retraction," answering the cover test through the *same* span-intersection index (a `≼`-prefix query). Cache the active set as a hint, recomputable from the two slices on demand. One index does both jobs.

**Views — active by subtraction, default by elementwise rewrite.**
Read audit straight from the journal segment; derive active by the nullification subtraction; derive default by applying the UV presentation rewrite — drop elements whose `filtered` predicate holds — to collection-valued results *only*, leaving verdicts, Booleans, traversal, and stored values untouched. And dispatch the term view *selectively*: it rebinds only `members`/`targets_of`/`is_K`/`M_K`; `A_K`/`L_K` and the behavior atoms read their own fixed slice at every view (at term-view `audit` the behavior atoms still read *active* state — they have no audit reading), so the lens must not be threaded blindly through every leaf. Eager (rewrite during enumeration) is simplest; the cost is the BH1 footprint increment (FP) every default-view collection term carries.

**Keep span arithmetic inside the leaf — this preserves the ceiling.**
Coverage tests are add-then-compare (form the span upper bound, then compare under the tumbler order). The udanax-green read path does exactly this — `tumbleradd` to form the reach, then `tumblercmp` — and notably it *reads* a link's home document out of the stored record rather than recomputing it from the address (`movetumbler`, never address arithmetic). Mirror both: the combinator layer sees only Booleans and finished values; arithmetic lives inside the base call. Exposing span-add as a composable operator would breach PC6's granularity restriction and lift the language above its proven ceiling. (It is also why neither self-emit nor home-grouping is a PL atom — self-emit is an emit-surface check, home-grouping has no atom at all; keep both out of the base.)

**Type checker & dynamics certifier — two bottom-up passes sharing a traversal.**
- *Typing:* a single synthesis pass, one rule per former, run at construction and cached; reject ill-typed terms at registration. The interesting cases (binder-guard narrowing, tuple sort, Reg instance-wise expansion) are local.
- *Dynamics:* a second pass computing, per subterm, its footprint (union over atoms, per FP — and FP's *cross-type* footprints are load-bearing, not incidental: BH4 atoms read home-wide, `targets_keyed` reads every BH3-attached Binary slice, active-view footprints include the retraction slice `L_R`, and a default-view collection term carries every BH1 slice) and its stability class (the ST/SF inductive rules, polarity-checked). Ship it **sound-but-incomplete** — the certified spellings are the quantified forms; extensionally-equal but unrecognized spellings are conservatively rejected. The minimum worth building is footprint analysis plus the ST/SF polarity check; that alone lets a protocol checker validate a termination condition mechanically.

**Reg-expansion — at construction.**
Expand class-quantified terms into their per-class instances when the term is built, checking each instance; the term then becomes an ordinary Boolean over closed instances with no runtime class-variable machinery. The registry is static, so this is a one-time substitution — and it matches Green's stance, where no read operation aggregates or enumerates over link types at all (types enter only as caller-supplied selectors).

**Concurrency & state representation — pin an immutable snapshot.**
Purity (PC4) makes concurrent evaluation safe with no read-side locking; "coordination is entirely an emit-side concern." Represent state as an immutable value with structural sharing between versions (persistent/immutable maps and sets): a writer produces a new version, readers cheaply pin old ones — MVCC by construction. Version the hint-indexes *alongside* the state so a pinned snapshot carries internally-consistent indexes. This sidesteps the whole class of read/write race the emit side must fight — the predicate evaluator is pure-read over a frozen snapshot.

**Make the common case fast — footprint-indexed re-evaluation.**
The dynamics theory is engineered for exactly this. After a step, PD2's frame analysis says which triggers *can* be perturbed: a trigger whose footprint is disjoint from the step's effect need not be re-evaluated at all. Index live triggers by footprint and dispatch only the affected ones. **This is sound only if the footprint honors FP's cross-type cases** — and a naive per-type-slice footprint *under-approximates* for exactly the terms that matter: a BH4 atom (`age`/`stale`) reads *home-wide*, so any same-home deposit of *any* type perturbs it; `targets_keyed` reads *every* BH3-attached Binary slice; an active-view term is perturbed by an `[R]`-retraction of *any* type (active = audit − nullified); and a default-view collection term carries *every* BH1 slice (FP's default-view increment). Miss any of these and the dispatcher skips a trigger that did change — a stale termination gate; name these exceptions in the footprint or be surprised by them. PD0 then sharpens the win: a ⊤-stable (ST) trigger, once fired, won't un-fire — stays true, so stop watching it; dually an ⊥-stable (SF) condition, once false, won't fire — stays false, so stop watching it then. Each drops out of re-evaluation at its stable value (ST at true, SF at false) — that is the pruning point. The read layer should expose the footprint and stability certificate so the protocol/scheduler layer can do this — the single biggest evaluation-cost win available, and it falls straight out of FP/PD0/PD2.

## Guarantees to uphold

*By construction (fall out of the design):*
- **Determinism/purity** (PC4) — a pure evaluator over the admitted vocabulary and a pinned snapshot.
- **Termination** (PC5) and **no fixpoint** (PC6a) — only the bounded forms are admitted.
- **Decidable, state-free typing** (WT); **vocabulary staticity** (V-STAT) — registration is construction-only.
- **One view per term** (PC3) — enforced by the AST (view is a top-level tag).
- **Explicit partiality** (PC2) — every ⊥-adjoined *atom* is built total (returns ⊥ as a value, never an undefined result), and the type system makes that ⊥ eliminable only through the guard; atom totality is the atom-side half that makes the composition-side guard discipline apply uniformly.
- **Set-semantics counting** (PC2a) — count a set, not a multiset.

*By active enforcement (a boundary you must police):*
- **Structural-reads-only** — the base adapter must expose membership for the document store and *nothing* for the content store; no path may dereference content or arrangement bindings. Guard this layer boundary.
- **The expressive ceiling** (PC6) — the three restrictions fail *differently* when dropped, so calibrate extensions by axis. Adding a *node combinator* (an ℕ-op over two counts, say) is benign — V-PRIM-style admission leaves the ceiling untouched. The *granularity* restriction is extensible too, but only by *paired admission* — granting an operation to V-PRIM and the base together, so both sides of PC6's equality move at once; the breach is the *one-sided* move, exposing span arithmetic as a composable operator with no matching base admission, which leaves a leaf with no PL spelling. Adding a *fold form* is categorically larger: it admits a computation class, not a combinator (an order-dependent fold over a T1-sorted domain is already an arbitrary finite-state pass), closable only fold-by-fold — the parity candidate is exactly such a fold. The two breaches to *police* are the one-sided granularity exposure just named and the *control* axis: do not add a feedback / repeat-until-stable evaluator. Either silently lifts the language above PL and forfeits the ceiling and the dynamics theory (PD0–PD2 classify *syntactic* terms). A feedback breach additionally *risks* non-termination — not as an automatic casualty (PC6's own `reach` loop halts over the finite substrate), but absent an explicit termination measure, which is precisely what OQ4's bounded-fixpoint successor must supply to keep PC5.
- **The grow-only / immutable-value assumption** the dynamics theory leans on is an *upstream* substrate guarantee (no address removed, no value rewritten); depend on it but assume no more — in particular, active membership is *not* monotone: a retraction drops a tuple from the active slice `A_K` while the audit slice `L_K` keeps it (PD1), so an active witness can vanish ⊤→⊥; and resurrection re-deposits at a *fresh* address, so not even value-identity is stable.

## How it fits

- **Leans on (below):** ASN-0128 for the atomic read surface (per-type predicates, the three views, the registration records) and the reachable-state class PL is indexed over (the `→_sh*`-reachable extended-record states, R-TR) — PL's vocabulary is the generated closure of these; ASN-0086 for the pattern-query / slice-enumeration read primitive, the active-subset machinery, and address injectivity — this is the read base; ASN-0126 for the state model (stores, the gated transition relation and its frames, the home/chain frontier); ASN-0034 for the address comparisons (total order, prefix, decidability) the primitives admit and the coverage tests use; ASN-0043 for the link/endset structure and coverage-vs-denotation.
- **Hands to (above):** the protocol/application layer — triggers, termination conditions, gating disciplines, schedulers — written *in* PL and typed *by* PD0–PD2. Explicitly *not* part of this note; the read layer supplies the language, its evaluation guarantees, and the dynamics certificates the protocol layer consumes. One edge to surface to that layer: a recency/"latest" trigger built from `max_{T1}`/`min_{T1}` is meaningful only *within a single home's chain* — cross-home extrema are well-defined but temporally meaningless (PC2a).
- **Sibling boundary:** ASN-0127, the content-region (arrangement-reading) query layer over the arrangement store. PL deliberately excludes it; the two meet only at the substrate both read, and neither subsumes the other. Reachability is likewise *handed out* to agent-time computation (unroll `succs`), and self-emit is checked by the emit surface itself (which recomputes the frontier via FrontierUnification) — neither is built into the predicate evaluator. This is a settled design stance resting on grammar facts alone — PC6a (no fixpoint) for reachability, and a *separate* read-off-V fact (the vocabulary supplies no self-emit spelling) for self-emit — independent of whether either is PL-*inexpressible*, which is conjectured (C-reach, C-emit) not proven.

## Decisions for the builder

(distinct from the note's spec-level open questions)

- **Interpreter vs. compiled closures**, and the threshold to switch — driven by how often triggers are evaluated.
- **Index design for the read base:** the per-type slice index and the symmetric span-intersection index — keying, granularity, and incremental-maintenance vs. rebuild-on-load. (These are hints; the only hard constraint is that they stay recomputable from the journal.)
- **Active-slice strategy:** compute-on-read by retraction subtraction vs. a maintained active-set hint, and how to invalidate it.
- **Default-view rewrite:** eager (during enumeration) vs. lazy (post-filter), and where to charge the BH1 footprint increment.
- **How much dynamics certifier to ship:** footprint-only, footprint + ST/SF polarity, or a fuller (still sound-but-incomplete) classifier.
- **Snapshot/recovery:** journal-replay only vs. periodic index snapshots for startup latency.
- **State & index representation for concurrent reads:** persistent structures with snapshot-pinning (recommended) vs. copy-on-read; whether indexes are versioned with the state.
- **Result caching/memoization** across evaluations — permitted by purity but only as a hint keyed to *syntactic identity plus a state-version*, never semantic equality (OQ3 leaves extensional equivalence of terms undecided); whether it pays depends on re-evaluation patterns (and is largely superseded by footprint-indexed dispatch).
- **Fold materialization:** stream vs. collect finite domains — a memory/latency tradeoff for large slices.
- **Where reachability lives:** since PL excludes closure by design, provide an agent-time iteration harness (iterate `succs`, unroll) rather than extending the evaluator — unless and until the bounded-fixpoint successor (the note's own future work) is adopted.
