## What this is

ASN-0093 defines the **allocation substrate** — the bottom mutable layer of the store. It fixes the three address-keyed stores (content `C`, links `L`, document registry/arrangement `M`) and the three append-only primitives that grow them (`K.σ` register a document, `K.α` allocate content, `K.λ` allocate a link), plus the address-minting discipline (deterministic per-document, per-subspace tumbler chains) and the permanence/partition invariants every higher layer rests on. It governs *where things live and how new addresses are minted* — not what they mean, and not how arrangements change (fixed empty here, deferred upward).

## Design commitments

**Forced** (downstream cannot violate):

- **Append-only, immutable, never-reused.** Content and link values are written once and never overwritten; addresses are never reclaimed or re-pointed; documents are never unregistered. `dom(C)`, `dom(L)`, `dom(M)` only grow (C0, L12, M1). This is the spine permanence guarantee.
- **Identity is by address (the full allocated address), not by value.** An element is identified by the tumbler it was allocated at, not by its bytes; two equal content values at two addresses are two distinct elements. This *forbids* model-level content-addressed storage (hash-as-identity would collapse distinct allocations) — though value blobs may still be content-addressed *underneath* for compression.
- **Content and link addresses are minted deterministically, not chosen; document addresses are validated, not minted.** For content and links, the next address under a document in a subspace is a pure function of the document and the same-origin addresses already present: a fixed first form `[d.0.s_C.1]`, then `inc(max-sibling, 0)`. The caller supplies a document and a value; the address is determined, not a degree of freedom. Consequence: no counter state is required, replay is exact, and freshness is automatic. Document addresses are the asymmetric case: `K.σ` takes `d` as a caller input and only *validates* it (`d ∉ dom(M) ∧ T4-valid(d) ∧ zeros(d)=2`) — that `{fresh, T4-valid, zeros=2}` gate is mandatory and part of the **isolation TCB**: `T4-valid ∧ zeros(d)=2` (M0) is exactly what makes two distinct documents' allocator anchors provably prefix-incomparable, hence their entire content/link address spaces disjoint (Cross-document disjointness). Documents may be *nested* — `K.σ` permits registering `d'=[1,0,2,0,5,3]` beneath an existing `d=[1,0,2,0,5]` (so `d≺d'`) — and the nested case is precisely where `zeros(d)=2` earns its keep: when two documents are already prefix-incomparable the zero-count plays no role, but when one extends the other, `zeros(d₂)=2` forces position `#d₁+1` to be nonzero, diverging from the shorter document's anchor separator `0` and so keeping their address spaces disjoint. `zeros(d)=2` is thus not local well-formedness a builder may relax but the structural foundation of *inter-document* isolation. Document-address *origination* is deferred to the entity-allocation layer.
- **The address space is partitioned by subspace, per document; the subspace digits are a hard substrate commitment.** Content under `s_C`, links under `s_L`, beneath each document's address (L0). Store disjointness (SD) is therefore *structural*, not a cross-store check. The specific numerals `s_C=1, s_L=2` are Green-grounded convention — the SubspaceConventionAxiom, pinned by `xanadu.h:144–146` / `granf2.c:162` / `do2.c:94` and realized by the anchor construction `b_C(d)=inc(d,2)=[d.0.1]`, `b_L(d)=inc(b_C(d),0)=[d.0.2]` — not an algebraic necessity: the C1c/L1c-mandated first step `k₁=2` forces only the *first chain element* onto `[d.0.1]`, not the content *anchor*, and a conforming chain could open content one sibling higher (`b_C(d)=inc(inc(d,2),0)=[d.0.2]`, chain `(d,[d.0.1],[d.0.2],[d.0.2.1])`), satisfying C1c and L0 yet yielding `s_C=2`. What the inc-algebra *does* force — given that the two anchors are minted as siblings — is their *distinctness* (`s_C≠s_L`), the load-bearing fact for SD (T7 needs exactly `s_C≠s_L`). The values belong here under *Forced* in the downstream-cannot-violate sense — once the substrate commits them a builder cannot renumber the subspaces without rebuilding the anchor construction, just as it cannot relax the atomic-total-order commitment beside it.
- **Every element homes to exactly one registered document, recoverable from its own address.** `origin(·)` projects the home document out of the element address — the projection `N(a).0.U(a).0.D(a)` defined in the State model — so the home is *recoverable* from the address and never *needed* as authoritative state (whether to also denormalize it as a cache is a builder's choice, below). Allocation is gated on the home being *registered* (`origin ∈ dom(M)`: C2 for content, L1a for links).
- **Registration is separate from arrangement; the substrate leaves arrangement empty.** `K.σ` records a document and fixes `M(d)=∅` (M2). Populating `M(d)` is a different, higher operation. This draws the layer boundary: registry + addressing here, arrangement above.
- **Transitions are atomic and totally ordered** (SequentialTransitionAxiom): one indivisible append per allocation, one serialization order.

**Conventional** (committed but replaceable):

- **Exactly two subspaces, content as the lower sibling and link as its next sibling**, is the genuinely conventional (Green-compatible, replaceable) choice: a different design could swap the ordering or add a third subspace (`s_C+2`, anchor `inc(b_L,0)`). Exactly-two is an axiom — a third needs it revisited. The *specific digits* `s_C=1, s_L=2` that follow from this choice are themselves convention (the SubspaceConventionAxiom, Green-grounded) — the inc-algebra forces only their *distinctness*, not the values `1` and `2` — and once committed they are downstream-immutable; see the partition commitment under *Forced*.
- The StandardTriple (from/to/type) reading of link slots is notation; L3 structurally requires only `N≥3` endsets with a non-empty slot 3.

## What must be built

- **A document registry** — records which document addresses are allocated, answers "is `d` registered?", admits fresh well-formed registrations (enforcing the document-level shape: `T4-valid`, `zeros(d)=2`), never forgets; reserves a (currently empty) arrangement slot per document. It must **not** reject a registration whose address *extends* an existing document (`d≺d'`): K.σ's precondition permits nested documents, and isolation still holds (Cross-document disjointness covers the nested case via `zeros=2`), so adding a prefix-incomparability check would be a bug that wrongly refuses legal registrations.
- **A content store** — maps content addresses → values, append-only; answers membership and value-at; allocates the next content address under a document.
- **A link store** — maps link addresses → link values (N-endset sequences), append-only; same allocate/lookup/membership operations; checks the L3 value shape. It is *value-only* — no from/to/type endpoint index (Green's spanfilade) is built here; link *discovery* is a higher layer.
- **An address allocator** — per document, per subspace: finds the current maximum same-origin address (or detects none) and applies the structural increment to yield the next address. The heart of the note.
- **A tumbler engine** — `inc(·,k)`, `zeros(·)`, `#E(·)`, the `origin(·)` projection, prefix comparison and lexicographic `max`, T4-validity. Inherited arithmetic, but the substrate's hot path.
- **Precondition gates** — per op: for `K.α`/`K.λ`, home registered (`origin ∈ dom(M)`) and value well-formed (`Val`; L3 for links); for these, freshness and structural address checks are needed only if you accept caller-supplied addresses rather than minting. For `K.σ`, the document address `d` is caller-supplied, so its structural gate is *mandatory*: fresh (`d ∉ dom(M)`), `T4-valid`, `zeros(d)=2`.
- **A persistence/recovery mechanism** *(deployment-prudent, not note-mandated)* — the note's permanence (C0/L12/M1) constrains the *transition relation* (no transition deletes or overwrites), which a purely in-memory store already satisfies; crash-*durability* across restart is a separate deployment concern. Where durability is required, this mechanism is what carries the stores across restart with permanence and order intact.

## Implementation approaches

**The stores' persistence.** The data model *is* an append-only log, so use one. Make a single **journal of allocation events** (register / content-write / link-write) in commit order the authoritative store, and rebuild in-memory maps by **replay on load** — an append-only event log with replayed maps, exactly the shape C0/L12/M1 ask for. Atomicity (SequentialTransitionAxiom) is one atomically-appended record; ordering is journal order; recovery is replay. Bound replay cost with periodic **snapshots** (checkpoint the maps, replay only the tail) — standard WAL+checkpoint. For the *live* representation, hold the stores as **persistent (structurally-shared) immutable maps**: each transition yields a new map sharing structure with the old, so every `Σ` is a cheap immutable value — useful for consistent reads and the versioned views the arrangement layer will want. Persistence ≠ durability: the maps are the working set, the journal is the truth. **Pick:** journal-as-truth + persistent-map-backed stores as a recomputable cache of the journal. This journal+replay shape is a deliberate fit for the substrate's append-only event model, *not* a port of Green — Green persisted via a paged enfilade B-tree with in-core caching and explicit disk flush (`orglincore` flags, `writeenfilades`, `leftbroorfather` back-pointers for cache eviction; evidence Q8), not a log.

**Address allocation (the cursor).** The note derives the next address from `max{a' : origin(a')=d}` (well-defined precisely because C-fin/L-fin make that same-origin set finite, so the `max` exists) — the cursor is **recomputable from the store, not authoritative state.** This is precisely Green's method: stateless query-and-increment — find the highest existing address below the document's subspace bound (`findpreviousisagr`), then increment; there is *no* global `Σ.next` counter. Two realizations:
- *Recompute-always:* on each allocation, query the store for the current max same-origin address in the subspace and `inc(·,0)` (or emit the first form). Nothing to persist, nothing to desync.
- *Cached hint:* keep an in-memory `(document, subspace) → next address` cache for an O(1) common case, **recomputed on a miss** (cold document, post-restart). Lampson's hint pattern verbatim: cache the answer, recompute on miss, never write it as truth.

**Pick:** recompute-always as the default — it is the simpler mechanism, matches Green's deliberately stateless query-and-increment, and the predecessor query is already O(log n); promote to the cached hint only if allocation-latency profiling shows the O(log n)→O(1) saving is worth the cache (mirroring the index decision below). Either way, explicitly **avoid a single global counter** — Green deliberately has none; it would be a concurrency bottleneck and authoritative duplicate state that can drift from the store.

**Indexing.** Allocation needs "max same-origin address under subspace `s`"; lookups need membership and value-at. Because tumbler order is lexicographic and (by ChainPrefixExtension) every same-origin address shares the anchor prefix `b_C(d)` (resp. `b_L(d)`) — so the max same-origin address is the lex-greatest tumbler under that prefix, and a prefix scan returns exactly `d`'s content — an **ordered map keyed by tumbler** answers "max under `d`'s subspace" as a predecessor-below-bound query in O(log n) and gives prefix-range scans (all content under `d`) for free. This is exactly the **granfilade/spanfilade** shape, and `findpreviousisagr` is exactly that predecessor query — the proven approach. A hash index gives faster point lookups but cannot answer predecessor/max-under-prefix and loses range scans, so it must be paired with the cursor cache. **Pick:** ordered tumbler map primary; add a hash index only if point-lookup latency demands it.

**Enfilade now, or not.** Green's granfilade/spanfilade are *enfilades* — cumulative-index trees that compress and do arithmetic over *spans*. But the substrate as specified allocates *singleton* element addresses and leaves the arrangement (`M(d)=∅`) — where span-compression earns its keep — deferred. A plain ordered tumbler map is the simplest thing that honors this note; the enfilade's machinery buys nothing until the arrangement layer arrives. Build the simple ordered store now; adopt enfilade structure later, where V-range→I-range mapping actually needs it. Don't generalize before you must.

**Document registry.** Smallest honoring: an ordered (or hash) set of document tumblers, plus a typed-but-empty arrangement placeholder per document so the deferred arrangement layer slots in without migration. Since `M(d)=∅` always (M2), the substrate need only store membership. A `register` journal record; replay rebuilds the set.

**Home document: recompute vs. denormalize.** `origin(·)` is a pure projection of the address — a hint, never needed as stored state. Default to recomputing it. Green nonetheless **denormalized** the home document into each span record (`homedoc`) to make "group all links under `d`" and follow-link fast — a deliberate locality cache, not a necessity. If insertion-grouping or per-document scans are hot, keep home as a denormalized secondary key; otherwise recompute and avoid the chance of drift.

**Tumbler engine.** Represent tumblers as immutable sequences of components; make the common case fast — `inc(·,0)` touches only the last component, so cheap "replace/extend last" matters. The pure projections (`zeros`, `#E`, `origin`) are recomputable hints — cache only if profiling says so.

A note on **replay safety:** because *content and link* allocation is *deterministic* (the note's whole chain discipline), replay re-derives exactly the same `K.α`/`K.λ` addresses it recorded — so replay can re-mint and self-check those against the journal rather than trusting the recorded addresses blindly. This does *not* extend to `K.σ`: a document address `d` is caller-supplied and only validated, never minted, so it has no derivation to replay against — it must be recorded as truth and re-applied verbatim on replay, not re-derived.

## Guarantees to uphold

- **Permanence** (C0, L12, M1) — *by construction* if the persistence layer is append-only with no update/delete path and the allocator never reuses addresses. Enforce *structurally*: expose no overwrite/delete API; the only writer appends.
- **Uniqueness / freshness** — *by construction* when you **mint** via the cursor, on three independent pillars: *within-document* (the first emission is fresh and each subsequent address strictly advances past the max sibling — ChainEnumerationInjectivity); *cross-document* (distinct documents have prefix-incomparable anchors, so their whole address spaces are disjoint — Cross-document disjointness, which holds *only because* every document passed the `K.σ` `T4-valid ∧ zeros(d)=2` gate); and *cross-subspace* (content under `s_C`, links under `s_L` — T7/SD). Becomes an *active check* (`a ∉ dom(C)∪dom(L)`) only if you accept caller-supplied addresses — a reason to mint, not validate.
- **Store disjointness** (SD) — *by construction* from the fixed subspace partition; no cross-store check needed if the allocator stamps the subspace id.
- **Scoping** (C2, L1a) — the one guarantee needing an *active gate*: reject allocation whose `origin ∉ dom(M)`. The origin itself is always *recoverable* (structural); registration-status is what you must check.
- **Total order** (SequentialTransitionAxiom) — *by construction* with a single serialization point (single-writer journal append); needs active discipline (lock / single-writer queue) under concurrency — but only for *commit-order assignment*: the per-document partition lets distinct documents' allocations proceed in parallel, so a single global writer is a simplifying choice, not a necessity (see *Concurrency granularity* under Decisions).
- **Well-formedness** of addresses (element-level, `#E≥2`, correct subspace, T4-valid) — *by construction* when addresses come only from the allocator. The *value* checks stay active, one per allocation op: `v ∈ Val` at `K.α` and the link-value shape (L3: `N≥3` endsets, non-empty slot 3) at `K.λ`.

Clean summary: for the cursor-minted K.α/K.λ addresses, correctness is *by construction* (mint via the cursor, partition by subspace, never delete), so the *active* gates reduce to (a) for K.α/K.λ, "is the home document registered?" and "is the value well-formed?" (`v ∈ Val` for content, L3 for links); and (b) for K.σ, whose document address is caller-supplied and only validated, the mandatory address gate (fresh `∉ dom(M)`, T4-valid, `zeros=2`).

## How it fits

- **Rests on the tumbler address algebra (ASN-0034)** for `inc`, `zeros`, projections, prefix/order, and the disjointness/partition theorems (T7, T10, T10a) that make freshness and store-disjointness structural.
- **Uses the sibling-stream discipline (ASN-0040)** for the per-document allocator chains (first emission + `inc(·,0)` recurrence, with the ordering/prefix/namespace-disjointness it guarantees).
- **Unifies the content store + arrangement (ASN-0036) and the link store (ASN-0043)** under one state `Σ`, adding the content-side invariants the inherited models lacked.
- **Hands up to:** the **arrangement-mutation layer** that will populate `M(d)` (the reserved, currently-empty slot); **entity allocation** layered on `K.σ` (which originates the document addresses `K.σ` currently only validates); **provenance** (a new `R` component recording who allocated what); **link discovery/indexing** — the substrate's `L` is *value-only* (a link address maps to its endset sequence, with no from/to/type index), so the endpoint-lookup structure that answers `find_links` (Green's spanfilade) is a higher layer, not built here; **coupling constraints** binding allocation to arrangement — Green's `CREATELINK` (`docreatelink`) *fuses three writes* in one operation: the link I-space orgl (the value store here), the spanfilade endpoint-index (the discovery layer just named, absent here), and the POOM/arrangement insertion (`docopy`), whereas `K.λ` here does only the value-store allocation and holds `M` in frame (`M(d)=∅`), so a builder porting from Green must not re-fuse them, assume `K.λ` touches `M`, or prematurely build the spanfilade index the note does not model; and **link withdrawal** — a retraction mechanism that must revisit L12's value-equality clause. (Green has no withdrawal at all: its spanfilade is write-only and there is no `DELETELINK`, so this is genuinely new ground above the substrate.)

This is the floor of the *mutable* store: above the pure address arithmetic, below everything that gives addresses meaning.

## Decisions for the builder

- **Mint vs. validate content/link addresses.** For content and links, the op signatures carry the address, but the precondition determines it. Decide whether your API *mints* (caller passes doc + value, you compute and return the address) or *validates* a caller-supplied one. Minting makes uniqueness structural and is simpler; validating matches the literal signature and supports a deterministic replay self-check (on replay, re-derive the address from the store and compare it against the recorded one rather than trusting the record blindly). Recommend: mint, and return the determined address. (Document addresses are not a choice here: `K.σ` always validates its caller-supplied `d` — minting documents is deferred to the entity-allocation layer.)
- **Cursor: cached hint vs. recompute-always.** Materialize a per-`(doc,subspace)` next-address cache (recomputed on miss) or query the store each time. Either is correct; pick on lookup cost. Never persist the cursor as truth.
- **Index: ordered tumbler map vs. hash (+cursor).** Ordered gives predecessor-below-bound and prefix-range scans (the granfilade shape); hash gives faster points but needs a side cursor and loses range scans. Default ordered; hybridize only under measured point-lookup pressure.
- **Journal shape.** One unified journal (doc/content/link interleaved in commit order — trivially preserves the total order) vs. per-store journals (parallel writes, but need a separate ordering record). Default unified.
- **Concurrency granularity.** The note states transitions as atomic and totally ordered and leaves "what discipline governs concurrent emission across multiple allocators?" open — but the per-document chains are *provably disjoint* (DisjointSubAllocatorChains, Cross-document disjointness), so allocations under different documents cannot collide and may run concurrently; only commit-order assignment (journal append) needs one serialization point. A single global writer is the simplest correct choice; partition writers by document if allocation throughput demands it.
- **Snapshot cadence.** When to checkpoint to bound replay; whether to snapshot the persistent-map root (cheap when the maps are structurally shared) or a flat dump. Pick a trigger (record count / size / time).
- **Tumbler component representation.** Components are conceptually unbounded digit strings; choose fixed-width-with-overflow-check vs. big-integer vs. variable-length encoding before building the engine, since it pervades every operation.
- **Per-document embedding vs. one flat map.** Green embeds per-document orgls inside the global tree (with back-pointers for *disk-cache eviction*). For an in-memory store, one flat ordered map keyed by full tumbler is simpler, and ChainMembershipForOrigin gives per-document range scans without nesting. Embed only if you need per-document paging/eviction; default flat.
- **Value storage.** The common-case link (StandardTriple) is small, but L3 admits arbitrary arity `N ≥ 3` and endsets are *sets*, not scalars — so don't hard-assume links are small; the inline-vs-out-of-line question applies to them too (and the note's Open Questions leave arity *unbounded*). Content may likewise be large. Decide inline-in-store vs. out-of-line blobs referenced from the store — and whether to content-address those blobs for dedup, which is permissible *underneath* the address-identity model but never as the identity itself.
