## What this is

The addressing and naming subsystem — the bottom of the stack. It defines the **tumbler** (the permanent, globally unique address carried by every server, account, document, and element) and the minimal algebra over tumblers — a total order, hierarchical containment parsing, position arithmetic, and an allocation increment — that every higher layer uses to compare, contain, reference, and allocate. Nothing else in the system can name a thing without this.

## Design commitments

These are the locks downstream design cannot violate.

- **An address *is* a finite sequence of unbounded naturals — nothing more.** Identity is sequence identity (T3): no mantissa/exponent, no normalization map, no quotient, no external interpretation. *Forced.* Any representation that admits two encodings of one tumbler breaks equality, breaks transitivity of the order, and breaks uniqueness. The note records this happening concretely in the reference implementation, where an un-normalized leading zero made a positive address compare equal to zero.
- **The space is unbounded in two independent dimensions** — unlimited siblings at a level (T0a) and unlimited depth (T0b). *Forced.* A fixed-width representation is a spec violation unless you separately *prove* the reachable state never reaches the bound. The note documents the reference design crashing on this and widening its bound after the fact.
- **Comparison and containment are intrinsic** — decidable from the two addresses alone, no index, no shared state (T2, T6). *Forced.* This is the whole basis of decentralization; the first containment test that needs a lookup collapses the coordination-free property.
- **Hierarchy is convention projected onto a flat line — not enforced by the algebra** (T4). Comparison and arithmetic treat every component identically; the four-field structure and zeros-as-separators live in the *allocator* and the *parser*. This is a *deliberate, strongly-motivated separation*, not a forced lock: you *could* build hierarchy-aware primitives (T2's intrinsic-comparison requirement does not forbid them), but Green keeps the arithmetic flat and uniform — so comparison and span computation stay simple — and puts hierarchy one layer up. A Lampson "do one thing well" choice, not a constraint whose violation provably breaks a guarantee.
- **Tree containment equals line contiguity** (T5). Every subtree is a contiguous interval; a span between two endpoints under a prefix captures exactly that subtree. *Forced* — spans, link endsets, and content reference all rest on it.
- **Allocation is permanent; the allocated *set* grows monotonically, and there is no deallocation** (T8, T9). The allocated set only grows; gaps are permanent. *Forced as an axiom* — the absence of free/reclaim is deliberate, because links, transclusion, and attribution all assume addresses are never reused or revoked. Read "monotone" narrowly: it describes the allocated *set* (T8), not the *order* in which addresses are handed out — globally, allocation does not proceed in increasing tumbler order (see the next commitment).
- **Tumbler order is structural (depth-first), not temporal.** The tumbler line as a whole does not grow by creation time: when a parent forks a child, the child is *inserted* between the parent and the parent's next sibling, so `2.1 < 2.1.1 < 2.2` holds even when `2.1.1` is created long after `2.2`. Monotone-by-creation order holds only *within* a single allocator's sibling stream (T9, per-allocator); across allocators, sort order is independent of creation time. *Builder gotcha:* the maximum tumbler is not the newest address, tumbler order is not a recency proxy, and any temporal/recency query needs its own ordering — you cannot read creation time off the address.
- **Uniqueness comes from the shape of names, not from consensus** (GlobalUniqueness). No two distinct allocations anywhere ever produce the same address, with zero coordination. *Forced.* It decomposes by how the two allocators relate. **Non-nesting** prefixes — two siblings, two unrelated subtrees — can never collide by T3 alone, since the prefixes diverge at some component and so do the addresses; this case is T10 (PartitionIndependence), and it holds with or without the discipline. **Nesting** prefixes — a parent and a descendant — split further: at *different* levels the addresses carry different zero-counts and differ by T4 level-determination; at the *same* level collision is blocked by **length separation** — every child address is strictly longer than every parent sibling. Length separation is the payoff of the **allocator discipline** (T10a): siblings advance only by shallow increment `inc(·,0)`, which preserves length, so a sibling stream is length-uniform and any child (one deep increment down) lands at strictly greater length. That is *why* `inc(·,0)`-only siblings are mandatory — uniform length is the mechanism that produces uniqueness, not an arbitrary rule; break the discipline, and length separation — and uniqueness — can break. Because the guarantee also rests on T3 and on T4 level-determination, that level-validity is part of the uniqueness story, not orthogonal to it — but for the system's own allocations it is *preserved internally* from a valid root by the discipline (T10a.4/TA5a), not re-checked at admission; admission validation supplies it only for externally-introduced addresses.
- **The subspace identifier is part of the address, not metadata** (T7). Text and link addresses are disjoint by construction.
- **This is an addressing calculus, not a counting calculus.** ⊕ advances a position, ⊖ recovers a displacement; a difference is a *boundary*, not a *count*. *Forced/scoping:* no multiplication, no division, no additive identity or inverse. The minimal structure — an ordered set with an order-preserving shift and an allocation increment — is the *whole* contract; anything more is unused machinery and unverified obligation.

## What must be built

- **A tumbler value** — an immutable, ordered sequence of unbounded-magnitude components, with equality as sequence equality.
- **An intrinsic comparator** — orders two tumblers by left-to-right component scan with "prefix is smaller," consulting nothing external.
- **A field parser and admission validator** — locates zero separators, extracts node/user/document/element fields and the level (zero-count) from the address alone; admits only well-formed addresses (≤3 zeros, no adjacent/leading/trailing zero, never all-zero).
- **The action point primitive** — the index of a displacement's first nonzero component, fixing the hierarchical level at which an operation acts; the shared kernel of ⊕, ⊖, `inc`, the ordinal shift, and the span-length convention, so it earns a named operation rather than inline recomputation.
- **Three position operations** — advance-by-displacement (⊕), displacement-between (⊖), and allocation-increment (`inc`, in sibling and child-spawn modes) — as pure functions on immutable tumblers.
- **Per-domain allocators** — one logical frontier per active prefix, advancing the sibling counter and spawning children under the discipline that guarantees uniqueness. The root base is not arbitrary: Nelson fixes the server field to begin with `1` (all servers descend from it), so the root frontier starts there.
- **A durable, monotone allocation frontier** — each allocator's high-water mark *and the set of active allocators* (a child's existence is recorded by its frontier entry), all of which must survive crashes and never regress. This is the *only* mutable persistent state the note implies.
- **A span** — a (start, length) pair with an intrinsic membership test and the subtree-capture (1-position) convention.
- **The ordinal-only shift apparatus** — element-local advancement that carries the subspace identifier as context, never as an arithmetic operand; an order-preserving, injective, additively-composing strict advance (TS1–TS5, inherited from ⊕).

## Implementation approaches

**Representing the tumbler.** Store the *literal sequence* and make canonical identity (T3) free. This is the single highest-leverage decision. The reference design's mantissa+exponent form required a normalization routine after every operation, a validation guard, and still admitted the leading-zero alias that broke transitivity. Storing the bare sequence deletes that entire bug class — no normalization, no canonicalization, identity by construction. That is the Lampson move: pick the representation where the invariant holds for free.

- *Component magnitude:* arbitrary precision (bignum) honors T0a exactly. This is cheap in the common case because ⊕ has **no carry propagation** — addition touches exactly one component, so bignum cost is paid at one position, not across the address. Bignum also preserves the abstract associativity of ⊕ that finite-width arithmetic can break via overflow — but treat that as a nicety, not a reason: the contract does not depend on associativity, since shifts are applied as single operations, never composed.
- *Sequence storage:* default to an inline small-sequence (most addresses are short). Reach for a persistent, structurally-shared vector only if profiling shows deep tumblers dominate — then siblings and parent/child addresses share their common prefix in memory, and increment is copy-with-change. The tradeoff is persistent vectors' higher constant factor against short flat arrays; for the common short address, inline wins.
- *Encoding at rest:* a variable-length integer encoding (Nelson's "humber") — compact for small components, expanding for large — decouples the journal/wire format from the in-memory form.
- *Fixed-width fast path:* acceptable only as an optional cache with bignum fallback on overflow, never as the system of record. The note is explicit that fixed-width without a proven bound *is* the violation.

**Comparison, parsing, containment.** A direct lexicographic scan is O(min(#a, #b)), intrinsic, and already the simplest correct thing — leave it alone; don't cache what's this cheap. The field parse (fields/zeros/level) is a single scan; if it turns out hot on a containment-query path, attach it to the tumbler as a *hint* — recomputable on a miss, never authoritative — but measure first, because #t is small. Run T4 validation **at admission only**: internal arithmetic operates on flat sequences and must not re-validate or normalize, matching the note's "flat and uniform" arithmetic layer.

**The arithmetic.** Every operation here pivots on one concept — the **action point** of a displacement: the index of its first nonzero component, which fixes the hierarchical level at which the operation acts. Leading zeros mean "stay at these levels"; the first nonzero component says "advance here"; the components past it describe the landing structure. ⊕, ⊖, `inc`, the ordinal shift, and the span-length convention are all organized around it, so make it a named primitive, not an inline recomputation. Implement ⊕/⊖/`inc` straight from the constructive definitions (action point, tail-replacement, divergence-zeroing). They are short, pure, total within their preconditions, and free of I/O and shared state — they belong in the value layer. Do *not* port the reference mantissa arithmetic; the note documents its add routine carrying an operand-order asymmetry that discarded an argument and lost information. A clean from-spec implementation avoids inheriting that. One caution worth wiring into the API contract: ⊕ is **many-to-one** (it discards the start's structure below the action point), so a start position cannot in general be recovered from result-plus-displacement. The displacement round-trip recovers endpoints only under specific length/divergence conditions (D0–D2); outside them, **store endpoints rather than recompute them.**

**Allocation — partition first.** Give each ownership domain (server, account, document, element-stream) its own allocator. These domains *nest* — a document's prefix extends its account's — so their independence is not the non-nesting case (T10). When a child sits at a *different hierarchical level* than its parent (an account minting a document), the two carry different zero-counts and cannot collide by **level determination** (GlobalUniqueness, Case 3). When a child is the *same type* as its parent (a document and its versions), zero-counts match and non-collision rests instead on **length separation** — every child address is strictly longer than its parent's siblings (GlobalUniqueness, Case 4). Both safeguards hold up and down a whole lineage. Truly non-nesting sibling domains are independent by T10 directly. Either way: allocate concurrently across domains, no cross-prefix locks, no coordination. Serialize only *within* a single allocator — it is a counter. The state per allocator is tiny: the current sibling high-water mark, with a new sibling = `inc(frontier, 0)`. **Spawning a child has two modes, and the mode is a real minting decision.** A *same-type* child — a new **version** of the same entity — is `inc(frontier, 1)`: it appends one component and introduces no separator. A *different-type* child — descending a hierarchical level, e.g. an account minting a document — is `inc(frontier, 2)`: it appends the `.0.` field separator and the first child ordinal. Each child gets a fresh sub-allocator. **The mode is gated on the parent's zero-count, and the allocator must enforce the gate:** `inc(·,1)` preserves T4 only when `zeros(frontier) ≤ 3`, `inc(·,2)` only when `zeros(frontier) ≤ 2`, and `inc(·,k≥3)` always breaks T4 (the extra separators create an empty field). Concretely, you may version (k=1) at any level through element, but you may descend a level (k=2) only from a node, user, or document address — never from an element. Because the arithmetic deliberately never re-validates, an allocator that skips this gate emits T4-invalid addresses that propagate silently and break the level-determination on which GlobalUniqueness rests — so the gate is the producer's obligation, not the caller's. (The note records a historical bug where the first document under an account failed to receive its `.0.` separator — the separator is constructed by the allocator, never implied by the algebra, so the allocator must pick the mode deliberately.)

**Durability and recovery of the frontier — the part that needs real care.** T8 plus the note's own open question on counter durability force a choice, and the invariant that decides it is sharp: **never hand out an address twice.** Over-shooting (leaving gaps) is harmless: a skipped slot lies in that allocator's *exclusive* domain — domain-disjointness (under GlobalUniqueness) means no other allocator can ever produce it — and a frontier that never regresses can never revisit it, so the slot is permanently unallocatable: a ghost element, claimed and forever empty. The frontier therefore still correctly bounds the allocated set, ghosts included. Under-shooting (reusing) is fatal once any consumer has persisted a reference. So the cheap, safe designs all lean on *skip-ahead, never reuse*:

- *Durable write per allocation* — synchronous persist of the frontier on every `inc`. Simplest correctness story, but a synchronous write on the hot path. Fine only if allocation is genuinely rare.
- *Batch reservation (hi-lo)* — durably reserve a block of sibling slots, hand them out from memory, persist only when a block is exhausted. On recovery, resume from the last durably-reserved high-water mark; the unused tail of the in-flight block is *skipped*, which is safe. This makes the common case fast (in-memory increment, one durable write per block) and the crash case correct by construction.
- *Journal + replay, with periodic snapshot* — append allocation events to an append-only log; recover frontiers by replaying and taking the per-prefix max; snapshot the frontier registry periodically to bound replay. The log carries atomicity and recovery; the registry is a *hint*, recomputable from the log if lost or corrupt.

These compose, and the composition is what I'd build: **a journal of record for what was allocated, a recomputable per-prefix frontier registry as a fast-start snapshot, and in-memory batch reservation to keep the hot path off synchronous writes.** If the surrounding system already provides an append-only journal with a replay-recovered registry, ride that same log so the allocation frontier shares one recovery story rather than introducing a second — but verify that substrate exists before assuming it; the journal+registry+reservation design above stands on its own if it does not.

One case the sibling-counter framing hides: **spawning a child activates a new allocator, and that activation is itself allocation state.** T10a lets each `(frontier, k')` spawn at most once; if the spawn record is lost on a crash, the child can be re-spawned from the same parent slot and its base re-handed-out — the very "never hand out an address twice" violation this section exists to prevent. So the new active prefix and its base must be journaled and made durable *before* any child address is handed out, and the frontier registry must carry child frontiers alongside sibling ones (recording a child's frontier is also how its existence is recorded). The "only mutable persistent state" is therefore the active-allocator set together with each allocator's frontier — still one structure, just not a single counter.

Note the corollary the note insists on: allocation and content are **decoupled**. An address can be permanently claimed (in the journal/frontier) with nothing in the content map — Nelson's "ghost elements." "Is this allocated?" and "does this hold content?" are two queries against two structures; keep them so.

**Spans.** Represent (start, length); membership is two intrinsic comparisons, and subtree capture is the 1-position convention (a length whose action point sits at the prefix's level). The constructor must enforce T12 well-formedness: **ℓ > 0 and actionPoint(ℓ) ≤ #s** (equivalently, ℓ has fewer leading zeros than #s) — that is exactly what makes the endpoint s⊕ℓ exist and the span non-empty (TA-strict). No index needed for membership. One subtlety the address-admission rule must *not* leak into the span layer: an all-zero tumbler is rejected **as an address**, but it is a legitimate span endpoint — the zero sentinel denotes an *unbounded* endpoint or a lower bound (TA6). How containment and intersection must behave when an endpoint is the zero sentinel is left open by the note (an open question, not a settled rule), so flag it as a builder concern rather than reusing the address validator at the span boundary. The *inverse* query — "which spans cover address *t*?", i.e. link-endset lookup — is an indexing problem you hand off; Green's **spanfilade** is the proven structure for it. Out of scope here, but the seam is at this boundary.

**Ordinal-only shift (TA7a).** Carry the subspace identifier as structural context and do the arithmetic on the bare ordinal. The note shows why this isn't optional: feeding a full `[subspace, ordinal]` position into ⊖ finds the divergence at the subspace identifier and no-ops. Bake the strip/shift/reattach into the element-stream API so a caller cannot accidentally shift a full position and silently get nothing. The shift inherits ⊕'s structure — it is order-preserving (TS1), injective (TS2), additively-composing (TS3), and a strict advance monotone in amount (TS4, TS5) — which is exactly what lets you maintain a sorted element stream and keep addresses distinct under shift, so I-stream allocation and V-enfilade traversal are safe to build on it.

## Guarantees to uphold

**Hold by construction** (free, given the representation choices above):
- Canonical identity (T3) — free *iff* you store the literal sequence, not a mantissa/exponent.
- Total order and intrinsic comparison (T1, T2) — free from the lexicographic scan.
- Contiguous subtrees (T5) — free from the order alone (T1 + T3 + the prefix relation); the proof takes an arbitrary prefix and never parses fields, so contiguity holds for *any* prefix whether or not the address is well-formed. No validation required.
- Decidable containment and subspace disjointness (T6, corollary of T4; T7, corollary of T3, T4) — free *provided* T4 validation holds at admission, since both read the parsed field structure.
- Global uniqueness (GlobalUniqueness — T10 is only its non-nesting case) — free *given* three things enforced elsewhere: the allocator discipline (T10a, below), canonical identity (T3, above), and well-defined T4 level-determination. Not unconditional — the parent/child cases turn on length separation and on the zero-count level being well-defined. For the system's own allocations that level-validity is *preserved internally* from a valid root by the discipline (T10a.4/TA5a), not re-checked at admission; admission validation supplies it only for externally-introduced addresses.

**Require active enforcement** (the runtime must guard these):
- Unbounded space (T0) — use arbitrary precision, or discharge the proof that the bound is unreachable. The default fixed-width choice silently violates this.
- Well-formedness (T4) — validate at every admission boundary; reject adjacent/leading/trailing zeros, more than three zeros, and any all-zero sequence used **as an address**. The "as an address" qualifier is load-bearing: the same all-zero tumbler is a legitimate **span sentinel** (unbounded endpoint / lower bound, TA6), so this rejection lives at address admission, not in the span layer. System-allocated addresses are already T4-valid by discipline (T10a.4), so this boundary guards *externally-introduced* addresses — which is exactly where the T6/T7 containment queries need validated fields.
- Allocator discipline (T10a) — siblings by `inc(·,0)`; children by one `inc(·,k∈{1,2})`, *gated on the parent's zero-count* (k=1 needs `zeros(t)≤3`, k=2 needs `zeros(t)≤2`; k≥3 always breaks T4). This is what *produces* uniqueness and what keeps every system-allocated address T4-valid without re-checking, so the discipline — gate included — must be enforced inside the allocator, not assumed of callers.
- Permanence and monotonicity (T8, T9) — no deallocation in the API, and a durable frontier that never regresses across crashes; the durable state must include the active-allocator set (so a child cannot be re-spawned and its base reused after a crash), not just sibling counters. This is the most important runtime invariant in the note and its only persistent mutable state.

## How it fits

This is the foundation; it leans on nothing below it but ℕ arithmetic. It hands tumblers *upward* as: the ordering key for content mapping (the **granfilade** and the enfilade family key on tumblers), the endpoint type for links and spans (endsets are span-sets; the **spanfilade** indexes them), and the position type for the element stream — **I-stream** element allocation and **V-enfilade** traversal, both driven by the ordinal shift. It deliberately does **not** provide version-derivation history: the document field records who allocated under whom, not what was copied from what (T6(d)); derivation needs a separate version graph — a handoff, not a responsibility of this layer. The prefix-as-ownership-domain model here is the substrate the ownership/baptism notes build on, and the span definition here is what span algebra and arrangement operations extend.

## Decisions for the builder

These are genuine engineering picks the note leaves open — distinct from its spec-level open questions.

- **Component representation:** bignum (recommended default) vs. varint-only vs. fixed-width-with-fallback. Fixed-width only with a discharged finite-model obligation.
- **Sequence storage:** inline small-sequence vs. persistent shared vector — decided by whether deep tumblers actually dominate your workload.
- **Durability scope — global-history vs. per-session** (the note's actual open question): must the frontier be durable as one global history, or only per-session? Because allocators are already per-domain, a per-session model is real — each session durably claims a per-domain block, hands out from it in memory, and recovers by skipping the unused tail, with no single global log. Weigh this against the global journal-of-record deliberately; don't default to one log.
- **Frontier durability strategy:** per-allocation durable write vs. batch reservation vs. journal+snapshot. Within a global-history design, default to journal-of-record + recomputable frontier registry + in-memory batch reservation, reusing an existing append-only journal if the surrounding system provides one.
- **Recovery cadence and granularity** (the operational knob beneath the scope question above): how often to persist the high-water mark — every allocation, every block, every checkpoint — and how aggressively to over-allocate on clean shutdown vs. crash. Over-allocation is always safe; reuse is never.
- **Where validation lives:** validate-at-admission and trust internal producers (recommended) vs. validate-everywhere. The former keeps the arithmetic flat and uniform.
- **Within-prefix concurrency model:** actor-per-prefix vs. lock-per-prefix vs. lock-free counter. Cross-prefix is coordination-free regardless; this choice is only about the single counter.
- **Subspace-context plumbing:** carry the subspace id alongside ordinals everywhere vs. an element-stream API that strips and reattaches it (recommended — it makes TA7a un-violable by callers).
- **Fixed-width hot cache:** whether to keep one for comparison/arithmetic with bignum fallback — a pure optimization, off until measured pressure justifies it.
- **At-rest encoding:** the humber varint vs. a simpler length-prefixed bignum sequence — a journal-format choice you can defer.
