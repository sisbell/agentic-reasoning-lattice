This is a strong, unusually complete digest — it addresses all six of the note's open questions, keeps Green specifics in their place as evidence rather than letting them drive the spec, and the hold-by-construction / require-active-enforcement split is exactly the right Lampson framing. I checked every approach against the note's commitments and found no *soundness* violation: the cached-counter, journal-as-commit, persistent-map, and lazy-materialisation proposals are all kept as hints/accelerators with the registry/journal as authority, and none of them reintroduces the value-based identity the note forbids. The defects below are real but modest.

## Revision list

1. **"What must be built" / "Guarantees → Require active enforcement": the caller-authorization precondition is never surfaced as an enforcement point.** The note makes `pfx(π) ≼ A` a *precondition* (CND.pre): the operation may only proceed for a principal who owns `A`. The digest mentions caller-ownership only descriptively ("on behalf of a principal who owns that account") and otherwise treats ownership purely as a property of the *result* (`owns(π, d)`). A builder reading the exhaustive build/enforce lists will not see that the operation must gate on `owns(π, A)` as admission control and reject unauthorized callers. Add an explicit item: *Authorization gate — verify `owns(π, A)` before baptism, reusing the same prefix oracle as admission control* — and distinguish it from result-ownership (precondition assumed in the contract, enforced by the implementation). This is the one load-bearing commitment the digest drops.

2. **"Design commitments → What is merely conventional": the allocation arithmetic is mis-filed as "conventional (not forced)."** The digest's own gloss — "a consequence of the sibling-stream definition, not a free choice" — contradicts the label. A fact derived from the allocator definition (`A_doc(A)=S(A,2)`) is neither a builder's free choice *nor* an independent lock; it is *derived*. Relabel: the arithmetic is a derived consequence (so not something a builder picks or must separately honor), and only the cached-counter creation *timing* is genuinely conventional. Don't lump a derived fact under "conventional."

3. **"Design commitments" (first bullet): the cost parenthetical overreaches its grounding.** "Logarithmic in registry size given that tree's balanced, B-tree-like structure" is supported by neither the note (which commits to *stores-nothing*, not a complexity class) nor the evidence (which shows a rightmost tree descent / "bounded scan" but never characterizes the granfilade as balanced or the lookup as logarithmic — Q11, Q20). It is also mis-placed: a performance characterization stated as a "lock." Soften to "a bounded tree descent (not a linear sweep) plus one index insert," and either drop "logarithmic / balanced / B-tree-like" or mark it explicitly as a *design expectation*, not a Green-verified fact and not a design commitment.

4. **"Design commitments" and "Decisions → Allocator materialisation": sharpen the CND.A-act "moot" framing.** Calling it "moot" undersells that in the abstract spec CND.A-act is load-bearing — it discharges `ActivatedEmission`, a conjunct of the correctness criterion. The right statement is that it is still *discharged*, not unnecessary: under the stateless default, `Activated(A_doc(A))` reduces to `A`'s own presence in the registry (the under-`A` scan is well-defined the instant `A` is recorded), so it costs zero extra per-account state. Say "discharged by `A ∈ E` itself," not "moot."

5. **"What must be built → document-address allocation contract": add well-formedness to the property list.** "Fresh, monotone, permanent, unique, level-distinct" omits *validity* — the note verifies `T4-valid(d) ∧ zeros(d)=2`, i.e. the allocator must emit a well-formed document-level tumbler. Add "valid/well-formed at document level (`zeros = 2`)" as a property the allocator must *produce*, not merely a consequence to assume.

6. **"Implementation approaches": reconcile the persistent map with the journal-as-authority.** The persistent structurally-shared map (allocation subsection) and the append-only journal (durability subsection) are pitched separately; a reader could take both as competing authorities. The digest resolves this in the durability subsection ("the journal is the authority; the in-memory registry and counter are hints") but doesn't carry that back to the persistent-map pitch. State it once where the persistent-DS idea is raised: the persistent map (and any frontier counter) is the in-memory *hint* rebuilt by replay; the journal is the durable *authority*.

Solid and worth leaving alone: the version/document-separation treatment (forced requirement, conventional mechanism), the place/content firewall (structural-not-conventional, well-grounded in Q16), the idempotency/retry decision (a genuine builder concern correctly derived from the note's own locks), and the altitude throughout — Green function names appear only as cited evidence, never as proposed signatures.
