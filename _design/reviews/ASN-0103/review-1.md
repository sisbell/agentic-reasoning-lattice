This is a strong, well-grounded digest — the allocation model, the version/document separation, and the crash-recovery reasoning are largely accurate and faithful to both the note and the evidence. Below are the improvements that still matter, real defects first.

## Revision list

**1. "Design commitments" → "A document is a place, not a value": drop or correct the O(1) claim.** The bullet says the place/content seam "is what makes 'create' an O(1), stores-nothing operation." "Stores-nothing" is right and well-grounded; **"O(1)" is not.** The forced mechanism is a max-query/frontier scan over the registry (your own default approach), and in green a tree insert with WIDTH propagation to the root (evidence Q16/Q20) — neither is O(1). The digest also contradicts itself: the cached counter is later said to "buy O(1)," conceding the default is not. And the *seam* (which stores are touched) bears on what is stored, not on cost, so it cannot be what "makes it O(1)." Fix: delete "O(1)," or state the honest cost — a bounded prefix-scan + index insert (log/depth in registry size), storing no content.

**2. "What must be built" / "Decisions for the builder" (Allocator materialisation): reconcile the per-account allocator *object* with the stateless default.** "What must be built" leads with "a per-account document-address allocator," and a builder decision asks when to "materialise" it (account-creation vs first-doc). But your recommended default — stateless recompute — holds *no per-account state at all*, and green confirms there is no per-account document allocator object (Q1-account: "single flat tree, no per-account sub-structure… nothing to pre-initialize"; the only thing built at first-doc-creation is the new doc's own POOM, not an allocator). So CND.A-act is discharged with zero per-account state, and the "materialisation timing" decision is *moot under the default* — it arises only if you choose the cached counter. Fix: name the requirement as the *contract* `next_document_address(A)` (fresh/monotone/unique/level-distinct), realizable with no per-account object; scope the materialisation-timing decision to the cached-counter branch.

**3. "Design commitments" → "Uniqueness is decentralised — no registry is consulted": restore the 'central' qualifier.** As written this collides with the rest of the digest, which *builds* "an entity registry (the baptismal record)" and *allocates* by "query the entity registry for the maximum… under A." The note's claim (CND.refer) is narrower: no *central* registry / no cross-owner coordination is needed for uniqueness, because `d` is minted under an owned subtree (B8) — the *local* registry is still read to find the frontier. Fix: say "no *central* coordination / no cross-owner uniqueness authority," and reserve "registry" for the local baptismal record the allocator does query.

**4. "Implementation approaches" → "Entity registry and durability": the links.jsonl / paths.json claims are ungrounded here.** "this repo's own working substrate (links.jsonl + a paths.json registry rebuilt by replay)… the proven local instance" and "the repo's atomic registry-write fix (the paths.json torn-read repair)" are specific source-level assertions grounded in neither the note nor the verified evidence (which is entirely udanax-green C). Presenting them as "proven" and "the concrete pattern" imports unverifiable specifics into a load-bearing recommendation. Fix: add a checkable citation to where that substrate/fix lives, or genericize to the design — "an append-only journal as the commit point; the in-memory registry and any frontier counter rebuilt by replay; an atomic registry write so concurrent readers never see a torn record." The green-evidence half of this section (Q20: find and record are separate steps, so monotonicity is not crash-safe) is solid — keep it.

**5. "What must be built" / "Guarantees" (Permanence): "never removes an address" forecloses an open question.** "Never *reuses*" is robust (GlobalUniqueness/T8 forbid reuse regardless). "Never *removes*" is exactly what the note leaves open — Open Question 4 asks under what conditions a created-but-never-populated document may be removed and what permanence guarantee removal must respect. Stating it as a closed lock drops a question the digest otherwise engages (recovery, concurrency, write-readiness, effective-owner). Fix: keep "never reuses"; for removal, say P1/entity-permanence is the current lock for this operation, but whether empties may ever be reclaimed is open (Q4), and any reclamation must still honor no-reuse.

(sharpen) "Implementation approaches" → "Subspace activation": separate the eager empty arrangement from the lazy subspaces.** Facts are right (and you correctly avoided the "zero crums" trap — green plants one zero-width crum, Q18), but "Do not pre-build the document's content or link stores" sits beside "the minimal birth state: a single zero-width placeholder (an empty POOM)," which reads as if nothing is built.
