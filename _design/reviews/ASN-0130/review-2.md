Most of this digest is accurate and well-grounded. The Green-evidence usage is correctly cited — single-insertion contiguity (Q5), caller-supplied query state with historical versions first-class (Q2), and the never-forwarded scope filter as a cautionary parallel (Q2, Bug 015) — and the split between the two Q5 readings on whether CREATELINK breaks cross-insertion contiguity is faithfully reported rather than papered over. "How it fits," the *canonicalization-not-required* call (which actively avoids the value-identity trap), and the hint/recovery discipline are solid; no change needed there. The items below, most-important first.

**1. Implementation approaches → Expansion: the structural-sharing recommendation needs a distinct-names caveat, or it invites a determinacy violation.** The note's determinacy contract is that two evaluators produce *the same concrete term, not merely α-equivalent* — and the renaming picks "the least-indexed names occurring nowhere in the term under construction." So in a diamond, the two occurrences of a referent get *distinct* expansion names (ν₁ vs ν₂): the post-rename subterms are **not identical**. Naive hash-consing of the named expanded term therefore won't share diamonds (defeating the stated memory goal), and any scheme that forces the occurrences identical produces a term the spec forbids. The memo recommendation (template + per-splice renaming) is the sound part; the sharing claim must add: *structural sharing must be on a name-agnostic / renaming-deferred representation (de Bruijn or explicit substitution), with canonical expansion names assigned only at materialization in the spec's order.* Without this, a builder either gets no sharing or breaks the determinacy two conformant implementations must agree on.

**2. Implementation approaches → Storing the artifact: the repo-storage mapping is asserted as fact but is grounded in neither the note nor the evidence.** "the term's bytes go into the content journal, the `pdef`/`pd_stable` tuples append to the `links.jsonl` audit journal, and the start-address index is a `paths.json`-style registry" — these specific artifacts appear in no source I can check against, and this looks like a primarily spec-side repo. If it is spec-only, or uses different storage, this misdirects the whole storing approach. Either cite where the repo establishes `links.jsonl`/`paths.json`/the content journal, or mark the mapping explicitly as illustrative ("if your store has X, then…"); do not state it flatly.

**3. What must be built / Registration validation: surface that the typing check (iii) and the active-check (iv) read different slices and can disagree.** The note makes a point of this ("The two are distinct"): (iii) rests on *ever*-registration (`sig` defined via the audit slice — permanent), (iv) on *active* registration. A new definition referencing a since-de-registered referent **type-checks fine at (iii) but is rejected at (iv)**. The digest separates the two lookups in the approaches section but never states they consult different slices or that they disagree. For the validator builder this is load-bearing: the type-checker must consult `sig` (ever-registration) and must *not* fail on de-registered referents — only (iv) gates on active status. Add this explicitly.

**4. Certification: lead with the per-definition one-atom query, not the caveated universal lint.** The note's headline downstream value is that "is this definition certified stable" becomes a one-atom PL query, and it says outright "the exact win is the per-definition atom `is_pd_stable(t)` itself" (exact at starts, caveat-free). The digest surfaces only the *universal* lint `(A t ∈ M_pdef :: …)` — which is the form the note flags as *spuriously tripped* by legitimately non-Boolean definitions. As written, the digest foregrounds the caveated form and buries the clean win. Present `is_pd_stable(t)` (the per-definition, exact, caveat-free query any consumer can run) as the primary deliverable, and the universal lint as the secondary, domain-caveated form.

**5. Guarantees → Surface seal vs the born-nullified caveat: distinguish PR-DISC from the additional surface-discipline (DR) that born-nullified-freedom needs.** The surface-seal guarantee says the seal establishes PR-DISC "to which every by-construction guarantee is scoped." That is right for permanence, uniqueness, `sig`-well-definedness, and acyclicity — but the note treats success ⟺ referenceable (no born-nullified deposit) as needing the *additional* "surface-disciplined" condition (PR0: "On derivations **additionally** surface-disciplined, C3 vanishes (DR)"), which is about retraction discipline, not the emit seal. The digest correctly flags surface-discipline in the registration bullet but never connects it to the seal guarantee, leaving a reader to infer "seal ⟹ PR-DISC ⟹ no born-nullified," which is false. State that the emit seal alone does not rule out born-nullified deposits — that requires the separate DR/retraction condition.

**6. Implementation approaches → Expansion: drop the "Rust's `im` crate" naming.** Naming a specific library is below design altitude. State the concept ("a persistent immutable-tree representation with structural sharing / deferred renaming") and leave the crate to the builder, or mark it as a bare example.

**7. (minor) Certification operation: note it is a separate call and a failed certification leaves the registration standing.** The note is explicit that `certify_pd_stable` is not a mode of `register_pred` — "rejection scope is the certificate alone." The digest treats them as separate operations but never states that a cert failure must not roll back or affect the underlying registration. Worth one line so a builder doesn't couple them.
