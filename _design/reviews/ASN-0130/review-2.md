This digest is unusually thorough and mostly accurate — the contiguity/concurrency analysis (grounded precisely in evidence Q1/Q5, correctly separating Green's intra-call run-minting from the consistency model's run-spanning critical section), the structural-reads-only guarantee with its "don't defer resolution into the evaluator" warning, the evaluation-grounding via Q2, and the lint-scoping builder decision are all genuinely solid. Two material problems remain.

## Revision list

**1. [DEFECT] — `Implementation approaches → Resolution and expansion`, recommendation (a): ST⁺ classification is NOT per-address memoizable, and grouping it with typing makes the recommended certifier unsound.** The digest claims "denotation, typing, and ST⁺ are all compositional and α-invariant" and recommends driving "ST⁺-classification by memoized recursion over the DAG." But `sig`/typing is soundly per-address memoizable (`sig(r)` is fixed regardless of arguments); ST⁺ is *argument-sensitive at reference nodes*. A referent's parameter is a bound constant in the referent's own classification — e.g. `helper(x:ℕ) = count(L_W) ≥ x` is ST⁺ *via the very aggregate extension the digest elsewhere explains* — but a call site substitutes an arbitrary term there: `host = helper(count(L_X))` expands to `count(L_W) ≥ count(L_X)`, which flips as the two grow apart and is *correctly refused* by the note's flat classifier (the threshold is neither an ℕ literal nor a bound parameter). A cached per-address `helper ∈ ST⁺` verdict reused at `host`'s node certifies it — an **unsound** certifier, violating the note's soundness guarantee; PR5a(iii) classifies the flat `expand(a)` precisely to avoid this. (The digest's own "What to cache" list correctly omits ST⁺ verdicts, contradicting this paragraph.) Fix: carve ST⁺ out — only `sig` and resolution are per-address memoizable; ST⁺ must classify the notional flat `expand(a)`, or any DAG-recursion must substitute call-site arguments into the referent's body *before* classifying (no per-referent verdict cache). Say plainly that this saves the string-materialization cost but not the classification work for ST⁺.

**2. [DEFECT] — `What must be built → registration surface` and `Implementation approaches → the active-pdef-by-start index`: the load-bearing two-gate (iii ever / iv active) distinction is missing, and the index paragraph mis-frames slice usage.** The note works an entire adversary check (step 5) around this: (iii) typing consults *ever*-registration (audit slice — is `sig(r)` defined? — catching never-registered / frontier-ghost referents) and (iv) endorsement consults *active*-registration (catching *de-registered* referents, which still type fine at (iii) yet are rejected at (iv)). The digest lists both checks but never states the distinction, never states (iv)'s purpose — *refuse new references to de-registered-but-ever-registered definitions while existing references and evaluation survive* — and its index paragraph ("active at registration, ever at the evaluate precondition") omits that registration's typing (iii) *also* reads the audit/ever slice. A builder could collapse (iv) into typing ("if it types, it's registered") and wrongly admit references to de-registered definitions. Fix: state the two-gate distinction explicitly; correct the index paragraph so the audit slice is shown used at *both* registration-typing and evaluate, the active slice at registration-endorsement.

**3. [SHARPENING] — `Design commitments`: add the certification lock.** The most load-bearing certification commitment is absent from the locks list: the certified object is the *expansion* (pure `expand(a)`), **not** the artifact's reference-bearing spelling (PR5 *Purity* — PD0's classes are on pure terms), hence certification is offered only on view-independent expansions and "uncertified = unknown, not unstable." Covered in implementation, but it belongs among the locks.

**4. [SHARPENING] — `Guarantees to uphold`: add certificate permanence.** PR5a's permanence-for-the-slice (a `pd_stable` tuple permanently proves the fixed expansion was Boolean-sorted, view-independent, and ST⁺ at deposit — all reading only immutable content/signature) appears only loosely as "permanent verdict" in implementation. Add it, with its parallel to PR1's (iv): a certificate *outlives* its target's active registration (a later de-registration leaves it standing; harmless, since the lint's active-view domain `M_pdef` drops de-registered targets).

**5. [SHARPENING] — `Implementation approaches → Resolution and expansion`, the monotonic-counter remark.** The α-invariance argument is sound, but pin the precondition: the fresh-name scheme must remain a *deterministic function of content* (per-expansion-local counter / fixed traversal). A global counter would make `expand(a)` depend on call history, breaking the cross-evaluator "same concrete term" property the commitments section locks (and which licenses shared caching). The counter yields a different *representative* than the note's rule, not non-determinism — state it that way.
