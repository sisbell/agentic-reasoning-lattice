## Revision list

**1. Allocator section + the "monotonic, document-scoped" commitment: state that monotonicity is over the *permanent content store*, not the current arrangement.** The note devotes a boundary case to exactly this (empty-subspace sub-case b): deleting all of a document's content empties the arrangement but leaves the content region non-empty, so re-insertion allocates *above* the old, now-unarranged addresses — addresses are never reused across a delete/reinsert cycle. The note is explicit: the K.α start "is selected by the content region `{a' ∈ dom(C) : origin(a') = d}` … *not* by the empty arrangement, because content is append-only and persists through arrangement contraction." The digest's commitment "advance past every address that document already owns" is ambiguous (currently-arranged vs. ever-allocated), and the recommended hint cache ("recomputable… by querying the max") never says max *of what* nor warns against resetting on an emptied document. A builder who keys the hint to the arrangement, or zeroes it when a document empties, will reuse addresses and silently break origin-identity — the very failure (a link binding the wrong occurrence) the digest elsewhere calls forbidden. Fix: pin freshness/monotonicity to the never-shrinking content store's full per-document allocation history; the hint recomputes from the content store and never resets on an empty arrangement. This also weakens the "freshness holds by construction" guarantee, which silently assumes this discipline.

**2. Approach 5 (and the unifying-move journal): the recover-by-replay recommendation is missing the checkpoint requirement, and "simplest" understates its cost.** "The in-memory structures are caches — rebuilt by replay, never repaired," with "optionally the content map itself," implies full-history replay on every restart. For a permanent, ever-growing hypertext — the actual use case — that violates common-case-fast and is not viable without checkpoints/snapshots, which the digest never mentions. The core move (log the composite for all-or-nothing atomicity) is sound and Lampson-endorsed; narrow it: (a) add the checkpoint/snapshot caveat; (b) drop the unqualified "simplest, atomic by construction" for full-replay-rebuild; (c) present persisting C/M/R directly (Green's proven approach — permascroll + maintained POOM/spanfilade, *no replay*, which the digest itself notes) as the durable baseline, with the log as a cross-structure atomicity + index-rebuild layer rather than the sole persistence ground truth. (C as the *semantic* ground truth while a log is the *persistence* ground truth is a legitimate layering — say that plainly instead of leaning on the "C remains conceptual ground truth" disclaimer.)

**3. Approach 4: "two different things, built differently" overstates the structural split between provenance and discoverability.** The *conceptual* distinction is correct and worth keeping — R (placing-document) must be materialized because it is not address-decodable under transclusion, whereas discoverability can be recomputed by scanning arrangements. But the digest then attributes *both* to the spanfilade/DOCISPAN, and in Green they are literally one structure: the DOCISPAN entry's `homedoc` field *is* the provenance record and the entry itself *is* the reverse index (Q1/Q16/Q19). As written, a builder could build two redundant stores. Clarify that a single reverse-index structure carries both, distinguished by what it must persist (the placing-document record) vs. what it can also recompute (the touch-sets).

**4. Pin down which structure realizes the content store `C`.** The unifying move calls "the permascroll" the append-only content store; approach 1 calls "the granfilade" the content store. These name different layers (the granfilade is the I-address-keyed map that resolves to content — the model's `C`; the permascroll is the underlying append-only log it indexes). State the relationship once so the builder knows `C` is realized by the I-address-keyed ordered map over an append-only log, and doesn't treat permascroll and granfilade as interchangeable.

**5. Ground the content-granularity decision in Green's precedent.** The digest rightly flags "what a content unit is" as open, but the evidence settles Green's choice: per-character/byte I-addressing — each unit gets its own I-address, while leaf crums pack many (up to `GRANTEXTLENGTH`) and remain individually addressable (Q14). Cite this so the decision rests on a precedent, while still leaving grapheme/blob alternatives open.
