Reviewed against the note, its claims, and the Q1/Q11–Q20 evidence. The digest is strong on the two-layer story, the piece-table-as-denotation move, the two-blade guard, and pull-model link resolution. The defects below are concentrated in one place — the treatment of provenance — and in what the digest drops from the note's open frontier.

## Revision list

**1. "Provenance is derivable, demote it to a cache" is wrong — the note makes R primary state, the evidence materializes it, and transclusion breaks decode-from-address.** (Design commitments → "Origin is recoverable…"; Approaches §4; Decisions → "Is provenance materialized or decoded?")
The digest says the provenance relation R is "derivable, not primary — a fact that turns one whole subsystem into a cache," "decode it from the address," "Don't persist it as authoritative duplicate state." This contradicts the note on three counts:
 - The note lists R as a **component of the extended state** `Σ = (C, L, E, M, R)`, writes to it (`R' = R ∪ {…}`, I-PROV), and imposes a coverage invariant on it (P7a: every address must carry a record). R is primary, maintained, monotone state — not a derived view.
 - R is defined as "the document that **placed** it." Under transclusion the placing document ≠ the address's origin, so R is *not* decodable from the prefix — which is exactly what the note's third Open Question raises.
 - Evidence Q1 is explicit: the document-coupling record (DOCISPAN, `homedoc = docisa`) is a **separate explicit write**, "not automatic from I-address allocation" (and APPEND omits it). Green materializes this coupling; it does not decode it.
What *is* true: `origin(a)` — the minting namespace — is in the prefix (`origin(a)=d`, Q1), so for INSERT-minted content R coincides with origin and is recomputable. Fix: distinguish `origin(a)` (decodable hint, INSERT case only) from R (primary/materialized, the note's and Green's choice); drop "turn the subsystem into a cache / don't persist it"; and flag transclusion as exactly where decode-from-address fails.

**2. The digest drops three of the note's four Open Questions, including the one that contradicts item 1.** (no "open/unsettled" section beyond the concurrency mention)
The note closes with four open questions; the digest carries only #2 (concurrent freshness). Missing:
 - **#3 provenance under transclusion** — directly tempers item 1; a builder told "decode R from the address" needs to know the note flags this as unsolved precisely because origin ≠ placing-document under transclusion.
 - **#1 insertion at a transclusion-shared position** — the digest's isolation story (insert into `d` is invisible to `d'`) is correct but does not address inserting *at a position whose content is shared*, which the note explicitly leaves open.
 - **#4 contiguity obligations after later fragmentation** — load-bearing for the coalescing recommendation (item 5): a coalesced run can be fragmented by later edits, and the note flags those obligations as unsettled.
Add a short open-issues note so the builder knows where the settled region ends; #3 must qualify item 1, #4 must qualify the coalescing advice.

**3. The "unifying move" mis-grounds event-sourcing as udanax and demotes the note's actual ground truth.** (Approaches → "The unifying move")
Claiming a journal-of-operations replayed on load is "exactly the spirit of the udanax permascroll: log the operation, recompute the views" overstates the grounding. The permascroll is an append-only **content** store (= the note's `C`, the stated "append-only ground truth"), and Green's enfilades (POOM/spanfilade) are **persisted and maintained**, not reconstructed by replaying an operation log. Recasting `C`, `M`, and `R` as "derived projections rebuilt by replay" inverts the note's model (C primary, M primary per-document, R primary) and departs from how Green actually persists state. Don't reject journaling — its atomicity payoff is real — but present it honestly as a *modern alternative layered for atomicity*, keep `C` (append-only content) as the faithful ground truth with maintained indices, and note that "store the allocated start" stops being a nice-to-have and becomes **required** once content is rebuilt by replay, since I-address permanence then depends on deterministic reproduction. Also soften "this repo's working substrate (`links.jsonl`/`paths.json`)" — that is asserted as fact but is grounded in neither the note, the evidence, nor documented Green structure.

**4. The contraction-then-extension decomposition is mislabeled "merely conventional" — within the K-vocabulary it is forced.** (Design commitments → "Merely conventional (not forced)")
The note *proves* no single atomic can do the arrangement change ("not itself one of these atomics… K.μ⁺'s prior-domain agreement forbids… K.μ⁻ and K.μ~ both forbid"), so given the atomics, the two-step structure is **forced**, not conventional. What is free is the *implementation* fusing the two steps (which the digest's surrounding prose gets right). Relabel: the spec-level decomposition is forced by the vocabulary; only the physical fusing is the builder's latitude.

**5. Sharpen the coalescing decision to its real predicate, and tie it to the fragmentation question.** (Decisions → "Do you coalesce…"; Approaches §3)
"Coalesce contiguous same-origin runs" is right but under-specified for a builder. The predicate is **same origin AND I-adjacency** — the new run's start must immediately follow the left neighbor's end on the same content chain. This is exactly the note's backward-merge condition (`a = inc(a_prev,0) = shift(M(d)(q_{J-1}),1)`, I-adjacent via M7) and Green's `isanextensionnd` check (`reach == origin` ∧ same `homedoc`, Q15). State the negative case the digest already half-raises: same-origin runs separated by an intervening allocation (e.g., a CREATELINK between two inserts, Q11/Q15/Q20) are **not** I-adjacent and must **not** coalesce. Connect to Open Question #4: coalesced runs are expected to fragment under later edits, and the obligations there are open.

**6. Minor accuracy in the commitments and allocator framing.**
 - "INSERT is *purely additive* on content, links, and the entity set" — INSERT is additive on **content** (and on **R**, which the sentence omits); on links and entities it is **frame-invariant** (F-LINK, F-ENT: unchanged), not additive. Tighten to "additive on content and provenance; leaves links and entities untouched."
 - "the spec forbids only authoritative duplicate counter state" — the note/evidence *describe* an allocator with no counter; they don't *forbid* a correctly-maintained one. The reason to avoid it is the Lampson don't-duplicate-authoritative-state principle (your own), not a spec prohibition. Attribute it correctly.
 - "a persistent structure gives [append-only] by construction" — persistence gives immutable versions/snapshots; the monotone-never-remove discipline (IP2's `dom(C) ⊆ dom(C')`) is an additional invariant you enforce, not a free gift of the data structure. (Naming "Rust over `im`" repeatedly also runs below design altitude — the design point is "persistent ordered map keyed by tumbler address"; the crate is incidental.)
