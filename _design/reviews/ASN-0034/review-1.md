Below is the revision list, ordered most‑important first. The digest is strong — its representation analysis (store the literal sequence, bignum, T3‑for‑free, correct diagnosis of the reference‑impl alias/asymmetry bugs) and its durability reasoning (never‑reuse invariant, skip‑ahead‑is‑safe, journal‑of‑record + recomputable frontier registry + in‑memory batch reservation, with the registry framed as a recomputable hint) are genuinely sound and well‑aligned with the note. Most items below are sharpenings; a few are real defects.

---

**1. "Design commitments" / "Guarantees to uphold": the uniqueness story is mislabeled and drops the load‑bearing mechanism.** The digest attributes global uniqueness to "T10" ("Global uniqueness (T10) — free given the allocator discipline holds") and grounds all collision‑freedom in non‑nesting prefixes. But in the note **T10 (PartitionIndependence) is only the non‑nesting case and follows from T3 alone — it holds with or without the discipline.** Full uniqueness is the separate **GlobalUniqueness** theorem (T3, T4, T9, T10, T10a, TA5) whose *subtle* case (Case 4: a parent and a descendant allocator at the **same zero‑count/level**, i.e. **nesting** prefixes) is prevented by **length separation**, a consequence of the discipline's **uniform sibling length** (inc(·,0) preserves length). Fix: (a) relabel the guarantee GlobalUniqueness, with T10 as one of its cases; (b) state that allocator independence for *parent/child (nesting)* prefixes rests on length separation, not T10 — the digest's "non‑nesting prefixes can never collide" does not cover the parent/child concurrency it recommends; (c) surface *why* siblings must use inc(·,0) only — uniform length is the mechanism, and it is what makes the discipline "produce" uniqueness — rather than treating inc(·,0)‑only as an opaque rule; (d) note uniqueness also leans on T3 and on T4 level‑determination, so admission validation is part of the uniqueness story, not orthogonal to it.

**2. Missing load‑bearing consideration: zero tumblers have a *dual* role; the digest records only the rejection half.** The digest's only mention of all‑zero tumblers is "reject any all‑zero sequence used as an address." But TA6 plus the note's own open question give zero tumblers a *positive* role: they are invalid as addresses yet legitimate **sentinels** — "denote 'unbounded' when used as span endpoints" and "act as lower bounds." The note explicitly leaves open *how span containment/intersection must behave when an endpoint is the zero sentinel.* A builder who applies the digest's blanket "reject all‑zero" at the span layer breaks the unbounded‑endpoint convention. Fix: add the dual role to the Spans approach and the validation guidance — reject all‑zero **as an address**, but accept it as a span sentinel/lower bound — and carry the span‑vs‑sentinel interaction forward as a builder concern (it is one of the note's open questions, not a settled point).

**3. "Implementation approaches → Durability": the `links.jsonl` / `paths.json` "proven substrate" claim is ungrounded and drives a real recommendation.** The instruction "ride that same log so the system has one recovery story" rests on a specific factual claim about existing repo infrastructure that is not in the note, not in evidence (none was provided), and not Green structure — and it is asserted as "proven." If that substrate doesn't exist or differs, the recommendation misleads (the digest already, correctly, describes how to build the journal+registry+reservation from scratch, so it doesn't *need* the claim). Fix: either mark this as an external assumption the reviser must verify against the actual repo, or soften to "if such an append‑only journal already exists, reuse it." Same altitude note applies to the parenthetical `im` library reference — name the property (persistent structurally‑shared sequence), not a specific library.

**4. "Guarantees to uphold": T5 is mis‑attributed to T4.** The digest lumps "Contiguous subtrees, decidable containment, subspace disjointness (T5, T6, T7) — emergent from T1+T4, free provided T4 validation holds at admission." But **T5 depends only on T1 + T3 (and the prefix relation), not T4** — its proof takes an arbitrary prefix and never parses fields, so contiguity holds for *any* prefix whether or not the address is well‑formed. Only T6 (corollary of T4) and T7 (corollary of T3, T4) require admission validation. Fix: split the bullet — T5 is free from the order alone and needs no validation; T6/T7 are the T4‑dependent corollaries that do.

**5. "Design commitments": "Forced separation of concerns" over‑marks a deliberate choice as a lock.** The note frames flat‑arithmetic‑with‑projected‑hierarchy (T4) as a **"deliberate design choice"** for simplicity — you *could* build hierarchy‑aware primitives (T2's intrinsic‑comparison requirement does not forbid it); Green chose not to. That is a Lampson "do one thing well" preference, not a forced lock in the sense T3, T0, and T2 are forced (where any alternative provably breaks a guarantee). Fix: recalibrate this commitment from "Forced" to "deliberate/strongly‑motivated separation," and reserve "Forced" for the commitments where an alternative is shown to break a guarantee.

**6. The k=1 vs k=2 child‑increment distinction (versioning vs level descent) is collapsed.** The digest says "children by a single deep increment (depth 1 or 2)" without the semantics the note draws: **k=2 creates a different‑type child** (introduces the `.0.` separator — descend a hierarchical level, e.g. account→document) while **k=1 creates a same‑type child** (versioning — no separator). This is a real builder decision ("how do I mint a version vs. a sub‑entity?"), and the note even cites the historical bug where the first document failed to get its `.0.` separator. Fix: state the two child modes and their meanings in the allocator approach.

**7. "What must be built" / "Implementation approaches": foreground the action point as the organizing primitive.** Every arithmetic contract — ⊕, ⊖, inc, shift, the span‑length convention, TA7a — pivots on the action point (first nonzero component = the hierarchical level at which the operation acts; leading zeros mean "stay at these levels"). The digest uses the term in passing but never defines it as *the* concept the operations are organized around. For a builder implementing the three position operations and the 1‑position span convention, this is the load‑bearing abstraction. Fix: define and surface it once, up front in the arithmetic approach.

**8. "Implementation approaches → Spans": state the span well‑formedness precondition as a constructor obligation.** The digest describes membership and subtree‑capture but omits T12's admission rule: a span (s, ℓ) is well‑formed only when **ℓ > 0 and actionPoint(ℓ) ≤ #s** (equivalently, leading zeros of ℓ < #s), which is exactly what makes the endpoint s⊕ℓ exist and the span non‑empty (TA‑strict). The span constructor must validate this. Fix: add the precondition to the Spans approach.

**9. Minor — "How it fits": tighten the handoff terminology.** "Endsets are spans" should be "endsets are span‑sets"; and the note ties OrdinalShift to "Istream allocation, V‑enfilade traversal," whereas the digest substitutes "permascroll" — both are Green structures, but prefer the note's stated targets or justify the remap. These are cosmetic against an otherwise accurate set of upward handoffs (granfilade keys on tumblers, spanfilade indexes endsets, version‑derivation explicitly out of scope).
