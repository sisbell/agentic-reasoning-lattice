# Revision list

**1. [DEFECT] — Durability and recovery, bullet 1 (WAL): the claim that an append-only WAL "recovered by replay" *"matches the udanax-green pattern"* is false and contradicts the digest's own bullet 3.** The evidence is explicit that udanax-green has **no journal and does not replay**: baptisms live in the in-memory granfilade and become durable only at a *lazy checkpoint* (idle / clean exit); on crash it "resumes from a consistent (if stale) on-disk state," discarding uncommitted baptisms — i.e., it restores from the last checkpoint, never replays a log. That is precisely the digest's *bullet 3* (snapshot/checkpoint). So bullet 1 ("WAL recovered by replay matches udanax") and bullet 3 ("udanax checkpoints lazily and rebuilds from the last checkpoint") describe udanax's recovery two incompatible ways. A builder could wrongly infer udanax provides replay-based crash recovery of recent baptisms when it actually loses them. **Do W:** stop equating WAL with udanax. Recommend the WAL because it fits *the note's* commitments — B4 = one atomic durable append, B0 = purely additive (no compaction), recovery = replay — and because per-append durability *matches the note's own "real only when committed to the persistent store" finality model*; note that this is **stronger** than udanax, not a match for it. udanax's actual mechanism is the snapshot/checkpoint of bullet 3. Present checkpoint-restore (udanax's reality) and WAL+replay (recommended default) as two distinct options, with snapshot+truncation as the replay-bounding refinement — and remove the internal contradiction.

**2. [SHARPENING] — Guarantees ("one active check: validate T4 on parents (B6(i))") and Design commitments ("provided parents are T4-valid"): B7 disjointness formally requires the *full* B6 on both namespaces, not just T4 parents.** The B7 proof's unequal-length-parents case leans on B6(ii) (`d∈{1,2}` bounds the parent-length gap to ≤1) as well as B6(i) (nonzero last component). Operationally the digest is safe — it mandates the complete B6 gate elsewhere — but the phrasing reads as if T4-on-parents alone buys cross-namespace uniqueness. Sharpen to: the full B6 gate is the precondition; B6(i) is the *specifically anti-aliasing* condition (dropping it alone reproduces the `([1,0],1)`/`([1],2)` collision), the other clauses being about T4-preservation of the stream.

**3. [SHARPENING] — Atomicity and concurrency / Decisions: per-namespace serialization assumes a single writer per namespace and does not address the note's open *shared-namespace, multi-replica* concurrency question.** The digest's per-namespace sharding correctly handles *disjoint* namespaces (B7's gift) but silently leaves the harder case — coexisting replicas allocating in the *same* namespace, outside B-Seq's single-authority scope — unflagged. The note explicitly lists this as open ("what must coexisting replicas... guarantee about cross-replica baptism ordering... without centralized coordination"). Add one sentence scoping the per-namespace recommendation to single-writer-per-namespace and naming the shared-namespace replica case as the note's unresolved concurrency question.

Sections that are solid and should not be churned: the registry-representation analysis (counters + auxiliary set, "exactly the length-1 node roots" is correct — every T4-valid length-≥2 address decomposes uniquely into a T4 parent), the B9/arbitrary-precision guarantee (including extending it to *every* component in the key-encoding decision), the finality-model reconciliation of udanax's reserve-then-confirm with B0, the genesis treatment, and the `allocated(s) ⊆ s.B` layered-vs-unified discussion. Accuracy on B0/B0★/B1/B2/B6/B7/B8/B10 and the forced-vs-conventional split is faithful to the note.
