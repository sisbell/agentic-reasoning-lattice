# Revision list

**1. [DEFECT] — "Durability and recovery": the checkpointing bullet and the Lampson bullet are internally contradictory about the snapshot.** The checkpointing bullet says to "truncate the log" and "rebuild from the last checkpoint," but the Lampson bullet says to "treat … any snapshot as a *hint* — recomputable on a miss by replay [and] on any snapshot/log mismatch, the log wins." These cannot both hold: once the log *behind* a checkpoint is truncated, the snapshot is authoritative durable state (the recovery base), not a replay-recomputable hint, and there is no retained log to "win" over it. Taken literally together — checkpoint+truncate *and* treat the snapshot as a discardable hint — recovery silently loses every baptism before the last checkpoint. Fix: scope the hint discipline. The **in-memory counter map** is the pure recomputable hint (rebuilt from the latest snapshot plus replay of the *post-snapshot* log tail). The **on-disk snapshot**, once the log behind it is truncated, is authoritative durable state; only the post-snapshot log tail remains a replayable hint over it, and "log wins on mismatch" applies to *that tail vs the in-memory map*, not to the snapshot. State explicitly that the "snapshot is a pure hint, recompute by full replay" framing holds only in the no-truncation (default WAL) regime.

**2. [SHARPENING] — "Atomicity and concurrency": "matches B-Seq verbatim" overstates.** A single global serializing writer *satisfies* B-Seq and is the discipline B-Seq is grounded in, but it is strictly *stronger* — B-Seq only requires a total order *within a single baptismal authority*, whereas a global writer serializes across all authorities/namespaces. Tighten to "satisfies B-Seq (its grounding case), though it serializes more than B-Seq strictly requires" — which also reinforces the very next bullet's point that per-namespace serialization is the *minimal* discipline B-Seq licenses.

**3. [SHARPENING] — "What this is": the one-line summary calls growth "globally unique" without qualification.** The body is careful that same-namespace uniqueness is the *one* uniqueness guarantee that is not free (it requires serialization under B8/B-Seq), while cross-namespace uniqueness is structural and unconditional (B7). The opening flattens these into a single "globally unique," which reads as if both are equally free. Qualify it ("globally unique — unconditionally across namespaces, and within a namespace under serialized commitment") so the summary matches the commitments and guarantees sections.

The remaining sections (design commitments, what-must-be-built, registry/allocation/validity/genesis approaches, guarantees, how-it-fits, builder decisions) are accurate, grounded, at design altitude, and committal where the note supports a position — no further changes needed.
