## Revision list

**1. Design commitment 3 ("Every theorem requires level-uniformity") is inaccurate and contradicts the digest's own "How it fits."** The note proves S0 (convexity) "solely from T1 being a total order," proves SC (classification) from the total order alone, and states S9 (uniqueness) with no level-uniformity precondition — yet "How it fits" *correctly* says "convexity, the exhaustive five-case classification, and normalized-form uniqueness all flow from positions being totally ordered." Both cannot be right. Fix commitment 3: scope level-uniformity to the **result-constructing** theorems (intersection, merge, split, difference, normalization-existence — everything that builds a span through WF, which needs `#s = #r`), and acknowledge that comparison, convexity, classification, and uniqueness rest on the total order alone. Keep "cross-level operations are out of scope" (true: the note proves no cross-level operation and lists it as open) but stop attributing the level requirement to the comparison layer. This also has a builder consequence worth stating: the level gate belongs *after* classification, *before* construction — the classifier itself doesn't need it.

**2. The normalization approaches never cite the note's two merge sites, which are the direct evidence for the digest's central design choice.** The note's intro states Green enforces adjacency/overlap (i.e., normalization) at **two** sites: the *enfilade-level* `isanextensionnd` and the *output-level* `putvspaninlist`. This is precisely the incremental-(insert-time)-vs-batch-(output-time) axis — and the eager-vs-lazy axis — that the digest discusses at length as pure design reasoning. Ground both discussions in this evidence: the reference implementation normalizes both during structural insertion *and* at output, which is why "default batch, reach for incremental" and "eager for stored, lazy for in-flight" are legitimate — Green does both, at two named layers. Leaving the one piece of implementation evidence that bears on the digest's most important "what to build" decision uncited is a real grounding gap.

**3. "Pairwise engine: merge is (min start, max reach)" needs the SC gate or it silently fills gaps.** Two non-empty spans always give `min start < max reach`, so this formula passes WF's `s < r` check and produces a span **even for separated inputs (SC case i)** — wrongly covering the gap; the union of two separated spans is a two-span span-set, not a merge (S3's precondition is overlap *or* adjacency). Intersection's `(max start, min reach)` is self-guarding — when the spans are disjoint, `max start ≥ min reach` fails WF's precondition and correctly yields ∅. Surface this asymmetry: intersection's emptiness falls out of WF, but merge's single-span result is valid only for SC cases (ii)–(v) and must be gated by classification. The "classify with SC, then build" framing implies this, but the flat min/max statement invites the bug.

**4. "Incremental … directly answers the note's open question about minimal update" overstates.** The open question asks what the *minimal update* to a normalized form is when **address allocation** changes the covered population — a characterization question about the minimal delta under an allocation event. A persistent ordered map makes updates *cheap* and *structurally shared*, but it neither characterizes the minimal delta nor addresses the allocation-changes-coverage scenario. Downgrade to: "the right structure for cheap, versioned incremental updates, and bears on (but does not resolve) the open question."

**5. "Green's `vspan` form" is not grounded.** The note names only `putvspaninlist`; it never states that Green represents spans as `(start, width)`, and `vspan` is not among the documented enfilade types (granfilade/permascroll/POOM/spanfilade). The `(start, width)` recommendation stands perfectly well on the note's own `σ = (s, ℓ)` form plus the edit-alignment argument. Drop the vspan attribution, or mark it as inference rather than asserting it as Green's representation.

**6. The incremental "O(log n) per update" claim ignores cascading coalesce.** A single inserted span that overlaps/bridges *k* existing spans must delete and merge all of them — O(k log n), worst case O(n) — not O(log n). State the qualifier: O(log n) holds for inserts touching O(1) neighbors; a coalescing insert scales with the number of spans it absorbs. The review cares whether stated tradeoffs are real, and this one is stated too flatly.

**7. S5 (split width composition, `d ⊕ d' = ℓ`) is entirely absent.** The "lossless subdivision" guarantee captures S4a/S3b but not the named width-composition law. Surface it as the invariant a builder implementing `split` can assert (and as the arithmetic check that the two parts' widths add back to the original). Minor, but it's a named guarantee the digest drops.

**8. Minor accuracy/polish:**
- "Sits below the content/storage layer … the note's open question leans that way" — the note presents unpopulated-vs-populated as *genuinely open* ("…or is this distinction purely a content-layer concern?"), not leaning. Either soften to "the content-agnostic framing suggests" or drop the "leans."
- Guarantees list: "split∘merge = identity, merge∘split = original pair" reverses standard composition order (∘ is right-to-left; split∘merge = merge-then-split = the *pair*, S3b). The `(S4a/S3b)` labels disambiguate, but reword to "split-then-merge = identity (S4a); merge-then-split = original pair (S3b)."
- "subsumes `normalize` itself (union-with-self)" is loose — `normalize` is the one-input sweep over Σ, not literally Σ ∪ Σ (idempotence makes them coincide, but the phrasing misleads).

## Solid as-is
The pairwise-engine reduction (one comparator + one constructor + min/max, with WF as the single result-building primitive), the infinite-denotation reading of S7, the join-semilattice/CRDT framing of S10 with the correct "union-only, difference is not commutative" caveat, and the correct refusal to promise a span-set-difference bound are all accurate and genuinely useful. The content-addressing proposal is sound and consistent with the note's denotational identity (it dedups *designators*, not content bytes — no conflict with permascroll's no-content-dedup discipline); no change needed beyond noting it relies on the WF-built canonical form the digest already mandates.
