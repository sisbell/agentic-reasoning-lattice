This is a strong, unusually well-grounded digest — the udanax-green claims hold up against the evidence throughout, and the provenance section's live-containment-vs-historical-provenance distinction is exactly the right Lampson hint-vs-authoritative-state discipline. Below are the improvements worth applying, defects first.

## Revision list

**1. § Atomicity/journaling/recovery — the arrangement splice is *not* idempotent, but "blind apply" implies it is. (Missing load-bearing consideration.)** The section stresses that provenance re-recording is idempotent (set union) and calls journaled replay "a self-contained blind apply." But re-applying the same `(destination, p, W, resolved runs)` record splices a *second* time — shifting trailing content by `2W` and double-placing the block. The two halves of a journal record have opposite replay discipline: provenance tolerates at-least-once, the arrangement requires exactly-once-from-snapshot. The snapshot+tail model already implies this, but the digest must *say* it, because "blind apply" + "idempotent re-recording" reads as "the whole record is safely re-runnable." Add: replay applies each arrangement record exactly once from the last snapshot; only the provenance side is safely repeatable.

**2. § Arrangement representation — the default over-credits option (3) and defends it on "first-class in the spec" rather than the common case.** Two problems. (a) The digest lists "free pre-state snapshots" as a reason to pick the persistent enfilade (3), then concedes in the "false tradeoff" passage that snapshots come from *persistence* — which persistent (1) and (2) have too. So snapshots do **not** differentiate (3); the only genuine differentiators are O(log n) shift-independent-of-trailing-size and the 2-D I→V query. (b) "Mid-document insertion is first-class for COPY" conflates *the spec handles the displacing case* with *mid-document inserts dominate the workload*. The note details the displacing case because it is the **harder** case (Lampson: rare-case-correct), not because it is common; nothing in the note bears on frequency. Per "cheapest mechanism that meets the contract," frame the default around the expected access pattern: append-dominant or small-document workloads are served more cheaply by (1)/(2); reach for (3)'s bookkeeping only when mid-document inserts into *large* documents are common, or when the substrate beyond COPY needs the 2-D query. Keep (3) as a strong option, not the unconditional default.

**3. § Atomicity/journaling — repo-specific references are unverifiable here and bind a domain-neutral spec to one codebase. (Ungrounded / altitude.)** "This is the repo's `links.jsonl` + replay pattern" and "this repo has been bitten by torn reads of shared registry state" appear in neither the note nor the evidence and carry no checkable citation; the note is explicitly domain-neutral. The actual design point — append-only journal, commit each record with an atomic write/rename to survive torn reads — stands without them. Either mark them clearly as local precedent or generalize them.

**4. § Concurrency — the rationale misattributes resolution-coherence to content immutability.** "Content immutability keeps resolved addresses stable under concurrent source edits" is loose: the *arrangement* `M(d_s)` is editable in place, so a concurrent source edit can change `M(d_s)(v)`. What makes resolution coherent under concurrent arrangement edits is the **captured arrangement snapshot**, not immutability; immutability (S0/S1) separately guarantees the resolved *addresses*, once read, keep their bytes and never vanish. Split the two clauses so no builder reads "immutability ⇒ I can skip the snapshot." The instruction itself (snapshot the source root, no lock) is correct — only the justification needs untangling.

**5. § Design commitments → Conventional — the "forward displacement (CP3)" bullet files a forced postcondition under "could differ."** Displacing trailing content by `W` (CP3a) and vacating old keys / closing the domain (CP3c) are postconditions every correct COPY must satisfy — forced, and your own "Require active enforcement" list already treats them so. Only the *mechanism* (reuse INSERT's placement primitive) is the conventional choice. The bullet half-says this but its header still reads as if forward displacement is optional. Retitle to make the split explicit: requirement forced (CP3a/CP3c, per-subspace by CP6); implementation-by-INSERT-reuse conventional.

**6. Tighten two padded passages.** (a) The "false tradeoff / earlier instinct" discussion debates a prior draft the reader never saw — state the conclusion once (persistent (2) and (3) both retain snapshots; udanax's "no snapshots" was its *mutability*) and cut the meta-narrative. (b) "Σ.R is authoritative, don't rebuild it from arrangements" is argued in full three times (Provenance, Recovery, Decisions); keep it once and cross-reference.
