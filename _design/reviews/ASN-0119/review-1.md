The digest is accurate and well-grounded on its spine: the seven design commitments correctly trace to the note's "single structural fact," the tile-by-placement technique correctly identifies the load-bearing decision and the Green bug, and the I-address-keyed index / frame-by-unreachability moves are exactly right. The improvements below are mostly grounding and a couple of missing builder caveats; only the first is a genuine misreading.

**1. Decisions §7 (Concurrency): the order-independence claim is attributed to the note as settled when the note leaves it open.** The digest writes "The note flags order-sensitivity (same final order only when composed permutations match; intermediates always differ)." But RA8a (`π₂∘π₁ = π ⟹ M'_comp = M'`) is about realizing *one* transposition via two moves — same target π, two decompositions — not about whether two *different* rearranges commute. Whether two independent rearranges leave the final arrangement order-independent is precisely the note's **Open Question 2**, which is *unresolved*. The recommendation (serialize; don't assume commutativity) is the correct safe answer to that open question, so keep it — but re-ground it: cite OQ2 (open) plus RA8b (intermediates always differ), and stop borrowing RA8a's "composed permutations match" language, which describes a settled, different fact.

**2. "What must be built" (cut/region resolver) and Decisions §8: the precondition checker must distinguish degenerate *branches* from degenerate *inputs*, and the digest doesn't.** The note is explicit ("An empty exterior is a degenerate *branch*, not a degenerate *input*"): a document whose active run is shorter than the minimum interval fails R-PRE and must be rejected, but `c₀ = min(V_{s_C})` (empty left exterior) and an interval covering the whole active run (both exteriors empty) are *valid* — π stays a bijection and extent is conserved. A builder who adds a guard against empty exteriors will wrongly reject whole-document and leading-edge rearranges. Add this as an explicit resolver requirement: accept empty-exterior branches; reject only true R-PRE failures.

**3. Run-decomposition canonicalization (S8★) and the "Identity is by I-address" commitment: make the value-blindness explicit, since canonicalization is exactly where a builder could violate it.** The digest's merge rule ("adjacent runs over contiguous I-addresses") is correct, but it never states *why* — and the note's whole second "two streams" paragraph plus S4 (OriginBasedIdentity) establish that identity is per-allocation, not per-value: two distinct I-addresses can hold equal `Val`, and REARRANGE's permutation is cut-determined and value-blind (R-PRE imposes no value condition; a value-degenerate `π ≠ id` with `M'(d) = M(d)` on shared content is legal). State the guardrail: runs coalesce *only* on I-address contiguity, never on content-value equality, because equal bytes at distinct origins are distinct content. This forecloses the content-dedup trap by rationale, not just by a rule a future "optimizer" might relax.

**4. "Atomic commit and durable recovery": `links.jsonl + paths.json` is ungrounded.** This specific claim about "the repo's own proven pattern / the working substrate" appears in neither the note nor the evidence answers, and is not Green structure — it carries no checkable citation here. The append-only-journal-recovered-by-replay recommendation stands on its own (it's the Istream philosophy, which *is* in the note), so either supply a citation for those filenames or drop them and keep the generic "append-only journal, recover by replay" framing.

**5. The arrangement store: "the arrangement *is* a maximal correspondence-run decomposition" overstates S8★.** S8★ says the arrangement *admits* a unique maximal run decomposition — that's a property, not a representation mandate. Reword to "admits," then keep the (sound) engineering inference that a span list is the cheapest representation honoring it. Minor, but it's the conceptual footing of the whole arrangement-store recommendation.

**6. The arrangement store (persistence bullet): retention and no-intermediate are conflated.** "Retaining prior roots" serves *recovery* (OQ5) and version coexistence — but the *no-observable-intermediate* property is delivered by the atomic root *swap* (single publish), not by keeping old roots; and the "observable-intermediate" point is a settled result (RA8b), not one of "the note's hard cases / questions." Split these: retention → recovery; atomic swap → no intermediate (as the later "Atomic commit" section already says correctly).
