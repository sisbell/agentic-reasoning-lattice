**Overall:** A strong digest. It reads the load-bearing commitments correctly, cites every udanax-green claim to the evidence channel (no ungrounded source-level claims), holds design altitude, and lands several genuinely sharp moves I'd protect through revision: the value-degenerate→cut-determined-π argument, the RA6-vs-R-NS freeze distinction, the RA8a-vs-OQ2 separation in decision 7, and the content-dedup foreclosure via OriginBasedIdentity. The items below are precision and completeness, not corrections to the engineering direction.

**Revision list (most important first):**

1. **Approaches → transposition engine (also Design commitment #3 and Guarantees → bijection/no collision): localize Green's bug — it is over-generalized.** The digest says "Green computes *each region's* shift from raw cut differences… which tiles only when the swapped regions are equal-width," and labels it a "uniform per-region offset." Q11 and Q19 show this is too broad: Green's **3-cut pivot is entirely correct**, and within the 4-cut swap the **middle** (`diff[2]=(c₃−c₂)−(c₁−c₀)=w_β−w_α`) and **back** (`diff[3]=−(c₂−c₀)=−(w_α+w_μ)`) offsets are exactly the tiling-correct values. The defect is a **single wrong offset in one path**: the swap's **α** destination, computed as `c₂−c₀ = w_α+w_μ` where the tiling needs `w_β+w_μ = c₃−c₁` — correct iff `w_α=w_β`. The *symptom* is global (no valid tiling, collision per Q14); the *cause* is one offset. Restate it that way: it's more actionable (a builder porting/auditing Green should not "fix" the offsets that are already right), and it matches the note, which itself names α specifically. Drop "uniform per-region offset" — the offsets are per-region and non-uniform.

2. **Guarantees → Cut boundary-hood; What must be built → cut/region resolver; Approaches → S8★ canonicalization: stop conflating "position boundary" with "run-decomposition boundary."** CutSequence requires a cut to fall on a *position* boundary, trivially met under dense indexing (D-SEQ★) — so "there is no non-boundary cut to reject" is right. But "the split *creates the run-decomposition boundary* there" misstates S8★: the maximal correspondence-run decomposition is a property of the V→I map's *values*, not the representation, and the note explicitly contemplates a cut falling *inside* one maximal run ("one maximal run before a pivot breaking into several after"). A cut splits the *representation* at a position; the canonical decomposition is then recomputed (R-BLK) and canonicalized (R-CANON), and that split may merge away. Relatedly, "you get S8★ for free [if canonical]" is backwards — S8★ holds for *any* arrangement (it's a theorem the note proves), and canonical merge-normal form is an optimization for *footprint minimality*, not a precondition for satisfying S8★. Reword to separate cut-validity (position boundary) from the recomputed map property.

3. **Approaches → transposition engine: subspace confinement is mis-attributed.** "A list splice cannot overlap or leave a gap, so the bijection, single-valuedness, *and subspace-confinement* hold by construction." Gap-/overlap-freeness gives bijection and single-valuedness; subspace confinement comes from splicing *only within the text (s_C) interval* — which the Frame-discipline section already states correctly. A gap-free splice that relocated a text span past the s_C/s_L boundary would still be gap-free yet violate confinement — that is exactly Green's Q17 hazard. Tie confinement to within-interval placement here, not to gap-freeness.

4. **Guarantees → Hold by construction: add referential integrity (S3★).** It's a named ASN-0047 invariant the note discharges and it's missing from the enumerated guarantees. State it: every rearranged content position still maps into `dom(C)` (and each link position into `dom(L)`) because π carries values unchanged and both stores are frozen. Automatic, but a builder should see it listed beside the others.

5. **Open-question handling: convert OQ3/OQ4 from "open" to "retired for the builder," and place OQ1.** The digest surfaces OQ2/OQ5/OQ6 but leaves OQ3/OQ4 implicit and OQ1 unmentioned. The valuable move is to say plainly that the recommended design *retires* OQ3 (an I-address-keyed index needs no arrangement-relative invariant — it isn't arrangement-keyed; discoverability is `coverage ∩ ran`, invariant by RA1/LP12) and OQ4 ("tolerate any fragmentation" makes the 3+-region run-structure guarantee unnecessary to wait on). Note OQ1 (cross-document boundary-hood) stays genuinely open but is out of REARRANGE's build scope, since d′ is wholly unaffected (RA9/Q20). This tells the builder why they aren't blocked.

6. **What must be built → Durable recovery; Approaches → Atomic commit and durable recovery: keep the durability/OQ5 line crisp.** Durability of the *current* map is a reasonable builder inference (M is authoritative state in Σ) but is *not* note-mandated; recovering a *prior* arrangement is OQ5 (spec-open). The journal section separates these well — carry the same caveat onto the "Durable recovery of the new order across restart" build-bullet so it isn't read as a note guarantee.

7. **Minor phrasings.** (a) "a cut is… always within the active run" — the final cut `c_{n-1}` sits at the *trailing* boundary, which may be one position past the last active position (worked pivot: c₂ = ord 6 on a 5-position doc); say "at a position boundary of the active run." The rejection criterion (interval `[c₀,c_{n-1})` within active text) is already stated correctly. (b) RA8a literally covers a *two-move* composite; "any realization of the permutation" is a sound generalization but worth marking as one. (c) "Inferring the permutation by diffing content before and after" — the precise risk is deriving π from content *values* (ambiguous under duplicate I-addresses), not a literal before/after diff; reword.

Sections I'd leave alone: "How it fits" (accurate dependency placement), the link-footprint resolution approach (correct, well-grounded in Q15/Q16/LP12), and frame-by-unreachability (correctly grounded in Q20 and stronger than check-after-the-fact).
