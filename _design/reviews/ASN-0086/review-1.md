This is a strong, buildable digest: it correctly identifies the active/audit distinction and the single-write-path as the architectural pivots, classifies forced-vs-convention soundly, and grounds its journaling/hint discipline in both Green's permascroll/granfilade and the evidence (the desync-on-crash lesson, stateless query-and-increment freshness, the spanfilade reverse index, the honest "Green had no retraction at all" contrast). The revision list below is therefore mostly sharpening, with a few real gaps.

---

**1. "What must be built"/"Guarantees" — Nullify's target precondition (P-tgt), including the self-emit branch, is missing entirely.** The digest describes Nullify only as "emit a retraction tuple naming a target address" and lists just "home-document existence" as the precondition. But the note's Nullify carries **P-tgt: `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` (P1) ∨ `a = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)` (self-emit)** — a deliberate, load-bearing capability: a single step can baptize *and* retract a fresh address (born-retracted), demonstrated in Worked Sketch Step 4 and used as the wp Case 1 weakest precondition. A builder who checks only "target ∈ store" will reject legal self-nullifying calls and mis-implement the contract. Add P-tgt to the Nullify build spec and to the precondition list, and note the "owned-at-Σ vs owned-at-commit" distinction the two branches encode.

**2. "Implementation approaches"/"Recovery" — repo- and language-specific claims are presented as established fact but are grounded in none of the note, the evidence, or Green.** "This is exactly this repo's own working substrate (the `links.jsonl` journal with a `paths.json` registry)," "this repo replays a journal," and "the `im` crate's idiom" assert specifics the review materials cannot confirm and presume a particular implementation/language. The journaling recommendation is independently grounded (Green permascroll/granfilade + the dual-enfilade desync evidence), so these add nothing but unverifiable surface. Either replace with a checkable pointer or mark them explicitly as illustrative; do not state them as "exactly" what exists. (The design point — persistent structurally-shared maps for free snapshot reads — should stay; just drop the crate name.)

**3. "Design commitments" (first bullet) — "identity is by origin, not by value" is inaccurate; it is identity *by address*.** `origin` is the home document (ASN-0036/Home), shared by every link homed at `d`, so it cannot be the identity — R1 (AddressInjectivity) + R0 make the *full address* the discriminator. The body already says this correctly ("distinct addresses … the substrate never merges them"), so the heading just contradicts its own body and could mislead toward home-keyed dedup. Change "by origin" to "by address."

**4. "What must be built" (coverage bullet) and "Implementation approaches → coverage" — coverage-*equality* is conflated with coverage-*membership/containment*.** The digest attributes "test whether a retraction covers a target" to the "coverage-equality decision," and frames the token trick as the coverage story. But there are two distinct operations: (a) coverage-*equality* (`coverage(K)=coverage(K')`, CoverageEqualityDecidable) for type slicing and selecting the `[R]` slice — interning to tokens fits this; and (b) coverage-*membership/containment* (`a ∈ coverage(G')` for nullification, `F̂ ⊆ coverage(F)` for Observe, decided by T2 span-membership) — tokens do *not* serve this; that is the reverse-index's job. State the two kinds explicitly so a builder doesn't try to answer Observe/nullification with class-token equality. (The digest's *index* section already gets the realization right; the defect is in how coverage is introduced.)

**5. "What must be built" (store) vs "Decisions for the builder" (multi-arity) — the store description under-specifies arity.** The store bullet says it maps each address to a `(from, to, type)` triple, but the note explicitly permits `|Σ.L(a)| > 3` links that inhabit `dom(Σ.L)` yet index no `L_K` tuple. The store must hold arbitrary-arity links; the *triple* restriction is the typed-relation **projection**, not a store constraint. Reconcile the two sections: the link store admits any arity, and "triples only" is a property of the `L_K` view (and the open builder choice is whether to *serve* non-triples, not whether to *store* them).

**6. "Implementation approaches → nullified set" / "require active enforcement" — make explicit that the simple "tombstone = set of named targets" realization *depends on* the unit-depth discipline.** Because `nullified(Σ)` is defined via `a ∈ coverage(G')`, a single retraction tuple with a non-unit-depth (shorter-prefix) to-span tombstones its *whole covered subtree of `A_rel`*, not one address. The cheap "feed each Nullify's target into a growing set" tombstone is only correct once every retraction is a unit prefix span; otherwise the builder must compute `coverage(G') ∩ A_rel` per retraction. The digest states both facts separately but never links them — surface the causal dependency so a builder who relaxes the discipline doesn't keep the now-unsound shortcut.

**7. "Decisions for the builder → Observe ordering" — "by address … is emission order" holds only within one home's chain, not across an `L_K` slice.** A typed slice spans multiple homes; tumbler-address order does not reflect global emission order across interleaved homes. The parenthetical "on a monotone chain" is technically present but easy to over-read. Clarify: address-order equals emission-order *per home*, and across the full slice it is an independent ordering choice (the note's open question stands).

**8. Minor sharpenings (terse):** (a) "Guarantees → unit-depth shape" could note the discipline buys a *second* guarantee beyond single-tuple scope — under it (plus R0a) a freshly emitted tuple is provably never accidentally born-nullified by a pre-existing retraction (wp Case 2 disciplined simplification); this is why the discipline, not just convenience, is load-bearing. (b) "Emission"/first design-commitment should record that `Emit_K` requires `K ∈ T_admissible` (non-empty type endset) — a real precondition a builder must check. (c) "How it fits" calls the downstream consumer "'Three Operations' work," but Three Operations is this note's own section defining Emit/Observe/Nullify; reword to avoid implying it is a separate downstream layer.
