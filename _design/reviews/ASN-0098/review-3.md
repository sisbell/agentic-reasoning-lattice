**1. Arrangement + edits (dense-V-position paragraph): narrow the "must re-derive" claim to LP12a — LP19/LP19a and LP12b's substance are untouched by V-position identity.**
The prose says "LP12b/LP19/LP19a are likewise phrased over the contiguous/canonical shape" and must be "re-derived over the new arrangement shape." This conflates two distinct notions both called "canonical/contiguous": canonical **spans** (the coverage / I-address side, `ℓ = δ(n,#s)`) versus contiguous **V-position arrangement** (ASN-0047 D-CTG★/D-SEQ★).
- LP19/LP19a are proved entirely on the coverage side — tight endset + strict-monotone allocation ⇒ a freshly allocated address ∉ `coverage(e)`. Neither lemma mentions V-positions at all, so the dense-vs-contiguous V-position choice leaves them *exactly* as written.
- LP12b's substance — `project(a,i,d,Σ) ⊆ V_{s_C}(d)`, `R ⊆ V_{s_L}(d)`, disjoint ⇒ wp false — holds for *any* retained set `R` inside the link subspace, not only a prefix; it needs the subspace partition, not contiguity.
- Only LP12a genuinely loses structure (its `R` stops being a D-SEQ★ prefix), and even there the master identity `project(e,d,Σ′)=project(e,d,Σ)∩R` survives for any `R`.

Decision #5 already gets this right ("reshapes LP12a's retention set away from a prefix"), so the prose contradicts the digest's own table. **Fix:** scope the re-derivation note to LP12a's retention set + the "contraction = remove a prefix's complement" framing; add one clause stating LP19/LP19a are independent of V-position identity (they constrain coverage vs the allocation frontier) and LP12b's subspace argument is preserved.

**2. Guarantees / subspace integrity: surface the note's open link-canonical asymmetry, since the digest leans on content-side purity to justify separate indexes.**
The digest presents LP12b's content-canonical orphaning as *the* subspace picture and justifies "index content-reaching and link-reaching discovery as separate structures" on coverage being "subspace-pure." But the note's final Open Question flags that the disjointness **inverts** for link-canonical endsets: at `X = s_L` the LP-Fin Corollary gives coverage ⊆ `s_L` addresses, and `dom(L) ⊆ s_L` addresses too, so `coverage ∩ dom(L)` need not be empty. **Fix:** add a line noting the clean per-subspace orphaning result has *no proven link-side mirror* — a builder must not assume emptying the link subspace orphans a link-canonical link the way emptying content orphans a content-canonical one; the note leaves it open.

**3. "What must be built" / projection capability: surface the result-shape decision the note leaves open.**
The capability is specified only as "return the V-positions." But Open Questions 2–3 (is a projection expressible as a finite union of contiguous V-ranges; does V-order reflect I-order) and the worked trace ("may become fragmented" under K.μ~) bear directly on what the projection call *returns* and how callers iterate it. **Fix:** add a builder decision on return shape (unordered set vs sorted V-list vs union of contiguous V-ranges), with the explicit caveat that contiguity is **not** preserved across reordering and that the order/contiguity guarantees are open in the note — so a caller must not rely on either.

**4. Link store / in-memory approach: mark the repo-substrate and Rust/`im` claims as assumptions, not facts.**
"the shape of the repo's working substrate" and "assuming a Rust/im-style persistent-collection stack" are asserted as given, but are grounded in neither the note (language/substrate-neutral) nor the evidence. The journal-replay-and-persistent-map *reasoning* is sound regardless; only the "this is what the repo already is" claim is unverified. **Fix:** reframe as explicit assumptions ("if the target substrate is an append-only journal…", "given a persistent-collection stack…") so the advice degrades gracefully if the substrate differs.
