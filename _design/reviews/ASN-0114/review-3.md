## Review of the FOLLOWLINK Design Digest

This is a strong digest — accurate on the forced/conventional partition (no inversions), precise on the F6 disclosure slice, and genuinely impressive where it threads contradictory-looking evidence. The improvements below are real but mostly localized; the bulk of the digest is sound.

### Revision list (most important first)

**1. "Implementation approaches → Link lookup" (and the F4 echo under "Guarantees"): trim the out-of-scope storage design, fix its grounding, and lighten the default.** Three problems compound in this one section:
   - *Grounding.* The note models the link store as a partial function `Σ.L` with permanence (LP13); the evidence and standard Green structure put links in the **granfilade enfilade**. "An append-only journal of link records as authority, recovered by replay, with the map as a recomputable hint" is supported by none of these and reads as imported. Attributing it to ASN-0093 is uncheckable here — either cite the specific ASN-0093 commitment or drop the journal/replay framing.
   - *Scope/altitude.* The digest itself says this is "the link store's concern (ASN-0093), inherited and assumed, not designed by FOLLOWLINK" — then designs it across a paragraph plus three bullets. That is scope creep into a delegated concern.
   - *Simplicity (heavy default).* The recommended default — a *persistent, structurally-shared* map for "consistent point reads under concurrent appends" — is heavier than the problem. The map is monotone and its entries immutable (LP13), so a single point read needs only a concurrent map with safe lock-free lookups; structural sharing and MVCC/snapshot machinery buy nothing here.
   
   Do: compress to the one FOLLOWLINK-relevant claim the note supports — *FOLLOWLINK is a by-address point read over ASN-0093's store; because the map is add-only with immutable entries, the read is trivial, lock-free, and needs no versioning/snapshot machinery of its own.* Keep the good monotonicity insight; drop the journal/persistent-map editorializing (and the duplicate F4-vs-map clarification).

**2. "The domain guard" and "Decisions → Where the bounds check lives" contradict "Slot accessor," and the contradiction breaks the n-set case the digest champions.** The slot-accessor section says (correctly) "derive bounds from the actual arity" — which is *post-lookup*, since `|Σ.L(a)|` comes from the looked-up link (F0's upper bound is literally `i ≤ |Σ.L(a)|`). But the domain-guard section says "check membership and bounds first, at the boundary," and the decisions section repeats "validate selector/address at the boundary." For the **upper** bound these cannot both hold once arity is unfrozen: dom-membership *is* the lookup, and the arity bound is known only after it. Green can whitelist `==1||==2||==3` at the boundary (Q12) *only because* arity is frozen at 3 — exactly the freeze the digest rejects. Do: state the guard as **lookup → (absent ⟹ ⊥) → arity bound, post-lookup → (out-of-range ⟹ ⊥) → slot read → (empty ⟹ ⟨⟩)**, noting that only the lower bound (`i ≥ 1`) and address syntax are boundary-checkable, and that Green's boundary whitelist is an artifact of frozen arity.

**3. "Design commitments" #1 / "Guarantees → F3": "you must not emit a stable order" overreaches the note.** The note's position is that order is *below the abstraction* — "a determinate artifact of one implementation, not a guarantee the contract makes" — and Green emits a deterministic V-sorted order (Q16) that the note treats as fine. "Must not emit a stable order" reads as an instruction not to be deterministic, which is impossible (every deterministic emitter produces *some* order) and contrary to the note. Reframe to the actual commitment: *do not promise or document an order, and keep it out of the contract; expect that callers may form Hyrum's-Law dependencies on whatever order you emit.* The point is non-promotion, not non-determinism.

**4. Add the conformance-testing consequence of F1/F3 (result fixed only up to coverage).** The digest establishes that decomposition and order are free but never states the concrete builder bite: *conformance tests and golden fixtures must assert coverage-equality (`⟦R⟧ = coverage(eᵢ)`), never span count, split points, or emission order.* This is the most actionable place "representation is free" lands on a builder and is currently only implicit — add it to the emitter approach or the F3 guarantee.

### Solid — no change needed
- **The F7 domain-guard treatment** is excellent: it correctly separates the boundary `⊥` from the *core* empty-`⟨⟩` decision and reads the Q17 anti-pattern (`putrequestfailed`) against the RETRIEVEENDSETS good-pattern precisely.
- **The recorded-vs-resolution de-bundling** correctly distinguishes the F7 empty-*recorded* bug from the resolution drop on a *non-empty* recorded end — threading the apparent Q17-fails/Q19-succeeds-empty contradiction the right way. The "(non-empty)" qualifier is doing exactly the right work.
- **The Q13 clarification** (the verbatim copy chain is the *exceptional* raw-I-span path, not evidence the reference emits verbatim) is sophisticated and correct.
- **The F6 disclosure analysis** (home document readable only over the T4-valid `zeros≥2` slice; node/user/ghost/interior tumblers name no document) matches the note exactly.
