Strong digest overall — it stays at design altitude, actively forbids the classic violations a builder would be tempted into (hash-addressing content against S4, global re-sort against R5, dedup/merge against R8), and reads the subtle parts of the note carefully. The defects below are mostly accuracy nits and a few sharpenings, not structural problems.

## Revision list (most important first)

**1. "What must be built" / "How it fits": delete the link-store read requirement — RETRIEVEV never dereferences `Σ.L`.** "What must be built" lists *"Read access to … the link store by address,"* and "How it fits" says it *"leans downward on the immutable content store **and link store** … for values **and link entities by address**."* This is wrong and contradicts the digest's own item-former. A link position delivers `⟨ref, a⟩`, where `a = Σ.M(d)(v)` comes from the **arrangement**; the operation tags it by `subspace(v)` and emits the address. It does **not** fetch anything from `Σ.L` — R10 is explicit ("the delivered item carries the link's address — *not* the link's endset structure, which is the concern of operations that read a link by address (out of scope here)"). This is risky, not just untidy: a builder told it needs "read access to the link store by address" may dereference `Σ.L(a)` and deliver the link entity, *violating* R10. Fix: delivery reads (a) the **content store** for values `Σ.C(a)` and (b) the **per-document arrangements** for V→address resolution (which yields the link address directly); it needs **no** read access to the link store. State plainly the load-bearing asymmetry this obscures: content *requires* a store dereference, links *never* touch their store.

**2. "Design commitments → Span width": the boundary clip is an *exactness* mechanism, not the cause of byte-length/width divergence.** The sentence *"Green's boundary clip is the realization that makes byte-length and nominal width diverge"* misreads both the note and Q14. The note says the opposite in so many words: the clip *"changes no abstract content, it merely realizes the half-open interval precisely."* For a fully-bound interval, clipping a run's overhang delivers byte-length **= V-width**. Byte-length falls *below* the V-width sum for exactly the reason `|act|` falls below the nominal extent — unbound positions (gaps) and depth-incompatibility, one byte per active position (the "width ≠ count" principle). Q14's KB synthesis agrees ("for a fully occupied V-span, returned byte count equals the sum of V-widths"; shortfall comes from deleted/unmapped positions). Keep the true lead clause ("byte-length is not the sum of V-widths"), but re-attribute: the divergence is gaps + depth-incompat; the clip realizes `[start, reach)` exactly (trimming run overhang past the requested edge).

**3. "Implementation approaches → Repeatability": the versioning guidance contradicts itself on copy-on-write.** Earlier the digest recommends a version fork be *"a structurally-shared clone — O(1) to create"* (i.e. a COW persistent fork); here it warns *"don't implement versioning as copy-on-write snapshots of one document's arrangement."* COW structural sharing is precisely the recommended fork mechanism — the real caution is that a version must stay **independently mutable** (S7d: distinct document tumbler; P3: its arrangement is mutable), not **frozen**. Fix: distinguish a *frozen immutable snapshot* (a re-deliverable handle for R7 caching, and an extension beyond R4's current-only contract) from a *mutable persistent version-fork* (structural-sharing clone that can diverge). Tell the builder not to **freeze** a version — not to avoid COW for it.

**4. "Implementation approaches → Repeatability": key the delivery cache on the consulted *slice*, not the whole arrangement.** The advice *"key any delivery cache on the consulted arrangement value"* is over-conservative against R7, whose hypothesis is equality of the restriction `Σ.M(dⱼ)|⟦σⱼ⟧` — the slice — not the whole map. The digest's own prose already says "repeatability holds only while the consulted **slice** is unedited," so this is a slip. Keying on the whole-arrangement value invalidates the cache on any edit, even ones outside the span; keying on the consulted restriction yields valid hits across untouched-slice edits. Sharpen to: key on the consulted restriction/slice.

**5. (Minor) "Guarantees" vs "Decisions for the builder": "the one … failure" names two different gates.** "Guarantees" calls the **allocation** precondition *"the one legitimate failure,"* while "Decisions for the builder" calls the **access/open** check *"the one sanctioned failure point."* These are two distinct gates — (1) the in-model well-formedness precondition (allocated document / well-formed span), a malformed-request rejection, not a delivery fault; (2) Green's out-of-model open/access gate. Since the note leaves "when may delivery fail outright" genuinely open (OQ2), be crisp: those two are the only failure points, and gaps/unbound positions never fail.

## Solid as-is (don't touch)
The R6 coalescing analysis ("never coalesce across a gap," scoped correctly to the *bindable slice*) is careful and correct; the R7 sufficiency-not-biconditional → don't-invalidate-on-byte-identity insight is exactly right (modulo item 4's slice granularity); the no-hash-addressing / no-global-sort / no-dedup guards are well-grounded in S4/R5/R8; and the Lampson-hint treatment of `m_S(d)` and the content index, plus "don't pay for provenance you don't deliver," are well-judged. Leave these.
