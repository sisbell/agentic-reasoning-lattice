This digest is strong and unusually well-grounded — most of it needs no change. Three contributions are genuinely sharp and should be left alone: the evidence-driven riders in §3 (Green's link-ISA is a *dedup* key only, the tiebreak among distinct links at one content boundary is a traversal artifact, so the (boundary, address) composite W0/W1 demand is a builder obligation Green does *not* already realize — a correct use of the evidence to overturn the note's "de facto low-order key, not a traversal accident" over-claim); the exact-match-scan-vs-key-cut insight (Green's empty-vs-exhausted ambiguity afflicts even an *address* cursor because it lives in the resume mechanism, not the key — which the note's W8 pins only on the position key); and the discoverability-fork analysis (the monotone reading would obviate the whole orphaning/resurrection apparatus). W9c reframed as "a reason to reject the position key, not a guard to implement" is also right and useful. The items below are the corrections worth making, most important first.

**1. §3 mishandles the matched-slot/least-covered distinction — wrong on cost, and it drops the one qualifier that makes it load-bearing.** §3 says the least-covered-tumbler key is "aligned with the content-addressed index, making (B) cheap." But §2 itself states the least-covered key "instead requires a *derived* link → least-covered-tumbler-of-designated-slice index," and that the cheap, native order the spanfilade/(slot, I-address) map hands you is *Green's matched-slot order*, not the least-covered key. So §3 contradicts §2 and mis-assigns the cheapness: native-cheap (B) belongs to the matched-slot order; the least-covered key costs a derived index and is *not* the cheap option. Worse, the digest characterizes matched-slot order as merely "query-dependent" (the riders) and "adequate for windowing a fixed query" (§2), but the note's exact words are "query- *and state-*-dependent" — and the dropped "state-dependent" is precisely the W5 hazard: matched-slot keys on the *matched* (in-image) content address, which moves under partial orphaning (a link's least matched endpoint leaves the image while a higher one remains, so its key slides up *while the link stays in Match*) — a clause-1 cut-point violation that can skip a live link. That is the whole reason the note builds the least-covered key (keyed on the *covered*, permanent address of a fixed slice, immune to image shrinkage). Fix: (a) restore "state-dependent" and tie it to the W5 hazard; (b) note matched-slot order is adequate only against a *frozen* set (W4), not a mutating one; (c) drop "(B) cheap" from the least-covered key and state its cost is the derived index; (d) present the real tradeoff the digest currently hides — native-cheap-but-state-dependent matched-slot vs derived-index-but-state-stable least-covered — so the builder sees that decisions 1 (key) and 3 (index) are coupled, and that the spanfilade's native order serves *neither* recommended permanent key safely.

**2. Guarantees → Termination: "both come free" conflates two unrelated conditions.** "Over mutation it requires finite cumulative tail inflow plus cut-point preservation. With a permanent key both come free." A permanent key makes *cut-point preservation* free and forecloses the W9c re-ascension livelock — it does **not** make *finite inflow* free (that is workload-dependent), and the next sentences half-admit this ("the only remaining non-termination is legitimate unbounded cumulative inflow"), contradicting "both." Reword: a permanent key discharges cut-point preservation by construction and rules out the W9c livelock; finite inflow remains a separate, workload-dependent condition whose violation is genuine unbounded growth (correct non-termination), not a livelock.

**3. Approach (C) hint-cache: its viability hinges on an invalidation signal the substrate lacks, and the digest doesn't say so.** A cache keyed by "(query, state-generation)" needs a cheap, correct way to detect query-relevant state changes — but W3 records that Green has *no generation counter*, and Match is non-monotone in the consulted arrangement, so "invalidated by any state change" is the *hard* part, not a given. Without a validity signal the hit/miss check costs a full re-derivation, which defeats the cache. State that (C) requires adding versioning the substrate does not provide, and that this — not the seek-on-hit — is what determines whether (C) ever pays off.

**4. §2 drops below design altitude and assumes a facility a spec-only repo may not have.** "Rust/`im`, a `links.jsonl` write log, this repo's substrate journaling pattern" names a concrete crate, a filename, and an assumed journaling substrate — implementation bindings, not design, in a repo that is a *spec* repo. The design-level content ("persistent, structurally-shared ordered map; append-only journal; rebuild by replay") is already stated; cut the concrete bindings. The "pending verification" hedge does not rescue altitude.
