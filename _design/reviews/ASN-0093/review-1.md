It is a strong, well-grounded digest — the by-construction/active-gate split, the cursor-as-recomputable-hint reading, and the "defer the enfilade/embedding until the arrangement layer needs it" reasoning are all correct and faithful. The revision list below is mostly sharpening, with two real defects up top.

### Revision list

**1. "Design commitments / Forced," "What must be built / Precondition gates," and "Decisions / Mint vs. validate" — document addresses are *validated*, not minted; the digest's uniform minting framing buries a load-bearing asymmetry and a mandatory gate.**
`K.σ` takes the document address `d` as a *caller input* and only checks it (`d ∉ dom(M) ∧ T4-valid(d) ∧ zeros(d)=2`). There is no minting rule for documents — the note explicitly *defers* document-address origination to the entity-allocation layer ("`K.σ` is the document-registration primitive without the entity-hierarchy machinery … deferred"), which the digest itself lists as a hands-up. So the address model is two-mode: **content/link addresses are minted by the cursor; document addresses are an input the registry validates.** But the digest's "addresses are minted deterministically, not chosen," its "recommend: mint," and its "Precondition gates" (which fold freshness/structural checks into the *optional* "only if you accept caller-supplied addresses" branch) all read as if minting is uniform and validation is the exception. For `K.σ` validation is mandatory, and skipping the `zeros=2 ∧ T4-valid` gate would admit a malformed document that breaks every downstream invariant. Do this: (a) scope "minted deterministically" to content/link; (b) state `K.σ` is validate-only, its `{fresh, T4-valid, zeros=2}` gate mandatory (not the optional caller-supplied path), with document *minting* deferred to entity allocation; (c) in the registry's "what must be built," name the document-level shape (`T4-valid`, `zeros=2`) the registry must enforce.

**2. "Design commitments / Conventional" — the subspace convention's load-bearing core is mis-stated.**
The digest says "only their distinctness and fixedness is load-bearing." But the note constructs the link anchor as `b_L(d) = inc(b_C(d), 0)`, which yields `[d.0.(s_C+1)]` and equals `b_L(d)` *only because* `s_L = s_C+1` — and that exact step appears in the L1c first-emit conformance witness `(d, b_C(d), b_L(d), ℓ)`. So the **consecutive/sibling relation `s_L = s_C+1` is also load-bearing**, not just distinctness and fixedness. The digest's own "a third subspace needs the axiom revisited" only makes sense *because* of this consecutive structure (`s=3` would be `s_C+2`, anchor `inc(b_L,0)`) — an internal tension. Fix: the load-bearing properties are distinctness, fixedness, **and** that the identifiers are consecutive integers each reachable by `inc(·,0)` off the previous anchor (the property that lets the allocator produce every anchor with its own inc-steps and underwrites C1c/L1c).

**3. "Design commitments / Forced" — "Identity is by address (origin)" mislabels the identity key.**
Identity is the *full element tumbler*, not `origin(·)`, which is only the home-document projection — many distinct addresses share one origin (`a` and `a'` in the worked example both have `origin = d`). The body ("the tumbler it was allocated at") is correct; the parenthetical "(origin)" is wrong and, on the single most foundational commitment, could mislead a builder into keying identity on the home document. Drop "(origin)" or replace with "(the full allocated address)."

**4. "Implementation approaches / Indexing" — the predecessor/scan justification cites the wrong lemma.**
"max under `d`'s subspace" and the prefix-range scans rest on **ChainPrefixExtension** (every same-origin address shares the prefix `b_C(d)`, so the max is the lex-greatest tumbler under that prefix, and a prefix scan returns exactly `d`'s content), not on ChainMembershipForOrigin's *contiguity* (no gaps in chain index), which is what the digest invokes with "a contiguous range under the anchor." The engineering conclusion (ordered map, predecessor-below-bound, prefix scan, = `findpreviousisagr`) is right; tighten the justification to the prefix bound.

**5. (Sharpening) "How it fits" — surface that the substrate deliberately *splits* what Green fused.**
Per the evidence, Green's `CREATELINK` (`docreatelink`) performs the I-space link allocation *and* the POOM/arrangement `docopy` in one operation across three layers; this substrate's `K.λ` does only the allocation and holds `M` in frame (`M(d)=∅`). That decoupling is precisely *why* "coupling constraints" is a named deferral. One line here would stop a builder — especially one porting from Green — from re-fusing allocation with arrangement-insertion prematurely or assuming `K.λ` must touch `M`.
