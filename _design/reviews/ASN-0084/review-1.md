Strong digest overall — it nails the core story (rearrange as a content-preserving bijection on M(d), referential integrity/permanence unbreakable by construction, partiality as a hard gate), its central approaches are sound (I checked: the implicit-position splice does exactly realize R-P1/R-P2/R-S1–3, and merge keyed on I-adjacency is structural, not value-based, so no dedup-vs-identity violation), and it uses the Green evidence well (cross-subspace corruption, `abort()`-on-straddle, `makeoffsetsfor3or4cuts`/`rearrangecutsectionnd`/`tumbleradd`, the `ran`-invariance justification for Green's commented-out index update). The defects are mostly wording/grounding/altitude, with one genuine accuracy slip.

**1. "What this is" + "Design commitments (Forced)": the "held-fixed / fixed middle" characterization of the 4-cut swap is inaccurate and self-contradictory.** Per the note's Displacement Analysis, μ is *not* positionally fixed — it displaces uniformly by `w_β − w_α` (forward when `w_β > w_α`, backward when `w_β < w_α`, fixed *only* when `w_α = w_β`). The note spends **three separate worked swap examples** on exactly these three μ sub-cases; "fixed middle" erases the lesson they exist to teach, and contradicts the digest's own later line that "each region (α, μ, β, …) moves by one constant offset." What is actually forced is `w_μ ≥ 1` and μ's *centrality and internal order*, not its position. Fix: reword to "a middle block that stays *between* the two swapped outer blocks and keeps its internal order, but itself shifts by `w_β − w_α`," and surface the μ sign-flip as a concrete builder pitfall — the implicit-position splice absorbs it for free, but an absolute-V-start implementation must compute it with the correct direction (this is the single most error-prone part of the displacement arithmetic).

**2. "Durability and recovery": reground it, and stop implying the note mandates it.** (a) The "this repo's proven substrate … the `links.jsonl` + `paths.json` pattern" anchor is ungrounded — it is in neither the note nor the Green evidence and refers to an unrelated storage substrate; drop it. The append-only-journal-plus-checkpoint advice is fine, but ground it in what the evidence actually supplies: Green's *content* store (permascroll/granfilade) is append-only/immutable, and Green logs each document mutation via `logbertmodified` (orglinks.c). (b) Durability is listed flat under "What must be built" beside genuinely note-mandated builds (validator, transform, merge), but the note is a pure Σ→Σ' spec and commits to none of it — mark durability explicitly as a system-integration inference beyond 0084, so a builder doesn't read it as a 0084 obligation. (Subspace confinement, by contrast, *is* note-implied via R-NS/R-FRAME, so keep that as a real build item.)

**3. Throughout "Implementation approaches": the digest silently defaults to a Rust + `im` + RRB-vector target** (`im::Vector`, structural-sharing history, the persistence bullet, the builder-decisions bullet). The note implies no language or library; naming a specific crate and structure drifts toward code altitude and bakes in an assumption the note doesn't support. Either explicitly flag Rust+`im` as *one worked instantiation*, or abstract the requirement to "a persistent ordered sequence with O(log n) split/concat and structural sharing" and demote `im::Vector` to an example.

**4. Sharpening — "Canonicalization (merge)": connect the two run-count bounds you already give instead of deferring run-count wholesale to the open questions.** You bound the increase (≤ one new run per interior cut, ≤ 4) and the decrease (seam merges) in separate places, then punt "run-count bounds" to the note's open questions. The operation-local net bound is immediate and useful for storage sizing: `|canonical runs|` changes by at most `±O(cuts)` (≤ +4 from splits, minus seam-merges). State it — it's the practical answer even though the *general* characterization the note asks for stays open. (Also worth one line: strict ordering CS2, not just coverage, is what forbids degenerate zero-width regions — that's why every region width ≥ 1.)

**5. Sharpening (minor) — "The arrangement map": the POOM wording reads as a contradiction.** The digest calls the flat sorted span list "exactly the udanax-green POOM's content" and then calls the enfilade "Green's actual structure" — both can't be the POOM. Clarify: the POOM's *logical content* is the span/run set; its *physical structure* is the enfilade (cumulative-width tree). One clause fixes it.
