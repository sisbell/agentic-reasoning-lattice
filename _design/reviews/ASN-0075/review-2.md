It is a strong, well-grounded digest — the set-algebra reformulation, the torn-read concurrency analysis, and the green-source claims all hold up. The defects below are mostly one accuracy slip and several sharpenings.

## Revision list

**1. Design commitments → "Classification is at I-address-set granularity": the attribution "Forced by `R`'s granularity, not by the predicate forms" is wrong — it contradicts the note and the evidence.** The note credits the *predicate forms*: "Each predicate reads only the *set* membership condition `a ∈ ran(M(d))`, never the number of V-positions." The binding masker is `CURRENT`/`DELETED` reading the *range* of `M(d)` (a set, which discards V-position multiplicity) — and that conjunct never consults `R` at all, so an occurrence-aware `R` would not change the result. The evidence agrees the masking lives on the *arrangement/POOM* side: green's comparison "collapses to I-space set membership" and "operates purely on set membership in the V-space arrangement" (Q1), not on the provenance side. Rewrite to credit the predicate forms (set-membership of `ran(M(d))`) as forcing; demote `R`'s element×document coarseness to a true-but-secondary corroborating fact, and delete "not by the predicate forms." As written the digest has the causation backwards on a commitment it marks "forced."

**2. Surface the witness-gated coverage boundary, and connect the document-forward `R` projection you already mandate to the out-of-scope no-witness recovery path.** Both output halves require `CURRENT(a, partner)`, so SHOWDELETIONS is *blind to any deletion lacking a live witness* — content deleted from both compared documents (or from every document) is DELETED yet appears in neither half. The note states this in the worked example ("if `d_B` had also deleted `b`… `b` would not appear") and elevates it to open questions 2–3. The "Recoverability" guarantee is correct only for *reported* addresses; state the coverage limit beside it. Then note that the doc-forward projection the digest already requires yields the no-witness primitive directly — `Rproj(d) \ ran(M(d))` is *all* content deleted from `d`, witness or not — so the substrate the builder is told to build is precisely the basis for the open-Q2/Q3 path, with the direct-I-address COPY (Q17) as its restoration leg (no-witness content has no V-position to copy from, which is why the V-space COPY interface alone can't reach it). This is the most valuable "what's out of scope and why" the digest currently leaves implicit.

**3. Implementation approaches → "The computation": the disjointness short-circuit is sound, but billing it as the answer to open question 1 conflates necessary with sufficient.** Non-disjoint `R`-projections is *necessary* for non-empty output (D-DISJ's contrapositive — correctly derived, and correct for the screening use that rules unrelated documents *out*) but not *sufficient*: a shared address CURRENT in both documents contributes to neither half. Soften "the shared-content history that predicts non-empty results is *precisely* non-disjoint `R`-projections" to a necessary screening condition, and give the actual non-emptiness predicate — some `a ∈ Rproj(d_A) ∩ Rproj(d_B)` that *diverges in arrangement* (in one of `ran(M(d_A))`, `ran(M(d_B))` and absent from the other).

**4. (Minor) The P4a faithfulness point is mis-labelled as an "active" SHOWDELETIONS guarantee and stated three times.** SHOWDELETIONS reads `R`; it cannot write it, so "append only for genuine placements" is ASN-0047's maintenance obligation — a *dependency* this operation consumes, not a guarantee it upholds. The digest already says so in "What must be built," then re-frames it as "active downstream" in the guarantee table and repeats it in commitment 4 and the journal paragraph. Collapse to one statement: DELETED is honest *because* ASN-0047 maintains P4a; flag spurious provenance as a foundation-level failure mode, not a row in "Guarantees to uphold."
