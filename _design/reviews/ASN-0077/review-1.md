This is a strong digest — accurate on the forced/conventional split for the core commitments, well-grounded in the Q11–Q17 evidence for the index discussion, and at the right altitude in its build list and "how it fits." The defects are concentrated in two evidence-anchored paragraphs that misfire, one overstated "forced" commitment, and one missing guarantee. Revision list, most-important first:

**1. "Implementation approaches → Permanence/recovery": cut the `links.jsonl + paths.json` identification — it is ungrounded and almost certainly a category error.** The abstract point ("permanence falls out of append-only content allocation with no address reuse," P0/P3) is correct and should stay. But the concrete claim that content writes "go to *this repo's* `links.jsonl` + `paths.json` registry, recovered by replay" and that "the journal already enforces it" is supported by neither the note nor the evidence — and these are the *spec-authoring* repo's artifacts (the note-link graph), not the docuverse substrate's content store. The grounded concrete anchor for Green's append-only content store is the **permascroll/granfilade** (cf. Q19's `2.1.0.x.x.x` permascroll addresses), not `links.jsonl`. Replace the repo-file sentence with the invariant itself (assign at write time, never reuse a content address); if you name a structure, name the permascroll.

**2. "Implementation approaches → Pointwise origin": the "allocation-time guard (Green Q19)" misreads the cited evidence, is out of scope, and mis-mechanises the failure — replace it.** Q19 establishes the *opposite* of a risk: a non-owned-document version still allocates content under the version *document* (`1.1.0.2.0.1`), a proper document-level tumbler, so the projection yields a document, not an account (the "account" in Q19 is only where the version document's ISA sits in the tree). Also, the projection *forces* `zeros=2` by construction (O0a), so "the zeros=2 invariant is exactly what breaks" is wrong — what rides on allocation discipline is O0(b) semantic correspondence, which Q14/Q19 confirm holds. And SHOWORIGIN is read-only (O10): an allocation-time guard belongs to the allocator (ASN-0047 DocumentAllocationDiscipline), not this operation. Replace with the genuinely useful Q19 fact: **versioning a non-owned document mints a new document under the versioner's account; its *copied* body still attributes to the original (shared I-addresses), but *native* edits in the version attribute to the new version document, not the original.** Frame document-scoped allocation as an inherited dependency, not a guard this builder adds.

**3. "Guarantees to uphold": add span-containment monotonicity (O8/O12) — it is missing and load-bearing for your own design.** The note proves ⟦σ₁⟧⊆⟦σ₂⟧ ⇒ origins(σ₁)⊆origins(σ₂) (I-span O8, V-span O12) as the formalization of Nelson's scale-invariance (name a chapter's home ⇒ name a character's home, Q8). The digest lists *state*-monotonicity (O6) but not *span-size* monotonicity. This is exactly the cross-scale consistency invariant binding the three I-span paths you propose (enumerate / boundary-walk / single-origin fast path): a narrow sub-span's origins must be a subset of any enclosing span's. State it, and flag it as the property those paths must jointly satisfy.

**4. "Design commitments → forced": "Any index or cache is a hint, never authoritative (O3)" overstates the note and collides with your own caching advice.** The note's summary explicitly sanctions index/cache realizations — "spanfilade lookup, granfilade traversal, or per-block `homedoc` records" — requiring only that "the abstract guarantees they deliver must coincide." What is *forced* is consistency with the projection (the address is the canonical definition; on conflict the address wins), not non-authoritativeness. "Never consulted as authoritative" also contradicts your later recommendation to keep a `homedoc` cache "to avoid a cheap parse" — consulting a cache to skip recomputation *is* trusting it. Reframe: the address is the source of truth and every acceleration structure must stay recomputable/validatable against it (forced); throwaway-hint vs. trusted-but-reconcilable cache is an engineering choice (recommended), not forced.

**5. "What must be built → V-span resolver/admissibility": the V-span precondition is `WF_V` (six conjuncts), not just "positions present."** The digest reduces admissibility to conjunct (vi). But the note is emphatic that a V-span must be **level-uniform and confined to a single subspace** (the C0/C0a argument; "Mixed V-spans … are excluded"), plus the common-depth coupling (v) — and these are load-bearing, since the block-decomposition / one-origin-per-block reasoning assumes a single-subspace, level-uniform input. Surface level-uniformity (ii) and single-subspace confinement as part of what the resolver validates, alongside the presence/contraction check.

**6. Two places: "single-origin / singleton is the common case" runs against the note's explicit stance — temper it.** The note states flatly that multi-origin "is not a degenerate case — it is the expected case for any document of mixed authorship." The digest frames single-origin as "the common case" twice (I-span resolver; fast-path justification). It is typical for *narrow, document-scoped* queries but not for whole-document attribution — so the single-origin fast path pays off mainly on scoped queries. Say *which* queries it helps rather than asserting single-origin is common in general.

**7. "Design commitments → conventional": "returning the set vs a 'mixed' sentinel" is closer to forced than conventional.** The note defines the output *as* the enumerated set, and O1.2 (multi-origin diagnostic) turns on knowing *which* documents are present — a sentinel would not meet the postcondition. At most the *report granularity* (full set vs. summary) is a choice; the enumerated set is fixed. Reword accordingly.

**8. "How it fits → above it": surface the discovery-probe use of the second wp.** The note presents `wp(SHOWORIGIN_V, d_q ∈ result)` as "the operational instrument by which a reader treats SHOWORIGIN as a discovery probe" (confirm/refute that material from a specific `d_q` is present in a region). The digest lists attribution consumers but omits this inverse "is `d_q` in here?" mode — a cheap, concrete capability worth naming.

Solid as-is, leave untouched: the "What this is" framing; the build list; the evidence-grounded separation of an *origin* index (key on the allocation event) from Green's *containment* spanfilade (write-only, superset including transcluders and deleted docs, ascending-tumbler order with no origin priority); the "stateless derivability is the one guarantee that requires active enforcement" insight; and the call to document that there is no "originating-document-first" ordering.
