The digest is strong where it counts most: it cleanly separates **origin (allocation)** from **containment (the spanfilade)** — the exact trap the evidence shows snared Green's `find_documents_containing` — grounds every Green-source claim in the evidence channel rather than inventing source behavior, and keeps Lampson discipline throughout (cheapest mechanism, hint-not-authoritative index, invalidation-free per-block cache). Altitude is well-judged: it names Green structures for grounding without sliding into types or signatures. The improvements below mostly complete the V-span edit-dynamics story and remove one recurring confusion.

**Revision list (most important first):**

1. **V-span edit-dynamics are half-built — O14 is missing and the O11 positive result is unstated** (*Implementation approaches* / *Guarantees* / *What must be built*). The digest invokes O13 (contraction → admissibility loss) three times but never O14 (reorder/K.μ~ → non-preservation), and never states the O11-family result that arrangement *extension* preserves `origins_V` **exactly** (equality, not mere inclusion). These three are the resolver's complete edit-stability contract, and the note lists O14 inside the explicit "must satisfy O0–O14" obligation with its own worked-example probe. The omission leaves the caching guidance dangling: the digest correctly says the per-block `homedoc` origin cache "never needs invalidation," but never warns that a cached *V-span result set* is safe only across extensions and must be dropped on reorder (O14) or contraction (O13). Add O14 and the extension-preservation result, and state the rule: V-span answers are exactly preserved under K.μ⁺/K.μ⁺_L, inadmissible after K.μ⁻, and incomparable across K.μ~ — per-block origin caches are always safe; result-set caches are not.

2. **"Single-origin" is repeatedly mis-equated with "narrow"** (*What must be built* — I-span resolver; *Implementation approaches* — fast path / wp). "singleton (typical of narrow, document-scoped queries)" and "the single-origin weakest-precondition characterises exactly the narrow … queries" are wrong: a single-origin span can be arbitrarily **wide** — a whole chapter natively authored in one document is single-origin. The note's wp characterises "any I-span whose allocated content lies wholly under one document's allocation prefix," with no width bound, and the digest's own Guarantees section asserts scale-invariance (O8/O12, Nelson's "any specific word or character"). As written it contradicts itself and undercuts a core theme. Drop "narrow"; say "any span whose allocated content comes from one document — a natively-authored chapter as much as a single character." (The endpoints-share-a-prefix fast path is in fact *sound* — document prefixes occupy disjoint, contiguous tumbler ranges — and it works *because of* scale-invariance, so the unnecessary "with known single-document allocation between" hedge can go.)

3. **Empty-document V-span inadmissibility and the I/V empty-result asymmetry are missing** (*What must be built* — V-span admissibility). The bullet enumerates WF_V conjuncts (ii), (v), (vi) but omits (iii) `V_{u₁}(d) ≠ ∅` — the very conjunct that makes the operation **inadmissible on an empty document**, the note's deliberate contrast with the I-span case where empty intersection is a *legitimate empty result*. A builder writing the admissibility check needs this asymmetry (V-span is document-relative; there must be ≥1 V-position to fix the depth `m`). Add conjunct (iii) and state: empty I-intersection → valid `∅`; empty document → reject. (Separately: "confined to a single subspace" is listed as if it were a check to perform — it is a *consequence* of level-uniformity via C0/C0a, not an independent test; flag it as derived.)

4. **Two entries in Forced/Conventional need tightening.** (a) "The granularity of the report" is filed under *Conventional* but its own text argues the enumerated set is *forced* by the postcondition (O1.2 needs which docs are present) — so it is a forced commitment mis-shelved. Restate: "the result is forced to be the enumerated set; only a summary layer on top is a free choice." (b) "Offering two arities … one could be omitted" overstates: the note presents I-span and V-span as serving *different inputs* (address-space vs the reader's document-relative view — "a reader more naturally has access to a V-span"), so they are not interchangeable. Recast: exposing both is conventional, but each answers a distinct query mode and neither trivially yields the other.

5. **Citation error** (*Implementation approaches* — pointwise origin). "(ASN-0047 DocumentAllocationDiscipline)" misattributes the foundation: the note sources both DocumentScopedAllocation (S7a) and DocumentAllocationDiscipline (S7d) to **ASN-0036**; ASN-0047 supplies the allocator-hierarchy / SubAllocatorBundle facts. Fix the attribution.

6. **Minor precision fixes.** (a) "origin's account ancestor names the owning account, though never a human" — the note says "not necessarily a *known* human," not "never"; an account *may* belong to a human, it just isn't guaranteed. Soften. (b) "a superset including transcluders and even deleted documents" reads as documents that were themselves deleted; per Q17 it is documents that **deleted the content** (the doc persists, the span left its POOM, the DOCISPAN ghost remains) — use the precise phrasing the digest already uses one section earlier. (c) The `homedoc` cache "never needs invalidation" is right, but the justification "because origin is permanent (O5)" is incomplete: O5 is about a *fixed I-address*; cache validity also requires that the cache is **keyed to the block** (which carries its I-content under reorder and vanishes under contraction). State that keying — it is what makes O5's permanence translate into never-stale, and it is the precise reason a V-position–keyed cache would *not* be safe.
